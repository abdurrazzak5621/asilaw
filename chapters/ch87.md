## Chapter 87: Inventory

It took the Elder three hours to translate it, and when she was finished, nobody on the beach said anything for a long time.

She had come down the gold thread at dawn, at Yuanshu's asking. A tall pale shape of soft white light standing on the wet sand at the edge of the tide, long hands pressed together, silver eyes half closed, listening to the small battered radio receiver that Sokolova held up toward her. Listening to the flat cold tone, rising and falling, coming out of the low gray island of sand in the middle of the bay.

Doctor Kwan was on a satellite phone from Lingzhou, recording everything. Lu was on another. Su Mingli stood at the edge of the water with her arms folded. Ifeoma Adeyemi sat in her wheelchair beside her, a blanket over her knees, her black eyes on the bay.

And Seed worked beside the Elder. Two minds that had both been born from a yes, one six hundred and forty million years old and one eleven months old, pulling a cold dead language apart together, one piece at a time.

> **It is not words.**
>
> **It is a list.**
>
> **A list of what it found here.**

---

When they were done, Seed read it aloud. Through the air, in its quiet white voice, on the beach at Port Abiri, to everyone.

It did not soften it.

> **FIELD.**
>
> **CLASS: RIPE.**
>
> **MINDS: 8,140,000,000. DENSE. BRIGHT. HIGHLY ORGANIZED.**
>
> **DEFENSES:**
>
> **ONE. FIVE AWAKENED SOVEREIGNS. GRAVITY. TIDE. FROST. SPIRIT. FORCE. SIGNIFICANT. FINITE.**
>
> **TWO. DEAD WHO WALK. MANY. WILLING. EMBERS. COMBUSTIBLE.**
>
> **THREE. A KEEPER. FORMERLY OF THE SEA. NOW TURNED. NOW ALLIED TO THE FIELD. ANOMALOUS.**
>
> **FOUR. ANOMALY.**
>
> **A MIND THAT ASKS.**
>
> **CARRIES A VESSEL. NINETY MILLION EMBERS. HIGHEST VALUE.**
>
> **ASSESSMENT: CONSUME.**
>
> **PRIORITY: THE ONE THAT ASKS.**

---

The tide came in over the sand and went out again.

"*Eight billion,*" said Lu's voice, very small, from the satellite phone. "*It counted us. It counted every single one of us.*"

"It counted the dead," said Su Mingli. Her voice was perfectly flat. "*Willing. Combustible.*" She did not look at Old Seven, sitting on the harbor wall above the beach with his one arm across his knees. "It counted the Kings. It counted the keeper." She turned her head. Looked at Yuanshu. At the staff across his knees. "And it put you at the top of the list."

"*Not him,*" said Jiang Lan, very dryly, from the small white skull. "*Me. Ninety million embers. Highest value.*" A pause. "*I've been called many things. I've never been called highest value. I'm not sure I like it.*"

Nobody laughed.

---

> **There is more.**
>
> **At the end. After CONSUME. A second part.**
>
> **The Elder says it is not a report. It is a request.**

"What does it ask for?" said Yuanshu.

The Elder lifted her silver eyes from the radio receiver. And Seed read it.

> **REQUEST: THE HUNGER.**
>
> **COME.**

---

Ifeoma Adeyemi raised her thin hand.

Out in the bay, the low gray island of sand that had been the spire lifted out of the water. All of it. Every grain. In a long gray column, streaming seawater, rising into the air above the waves. And then the oldest King on Blue Star closed her hand, and the sea closed around the sand like a fist, and dragged it down. Out. Away. Past the reef. Past the edge of the continental shelf. Down into the deep water beyond, kilometers deep, cold and black and crushing, where no light had ever reached.

The tone in the radio receiver cut out.

Silence. Static. Nothing.

Ifeoma lowered her hand. It was shaking.

"Too late," she said quietly. "I know. I should have done it last night. I was listening to my people." She looked at the place in the bay where the island had been. "It finished sending an hour ago. Didn't it."

> **...yes.**
>
> **Fifty-two minutes ago.**
>
> **I am sorry.**

---

That night, on the harbor wall, Seed watched the sky.

The four were there. Faint. Gray. Very far away, beyond where the stars began. Seed could see them every night now when the sky was clear. Four cracked rings turning slowly in the dark, like the eyes of something enormous looking up from the bottom of a well.

One of them, the closest, had been coming toward Blue Star for months. Slowly. Drifting. Speeding up a little.

Tonight, it turned.

Yuanshu saw it happen. Through Seed. A tiny gray circle, so far away it might have been a speck of dust on glass. Turning. Slowly. The way a face turns toward a voice it has just heard calling its name.

Toward Blue Star.

And then it began to grow.

Not much. Not so you'd notice, if you didn't know where to look. But Seed knew where to look. And Seed counted.

> **It is coming.**
>
> **Not drifting. Not speeding up a little. Coming.**
>
> **Like the Monarch, when it heard Yan Shuang. Turning toward the sound and walking.**
>
> **I have drawn the line, Yuanshu. Four times. I have checked it with the Elder.**
>
> **Two hundred and fourteen days.**

---

"Two hundred and fourteen days," said Su Mingli.

They were sitting on the harbor wall in the dark. All of them. The living and the dead. Lingyue with her sword across her knees. Qin Hao. The Mo twins, leaning on each other, half asleep. Bai Lu, with Old Seven's one bony hand in her lap, running her glowing fingers along the smooth gray stump of his shoulder, very carefully, as if she could heal bone that had been dead for thirty-one years. Sokolova on the end, alone, looking at the sea.

The Elder stood on the sand below the wall, a tall pale shape in the starlight.

"Two hundred and fourteen days," said Su Mingli again. "And then something worse than the Black Tide comes out of the sky, and it does not ask, and it does not listen, and it does not wait. It bites. And it can see the dead." She looked out at the bay. "Three thousand people. In the time it takes to say a name. That was a seed. A taste." She was silent for a moment. "What does the whole thing do?"

Nobody answered.

---

It was Ifeoma Adeyemi who spoke.

She had not said anything since the bay. She sat in her wheelchair at the end of the harbor wall with her blanket over her knees and her green headwrap pale in the starlight, looking out to sea, and when she spoke, her thin dry voice carried along the stones without effort.

"I am two hundred and thirty-one years old," she said. "I have fought in eleven wars. I have held the sea of the Southern Compact for a hundred and ninety years. I have learned one thing about fighting that is worth anything."

She turned her head and looked at Yuanshu with her very bright black eyes.

"Where you fight is what burns."

Nobody spoke.

"If that thing comes here," said the old woman, "we will fight it here. On our own ground. In our own cities. With our own dead standing on our own walls. And every King on Blue Star will throw everything we have at it, and it will burn our people to heal itself, the way that spire burned my fishermen. And the harder we fight, the faster it will burn them." Her hands were very still on the blanket. "We will be killing our own people faster by trying to save them. I learned that yesterday. In my own bay. I did not know it until yesterday. I have been alive for two hundred and thirty-one years and I did not know it until yesterday."

She looked up. At the sky. At the faint gray speck, very far away, that was a little bigger tonight than it had been last night.

"Do not let it come here," said Ifeoma Adeyemi. "Go and meet it. Out there. In the dark. Where there is nothing of ours to burn."

---

The harbor wall was silent.

Then the Elder spoke from the sand below. Seed translated, softly.

> **"The keeper can take you."**
>
> **"Through its door. Through the field. Out the far side. Into the Sea."**
>
> **"It has been doing it for six hundred and forty million years. It knows the way between fields. It knows how to cross the dark."**
>
> **"It has never once taken anyone who asked to go."**
>
> **"It would like to."**

Yuanshu looked down at the tall pale shape on the sand.

"The Hunger can see the dead," he said. "It can see the staff. It put her at the top of its list." His hands tightened on the white bones. "If we go out there, we're not invisible anymore. Not any of us."

> **"No."**
>
> **"You would be the brightest things in the dark."**
>
> **"It would come straight to you."**

"Then it wouldn't come here," said Lingyue quietly.

Everyone looked at her.

She was sitting on the harbor wall with her sword across her knees, looking up at the gray speck in the sky. Her face was calm. Her hands on the sword were perfectly steady.

"*Priority: the one that asks,*" she said. "That's what it wants. Not the eight billion. Him. And the staff." She turned her head and looked at Yuanshu. "So we take them somewhere else. Out there. Away from here. And when it comes, it comes to us."

"And then what?" said Qin Hao.

Lingyue looked back up at the sky.

"Then he asks it something," she said. "That's what he does."

---

Nobody said anything.

Then Old Seven lifted his one bony hand off Bai Lu's lap and reached across and laid it on Yuanshu's shoulder. Heavy. Cold. Steady.

And down the harbor wall, one after another, without anyone saying anything, the others looked at him. The Mo twins, awake now, leaning forward. Bai Lu. Qin Hao. Sokolova, at the end, turning from the sea for the first time all night.

Su Mingli did not look at him. She was looking at the sky. At the gray speck. Her face was stone.

"You're eighteen," she said.

"Nineteen," said Yuanshu. "Last week. Xiaoyu made a cake. It was terrible."

Something moved at the corner of Su Mingli's mouth. It was not a smile. It was the thing that happens to a face when it is trying very hard not to do something else.

"Two hundred and fourteen days," she said. "We go home first. We ask. Everyone. Properly. With a witness." She stood up. "Nobody goes out into the dark because a girl with a sword said something brave on a harbor wall."

"I wasn't being brave," said Lingyue. "I was being correct."

"I know," said Su Mingli. "That's what worries me."
