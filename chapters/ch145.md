## Chapter 145: The Inner Gate

"We don't go through them," said Yuanshu. "We go under."

It was four in the morning in the gatehouse at the bottom of the mountain, and everyone was awake, and the camp table had a map on it that was not Wei Desheng's. It was older. It was drawn on yellowed linen in faded brown ink, and General Qin had taken it out of a steel case under his camp bed that he said nobody had opened in eight years.

"The inner gate," said the general.

He put his one finger on the linen. On a line that ran through the heart of the mountain, under the summit, under the saddle where the fortress stood, from the near side of the pass to the far side. A tunnel. Old. Older than the fortress.

"The Western Pass isn't one pass," said General Qin. "It's two. The road over the saddle, which everyone knows. And the inner gate, three hundred meters down, which the army dug two hundred years ago so that if the saddle fell, they could still move men through the mountain." His finger moved along the line. "It's been sealed since the silver rain. My son died in it. The rift opened inside the tunnel, eight years ago, and he held the inner gate for four hours with a company of the Fourth while they sealed it behind him." He took his finger off the map. "Nobody's been down since."

"Three hundred meters," said Yuanshu. "The seeds are four hundred."

> **The tunnel passes eighty meters above them. And a hundred and ten meters to the north.**
>
> **From the tunnel floor, the rock between it and the seeds is old and cracked. The rift damaged it. Kwan's instruments would say so. I can feel it through the staff.**
>
> **The dead could dig eighty meters through cracked rock.**
>
> **In the Banked Field, the seeds would not see them coming.**

---

"The dead," said Qin Hao. "Going under the mountain. Into the seeds. While the Ninth sits in the courtyard on top."

"The dead don't go anywhere near the Ninth," said Yuanshu. "Article Seven. They're not going up against the living. They're going down against the seeds." He looked at Dabao. "Nobody uses the dead against anyone. Nobody touches the four hundred."

Dabao let out a breath he had been holding since the switchback.

"And when the seeds die," said Lingyue, "the spires die. No voice. Nobody gets kept. Nobody gets hurt."

"Nobody gets hurt," said Yuanshu, "unless the seeds notice what's happening before they die."

> **They will notice.**
>
> **When the dead reach them, they will be attacked, and they will know it. Even blind. Even through ash.**
>
> **And when a seed is cornered, it does what the sphere did in Port Halvard. It pulls everything it can reach, all at once. It grows roots toward anything warm.**
>
> **The four hundred are kneeling around the spires, Yuanshu. The spires are the seeds' arms. They are the nearest warm things on the mountain.**

"So when the dead go in from below," said the general slowly, "the four hundred on top have to be gone."

"Away from the spires. Off the courtyard. Before the roots come for them." Yuanshu looked around the table. "Without killing them. Without hurting them badly. While they're asking to be the first thing it eats."

General Qin Zhenyue looked down at the faded linen map for a long time.

"Then I'll go up," he said. "To the gate. And talk to my men."

---

Nobody tried to talk him out of it. Qin Hao opened his mouth, and his grandfather looked at him, and he closed it again.

"They're the Ninth Mountain," said the general. "I raised that battalion. Thirty-one years ago, on this mountain, out of the ones who were left after the Black Tide. Every officer in it, I promoted. Half the men in it, I've been to their weddings." His face did not move. "Wenli has been my chief of staff for twenty years. He's never once disobeyed an order. He didn't disobey one last night. I never gave one." He picked up his cap from the table. "So I'll give one."

"They won't obey it," said Yuanshu.

"No," said the general. "But they'll listen to it. And while they're listening, your people can be doing whatever they're going to be doing."

---

There was one more thing to do before they went down, and it was the thing Yuanshu had been waiting to do since he arrived.

He asked the mountain.

Not the way he had asked it a year ago, standing on the first terrace with nothing in his hands, shouting at forty-one thousand stones all at once. He did it the way he had learned since. Through the staff. Through the Banked Field. Row by row, with Jiang Lan carrying the question and Seed counting and Adjutant Xu passing it down the lattice and the general sitting on his son's grave as witness.

They could hear him now. That was the thing. Banked, under ash, the embers in the graves were no longer burning fast enough to drown out a voice. They were dim, and frightened, and very tired, the way the minds in the Low Fire had been tired. But they could hear.

*I'm the one who asked you, a year ago. I'm sorry I didn't come back. Something is eating you from below. We've put the fire down. It's still there. I want to go under the mountain and kill it. Will you come? You can say no. You can say quiet. Nobody will ask you twice.*

He did not ask the eight thousand two hundred and fifteen.

He had covered them, the night before, under the Council's ruling. *Shelter is not holding.* He had put out the fire in their graves without asking, the way you put out a fire in a house without knocking. He did not reach for them now. He let the question go past their graves the way water goes past a stone.

It took until three in the afternoon.

> **Yes: 30,614.**
>
> **Only to speak: 1,420.**
>
> **Quiet: 1,046. New. They had said yes, a year ago. Now they say quiet. They are very tired. I let them go.**
>
> **And eleven did not answer.**
>
> **Row eighty. You know which eleven.**

He knew.

---

He raised them at dusk. Thirty thousand six hundred and fourteen, in the Banked Field, in the gray light.

They came up out of the warm wet ground of the mountain the way the dead always came up for him, bone by bone, out of every grave on every terrace from the gate to the cloud. But dim. Every gray flame in every rib cage banked to a dull red point, folded in on itself under a skin of ash, so small that from the bottom of the mountain you could barely see them. Thirty thousand soldiers of the Western Pass standing up in their rows in the dusk, almost invisible, almost silent, with their rusted rifles and their bayonets and their bare bone hands.

Out of the empty graves behind them, nothing came. No roots. The seeds could not see them.

> **It holds.**
>
> **They are up. They are banked. The roots do not know they have gone.**
>
> **Load: eighty-four percent. With the Relay, the Lattice, and the Banked Field.**
>
> **Yuanshu, that is very high. You can carry them. You cannot carry them and do anything else.**

On row two hundred and twelve, the general had stood up off his son's grave.

Colonel Qin Zhaolin had said ONLY TO SPEAK. He did not stand up. The grave stayed closed. But around it, in a ring, the soldiers of row two hundred and twelve had stood up out of theirs, and turned, and were facing the colonel's stone, the way soldiers face an officer they are waiting for an order from.

General Qin looked at them for a long time.

"Fourth Company," he said at last, very roughly. "Inner gate. The colonel's company." He put his hand on his son's stone. "They held it with him for four hours."

---

At nine o'clock that night, they opened the inner gate.

The near-side entrance was a steel door in the rock at the foot of the mountain, two hundred meters east of the cemetery gate, welded shut eight years ago and painted over with a warning nobody had needed to read. Qin Hao cut the welds with a line of white fire so thin and hot that it went through the steel like a knife through paper, and didn't look at anyone while he did it.

Behind the door, a tunnel. Old stone. Old rails on the floor for ore carts. Dark. Going down into the mountain at a slant, toward the place where a rift had opened eight years ago and a colonel had held a gate.

The dead went in first.

Not the Spear Corps. Not tonight. The Fourth Company of the Western Pass, out of row two hundred and twelve, a hundred and sixty of them, banked to dull red points, walking into the tunnel their colonel had died in, in their nines, asking each other every minute, *are you still here.* Behind them the rest. Thirty thousand of the dead of the Western Pass, filing into the mountain in the dark, almost invisible, almost silent, carrying picks and shovels and rifle butts and bare bone hands, to dig eighty meters through cracked rock to the thing that had been eating them.

The Spear Corps stayed with Yuanshu. Old Seven stayed with Yuanshu. They always did.

> **The Lattice is holding. The Relay is holding. Adjutant Xu says the Fourth Company has a sergeant who keeps counting out loud in a mountain accent, and he likes him.**
>
> **Dig time to the seeds: eleven hours. Perhaps twelve.**
>
> **At dawn, Yuanshu.**

At dawn, the dead would break through into the seeds from below.

At dawn, an old general with one arm would walk up the summit road alone to a fortress full of men who wanted to die, and give them an order.

Yuanshu stood at the mouth of the inner gate with the staff in his hands and the Field folded over the whole mountain like a blanket over a fire, and did not sleep.
