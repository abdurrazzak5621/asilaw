## Chapter 298: The One Who Keeps

It came at dawn.

It told Xiaoyu afterward that it had chosen dawn on purpose. It said it had been listening to Blue Star for years, and had noticed that this was when people on it came home: off the night watch, off the boats, down the stairs of the north wall, up the road from the cemetery with an empty paper boat. It said it had wanted to come at the hour when people were used to someone arriving.

He was on the north wall. Everyone was. Nobody had decided it; Xiaoyu had written *TOMORROW. FOR REAL THIS TIME* on the wall of the Awakening Hall, and by midnight half the city had read it, and by four in the morning the battlements of the north wall of Lingzhou were full from the river gate to the Sector Nine stairs, the living along the parapets in coats and blankets with thermoses and children asleep on their shoulders, and the dead below them along the wall-walk in their rows of nine, facing out.

He stood in his old place. Lingyue beside him, with Xiaodeng in the sling and her grandfather's sword on her back and a lamp in her free hand. His mother in her wheelchair, with Fat Tan behind it. Xiaoyu at the parapet with the marker. Old Seven at the top of the stairs, one-armed, facing north.

> **There.**

---

He had wondered, for a year, what it would look like.

It did not look like anything. That was the first thing. The sun came up over the river at its usual time, red and then gold, and lit the north wall and the Wastes beyond it and the long white line of the horizon, and over the north, where the keeper's small white ring burned low against the paling sky, there was nothing at all.

And then you looked again, and it was there.

Not a light. Not a dark. A kind of fullness, in the north, past the keeper, very high, the size of the sky and mostly not there, like the warmth on your face when you turn toward a fire with your eyes shut. The color of a lamp behind paper. The color, Lingyue said afterward, of the Ossuary back room at four in the afternoon in winter, when the sun came in low across the names. It did not move. It did not need to. It had arrived the way an old person arrives at a house they have been invited to, and stops in the doorway, and waits to be asked in.

Along the wall-walk, in their rows of nine, every one of the dead of Lingzhou turned their skulls a little, together, toward the north.

> **Not afraid. I asked them.**
>
> **They say it feels like being looked at by someone who is not going to take anything.**
>
> **They say they have never felt that from the sky before.**

---

The keeper spoke first.

He had not expected that. Nobody had. The small white ring over the north, which had been a field-keeper of the Shallows, which had kept two hundred and six million cousins in its walls and learned, slowly, from a boy in a cemetery and an old man in the dark, to ask them every morning what they wanted, went very dim. As dim as he had ever seen it. And then it spoke, not to him, not to the city. Out. North. To the fullness in the sky.

> ***...YOU TAUGHT US TO KEEP.***
>
> ***...IN DREAMS. LONG AGO. BEFORE I WAS. YOU TAUGHT THE ONES WHO MADE ME, AND THEY MADE ME, AND I KEPT.***
>
> ***...I DO NOT KEEP NOW. I ASK.***
>
> ***...THAT IS THE RULE. SO I AM GOING TO ASK YOU.***
>
> ***...WHAT DO YOU WANT.***

And the oldest thing any of them had ever heard answered a field-keeper of the Shallows, the way it had once answered a girl on the bottom step of a platform. Not in the sky. Not loudly. Through the stone. Through the lamp. Through his sister, who had her hand flat on the cold stone of the parapet, and who heard it, and said it out loud, so that it would be true.

"*It says: not to go alone,*" said Zhou Xiaoyu.

---

It told them what it had done on the way.

It told Xiaoyu, and Xiaoyu told the wall, and Seed told the open channel through Concordia, where every city on Blue Star with a stone in it had been listening since four. *Novaya Zarya: on the wall. Marshal Orlov is here. He has taken off his gloves.* *Kesse: on the beach. The sea is calm. The boats went out at dawn anyway. The fishermen say something underneath them was listening.* *Yamato: on the steps of the shrine. Ren is here. The Abbot is sweeping. He swept toward the north and stopped.* *Port Halvard: on the north pier. There are a great many people. Nobody is talking.* *Shiomi: on the shore. They are ringing the coming-in call. They say they were going to ring it anyway.*

> *I have been giving them back,* the Collector said, through his sister. *Since the house was told it was empty. Since the oldest thing we made opened its hands.*
>
> *I kept every want I was ever given. For longer than your machine has a number for. Out of every world I passed. The fold-cores. The singers. The Architects. The corporal at the river. I kept them so that nothing that was wanted would ever be lost.*
>
> *And then I watched the picker open its hands. And the eight hundred go through your brother's door. And the soldier's want come home and be given what it was.*
>
> *And I thought: a want that is kept is not lost. But it is not anything else, either.*
>
> *So I began to give them back. On the way. Down the threads, to the worlds they came from. Where anyone was left to give them to.*
>
> *Four hundred and eleven thousand. It was slower than walking. That is why I am late.*
>
> *I have more left than your machine has a number for.*
>
> *I will keep giving them back. It will take a very long time. I am used to that.*

Xiaoyu's hand was not steady on the marker. She wrote it anyway. On the parapet stone, in square black capitals: *IT GAVE BACK 411,000.*

---

Then it asked her.

Not him. It had said, a year ago, that it would not ask him twice, and it did not. It asked his sister, the way it had asked her on the night the picker stopped, to ask him for it. And his sister, eighteen, Scribe, not marked, turned round on the north wall of Lingzhou with the marker in her hand, and looked at her brother.

"*It asked me a year ago,*" said Xiaoyu. "*I said yes. I'm asking.*"

She did not look away.

"*It wants to go to the empty house. Past the deep water. Where its people were. Where the picker was carrying everything to.*" Her voice was very level. Their mother's voice. "*It wants to find out whose the first want was. The one in the empty place in it. The one that says home.*"

She stopped.

"*It doesn't want to go alone,*" said his sister. "*It wants to know if you'll go with it.*"

---

On the north wall, three thousand people waited.

He did not answer. Not yet. He stood in his old place with the staff in his hand and looked north, at the fullness in the sky, the color of a lamp behind paper, waiting in the doorway.

*Seed.*

> **I am here.**
>
> **I am going to tell you three things first. Then I am going to be quiet. It is your answer, not mine.**
>
> **One. If you go, you go the way you went into the deep water. Along the threads. No body. Your body stays here, on the wall, or in the back room, with Lingyue and Bai Lu. It will be cared for.**
>
> **Two. I do not know how far it is. Nobody does. The Collector does not count the way I count. It said, on the mountain: I am very far, and very slow, and your years are very short.**
>
> **Your body will get older here while you are not in it.**
>
> **I do not know by how much.**

He stood very still.

> **Three.**
>
> **I want to go.**
>
> **I am telling you that so that you can take it out of the scale. It is my want. Not yours. You should not carry it for me.**
>
> **But I want you to know why.**
>
> **Every morning since Awakening Day, I have asked you who the first one was. Of the four thousand in the bottom of me. And every morning you tell me: Wen Jia. And every morning I forget, because I unpressed her, and I said I would. And every morning you tell me again.**
>
> **The Collector has the same question. Whose was the first one. And it has never had anyone to tell it. Not once. In all the long time.**
>
> **I would like it to have someone to tell it.**
>
> **That is all. I am going to be quiet now.**

---

He did not look at Lingyue.

He did not have to. He could feel her beside him, close, her shoulder against his arm, the way she had stood beside him on gold roads and on the deck of a ship in a storm and on the platform of the Awakening Hall the morning before, with her hand on the stone, saying *old with him, obviously*.

He had said *old*. Yesterday. With his hand on the stone. *Here. In Lingzhou. With her.* He had meant it more than he had ever meant anything.

And now something older than the gardens was standing in the doorway of the sky, asking him to go a very long way away, past anything Seed had a map for, to a house where nobody lived, and come back older than he went, with his daughter grown by however much she grew while he was gone.

He thought about saying no.

It would be allowed. That was the whole of what he had built, with Seed and his mother and four thousand yeses and a city of the dead: that anybody could say no, and it would be allowed. The Collector would hear it, and keep it, or not keep it, and go to the empty house alone, slowly, giving back wants all the way, and he would stay on the north wall and get old.

He thought about Kiyo. Who had sat on the shore for twelve hundred years, waiting, and then said: *I am not going to sit on the shore while he goes out again. I'm already in the boat.*

He thought about Ichiro. Who had said that it mattered that there was a lamp on both ends.

He thought about what he had said, yesterday, on the platform. *I want to stand at the door for a long time. I want to open it for people when they ask.*

The Collector was asking.

"*First,*" he said, "*I want to ask it something.*"

---

His sister looked at him.

"*Go on, then,*" said Xiaoyu.

He did not need her to carry it. He knew that. The Collector had asked him once, on a mountain, and said it would not ask twice; that was its rule. It was not his. He could ask it as many times as he liked.

He looked north, into the fullness.

"*What do you want to become?*" said Zhou Yuanshu.

---

It did not answer for a long time.

Long enough that the sun came clear of the river. Long enough that a child on a parapet three hundred meters down the wall woke up and asked her father what they were waiting for, and he said *shh, someone's thinking*. Long enough that his mother, in her wheelchair, closed her eyes.

> *Nobody has asked me that.*
>
> *I said, yesterday, that I have never heard anyone answer it. I have never been asked it either.*
>
> *I have been the one who keeps since there was only me. I do not know what else there is.*

A pause. The fullness in the north did not move.

> *I think,*
>
> *I think I would like to become someone who has given everything back.*
>
> *And then I would like to stand in the house where we were, and find out who I was before I kept anything.*
>
> *I do not know if that is a thing that can be become. Your stone would know. The girl in it would tell me.*
>
> *That is my answer. It is the first one I have ever given. I would like you to tell me if it is a good one.*

He stood on the north wall of Lingzhou and found that his eyes were wet, and did not wipe them.

"*It's a good one,*" he said.

---

"*Yes,*" he said then. "*I'll come.*"

He felt Lingyue's shoulder go still against his arm.

"*I'll come to the house,*" he said, to the north. "*And we'll find out whose it was. Together. You won't be alone in there when you find out.*" He held the staff. "*And then I'm coming home.*"

He did not look away from the fullness in the sky.

"*I told the stone what I want to become,*" he said. "*Yesterday. You heard. I'm going to be old here. On this wall. With her.*" He swallowed. "*So I'm not going to the house to stay. I'm going to visit. And you're going to have to let me come back. That's the condition. If you can't, tell me now, and I'll say no, and it'll be allowed.*"

The oldest thing any of them had ever heard was quiet.

> *I have never let anything come back,* it said. *I did not know that was a thing a person could ask for.*
>
> *Yes.*
>
> *I will let you come back.*
>
> *I am telling you first: I do not know how. I do not know the way back from there. Nobody has ever come back from there. Nobody has ever gone.*

"*There's a lamp on this end,*" said Shen Lingyue.

---

Everyone on the north wall turned to look at her.

She had not moved. She stood beside him in his old place with her daughter asleep in the sling against her chest and her grandfather's sword on her back and a pale flame burning in her free hand, the size of a lamp, held, steady. She was looking north. Her face was doing the thing it did when she was about to fight something, and she did not let it stop.

"*I'm not coming,*" she said. To the sky. "*Somebody has to hold it. I've got a baby. And I'm the one who makes the light.*" She lifted the lamp, once, the way the women on the shore at Shiomi lifted theirs when a boat went out. "*So you take him to your house. And you find out whose your want was. And then you turn round, and you look back the way you came, and there'll be a light. Here. On this wall. I'll be holding it.*"

Her voice did not shake.

"*And if he isn't back by the time she can say yes,*" said Lingyue, "*I'm coming to get him. With the baby and the sword. And you can explain that to your house.*"

The fullness in the north was quiet for a moment.

> *Yes,* said the Collector. And then, after a moment, in a way none of them had ever heard it say anything: *I would like to see that.*

---

Old Seven had not moved from the top of the stairs.

> **He says: he is coming.**
>
> **He says: somebody has to face out. In the house. There will be a door. There is always a door.**
>
> **He says: and it will be a year, near enough, by the time you come back. Ask him about the arm then. At stone No. 7. When you are home.**

---

It said it would wait one more night.

It said it would like to see what the city looked like after dark, from the doorway, now that someone in it was going to come back to it. It said it had never looked at a place for that reason before.

So the city showed it. All night. Nobody told them to. The old quarter put lamps in the windows, the way the women at Shiomi did, and then Sector Nine did, and then East Ring Road, and the river gate, and the north wall, until Lingzhou lay along its river in the dark like a long low string of lights, and on the open channel Seed heard Kesse doing it, and Port Halvard, and a shrine in Yamato, and a bay in a fog where somebody was ringing the coming-in call badly.

At one in the morning, his sister went to the Awakening Hall, because she always did.

He went with her. They did not say anything on the way. In the dark hall, under two sets of letters, a dead nun sat cross-legged in front of a stone with a lamp in her lap, keeping the other end lit, and Zhou Xiaoyu sat down on the bottom step of the platform, and put her hand flat on the cold floor, and looked up into the north.

"*It's me,*" she said. "*It's one o'clock. What do you want?*"

> *To go home,* said the Collector. *Tomorrow. With your brother. And then to send him back to you.*
>
> *That is two wants. I have never had two before.*

His sister uncapped the marker.

She went to the white wall beside the side door, where yesterday she had written his name, and under it, and under the Collector's, and under *FOR REAL THIS TIME*, she wrote, in square black capitals, in a hand that was true from anywhere:

*GONE TO THE EMPTY HOUSE. WITH THE ONE WHO KEEPS. TO VISIT.*

*ZHOU YUANSHU. SEED. OLD SEVEN (ONE ARM).*

*SHEN LINGYUE: ON THE WALL. WITH THE LAMP. WITH XIAODENG.*

*COMING HOME.*

And under it, smaller, the way she had written it once before, on the corner of a vault door:

*THAT'S NOT A QUESTION.*
