## Chapter 15: Hundred-Arrow Lin

There was a story every child in Lingzhou knew.

On the last night of the Black Tide, when the north gate fell, an archer climbed the old signal tower above it with three quivers and no way down. She had a hundred arrows. By dawn there were a hundred beasts dead at the foot of the tower, each with one arrow in the eye, and not a single shaft had fallen to the ground unbloodied.

The children called her Hundred-Arrow Lin. They played her in schoolyards with sticks and rubber bands. Most of them assumed she was a legend.

She was not a legend. She had a daughter.

---

The old signal tower stood at the north end of the city, where Lingzhou's wall bent around to face the Wastes.

It was forty meters of gray concrete, square and plain, like a chimney with no factory. Nobody used it anymore. Ivy had climbed halfway up one side and given up. The top floor, where the windows had been, was stained black, as if the tower had been dipped in soot up to its neck, and the eight narrow slits around its crown had been covered over with rusted steel plates. At its foot, a small bronze plaque, polished bright by thousands of children's fingers.

*HUNDRED-ARROW LIN. SHE HELD THE NORTH.*

At six in the morning, the wind off the Wastes came across the wall smelling of cold glass and dust.

Captain Lin Wen of the Lingzhou Defense Corps was forty-four years old, with a sharp face and very short hair and the stance of someone who had spent her life standing on walls. She was waiting at the foot of the tower with her arms folded and a folded Accord form sticking out of her breast pocket. She did not say good morning.

General Han had arranged it. He stood off to one side now, uncharacteristically quiet. A little further off, on a folding stool she had brought herself, sat Sister Ning, with an unlit candle in her lap.

"My mother's not in the cemetery," Captain Lin said, before anyone could say good morning. "They never got her down. The tower was surrounded for three days after, and when they finally cleared it there was nothing left to bring down. So they sealed the top floor and put a plaque on it." She nodded upward at the old concrete tower, forty meters high, stained black at the top. "She's still up there. In the stone."

"I know," said Yuanshu. "I can feel her from here."

That was true. Standing at the foot of the tower felt like standing under a drawn bow.

> **Residue concentration at the top floor: extreme.**
>
> **Not Death, mostly. Something sharper. An Intent I have not seen yet.**
>
> **Archery. Or something past archery. It does not feel like "shooting." It feels like "arriving."**
>
> **And there are no bones up there. None.**

Captain Lin looked at him for a long moment.

"I was thirteen," she said. "I watched from the shelter. I could see the tower lit up from the inside every time she drew. I counted. I lost count around sixty." Her voice did not shake once. "I've spent thirty-one years wishing I could ask her one thing. When I saw you on the news, I thought: he could let me ask. And then I thought: what if she's angry at being woken up? What if she's not *her* anymore?" She unfolded her arms. "So I came to tell you. If she says no, that's the end of it. I don't want a weapon made out of my mother."

Yuanshu nodded once.

"Neither do I," he said. "Come up with me. You can ask her yourself."

---

The stairs inside the tower were narrow and crumbling. The top floor had been sealed with a steel plate welded over the doorway. General Han tore it off its hinges with one hand, set it carefully aside, and stayed outside.

The room beyond was small and round, with eight narrow arrow slits facing every direction. The walls were black. The floor was black. In the center of the floor, where an archer would have stood to turn and shoot from every slit in sequence, the concrete had been worn into two smooth hollows the shape of feet.

There were no bones. There was nothing at all.

And yet the room was full.

> **She is everywhere in here. Her residue is in the walls, the floor, the slits. When she died there was nothing left of her body, so the echo soaked into the stone instead.**
>
> **I can call her. But there is nothing for her to rise into.**

*What about the arrows?*

> **…**
>
> **There is one. Under the floor, in a crack, by the north slit. An iron arrowhead. The hundred-and-first.**
>
> **She did not get to shoot it.**

Yuanshu knelt by the north slit, pried at a crack in the concrete with his fingers, and drew out a rusted iron arrowhead no longer than his thumb.

Captain Lin made a sound in her throat.

He held it in his palm, between both of them, and reached for the echo.

"Archer Lin," he said quietly. "Your daughter is here."

---

The temperature in the room dropped.

Not cold, exactly. *Still.* The stillness of a breath held at full draw. Dust lifted off the floor in a slow spiral and hung in the air, and the eight arrow slits each let in a single beam of morning light that turned to meet in the center of the room, where the two worn footprints were.

And between one heartbeat and the next, she was there.

Not bones. Something thinner. A shape made of dust and gray light and the iron arrowhead, standing in the footprints she had worn into the floor thirty-one years ago. Tall. Narrow. A bow drawn in her hands that wasn't really there.

She turned her head, and looked at her daughter.

> **Will detected. Strong. Very clear.**
>
> **She is asking something. It is not a question for you.**

Yuanshu did not try to translate. He reached out and took Captain Lin's hand and set it, gently, on the arrowhead in his palm, so that both of their hands were on it.

The captain gasped.

He never knew what passed between them. He didn't try to know. He kept his eyes on the floor and let the thread run through him and into her, and for almost a minute nobody in the room said anything at all.

When he finally looked up, Captain Lin Wen of the Lingzhou Defense Corps was crying without any sound, her other hand pressed over her mouth, and the gray figure in the center of the room had lowered its bow.

"She asked if I made it to the shelter," the captain whispered. "That's all. Thirty-one years. She just wanted to know if I made it to the shelter." She laughed, wetly. "I told her I'm a captain now. She said my stance is terrible."

Yuanshu waited.

Captain Lin wiped her face hard with the back of her wrist and stood up straight.

"She says yes," she said. "She says she has one arrow left, and she'd like to shoot it at whatever's coming." The captain looked at the gray figure, then back at Yuanshu, and her voice went hard and level again. "She also says you'd better be worth it."

---

**[Calamity Raise]**

> **No body. The arrowhead will be her anchor.**
>
> **The core will be unusual. I am improvising.**
>
> **Forging.**

This time the pain did put him on both knees. The white nail went in behind his eyes and kept going. Blood ran from both nostrils. Captain Lin grabbed his shoulder to keep him upright.

> **[Law Core forged: The Hundred-and-First (Arrow Intent 24% + Death 21.1%)]**
>
> **[Bound Undead: "Hundred-Arrow Lin" — Commander. Permanent. Grows with host.]**
>
> **[Special: Anchored to relic. Form: Shade.]**

The gray figure drew in on itself, condensing out of the dust and the light, until what stood in the center of the room was something like a woman made of smoke and ash, holding a bow made of the same, with a single iron arrowhead glowing dull red at the tip of a nocked shaft.

She turned to the north slit.

Drew.

And loosed.

The arrow left the tower with no sound at all. Seed tracked it for exactly two point one seconds before it went out of range.

> **Target: a crow, perched on a power line, 2.4 kilometers north.**
>
> **She hit it in the eye.**
>
> **She did this to make a point.**

The arrowhead appeared back on her string a heartbeat later, as if it had never left.

Outside the door, General Han Tieshan, who had seen a great many things in forty years of war, quietly said a word that was not suitable for a children's story about Hundred-Arrow Lin.

Far below, at the foot of the tower, Sister Ning struck a match and lit her candle.

> **Three Law Cores.**
>
> **Spear. Shield. Arrow.**
>
> **This is the beginning of an army.**

Yuanshu wiped his face, stood up with Captain Lin's help, and looked at the archer made of ash.

She looked back at him for a long, measuring moment.

Then she nodded once, the way you nod at a recruit who might, someday, be acceptable.

He decided to take it.

---

At ten that night there was a knock on the door of the Tianshu training hall.

Dabao came in in his pajamas, carrying his tower shield, the gouges from Bonefield Hollow still across its face. He had been told to come at once and not why. He stopped dead in the doorway.

Iron Wall Zhao stood in the middle of the white floor with the empty black shield on his back. He turned his skull slowly toward Dabao and took in the pajamas, the shield, the bare feet. He tilted his head.

"Dabao," said Yuanshu, from the wall. "This is Captain Zhao Gangfeng. He held the north gate for eleven hours in the Black Tide. He's going to train you."

Dabao's eyes filled up instantly. "*Me?*"

> **Captain Zhao's intent: "This one is soft."**
>
> **A pause.**
>
> **"But he came when the boy called, at night, in his sleeping clothes, carrying his shield. Good. That is the whole job."**

Zhao took his stance. Dabao wiped his eyes on his pajama sleeve, set his feet, and raised his shield.

The first blow from the dead captain sent him skidding six meters across the floor on his back.

He got up laughing.

"*AGAIN!*" Dabao bellowed. "*UNBROKEN!*"

Beside Yuanshu, Old Seven creaked softly.

> **Translation: "Oh no. There are two of them now."**
