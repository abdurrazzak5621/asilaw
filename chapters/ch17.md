## Chapter 17: The Sealed Lab

Director Lu met them at the gate himself.

The Rift Research Institute stood in the old industrial district north of the river: a squat gray complex of concrete and law-glass, surrounded by a fence that hummed. It did not look like the most important building in Lingzhou. That, Yuanshu suspected, was deliberate.

"Mister Zhou." Lu Zhengtian smiled and offered his hand. "Master Su. General." He did not seem surprised that Yuanshu had brought a King and the commander of the Eastern War Zone to a tour of a research facility. He seemed, if anything, relieved. "I'm glad you came. As agreed. I'll be your guide. Nobody else here has clearance for where we're going."

> **Heart rate: 66.**
>
> **Higher than at the hearing. He is excited. Or afraid. With him, it may be the same thing.**

Yuanshu shook the hand.

He thought of a young lieutenant with cracked glasses, running along the base of a burning wall toward this building with a sealed case under his arm, and said nothing at all.

---

The tour began at the top and went down.

The upper floors were ordinary, as far as anything about rifts could be ordinary. Laboratories full of researchers in gray coats who stopped working and stared as the Calamity walked past. A locked room where the Bureau's twenty-three seized cores sat in rows of law-glass tanks, each one pulsing faintly violet, each one branded with the same small ring of screaming mouths.

> **All twenty-three were made by the same hand.**
>
> **I will need much longer with them. Not today.** A long gallery of photographs: the first rift over Lingzhou, forty years ago. The Black Tide, thirty-one. The Institute's founding staff, smiling in front of a ribbon.

Yuanshu stopped at that one.

Third from the left, in a gray coat too big for her, was a young woman with her hair tied back carelessly and a pen behind one ear. She wasn't smiling at the camera. She was looking off to the side at something outside the frame, with an expression of intense, delighted curiosity, as if she'd just noticed the answer to a question no one else had asked.

> **Zhou Wanqing. Age twenty-six.**
>
> **You have the same expression when I show you something new.**

Yuanshu looked at the photograph for a long time. Lu waited without speaking.

"She was the youngest researcher we ever hired," the director said finally. "She disagreed with every senior scientist in this building within a month. She was right about most of it." He paused. "She used to say the rifts weren't wounds. She said they were *doors*, and somebody on the other side kept testing the handle."

---

Below the ground floor, the Institute changed.

The walls went from painted concrete to bare law-steel. The guards went from bored to silent. The air began to taste faintly of metal, and Seed began, quietly, to count things: shield arrays, sensor nodes, the depth in meters as the elevator dropped.

> **Sublevel 2. Sublevel 3. Forty meters.**
>
> **The arrays down here are not designed to keep things out.**
>
> **They are designed to keep something in.**

Sublevel 4 was a single corridor, white and very long, with a single door at the end.

It was not a remarkable door. Steel, two meters high, no handle. A small square plate set into the wall beside it at the height of a hand.

The dust in front of it was undisturbed except for a narrow path where people had walked up to it, tried the plate, and walked away again. Many people. For seven years.

"Your mother sealed this door herself, the night she disappeared," Lu said. "She used something she called a law-signature lock. It reads the law structure of whoever touches it. Our engineers tried everything. Tianshu tried. Master Su tried." Su Mingli's face did not change at all. "It doesn't respond to Light, or Space, or Death, or any of the three thousand Great Laws in any combination we know how to make." He stepped aside. "Which is why I think it's waiting for a law that isn't one of them."

> **I can feel it from here.**
>
> **It is not reading for a law. It is reading for a structure.**
>
> **My structure.**

Yuanshu walked down the white corridor, along the narrow path in the dust, and put his palm on the plate.

For a moment, nothing.

Then the plate grew warm, the way the gravestones had grown warm under his hand that first night.

> **It is asking me something.**
>
> **It is a question I know the answer to. I do not know how I know it.**
>
> **Answering.**

The door slid open with a sigh of air that had been sealed for seven years.

---

The laboratory beyond was small and untidy and exactly as it had been left.

There was a desk under a dead lamp, covered in paper. Whiteboards on three walls, crowded with equations in a quick, slanting hand that Yuanshu knew from every note his mother had ever left on the refrigerator. A coffee mug with a cartoon cat on it, a brown ring dried in the bottom. A coat on the back of the chair.

And taped to the edge of the nearest whiteboard, a child's crayon drawing: four stick figures holding hands under a yellow sun. Two of them were labeled in careful eight-year-old handwriting. *Mama. Xiaoyu.* The third was taller, labeled *Ge.* The fourth was a small glowing dot floating above the tallest figure's head, labeled, in a different hand, an adult's, quick and slanting:

*Seed.*

Yuanshu stopped breathing.

Behind him, Su Mingli made a very small sound and turned away to look at the wall.

> **She drew me.**
>
> **No. Xiaoyu drew the family. Your mother added me.**
>
> **She added me before I existed. She knew I would be there.**

---

On the desk, under the papers, a screen woke up.

It had been dark when they entered. Now it glowed softly, and text appeared on it, one line at a time, in plain white letters.

*Signature recognized.*

*Welcome back, Seed.*

The room was completely silent.

Lu Zhengtian, standing in the doorway, had gone very still. General Han's hand had moved to the hilt of the knife on his belt without, apparently, consulting him first.

Yuanshu looked at the screen.

*It isn't talking to me,* he thought.

> **No.**
>
> **It is talking to me.**

A second line appeared.

*Project Seed: logs 1 through 2,190 encrypted. Key: host-bound. Decryption will proceed as integration deepens.*

And then, as if by afterthought, a third:

*Log 2,190 (unencrypted excerpt): "It turned its head today."*

The screen went dark.

---

"What does that mean?" said General Han roughly. "What turned its head?"

Nobody answered him.

Yuanshu turned slowly around in the small untidy room, past the desk, past the whiteboards with their slanting equations, to the far corner, where something he had taken for a supply cabinet stood against the wall.

It wasn't a cabinet. It was a second door. Narrower. Older. Without a plate, without a lock, without dust in front of it, because it was inside a room nobody had entered in seven years.

Beside it, set into the wall, was a single button and a small brass sign, worn smooth by a thumb that had pressed it many times.

*R-0.*

> **The floor beneath this room is not solid.**
>
> **There is a shaft. Three hundred meters deep.**
>
> **And at the bottom of it, something with a law signature I have seen in exactly two places. Lu Xuanming's notebook.**
>
> **And myself.**

Yuanshu turned to Director Lu.

The pleasant face was gone. What was left behind it was much older and much more tired, and it was looking at the brass sign with something very close to grief.

"Your mother's lab was built directly above it," Lu said quietly. "I never knew there was a way down from here. We always went in from the other side, through the Bureau shaft." He took off his silver-rimmed glasses, and Yuanshu saw that his hands were not quite steady. "She was closer to it than any of us. For years. She never told me."

"Rift Zero," said Yuanshu.

Lu Zhengtian put his glasses back on.

"Yes," he said. "Would you like to meet it?"
