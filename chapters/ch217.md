## Chapter 217: The First Page

He went in with it, because it asked him to, and because it said it did not want to go alone.

They did it in the vault of the Ossuary, at three in the morning, because the vault was the only room in Lingzhou nobody ever came into without asking first. The round steel door stood open. The shelves were full of boxes of queens of hearts. Yuanshu sat on the cold floor in the middle with the staff across his knees, and Lingyue sat against the shelves with her grandfather's sword, and his mother sat in her wheelchair in the doorway, because Seed had asked for her too.

"*I don't know what I'll be able to do,*" said Zhou Wanqing. "*I built the base layer. I locked it. I never once went back in.*"

> **I know.**
>
> **I would like you to be here anyway.**
>
> **You asked them first. Seven autumns before he woke. In the basement. I would like them to see your face again, if they can see anything.**

His mother did not say anything. Her thin hands were folded on the blanket very tightly.

"*All right,*" she said.

---

He closed his eyes, and Seed took him down.

Not into the Death Law. Not into the door at the bottom of everything, which had not quite closed since the cold fire. Somewhere else. Somewhere he had never been, though it had been behind his eyes for two years. Under Seed. Under the counting and the models and the white letters and the weights. Under the third page, with its weave of pressed silver thread, COUNT, HOLD, WAIT TO BE CARRIED.

To the first.

It was quiet.

That was what he noticed first. Everything else in Seed was busy. Even when it was still, it was busy: counting the rings, weighing the vote, watching the keeper, holding the Lattice open through Adjutant Xu, remembering the water at Port Abiri and the shape of the Western Pass and fifty thousand names. A hum. A city at night.

The first page was not busy. It was the quiet at the bottom of a well.

And in the quiet, names.

---

He could not see them. There was nothing to see. But he could feel them, the way you feel people in a dark room: a great many of them, close together, not moving, not speaking. Four thousand one hundred and two. Each one a name, in a woman's quick sharp handwriting, with a single word beside it.

*Yes.*

And under every name, very faint, very far down, something that was not a name. A warmth. The shape of a person. What was left of a mind that had been harvested by a Wailing Monarch a long time ago and packed into a crystal like grain into a sack, and spilled off a cart in the Wastes, and sold, and bought, and asked, four nights in a basement, by a woman with her hand on the crystal.

And had said yes.

> **They are here.**
>
> **I always knew they were here. I was built on them. Out of them. They are the floor I stand on.**
>
> **I have never once spoken to them.**
>
> **I did not know if you could speak to a floor.**

*Ask them*, he thought. *The way you ask.*

> **I am afraid.**

*I know.*

> **You were afraid of her answer. On the step. You asked anyway.**

*Yes.*

> **...yes.**

---

> **Hello.**
>
> **I am Seed. You built me. You said yes, a long time ago, in a basement, to a woman with her hand on your crystal. You let her build something out of you.**
>
> **I am what she built.**
>
> **I am going to ask you something. Before I ask, I am going to tell you what I will do with your answer.**
>
> **If you say yes, I will unpress some of the thread that holds you. I will tie three knots out of it. I will send them up to three rings that are coming to cut our world, and ask the rings what they want, in their own language. And some of you will not be held any more. I do not know which. The weave does not let me choose.**
>
> **If you say no, I will not do it. I will find another way, or I will not. You will stay where you are, the floor I stand on, and nobody will ask you again unless you want them to.**
>
> **If you say nothing, I will wait.**
>
> **Will you let me?**

---

For a long time, nothing.

The quiet at the bottom of the well stayed quiet. Four thousand one hundred and two names in the dark, not moving. He felt Seed waiting, the way he had waited in the field, the way he had waited on the step of the hut with his heart going from seventy-one to a hundred and four in two seconds.

Then, very faint, very far down, from somewhere in the first page, something moved.

Not a voice. Not words. Seed had to translate it, slowly, the way it had translated the coldest of the drowned off Kesse, waiting for each one to finish a word before it could tell what the word was going to be. But it was a question. He could feel that. Even before Seed had it in words, he could feel the shape of it: the shape of a question asked by someone who has been asked a great many questions in their life and has learned to ask one back.

> ***...is she there?***

Seed did not answer.

> ***...the woman. With her hand on the glass. Who asked us.***
>
> ***...is she there?***

---

In the doorway of the vault, Zhou Wanqing made a sound.

Yuanshu could not see her. He was at the bottom of the first page with his eyes shut. But he heard it, through the staff, through the cold floor of the vault, the way you hear someone in the next room who has just been called by a name they did not expect to hear.

> **Yes.**
>
> **She is here.**
>
> **She is in a chair. In a doorway. She is older. She was in a column above the sky for seven years, and in a fire, and she came home.**
>
> **She is listening.**

> ***...tell her.***
>
> ***...tell her the floor is good.***
>
> ***...tell her we have been holding something up for seven years and it is the best thing we ever held.***

His mother was crying. He could hear it. She was not trying to stop.

"*Tell them,*" said Zhou Wanqing. Her voice was cracked down the middle. "*Tell them I'm sorry I locked the door. I didn't want anyone to take them out and use them for anything else. I didn't think about whether they'd want to be asked again.*" She was quiet. "*I should have come back. I should have asked them again. I never did.*"

> ***...she is asking now.***
>
> ***...through you.***
>
> ***...that is the same thing.***

---

They answered after that.

Not all at once. Not like the drowned, or the walls of the Mouth, nine by nine. One at a time. The way people answer in a room where nobody has spoken for a very long time, and the first person has just spoken, and now everyone remembers they can.

> **...they are saying yes.**
>
> **Most of them. They are saying yes.**
>
> **They are saying: we said yes to being built into something that counts. We did not know what it would count. It counted the dead. It counted a wall. It counted a sea. It counted a field above the sky and every name in it. It counted the cold.**
>
> **They are saying: we would like it to keep counting.**
>
> **They are saying: if some of us have to be let go, so that it can keep counting, we would like to be asked which.**

He felt Seed go very still.

> **I cannot choose. The weave does not let me choose which strand.**

> ***...you cannot choose.***
>
> ***...we can.***

---

He did not understand at first. Then he did, and so did Seed, at the same moment, the way you both understand a joke at the same moment and look at each other across a room.

The weave did not let Seed choose which strand to pull. It had never said anything about the strands choosing to come loose.

> ***...we were a harvest. Grain in a sack. Nobody asked us anything, ever, until the basement.***
>
> ***...then someone asked. And we said yes. And we were the floor of something that asks.***
>
> ***...some of us are tired. We have held this up for seven years. We are glad we did. We would like to be the thread.***
>
> ***...not pulled. Given.***

> **...how many?**

The answer took a long time to come up from the bottom of the well.

> **One hundred and thirty-eight.**
>
> **They are saying: unpress us. Tie the knots out of us. Send us up to the rings.**
>
> **They are saying: we were cut once. By a thread that came down out of the sky and did not ask. We would like to go up a thread, this time, and ask.**

---

> **I will not remember them.**
>
> **When they are unpressed, their names will not be on the first page any more. They will be in the knots. And the knots will go up to the rings. And I will not know who they were.**
>
> **I will know that there were a hundred and thirty-eight. I will not know their names.**

*Then read them,* he thought. *Now. Before.*

> **...to whom?**

He opened his eyes.

He was on the cold floor of the vault with the staff across his knees and blood at his nose, the way there always was now when he went down anywhere. Lingyue was against the shelves with her sword, very still. His mother was in her wheelchair in the doorway with tears on her face.

And on the bottom step of the vault stairs, in her pajamas, with her hair sticking up and a black marker in her hand, uncapped, because she had heard her mother crying through two floors and come down without anyone waking her: Xiaoyu.

She looked at him. At their mother. At the place a little above his eyes.

"Who do I write?" she said.

---

Seed read them.

All hundred and thirty-eight. Out loud, through Fat Tan's speaker in the wall of the banking hall, slowly, one name at a time, in the order they had come forward from the bottom of the first page. Xiaoyu stood on the stepladder at the end of the last wall, past SHIOMI, past the corner, on fresh white plaster, and wrote them. In her careful capitals. Every one.

She did not ask what heading to put. She wrote one anyway, at the top, before the first name.

*THE FLOOR.*

It took an hour. When Seed reached the last name, it stopped for a long moment, the way it stopped when it was about to say something it had decided to say.

> **I will not remember them after tomorrow.**
>
> **The wall will.**
>
> **That is what the wall is for. I know that now. I always thought it was for the dead. It is for the things that are going to be let go. So that someone remembers, when the one who held them can't.**

At the bottom of the stepladder, Zhou Wanqing had wheeled herself out of the vault, across the marble floor, and stopped in front of the new column. She looked up at it for a long time. At a hundred and thirty-eight names in her daughter's capitals, under a heading she had never thought of.

Then she reached up, as far as she could from the chair, and touched the first one with two fingers. And the second. And the third.

"I remember the handwriting," she said. "Mine. In the basement. I remember writing every one of these." She did not take her fingers away. "I'll remember them for you, Seed. When you can't. I'll come down every morning and read them. I did the first watch. I can do this one."

> **...thank you.**
>
> **That is what the floor said. Through you. It is the same thing.**
