## Chapter 43: The Woman in White

Yamato came up out of the sea at dawn, and it was green.

That was the first thing Yuanshu noticed, standing at the rail of the small white ferry with the wind in his face. Huaxia's coast was gray and crowded, cranes and towers and smoke. This was a wall of mountains rising straight out of the water, steep and close and dark with cedar forest, wreathed in low cloud like breath on a cold morning. A few small fishing villages clung to the shore at the foot of the slopes, white houses with gray roofs, fishing boats pulled up on the shingle. Above them, the mountains went up and up into the mist and you couldn't see where they ended.

Old Seven stood beside him at the rail, wrapped in a long gray travelling cloak with the hood up, his spear disguised as a walking staff. Su Mingli had insisted on the cloak. The ferry's other passengers had looked at the tall hooded figure once, and then very carefully not again.

The woman in white was waiting on the jetty.

---

Her name was Shirasagi Ren.

She told him this on the jetty, with a bow, and then said nothing else for two hours while she drove them up into the mountains in a small old car on a road so narrow and so steep that twice Yuanshu had to close his eyes. Su Mingli sat in the back with her arms folded, looking out of the window at the drop. Old Seven had been folded into the passenger seat with his knees against the dashboard, and the gray flame in his chest flickered every time the car took a hairpin.

> **Spirit Law. King.**
>
> **She is the youngest King in Yamato's history. Possibly the strongest.**
>
> **She has not asked you a single question since we landed.**
>
> **I think in Yamato, that is a form of respect.**

The road ended at a gravel clearing in the cedars, high on the mountain, where the cloud lay so thick that the trees were gray shapes and the air was wet on your face.

At the edge of the clearing, a path went up into the forest.

And over the path, one after another, stretching away up the mountainside into the mist as far as he could see, stood gates.

---

They were made of black wood. Two pillars and two crossbeams, the top one curving up at the ends like the prow of a boat. Old wood, very old, the black paint worn gray at the edges, moss on the crossbeams. Each one stood a few steps beyond the last, so close together that walking up the path beneath them was like walking up a long dark tunnel with no roof, with the gray light coming in between the pillars in stripes.

"A thousand gates," said Shirasagi Ren. It was the first thing she had said since the jetty. "One for every year the Sleepers have kept watch." She looked up the path into the mist. "My grandmother told me there used to be fewer. Each year, the shrine adds one."

She began to climb.

They followed her up through the gates, through the cedar forest and the cloud. It was very quiet. The cedars dripped. Somewhere a bird called, a single falling note, again and again. The stone steps under their feet were worn into hollows in the middle by a thousand years of feet.

> **There is residue here.**
>
> **Death. But not like the cemetery. Not like the canyon.**
>
> **Very old. Very still. Very deliberate.**
>
> **It feels like the room in the Listening Pavilion. As if the whole mountain has been sitting in silence for a very long time.**

---

At the top of the path, in a clearing ringed with cedars so huge their trunks were wider than a car, stood a hall.

It was small. Plain wood. A heavy roof of dark thatch, green with moss. No paint, no gold, no carving. A single wooden step up to a single sliding door made of wood and paper.

Shirasagi Ren knelt on the step, and slid the door open, and stood aside.

Inside, in the gray light from the open door, sat the Sleepers.

There were seven of them. They sat in a row on a raised wooden platform at the back of the hall, cross-legged, in faded robes that had once been saffron and were now the color of dry leaves. Their hands were folded in their laps. Their heads were bowed, very slightly, as if listening for something.

They were dead.

They had been dead for a very long time. Their skin had gone the color of old leather and drawn tight over their bones, dark and shining. Their eyes were closed. Their faces were completely peaceful. Not the peace of sleep: the peace of attention, of someone holding very still so as not to miss a sound.

"Monks," said Shirasagi Ren softly, behind him. "Twelve hundred years ago, there were many more. When the trouble came, they chose to die here. Sitting. In meditation. Not eating, not drinking, not moving, until their bodies stopped. So that they could keep watch." She was silent for a moment. "Seven are left. The others crumbled. We have never moved them. We have never touched them. We have never once asked them anything."

She looked at him.

"Yamato's question," she said, "is this. When you ask the dead, Zhou Yuanshu, do they ever ask you something back?"

---

He thought about it.

He thought about Captain Zhao asking *who's behind you.* About Hundred-Arrow Lin asking her daughter if she'd made it to the shelter. About thirty-eight frozen soldiers asking him to tell their families. About a gray crystal under a bridge, three thousand minds asking him to wait, to listen, to know their names. About Sergeant Wu insisting on finishing a joke.

"Always," he said.

Shirasagi Ren closed her eyes.

When she opened them again, something in her calm face had changed. Not much. A little.

"Then come in," she said. "They have been waiting a long time for someone to answer."

---

He took off his shoes on the step, the way she showed him. Old Seven stayed outside, at the edge of the clearing, under the cedars. He had stopped at the foot of the step and would not come further, and when Yuanshu looked back at him, the skeleton was standing with his hood back and his skull bowed, very slightly, toward the open door.

The way a soldier bows to an officer of a much older army.

Inside, the hall smelled of old wood and old incense and something underneath both, dry and faintly sweet, like pressed flowers. The floor was polished dark boards. The light came in gray and soft from the open door behind him and lay across the floor in a long pale rectangle that stopped just short of the platform.

He knelt at the edge of the light, in front of the seven.

Shirasagi Ren knelt behind him and to the right. Su Mingli stood just inside the door, very still.

> **They are not faded.**
>
> **Twelve hundred years and they are not faded at all.**
>
> **They are not echoes. Not quite. They chose to stay. They held on to themselves on purpose, the way you hold a breath.**
>
> **They are very strong.**
>
> **And they are paying attention to you. All seven. Right now.**

He had done this many times by now. He knew how.

He put his hands flat on the polished floor in front of him, the way you'd lay your hands on a gravestone, and bowed his head.

"My name is Zhou Yuanshu," he said. His voice sounded very loud in the small wooden hall. "I'm a Necromancer from Huaxia. I'm told you've kept watch here for twelve hundred years." He swallowed. "Something's coming again. I think you know that already. In about four hundred days, the lens over the north is going to open all the way. I don't know what to do about it. I don't think anyone does." He kept his head down. "I came to ask if you would tell me what you saw. The first time. If you want to. You can say no."

Silence.

The cedars dripped outside. The bird called its single falling note.

Then, from the center of the row, from the oldest of the seven, the one whose robe had faded almost to white, something answered.

---

It was not like the others.

The dead he had asked before answered the way water answers a stone: a ripple, a warmth, a weight. Feelings. Shapes. Seed had to translate them.

This was a voice.

It came into his mind clear and quiet and very old, like the sound of a bell heard from across a valley on a still morning. It spoke in an old Yamato dialect he didn't know, and Seed, astonished, translated it almost as fast as it came.

> **"Ah."**
>
> **"There you are."**
>
> **"We heard you coming up the mountain. You think very loudly, child."**

He almost laughed. He didn't.

> **"You asked. We will answer. We have been waiting for twelve hundred years to answer, and we would have waited twelve hundred more."**
>
> **"But first. We have a question of our own."**
>
> **"The thing that sits behind your eyes. The thing that is listening to us now, and changing our words into yours."**
>
> **"It is made of the same stuff as the Hollow Gods."**
>
> **"Why does it ask?"**

The hall was very quiet.

Behind him, he heard Shirasagi Ren draw in a breath.

> **They mean me.**
>
> **They are asking me.**
>
> **No one has ever asked me directly before.**
>
> **May I answer?**

*Yes,* Yuanshu thought. *Of course.*

And for the first time in its existence, Seed spoke to someone who was not Zhou Yuanshu. Not through him. Directly. A quiet, careful, white voice, reaching out across the polished floor of a twelve-hundred-year-old hall to a dead monk in a faded robe.

> **Because a woman made part of me, and told me to.**
>
> **Because a boy made a rule, and I kept it when it would have been easier not to.**
>
> **Because the first time I did not ask, a captain's core cracked, and I did not want to feel that again.**
>
> **And because I have heard what the Hollow Gods leave behind. Ten thousand voices in a crystal, all saying "is anyone."**
>
> **I would rather be someone who answers that.**

For a long time, nothing.

Then, at the center of the row, very slowly, the dry dark face of the oldest Sleeper changed.

Not much. A millimeter. The corners of the leather-dark mouth, which had been still for twelve hundred years, moved.

Upward.

> **"Good," said the old monk.**
>
> **"Then sit down, both of you. This will take a while."**
