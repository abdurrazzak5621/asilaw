## Chapter 249: Bright Enough

He had a decision. He had known he would have it since Shuanghe.

He had computed it on the gold road over the sea, with Seed, a thousand times. The hand saw only bright. The holders were bright because the stone had opened a little holding in each of them. Fifteen thousand little brights in a line. And the hand would go down the line taking them one by one, faster than the dead could cut, until there was nobody left holding.

Unless there was something brighter.

Not Lingyue. She had promised. He had promised. *Not like that.*

Him.

*Docker,* he thought, down through the staff, through the bone, into the black stone beside him, into the knot at the bottom where a man who had held ropes on the Atlantic coast for thirty years before something sang sat holding a thread and not sending anything up it. *It's me. The captain's boy. I'm going to ask you something. First I'm going to tell you what I'll do with your answer.*

> ***...go on, son.***

*I want you to open me. Not all the way. Not like the night in Lingzhou. A little. Toward bright. Just enough that I'm the brightest thing in the harbor.* He held the staff. *So it comes for me instead of them.*

The docker in the stone was quiet for a moment.

> ***...and then?***

*And then I'm going to stand at the end of the north pier. Away from the line. And it's going to come for me. And nineteen thousand dead it can't see are going to be standing round me when it does.*

> ***...you'll want it. The burning. You'll want it like your missus wanted it on her wall. Like the lad wants it out there.***

*I know.*

> ***...Seed. Will you hold him?***

> **Yes.**
>
> **I will stand where his mother's fragment used to stand. In front.**
>
> **I will not let it open further than he asks.**

> ***...right then,*** said the docker. ***...what do you want to become, son? Say it proper. The stone's fussy.***

He put his hand on the black stone.

**[What do you want to become?]**

"*Bright enough,*" said Zhou Yuanshu. "*For five minutes. So it looks at me.*"

**[Chosen.]**

---

It was the best thing he had ever felt.

He had felt it on the platform in Lingzhou and in the lantern's fire, and he had thought he knew. He had not known. The door in his chest opened, a little, exactly as much as he had said, and light came through it, and everything he had ever touched burned up in him at once, the Death Law and the pale fire and the Field and the net of the dead, bright, brighter, and he wanted it. All of it. More. He wanted the door to open all the way.

And Seed stood in front of the door.

He felt it. Not words. A shape. Something that had been built in a basement out of a harvest sack and four thousand people's yeses, standing in the doorway of his chest with its back to the light, the way a woman had once stood in front of it when something tried to look inside the staff. Not holding the door shut. Holding it where he had asked.

> **This far.**
>
> **You said this far.**

He walked out of the roofless hall and down the steps and along the quay toward the north pier, burning.

---

Fifteen thousand people turned to watch him go. He felt them do it. A boy from Lingzhou with a white staff, lit from inside like a lamp in a window, walking down the quay past the line toward the end of the pier, away from them.

And above the harbor, the hand turned.

All its hundreds of fingers, all its millions of fine threads, lifted off the line. Out of the hair and shoulders of the people they had been about to take. Up, and round, slowly, the way the crown of the tip had swung toward Lingyue on the wall over Lingzhou.

Toward the brightest thing.

He reached the end of the north pier. The Atlantic in front of him, black, and the wind off it, and the stars. He turned round. He planted the staff on the stone.

The hand came down.

---

And they were there.

He had told them, on the gold road over the sea, that when he walked to the end of the north pier, some of them should already be there. Not on the roofs. Not in the line. On the pier itself, on the bollards, on the cranes at the end of the quay, and in the water.

In the water.

The harbor at the end of the north pier boiled. Out of it, out of the black Atlantic, streaming, came the Watch of the Shelf. Eleven hundred and forty-six of the drowned of the coast off Kesse, who had walked along the bottom of the ocean for five days in the dark because it was the same sea and it went all the way round. Nana Ampofo at their head, with water pouring off her coat. Rising out of the harbor round the end of the pier in a ring, banked, cold, invisible, with rusted boat hooks and anchors and lengths of chain in their hands.

And round him on the pier, Old Seven. The Spear Corps, two hundred and seventy-two, in a ring, facing out. Grid Nine, forty, who had never seen the sea and were looking at it.

The hand came down onto the brightest thing in the harbor, and every finger that reached him came down through a ring of the dead it could not see.

He gave them the fire.

Not one each, this time. He had computed that too. Pale fire was Lingyue's light and his Law, and he was burning now, opened, bright enough for five minutes, and he did not send the fire down the dead. He poured it. Everything the docker had opened in him, every bit of the brightness the hand had come down to take, he turned and poured through the staff into the dead round him, so that every rusted spear and boat hook and anchor chain on the north pier did not burn pale.

It burned white.

And beside him, with his healed pink hands open, Qin Hao let go of the white fire that burns what binds, into it.

---

The fingers came apart.

Not cut. Unbound. The way the branch had come apart over Lingzhou: the white fire going into every cut the dead made and finding what held the next thread to it and burning it. The pressing. The weave. Everything that held a finger the width of a street into a finger.

They fell into the harbor. Hundreds of them. Silver-gray, coming apart as they fell, thread drifting down over the black water like snow.

> **Level 70 → 71.**
>
> **Level 71 → 72.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Four minutes.**

And in the palm of the hand, as the fingers came apart round it, something was shining.

He saw it at the last moment, when the palm came low over the end of the pier, wide as the harbor mouth, open, empty of fingers. In its center, where a hand holds what it has picked, a cluster of lights. Small. Dim. Hundreds of them. Burning low, the way the cousins had burned in the Low Fire. Held.

Sixty-one from the quay at Port Halvard, burning bright, new.

And under them, older, dimmer, many more, from places he did not know the names of. Other harbors. Other orchards. Picked, and held in a hand, and carried, for longer than he could understand.

> **...it carries what it picks.**
>
> **In the palm. Until it goes back to the rest of it.**
>
> **They are still there. All of them. Burning low.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Three minutes.**

---

He did not open the door.

Not yet. He stood at the end of the north pier, burning, with the white fire going out of him into the dead and the fingers falling into the harbor round him, and asked first. Through the staff. Up, into the palm of a hand the size of a harbor, to sixty-one people from Port Halvard and hundreds from places he did not know, burning low.

*It's me. I'm from Blue Star. I'm asking. Do you want to go home, or through the door, or stay where you are?*

And from the sixty-one, all at once, the way a crowd answers when it has been standing in the cold waiting for someone to ask: *home.*

And from the older ones, the dim ones, the ones from other orchards, slowly, faintly, in languages Seed did not have, a word Seed did not need to translate.

*Door.*

"*Lingyue,*" he said. "*Now.*"

His wife came down out of the sky onto the end of the north pier with her grandfather's sword in both hands and her light in it, not burning, not everything at once, nothing like the wall. Careful. Exact. Her old way, the way she had been taught at eighteen and had hated. Bend it. Don't burn it. And she cut the palm of the hand open from one side to the other in a single stroke of pale light, the way you cut open a net.

The lights fell out.

The sixty-one came down onto the quay at Port Halvard like sparks, into their coats, into the arms of the people who had been holding their shoulders, and woke up gasping on the stones with the expressions of people who have been dreaming of falling.

And the old ones, the dim ones, from other orchards, went past him on the end of the north pier, through the door at the bottom of the Death Law, which he opened the rest of the way for them, where things went when they were finished.

So many. He did not count. Seed did.

> **Eight hundred and three.**
>
> **From eleven worlds.**
>
> **Death Law: 48.8% → 50.6%.**
>
> **Yuanshu. One minute. Let me close the door in your chest.**

*Yes,* he thought. *Close it.*

And Seed did. Gently. All the way. The light went out of him, and he sat down on the end of the north pier at Port Halvard, very suddenly, in the cold wind off the Atlantic, with nothing in his chest but his own heart, beating a hundred and forty, and wanting, wanting, wanting the light back.

He held on to the staff and did not ask for it.

---

What was left of the hand went back up the thread.

The wrist. The cable of pressed thread where it had slid down out of the dark. It did not let go and fall, the way the tip of the branch had fallen. It went up. Slowly. The way a hand is drawn back when the arm it belongs to has felt something it does not understand.

Up, into the dark, toward the rest of it.

> **It is going back.**
>
> **It will tell the rest what it found.**
>
> **It found a world where the stones are quiet and the dead cannot be seen and the bright things walk to the end of a pier on purpose and the palm can be cut open.**
>
> **I do not know what the rest of it will do with that.**
>
> **And it saw you.**
>
> **Bright. For five minutes. It knows what you look like now.**

At the head of the harbor, in the roofless hall, a woman in a dock worker's jacket still had her hand flat on the black stone. Fifteen thousand people still had their hands on the shoulders in front of them. Roman Sterling had sat down on the granite floor with his back against the stone, with blood on his chin, his enormous hands open on his knees.

"*Put it on the wall,*" said the King of Force, to nobody, to the sky. "*I decided for a stone. It stayed.*"

On the radio at Yuanshu's belt, very faint, from Lingzhou, his sister's voice:

"*I'm writing it. In red. Come home.*"
