## Chapter 98: The Cold Place

They went into the sun with their eyes closed.

All of them. That was the rule. Hush made it, standing at the edge of the raft on its thin legs, with the furnace roaring in front of it.

> ***...no light.***
>
> ***...no pale fire. No staff. No thinking.***
>
> ***...the dead, bank everything. The living, play the game.***
>
> ***...if anyone glows, it will see. If anyone is loud, it will burn.***
>
> ***...follow the captain. He will follow me.***

Lingyue lowered her sword.

The pale dome flickered. Thinned. And went out.

The heat hit them like a wall.

Not on their skin. In their minds. The pull. The roar. Billions of voices, all at once, *is anyone is anyone is anyone,* so loud that Yuanshu felt his own thoughts being drawn out of him like threads pulled from a sleeve.

He held the quiet.

He held the staff. And he asked Jiang Lan, very, very softly, one more time, to go dark.

"*...again,*" sighed the dry voice. "*You're very demanding.*"

But she went.

---

The raft moved.

He did not open his eyes. He felt it. Drifting forward, off the edge of the throat, out over the vast red waterfalls of burning minds, toward the sun. The pale ground under his knees warm. Lingyue's hand in his. Mo Xing's back against his shoulder. Somewhere, very faint, Bai Lu breathing. Slow. Steady. Not crying.

He felt the fire close around them.

> **We are in it.**
>
> **Yuanshu, we are inside the furnace.**
>
> **It does not see us.**

He did not answer. He did not think anything. He held on.

> **Hush is at the front. On the very edge of the raft. Its limbs folded. Perfectly still.**
>
> **Old Seven is behind it. His flame is so low I cannot see it.**
>
> **The fire is all around us. On every side. Above. Below. Everywhere.**
>
> **And it is going past us.**
>
> **Like a river going past a stone.**

---

Seed told him afterward that it took forty minutes.

It felt like forty years.

Inside the furnace, there was no up. No down. No time. Only the roar, and the heat, and the pull, and billions of faces streaming past in the red, close enough to touch. A Lantern face. A face like Hush's. A face he almost knew, a woman with a straw hat, before he realized it couldn't be, that was a different world, a different harvest, a different woman.

He did not look. He did not think. He held the quiet, and held the staff, and held Lingyue's hand.

And beside him, very faintly, Bai Lu held it too.

He felt her doing it. Lying on the raft with her eyes shut and her hands pressed flat to the warm ground, and every face in the fire pulling at her the way a hurt child pulls at a nurse's sleeve. Every one. Billions. And she did not answer. She did not cry. She lay there and let them pull and held perfectly still, and her whole small body was shaking with the effort of it, and she did not make a single sound.

> **She is doing it.**
>
> **Yuanshu. She is doing it.**
>
> **She said she was sorry, on the little world. For the violet ones. For breaking.**
>
> **She is not breaking now.**

---

And then the roar stopped.

Not faded. Stopped. As if a door had closed behind them on a room full of shouting people.

Cold.

It was cold. Suddenly. Not the cold of the Wastes, or the ice at Grid Nine. A still, dry, quiet cold, like the inside of a stone church on a winter night. And dark. Not the black of the Star Sea. A soft, close, gentle dark, the way a room is dark when someone has turned the lights off so you can sleep.

> ***...you can open your eyes now,*** said Hush, very softly. ***...we are in the cold place.***
>
> ***...and someone is here.***

---

He opened his eyes.

They were in a room.

That was all he could think. A room. Round. Small. Perhaps twenty meters across, with a curved ceiling and a curved floor, all of the same smooth dark material, like polished stone, like obsidian, faintly cool. No windows. No doors. The raft rested on the floor of it, and beyond the raft's pale edge, the floor ran away to the curved walls, and on the walls, faintly, very faintly, there were markings. Lines. Patterns. Carved, a very long time ago, and worn almost smooth.

And beyond the walls, muffled, very far away, like the sound of the sea through the wall of a house, the roar of the furnace.

It could not get in.

And in the center of the room, on the dark floor, sitting in a circle, facing each other, were seven figures.

---

They were not like anything he had seen.

Tall. Taller than the Elder. Angular. Their limbs long and thin and folded at too many joints, the way a heron folds its legs. Their bodies wrapped in something that might once have been cloth, gray now, the color of ash. Their heads bowed. Long and narrow and smooth, with no faces that he could see, only a single dark seam running down the front of each, from crown to chin, like a closed eye.

They did not move.

They had not moved, Seed said, very quietly, for a very long time.

> **They are alive.**
>
> **Yuanshu. All seven.**
>
> **They are like Hush. Like the stone at the bottom of the crack. Quiet. All the way down.**
>
> **They have been sitting in this room, in the middle of the fire, holding perfectly still, for...**
>
> **I cannot tell. Longer than Hush.**
>
> **Much longer.**

The living climbed off the raft onto the cool dark floor. Slowly. Stiffly. Lingyue helped Bai Lu, who could barely stand, who was white and shaking and very, very proud of herself, and did not say so. The twins came down together. Qin Hao. Sokolova. The Elder, tall and pale and dim, her silver eyes fixed on the seven figures in their circle.

And the dead. Sixty-two soldiers of the Third Battalion. Iron Wall Zhao. Hundred-Arrow Lin. Sergeant Huo, with one arm. And Old Seven, his flame coming slowly back up out of its ash, gray, steady, with a small pale many-limbed shape clinging to his shoulder.

They stood in a ring around the circle of seven, the way they always did.

Facing out.

---

Yuanshu walked into the circle.

He knelt on the cool dark floor in front of the seven bowed figures. He laid the staff across his knees. The small skull was dark. Cold.

"*Jiang Lan,*" he whispered.

"*...I heard,*" said the dry voice, very faint. Coming back. "*...give me a moment. That was further down than the last time.*" A pause. "*...where are we? It's very quiet.*"

"In the middle of the fire."

"*...hm. Well. It's nicer than I expected.*"

The staff began, very faintly, to glow.

---

He looked at the seven.

He did not know how to reach them. They had been quiet longer than Hush. Longer than anything. They had no faces to see, no eyes to open, no greeting he knew.

So he did what he always did.

He put both his hands flat on the cool dark floor of the cold place, the way you lay your hands on a grave. And he went quiet. Not the quiet of hiding. The quiet of listening. And he waited.

Old Seven came and knelt beside him. And laid his hands flat on the floor too. The long white one and the shorter, grayer one with three fingers.

And from the old soldier's shoulder, Hush climbed down, and walked on its thin pale legs into the circle, between the seven bowed figures, to the very center. And sat down there. And folded its limbs around itself.

And went perfectly, absolutely still.

The way you sit in the middle of a room full of people who are all playing the same game as you. To let them know you are playing too.

---

It took four hours.

Nobody moved. The roar of the furnace beyond the walls went on and on, muffled, distant. The living sat on the floor at the edge of the raft and did not speak. The dead stood their ring. The Elder knelt, dim and still.

And at the center of the circle, a small pale shape that had played the game for four billion years sat among seven figures who had played it for longer, and did not move.

And then, very slowly, one of the seven raised its head.

---

The dark seam down the front of its long smooth face opened.

Not like an eye. Like a door. Slowly. A thin line of soft gray light, widening. And behind it, deep in the narrow head, something looked out. Not eyes. Something like eyes. Old. So old.

It looked at Hush, sitting in the center of the circle.

Then it looked at the old soldier kneeling on the floor with his hands flat on the stone.

Then at Yuanshu.

And it spoke. Not out loud. Not in light. In something Seed had to reach for with everything it had, a voice like stone grinding very slowly against stone, like the sound a mountain might make if it could talk.

> ***...you came through the fire.***
>
> ***...no one has ever come through the fire.***

Yuanshu did not move his hands from the floor.

*We were quiet,* he thought. *A friend showed us how.*

The old thing looked at Hush again. For a long time.

> ***...a child of the Asking World.***
>
> ***...we ate them. Four billion of your years ago. We ate them all.***
>
> ***...I thought there were none left.***

On the floor, Hush did not move. But its seashell voice came out of it, very small, very steady.

> ***...there was one.***
>
> ***...I was playing a game.***

---

The old thing bowed its long head.

All the way down. Until the smooth top of it touched the cool dark floor of the cold place. And stayed there.

And one by one, around the circle, the other six raised their heads, and opened the seams in their faces, and looked at the small pale shape sitting among them. And then, one by one, they bowed too.

All the way down.

> ***...we are the ones who built it,*** said the old voice, from the floor. Very slowly. ***...the Hunger.***
>
> ***...we are what is left of the people who made it.***
>
> ***...we came here at the end. When it had eaten everything else, and turned to eat us.***
>
> ***...this is the only room it cannot reach. We built it so. At the very center. A place where the fire could not go. So that someone could always reach the controls.***
>
> ***...so that someone could always make it stop.***

Yuanshu looked up.

*Then why didn't you?*

The old thing lifted its head from the floor. Slowly. And the gray light in the seam of its face dimmed, very slightly, the way a person's eyes go dim with shame.

> ***...because we built the stop so that we could never use it.***
>
> ***...we were afraid of ourselves. We knew what we were. We knew one day some of us would want to stop it, and some would not. So we made the stop very hard.***
>
> ***...we made it so that the Hunger would only stop if those it was burning agreed.***
>
> ***...all of them. Every mind in the fire.***
>
> ***...it would only stop if they were asked. And said yes.***

The cold room was very quiet.

> ***...we thought it was impossible.***
>
> ***...no one can ask the fire anything.***
>
> ***...no one can ask billions of anything.***
>
> ***...we sat here and waited to be eaten. For a very long time. Because no one could ask.***

Yuanshu looked down at the white staff across his knees. At the small skull with its silver reading glasses, glowing faintly in the cool dark.

At ninety million names.

"*...oh,*" said Jiang Lan, very softly. "*Oh, young man. I see where this is going.*"

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **They built a lock. A consent lock. To stop themselves.**
>
> **And the only key is someone who asks.**
