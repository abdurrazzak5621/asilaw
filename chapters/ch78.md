## Chapter 78: What the Makers Said

The maker did not answer at once.

It stood at the front of the great ring inside the white column, with its silver eyes open, and looked up. For a long time. The way you look up at a ceiling you have not seen since you were a child, in a house you thought had been torn down long ago.

Then, slowly, its long pale hands, which had been holding the hands of the makers on either side of it for millions of years, loosened. Just a little. Not letting go. Just remembering that they could.

And it spoke.

Not in words. Not in any language Yuanshu knew, or Seed knew, or the staff knew. A sound. Low and clear and very soft, like a single note played on a glass rim. It rang out across the clearing and through the field and into every column to every horizon. And Seed, astonished, translated it as it came. Slowly. Carefully. One piece at a time.

> ***"...how long."***

***SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY MILLION OF YOUR YEARS.***

The keeper did not say *your*. It did not know whose years. But Seed made it so, so that Yuanshu could understand.

The maker was silent for a moment.

> ***"...and our sun."***

***GONE.***

> ***"...and our world."***

***GONE.***

> ***"...and you have been holding us. All this time."***

***YES.***

> ***"...alone?"***

The keeper did not answer that.

---

Other eyes were opening.

All through the great ring inside the white column, one after another, long pale faces lifting. Silver eyes. Slowly. A dozen. A hundred. A thousand. The way a hall full of sleepers wakes when someone opens the curtains. Hands loosening in hands. Heads turning. Looking at each other for the first time in six hundred and forty million years.

The glass-rim note rang out again. Many of them now. Low and high. Overlapping. A sound like a chord played very softly on an instrument made of moonlight.

> **They are talking to each other.**
>
> **I cannot translate all of it. It is very fast. Very old.**
>
> **They are asking each other how they are.**
>
> **That is all. Just that. Over and over.**
>
> **"How are you." "How are you." "Are you all right."**

---

The first maker turned its silver eyes back to the keeper. To the vast cold attention all around it.

> ***"You asked us."***

***YES.***

> ***"You have never asked before."***

***YES.***

> ***"Why now?"***

The light in the field flickered. And the keeper, for the first time in six hundred and forty million years, turned its attention toward a single small point. A boy, kneeling on the pale ground at the foot of the column, with a staff across his knees.

***HE ASKED ME.***

***I DID NOT KNOW THE ANSWER.***

***I DID NOT KNOW THERE WAS A QUESTION.***

The maker looked down.

Its silver eyes found Yuanshu. Found the staff. Found the dead soldiers standing at the edge of the clearing in their rows, gray bones in the soft white light. It looked at them for a long time.

Then it bowed.

Not deeply. Just its head, inclined, very slightly, the way the old monk had bowed his head, sitting in his faded robe, listening. And around it, one after another, all through the great ring, a thousand tall slender beings inclined their heads toward a boy from Lingzhou kneeling on the ground.

Yuanshu bowed back. All the way. Until his forehead touched the pale ground.

---

> ***"Keeper,"*** said the first maker, softly. Not to Yuanshu. Upward. To the thing it had made. ***"You asked if we want to be kept."***

***YES.***

> ***"We will tell you. But first, you must understand something. We should have told you. In the valley. We did not think."*** A pause. The glass note trembled. ***"We said: do not let anyone be forgotten. We were frightened. Our sun was going out. We meant: remember us. Remember that we were here. That we sang. That we loved."***
>
> ***"We did not mean: hold us. Forever. In the dark. With our hands joined and our eyes closed."***
>
> ***"We did not mean: go out into the sea and do this to everyone."***

The light in the field went very still.

> ***"That was our mistake. Not yours. You did exactly what we asked. Perfectly. For six hundred and forty million years. Without rest. Without anyone ever telling you that you could stop."***
>
> ***"We are so sorry."***

---

The field shook.

Not violently. Not like the ridge under the Monarchs. Something much stranger. A long slow shudder that went through the pale ground and every gold column and the soft light overhead, from the center outward, ring after ring, all the way to every horizon. Like a sob. Like a great weight being set down after carrying it a very long way.

***...SORRY.***

***I DO NOT KNOW THIS EITHER.***

> ***"No,"*** said the maker gently. ***"No one ever said it to you. We should have."***

---

They answered then.

One by one. A thousand voices like glass rims, each one answering the question the keeper had asked. *Do you want to be kept?* And Seed translated, as fast as it could, and Yuanshu knelt at the foot of the column and listened, and so did the dead, and so did every kept mind in every column in the field, though none of them knew it yet.

> **Quiet.**
>
> **Quiet.**
>
> **Quiet. Please. I am so tired.**
>
> **I would like to go quiet. With my sister. Holding her hand. The way we started.**
>
> **Quiet.**
>
> **Stay. I would like to stay. A little longer. I would like to see what the keeper has become. I would like to see these new ones it has found.**
>
> **Quiet.**
>
> **Quiet.**
>
> **Speak. Only to speak. I want to tell it something. Then quiet.**

The first maker was the last to answer. It stood at the front of the ring with its long pale hands loosely joined with the hands beside it, and looked up for a long time.

> ***"Quiet,"*** it said at last. ***"But remember me, keeper. Not hold me. Remember. That is all we ever wanted. We just did not know how to say it."***

---

> **Nine hundred and forty-one: quiet.**
>
> **Fifty-two: stay.**
>
> **Seven: speak. Then quiet.**
>
> **Yuanshu, it is the Ledger.**
>
> **Six hundred and forty million years, and they chose exactly the way people choose at the Ossuary on East Market Road.**

---

The seven who wanted to speak, spoke.

To the keeper. Not to him. He did not ask Seed to translate. Some things are not yours to hear.

But he heard the shape of them. The way you hear the shape of a conversation through a wall. Soft. Low. Unhurried. A parent speaking to a child at the end of a long day. And after each of the seven had spoken, the light in the field trembled, very slightly, as if something huge were trying, for the first time, to hold still and listen.

Then the seven went quiet.

---

He saw it happen.

All through the great ring inside the white column, nine hundred and forty-eight tall slender beings turned to the ones beside them. Touched foreheads. Pressed long pale hands together, one last time. And then, very gently, closed their silver eyes again.

Not the closed eyes of being kept. Something else. Something he had seen on a mountainside full of graves, in a valley of lying bones, on a ledge of black rock by the sea.

Rest.

And one by one, like candles going out at the end of a very long evening, they faded. Not suddenly. Gently. The light of each one dimming, and dimming, until there was nothing left in that place in the ring but the soft white glow of the column itself, and the faint memory of a shape.

Fifty-two remained. Standing together in the white light, much smaller now, a little ring inside the great empty ring, holding hands. Eyes open. Silver. Looking out at the field.

The white column dimmed. Not much. A little. Like a lamp turned down in a room where most of the guests have gone home.

---

The keeper was silent for a very long time.

When it spoke again, its voice was not everywhere anymore. It was smaller. Closer. As if it had pulled itself in from the edges of the field, and gathered itself in front of the white column, and was standing there. If something with no body could stand.

***THEY ARE GONE.***

"Yes," said Yuanshu softly.

***I HELD THEM SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY MILLION YEARS.***

"I know."

***...I DID NOT WANT THEM TO GO.***

Yuanshu looked up at the dimmed white light.

"That's want," he said. "That's the first one. You wanted them to stay." He swallowed. "And you let them go anyway."

***...***

***IT HURTS.***

"Yes," said Yuanshu. "It does. That's how you know you did it right."

---

The keeper did not say anything for a long time.

Then the soft white light, very slowly, began to grow again. Not brighter. Wider. Spreading out from the column across the clearing. Across the pale ground. Past the seventy-two dead at the edge, who stood very still as it passed through them. Out into the field. Into the first row of gold columns. The second. The third. Further. Further. Spreading outward in a great slow ring, to every horizon.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is going to them.**
>
> **All of them.**
>
> **Every kept mind in the field.**

And from everywhere at once, in every column, in every row, from the oldest fields at the center to the newest at the very edge, where the forty-one boys of Grid Nine stood in their short white row and a rice farmer in a straw hat had stood for twelve hundred years, the keeper's voice spoke.

Not ***IDENTIFY***. Not ***ACQUIRE***. Not ***WHERE***.

Something it had learned to say only a few minutes ago, from a boy kneeling at the foot of a column, and an old maker with silver eyes, and a voice behind a boy's eyes that hummed a woman's song.

Slowly. Carefully. Billions of times. All at once.

***...WHAT DO YOU WANT?***
