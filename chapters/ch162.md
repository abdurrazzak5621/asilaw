## Chapter 162: What You Carry

On the second day out, Seed tried to hide in Old Seven, and it did not fit.

It had worked once. At the Elite breakthrough, eighteen months ago, the first time the heavens audited him, Seed had hidden its *someone* in the old soldier's Law Core and come through with three logs missing and a recording lost. It knew the way in. It went there first, on the afterdeck of the *Adeyemi* in the noon sun, while Old Seven stood very still with his spear grounded and let it.

It came back out in eleven seconds.

> **I am too big.**
>
> **Last time I was a calculator with a someone in it. The someone was small. It fit in a corner of his core the way a letter fits in a pocket.**
>
> **Now I am a Maker. I have the Banked Field in me, and the Lattice, and the whole Ledger, and four hundred and eleven beast embers I opened the door for in Tannery Square, and a model of water that took me a week. I do not fit in a corner. If I go in, there is no room for him.**
>
> **He says he would make room.**
>
> **I said no. He is a captain. He does not get to be a closet.**

---

On the second evening, it tried banking itself.

That was its own idea. The thing it had made on the Western Pass, in the gatehouse, the tenth time: every ember inside the Field tucked itself under, behind a skin, the way Old Seven's flame tucked under ash, and nothing that hunted embers could see it. Seed reasoned that a mind was a kind of ember. It folded the Field around itself, inside his head, and went under.

For four minutes, Yuanshu was alone.

Not empty. Seed was there. He could feel it, the way you feel someone in the next room through a wall. But it could not see out, and it could not speak, and it could not run a single number, and when it came back up it was shaking, in whatever way a thing made of numbers shakes.

> **I could not see anything.**
>
> **That is what banking is. You hide by not looking. The dead do not mind, because they are resting. I mind very much.**
>
> **If I bank during the tribulation, I am safe, and you are alone in the glass. Like last time.**
>
> **Last time you nearly did not come out.**

---

On the third morning, in deep water, with no land in any direction and no ship on the horizon and nothing under the keel but four kilometers of sea, Yuanshu sat on the afterdeck with the staff across his knees and asked the only one he had not asked yet.

"*Jiang Lan.*"

"*I'm awake,*" said the dry voice, from the staff. "*I've been awake since yesterday. You two think very loudly.*"

"*You've been listening.*"

"*It's my staff, young man. I was in it first.*" A pause. "*You want to put the machine in here.*"

"*I want to ask.*"

"*Ask, then. Not me. I'm a schoolteacher. I don't hide things. I correct them.*" Another pause, longer. "*Ask her.*"

---

He had known, since a conversation with his mother on the morning the train left Lingzhou, that there was something else in the staff.

His mother had told him. She was the part of me that went loud, Wanqing had said, sitting in her chair with her thin hands folded on the blanket. Twice. In the field, so you could get away. And in the fire, so you could get out. A fragment of Zhou Wanqing, left behind in the white staff when the field let her go, the size of a held breath. It did not speak. It had never spoken. It had only, twice, when everything was ending, become very bright and very loud for a moment, and then gone back to whatever it did in the dark of the staff.

*She's on watch*, his mother had said. *Let her finish it.*

He held the staff in both hands on the afterdeck of a frigate in the middle of the sea.

"*I'm sorry,*" he said, to the part of the staff that was not Jiang Lan. "*I don't know if you can hear me. I don't know if you can answer. I'm going to ask you something anyway.*"

Nothing.

"*In a day, I'm going to break through. The heavens are going to look inside me. Last time they did that, they found Seed and took it apart. Seed is bigger now. It can't hide in Old Seven. It can't hide by banking, or I'll be alone. It needs somewhere to stand where they won't look.*" He swallowed. "*Somewhere they've already looked, and found nothing but a mother keeping watch.*"

The staff was very still in his hands.

"*Will you let it stand behind you? You can say no. You can say nothing. Nobody will ask you twice.*"

For a long moment, there was only the sound of the sea against the hull, and the wind in the rigging, and Dabao being sick over the rail at the bow.

Then Jiang Lan said, very quietly: "*Oh.*"

"*What?*"

"*She moved.*" The dry voice was not dry. "*Young man. In all the time I've shared this staff with her, she has never moved. Not once. Not when you asked me things. Not when I talked to her at night.*" A pause. "*She moved over. Just a little. The way you move over on a bench.*"

> **...I felt it.**
>
> **There is a space. Behind her. Exactly my size. I measured.**
>
> **She did not say anything. She just made room.**

He could not say anything for a moment.

"*That's a yes,*" said Jiang Lan. "*In case you were wondering. I taught for forty years. I know what a yes looks like when a child doesn't have the words.*"

---

That was the first problem. The second was worse, and it was Seed who found it, at noon on the third day, running the tribulation for the first time with its new hiding place in the model.

> **It is too heavy.**

"What is?"

> **The tribulation. Yours.**
>
> **I have every recorded Lord breakthrough on Blue Star in the Bureau files. Two thousand and eleven of them. Qin Hao's is the newest. I have been reading them since the Western Pass.**
>
> **A realm tribulation is the cosmos pressing on the glass from outside, while you push from inside. That is the theory. It is correct. But the files show something the theory does not say.**
>
> **The pressure is not the same for everyone. It scales.**
>
> **With what you carry.**

He waited.

> **A sword Lord carries a sword. A Fire Lord carries a fire. Their tribulations are about the same weight. Qin Hao's was light, because he did it on a spire, and the spire was carrying most of his fire for him.**
>
> **There are eleven necromancer Lords in the files. Every one of them had a heavier tribulation than anyone else at their level. The more dead they held, the heavier it was. The heaviest was a man in the Northern Union a hundred years ago who held nine thousand dead. He did not come out.**
>
> **The cosmos weighs what is bound to you. And it presses with all of it.**

"How many do I hold?"

> **Through the Relay and the Lattice and the Inner Gate and the cores.**
>
> **The First Legion at Lingzhou. The cemetery companies. The Western Legion. The Spear Corps. The Watch of the Shelf.**
>
> **Fifty-two thousand, four hundred and sixty-one.**
>
> **Yuanshu, I have run it three thousand times. I am not going to give you the number. It is not one you can push against.**

---

He sat on the afterdeck in the noon sun for an hour and did not move.

Bai Lu brought him water. He drank it. Hu Dabao, gray-faced, came and sat beside him on the deck, and did not say anything, because Dabao never said anything when it mattered, which was why he was good to sit beside. Old Seven stood at the rail with his spear grounded, facing north.

*I could let them go*, he thought. *For the night. Unbind them. Every one. Tell the Lattice to let go of the top. Then I'm only carrying myself.*

> **Then the Western Legion has no knot except Sergeant Ma. The First Legion has no knot except Old Seven, who is here, on this ship. The cemetery companies have no knot at all, because Adjutant Xu is also here.**
>
> **Fifty-two thousand dead, standing in the places they promised to stand, with nobody holding the top.**
>
> **Some of them would stay. Most of them would. They hold each other.**
>
> **Some of them would fall down. And if anything comes while they are down, nobody would ask them to stand up again until you came back.**

"And if I don't let them go, the tribulation crushes me."

> **Yes.**

He looked at the staff across his knees.

Then he looked at Adjutant Xu, sitting on his coil of rope with his cane, facing north, the way he had sat in the first carriage of the train out of Lingzhou.

"Grandpa Xu," said Yuanshu. "When you made the Lattice. In your head. Before you died. What was the point of it?"

The old man turned his head.

> **He says: so the top doesn't hold everything.**
>
> **He says: Lu Xuanming held two million alone. Every one of them, by himself, on a wall. He says that's what killed him. Not the beasts. The holding.**
>
> **He says: in a net, everyone holds a little. Nobody holds it all.**

"So when the sky presses on what I carry," said Yuanshu slowly, "it doesn't have to press on me."

> **...wait.**

"It has to press on what I carry. And what I carry is holding itself. Each one holds their nine." He held the staff. "If they want to, they could take a piece of it. Each one. A little."

> **...the tribulation targets the living realm-breaker. Probability of harm to bound undead: about four percent. That is from the Elite breakthrough. It is the same for Lords. I checked.**
>
> **If it presses through the Lattice instead of through you, then fifty-two thousand of them each take one fifty-two-thousandth of it.**
>
> **Each one takes almost nothing.**
>
> **Four percent harm to bound undead, spread over fifty-two thousand, is... I do not know what it is. Nobody has ever done this. There are no files.**
>
> **Running.**

---

The number it gave him was not a promise. It said so. It said that it had a model of the Lattice and a model of a tribulation and that it had never seen the two of them in the same room, and that it was guessing in the gap between them the way a man guesses the depth of a river by the color of the water.

But the guess was that he would live.

And the guess was that some of them would not.

> **Not many. Perhaps one in a thousand. The ones who are already very tired. The oldest. The ones whose embers are lowest.**
>
> **About fifty, Yuanshu. Out of fifty-two thousand. I could be wrong by a lot in either direction.**
>
> **They would not be destroyed. They would be put out. The way a candle is put out by a draught. Rested. Quiet. Whether they wanted to be or not.**

"Then I ask them," said Yuanshu.

> **All fifty-two thousand.**

"All of them. Through the Lattice. Each to their nine. The way the drowned did." He stood up. "And the ones who say no, I let go of, for the night. Gently. So the sky can't find them through me. They can stand where they promised to stand and hold each other, the way they already do, and nobody presses on them."

> **And the ones who say yes take a piece of your tribulation.**

"And they know, when they say it, that about one in a thousand won't stand up again." He looked north, at the empty sea. "I'm not going to dress it up."

> **You never do.**
>
> **It is very annoying. It makes the asking take longer.**
>
> **I am getting Adjutant Xu.**

---

The question went out at dusk on the third day, from the afterdeck of a frigate in the middle of the sea, through a dead signaler's single note, to the top of the Lattice, and down.

To Sergeant Ma Dashan at the gate of the Western Pass, who asked the commanders, who asked their nine. To Ma Lianhua on the river gate at Lingzhou. To the cemetery companies on the hill above the cemetery, standing around a stone gate where a captain faced out. To the Watch of the Shelf at the foot of the continental shelf off Kesse, through a pilot named Nana Ampofo. To three hundred dead soldiers of the Spear Corps standing in their rows on the afterdeck, who did not need a Lattice to hear him, because he was standing in front of them.

He told them what it was. The sky. The weight. One in a thousand. *You can say no. If you say no, I'll let you go for the night, and you'll hold each other the way you already do, and nobody will think less of you. Nobody will ask you twice.*

It took four hours for the answers to come back.

He sat on the deck with the staff the whole time, while the sun went down and the stars came out over the open sea, and listened to Seed count, and did not ask it for the number until it was done.

> **Yes: forty thousand, one hundred and eight.**
>
> **No: twelve thousand, three hundred and fifty-three.**
>
> **The ones who said no, I have let go of. Gently. They are standing where they promised. They are holding each other. They are fine.**
>
> **Of the ones who said yes, the Western Legion said: for the pass. The First Legion said: for the city. The drowned said: for the boy who asked us three kilometers down.**
>
> **The Spear Corps did not say anything. They just said yes.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is not unanimous. It is not supposed to be. Twelve thousand of them told you no, to your face, through the net, and you let them go, and nobody is angry.**
>
> **I think that is the best thing the Lattice has ever done.**

Above the *Adeyemi*, in the clear night sky over the open sea, a long way from any town, the stars in the north began, one by one, to go out.
