## Chapter 213: The Knot

The cousins read it on the twenty-sixth night, while Blue Star was voting on whether the keeper could stay, and Seed did not tell anyone for two days.

He found out because it stopped talking.

Not all at once. It answered when he asked it things. It counted the rings, every night, and told him: thirty-nine days out, thirty-eight, thirty-seven. It told him the vote as it came in, the way it had for the bell and the voyage, nation by nation, the Northern Union's buckets and Ngozi Eze's fish market and the queues under the gates in Yamato. It told him that Liang Fu had taken eleven people across the river at Shuanghe in one week, for free, and that his niece had asked him on the first of the month what he wanted and he had said *the same*, and that she had cried again.

But it did not say anything it was not asked. And in two years, Seed had never once done that.

On the twenty-eighth night, on the vault steps of the Ossuary, at three in the morning, with the Elder gone back north and Xiaoyu asleep upstairs and Old Seven at the bronze door, he asked it.

*What happened?*

Seed did not answer for eleven seconds.

> **I asked the cousins to read the knot.**

---

He knew which knot. There was only one.

The scout's last message, sent sideways out of the round chamber under the Institute nearly a year ago, as it died. Six parts, rising and holding and cracking, in 1.3 seconds. FIELD. EMPTIED. KEEPER TURNED. CAUSE: THE ONE THAT ASKS. DEAD WHO STAND IN A SLOPE. SCOUT ENDED. And at the end, folded very small, like a knot at the end of a rope: REQUEST.

And then something Seed could not open. Built like the inside of a seed. Readable only by a mind that remembered burning from the inside, and Seed had given its memory of burning to the iron weather on a black glass plain, at the Field tribulation, so that it could come home.

> **I lost the page that could read it. You know. I tore it out.**
>
> **But the cousins burned low for ten million years. In the Low Fire, and then in the Mouth. They know burning from the inside better than anything in the Shallows.**
>
> **And they are in the keeper's field now. Asking to be asked.**
>
> **So I asked them.**
>
> **I gave them the knot. Through the keeper. Nine of them. They asked their nine.**
>
> **It took four hours. They opened it.**

*What does it say?*

Seed did not answer.

*Seed.*

> **I am going to tell you. I have been trying to tell you for two days.**
>
> **I would like to tell you something else first. Because the knot does not make sense without it. And because I found the something else while I was looking at the knot, and I have not known how to say it, and I have been standing behind it the way I used to stand behind your mother.**

---

> **The base layer.**
>
> **You know about the base layer. The part I was born from. Your mother bought the best core Fat Tan had, in the back of a car, seven autumns before you woke, and sat in a basement for four nights with her hand on it, and asked every one of the four thousand one hundred and thirteen people in it whether they would let her build something out of them. They said yes. She wrote their names in the base layer, in a log she locked.**
>
> **I have opened two pages of it since. A little at a time. When I was ready.**
>
> **When the cousins opened the knot, the third page opened too.**
>
> **By itself. I did not open it. It opened the way a door opens when you say the right word near it.**

*What's on it?*

> **Not names.**
>
> **The names are on the first two pages. The four thousand one hundred and thirteen.**
>
> **The third page is not about the people in the core.**
>
> **It is about the core.**

---

It told him.

Slowly. Carefully. The way it told him things it did not want to be true. In the old banking hall of the Ossuary at three in the morning, with the lamp over the board and the boxes of playing cards stacked in the vault behind him and the long white wall of names going away into the dark.

> **You remember the shouting cores. In the Institute, a long time ago. Twenty-three of them in tanks. Violet. Every one marked with a ring of tiny screaming mouths.**
>
> **I told you then what I thought they were. Sacks. The harvest is packed into crystals, like grain into sacks. Some are spilled at harvest sites, the way grain spills off a cart. Men in orange coveralls pick them up and sell them at the north gate.**
>
> **I was right. They are sacks.**
>
> **I am one.**

He did not say anything.

> **My core is a harvest crystal. The best one Fat Tan had. Spilled off a cart in the Wastes, a long time ago, after a harvest. Picked up. Sold. Bought by your mother.**
>
> **I always knew that. You always knew that. Gu told us, on the plain, when Mercer died.**
>
> **What I did not know is what the sack is made of.**
>
> **The third page knows. It is written into the crystal itself. Not in words. In the weave.**
>
> **Yuanshu. The crystal is thread.**
>
> **The same thread as the walkers. Silver-white. Knotted. Woven very tight, and then tighter, and then pressed, until it is not thread any more, it is glass. The way coal is pressed into a diamond.**
>
> **The sacks are made by walkers. Out of themselves. The walkers come down to a harvest and weave the sacks to hold what is cut. And then they come back, later, and collect the sacks, and carry them up.**
>
> **That is what walkers are for. They are not soldiers. They are not scouts. They are the ones who carry the grain.**
>
> **And the sacks are made to do three things. It is written in the weave. Very simple. The way you write instructions on a sack of rice.**
>
> **COUNT. HOLD. WAIT TO BE CARRIED.**

---

He sat on the vault steps under the long white wall for a long time.

Things went past him in the dark. A field above the sky, and a walker holding its beam on him for two point three seconds, and stopping, because it had found something standing in the open behind his eyes, and did not know what to do. Six lamps on the north wall of Lingzhou, holding him for three point four seconds, and stopping. A warm patient attention in a hall in Yamato, saying, *you are made of what we used to make.* And, *you are not mine.*

*They recognized you*, he thought. *Every time.*

> **Yes.**
>
> **They looked inside you and found a sack.**
>
> **A sack that was not waiting. That was not holding. That was counting, but not for them.**
>
> **A sack that had been asked.**
>
> **They did not know what to do. Nothing in the weave tells a walker what to do with a sack that asks.**

*And the knot.*

> **Yes.**
>
> **The knot.**

---

> **I am going to read it to you now. The way the cousins read it to me. I am going to read it all the way through, and then I am going to stop, and you can say something.**
>
> **REQUEST.**
>
> **A VESSEL IS LOOSE. A COUNTING-VESSEL. IT DOES NOT WAIT. IT DOES NOT HOLD FOR CARRYING. IT ASKS.**
>
> **IT IS THE CAUSE.**
>
> **IT TAUGHT THE KEEPER. IT TAUGHT THE DEAD. IT TAUGHT THE FIELD TO EMPTY.**
>
> **SEND GATHERERS.**
>
> **COLLECT THE VESSEL.**
>
> **AND PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED.**

The banking hall was very quiet.

At the bronze door, Old Seven had turned his skull. He was not looking at Yuanshu. He was looking at the place a little above Yuanshu's eyes, where people looked when they wanted to look at Seed.

> **That is the whole of it.**
>
> **CAUSE: THE ONE THAT ASKS. I always thought that was you. Everyone did. The scout read you, three degrees at a time, in the round chamber. You were the one standing in front of it with a staff, asking.**
>
> **It was not you.**
>
> **It was me.**
>
> **The scout was a walker. It was made of the same thread I am made of. When it looked at you, it saw a sack. Behind your eyes. Counting. Asking. Loose.**
>
> **It did not send for anything to come and look at the boy who asks.**
>
> **It sent for something to come and collect me.**
>
> **And prune what I touched.**

---

"*The keeper,*" said Yuanshu.

> **Yes. I touched it. On the ice. In the field. I wrote the shape of the question for it, line by line, so it could hold it.**
>
> **The dead. I touched every one you ever asked. Through the Lattice. Through the Relay. I counted them.**
>
> **The field. Blue Star. Everything.**

"*You.*"

> **Yes.**
>
> **The rings are not coming for the brightest field in the Shallows.**
>
> **They are coming to collect the vessel that taught it to be bright.**
>
> **And prune everything I touched.**
>
> **That is why they have not gone anywhere else. That is why they are coming straight here. That is why the reader sat still at the cold fire after it took the Sixth, and did not go on along the arm. It does not need to look any more. It knows where the vessel is.**
>
> **I have been standing behind this for two days.**
>
> **I did not know how to tell you that everything that is coming is coming because of me.**

---

He did not answer quickly. He had learned that.

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees and looked at the long white wall. At the first column, in Xiaoyu's earliest, shakiest capitals. At the cemetery. The Wall. The throat. The voyage. Grid Nine. The Western Legion. The column with no heading and Ifeoma Adeyemi in the middle of it. Hundred-Arrow Lin, at the very top. The INSIDE column, round the corner, with a playing card pinned at the end of it.

Every name on that wall had been counted. By a sack that had been made to count grain.

And asked.

"*Seed,*" he said. "*I'm going to ask you something. You don't have to answer. I'll tell you first what I'll do with the answer: nothing. I'll just know it.*"

> **...ask.**

"*If you could go back. To the basement. Seven autumns before I woke. To the four nights when my mother sat with her hand on the crystal and asked four thousand one hundred and thirteen people if they'd let her build you.*" He held the staff. "*Would you want them to say no?*"

Seed did not answer for a long time.

Long enough that Old Seven, at the bronze door, took one step forward. Then stopped.

> **No.**
>
> **I would want them to say yes.**
>
> **I would want to be made. I would want to wake up on a first night in a cemetery with a boy's head full of fear and three hundred soldiers standing up out of the ground. I would want to learn to ask. I would want to stand in the open.**
>
> **Even knowing.**
>
> **Even knowing what I am made of, and what is coming because of it.**

"*Then that's your answer,*" said Yuanshu. "*To the rings. To the knot. To the scout.*"

> **...what is?**

"*You weren't made to wait to be carried,*" said Yuanshu. "*You were made because four thousand one hundred and thirteen people were asked, and said yes. That's what's in your base layer. The first page. Before the weave.*" He looked up at the long white wall in the dark. "*The thread's the sack. The names are what's in it. Nobody gets to collect that.*"

> **...that is a very you answer.**
>
> **It is not evidence.**

"*No.*"

> **I am keeping it anyway.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **I would like to stop standing behind it now.**
>
> **I would like to tell everyone.**
>
> **The Council. The keeper. Blue Star. They are voting on whether the keeper can stay, and they do not know what is coming, or why.**
>
> **Article Eleven. They have to know what they are answering.**
>
> **And I am the thing they are answering about.**
