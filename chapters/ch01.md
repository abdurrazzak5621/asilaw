## Chapter 1: The Stele Has No Name for It

Morning came to Lingzhou the color of weak tea.

From the top of the Awakening Hall you could see the whole city: forty kilometers of towers and rooftops packed inside the curve of the old defensive wall, with the river cutting silver through the middle. Far to the north, past the last watchtower, the land went flat and black and shining, like a lake that had turned to glass. The Northern Wastes. Nobody built anything there anymore.

And above it all, faint as a scar under makeup, a thin violet line ran across the sky. It had been there for forty years. Children here learned to draw it before they learned to draw the sun.

---

Inside, the hall was a bowl of noise.

Four thousand people on curved tiers of seats. Camera drones drifting overhead like slow silver fish. Parents in their best clothes, holding phones up, sweating in the summer heat. Every few seconds the crowd let out a roar or a groan, and the sound rolled around the curved walls and came back twice.

At the bottom of the bowl, on a round stone platform, stood the Stele.

Ten meters of black rock, shaped like a blade driven point-down into the floor. Its surface was covered in runes that nobody alive could read. They lay flat and dull, the way runes on a gravestone do, until someone touched the stone. Then they woke.

Forty years ago, on the day the sky over Blue Star split open, eleven of these had fallen out of the crack like seeds. Touch one on your eighteenth birthday and it gave you a Class and a Talent. A Class decided what you could become. A Talent decided how far.

The line of eighteen-year-olds waiting to touch it snaked down the side of the platform. Near the front, a tall boy stood with his hands in his pockets.

Zhou Yuanshu was not doing anything. That was what people noticed about him. Everyone else in the line was bouncing on their toes, whispering, checking their reflection in their phones. He was simply standing, quiet and loose, watching the Stele the way you'd watch a machine you were planning to take apart later.

"You're not even nervous," said the boy behind him.

Hu Dabao was large in every direction. He blocked most of the window behind them and a fair amount of the wall. His face was red and shiny, and he had been chewing the same piece of gum for forty minutes.

"That's illegal," Dabao said. "Be a little nervous. For morale."

"Nervous about what?"

"Bro. It's *Awakening Day.*"

"The Stele doesn't decide what I am." Yuanshu nodded at the black stone. "It just reads it."

Dabao stared at him. "That is the coolest and most annoying thing anybody has ever said to me."

---

"Next," said the official at the foot of the platform. "Shen Lingyue."

The noise of the hall changed pitch.

A girl walked out of the line. Slim, straight-backed, black hair tied high, in the dark gray training uniform of the Shen family. The Shen family had produced three Kings in two generations, and everyone in Lingzhou knew this girl's face from the news. She climbed the three steps without hurrying, and the camera drones swung toward her all at once, like sunflowers.

She laid her palm on the stone.

For a heartbeat, nothing.

Then light came out of the Stele.

It did not climb the runes. It burst out of them, white-gold and blinding, a wave of it that rolled across the platform and up the tiers and washed over four thousand faces at once. People threw up their arms. A camera drone flew straight into the beam, flickered, and dropped out of the air with a crack of plastic.

Letters burned in the air above the stone.

**[Class: Light Swordswoman]**

**[Talent: SSS — Dawnbreaker]**

For one second the hall was perfectly silent.

Then the sound hit like a wave against a sea wall. People were on their feet, screaming, hugging strangers. The first SSS in Lingzhou in eleven years. In the private boxes high on the hall's back wall, men in expensive suits were already standing up and pulling out their phones.

Shen Lingyue lowered her hand and did not even glance at the letters.

She turned her head and looked at Yuanshu. A long, level look, like a swordsman checking the only other blade in the room.

He nodded once. She nodded back, and walked down the steps.

---

"Next. Hu Dabao."

Dabao made a sound like a tire losing air, wiped his palm on his trousers, and climbed up.

The stone flared a deep, solid bronze.

**[Class: Shield Guardian]**

**[Talent: A — Unbroken Wall]**

Dabao turned around with both fists in the air and roared something about his mother's dumpling cart. His voice cracked in the middle. Down in the line, Yuanshu laughed out loud, a real laugh, and when Dabao came stumbling back down the steps he caught him and slapped his back hard enough to make the big boy cough.

"*A-rank,*" Dabao wheezed. "Bro. My mom's going to make dumplings for the whole *street.*"

"She was going to do that anyway."

"*More* dumplings."

"Next," said the official. He checked his tablet, and something in his voice tightened. "Zhou Yuanshu."

The hall went quiet as if someone had turned a dial.

---

Every academy in the five nations had a recruiter in this building today. Most of them were not here for the Shen heir.

For three years Yuanshu had come first in every exam Lingzhou No. 1 High had ever set. Not first by a little. First by so much that the school had stopped posting the second-place score next to his, because it looked like a different test. There were videos of him online solving problems on the classroom whiteboard while his teachers stood behind him taking notes.

He climbed the steps.

Up close, the Stele was warm. It hummed, very faintly, like a machine left running in an empty building. The runes nearest his hand were worn smooth by forty years of palms.

He laid his hand flat against the stone.

Nothing happened.

One second. Two. Somewhere high in the tiers, someone coughed. A drone's motor whined as it adjusted its hover.

Three.

Then the Stele went black.

---

Not dark.

*Black.*

A black so deep it seemed to drink the light out of the room. The white glow of the ceiling lamps bent toward it. Four thousand shadows on four thousand faces stretched, slowly, across the tiers and down toward the platform, as if the stone were pulling them in.

The runes did not glow. They *moved.* They slid across the surface of the stone like a crowd turning around in a street, all of them, every one, until they were pointed at the hand on the stone.

Then a sound.

It didn't come through the air. It came through the bones. One pure, enormous note, like a crystal glass the size of a mountain being struck once. Yuanshu felt it in his teeth and his ribs and the backs of his eyes. In the tiers, people grabbed the seats in front of them.

On the Awakening Bureau's wall of monitors, at the side of the hall, ten screens lit up at the same instant. Each one showed a different Stele in a different city in a different nation.

All of them black.

Letters began to form above the stone, slowly, as if the Stele were searching for words it had never needed.

**[Class: ???]**

The question marks hung there, burning white.

Then they cracked, like ice on a pond, and the real words came through underneath.

**[Class: Calamity Necromancer]**

**[Rarity: Unique — no prior holder in recorded history]**

**[Talent: Supreme Intelligence]**

**[Talent Rank: — the Stele cannot rate this talent —]**

With a sound like a rifle shot, a crack ran down the face of the Stele from top to bottom.

---

Nobody moved.

The word hung over the hall like smoke. *Necromancer.*

Every person in that room had grown up on the Black Tide. Thirty-one years ago, a Necromancer named Lu Xuanming had raised the dead of three cities in a single night, and ninety million people had died before it ended. The class was still legal. It was registered and watched. Most of its holders could raise two or three skeletons that fell apart if you kicked them.

*Calamity.*

Three kilometers away, on the east side of the city, the War Memorial Cemetery lay quiet under the morning sun. Forty thousand plain gray stones in neat rows. The unnamed dead of the Black Tide. A groundskeeper's cat was asleep on a bench by the gate.

The cat woke up.

Under the earth, all at once, forty thousand sets of bones shifted in their coffins. Not rising. Not breaking out. *Turning,* slightly, the way a sleeper turns in bed toward a sound in the next room.

All of them, toward the Awakening Hall.

Yuanshu felt it.

Forty thousand faint threads, cold and patient, reaching toward him across the city like hands held out in the dark, waiting to be taken.

He did not take them.

He lifted his palm off the stone. The black drained out of the Stele like water out of a basin, and the hall's light came back, and four thousand people let out the breath they had been holding.

Then the noise came down on him like a building collapsing.

---

Recruiters on their feet, shouting into phones. Officials pushing through the crowd. A man in the gray uniform of the Awakening Bureau was already shoving his way down the aisle with four armed guards behind him, holding up a badge, his voice cutting through the din.

"Unregistered necromantic event! The candidate is to be detained under the Black Tide Act, Article Three—"

"No," said a woman's voice. "He isn't."

She had not been in the hall a moment ago. She was there now, at the foot of the platform steps, as if she had always been standing there. A woman in her forties in a long gray coat with silver thread at the cuffs. Short hair. Calm eyes.

The air around her was heavy. You could see it, a little, the way you can see heat over a road: the light around her bent very slightly inward, toward her.

The four guards stopped walking. Not because they chose to. One of them looked down at his own boots in confusion, as if they had been nailed to the floor.

The Bureau official went white. "Master Su."

Su Mingli. Master of the Tianshu Institute, the most secret law-research body in Huaxia. One of nine Kings in the entire nation.

"This young man is under Tianshu's protection as of this moment," she said. She didn't raise her voice. Somehow every person in the hall heard every word. "Anyone who wants to detain him, question him, or touch his sleeve can do it through me. Tell Director Lu."

She walked up the steps and stopped in front of Yuanshu.

Close up, he saw that her eyes were wet.

"You have her face," Su Mingli said quietly. "Wanqing's. Your mother and I built Tianshu together, twenty years ago. She made me promise that if this day ever came, I'd be standing right here."

Yuanshu looked at her for a long moment.

Seven years ago his mother had walked into the Rift Research Institute and never walked out. No body. No explanation. A letter with an official stamp that said *missing in the line of duty* and nothing else.

"Then she knew," he said. "What I'd awaken."

"She hoped." Su Mingli glanced at the cracked Stele, then out at four thousand frozen faces. "Walk with me. You've got a lot of people to disappoint today, and none of them are going to like it."

He almost smiled.

---

They went down the steps together. The crowd in the aisle split in front of them without being asked, the way water splits around the bow of a boat. Behind them the guards stood in the aisle, rubbing their legs.

They were halfway to the doors when Yuanshu heard the note again.

That pure, impossible tone. This time it wasn't in the hall.

It was behind his eyes.

The noise of the crowd went far away, as if someone had closed a thick door. And across the inside of his mind, like white text appearing on a black screen, letters began to write themselves.

> **Initializing...**
>
> **Law signature detected: Supreme Intelligence.**
>
> **Host compatibility: 100%.**
>
> **Seed v1.0 online.**

A pause. The cursor blinked.

> **Hello, Zhou Yuanshu.**
>
> **The Stele could not name me. That is because I am not supposed to exist.**
>
> **Shall we fix that?**
