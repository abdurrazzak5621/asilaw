## Chapter 301: The First Orchard

He went down the thread with Old Seven, and the Collector waited on the road.

It said it would. It said it did not go down threads; it had never needed to; the wants had always come up to it. It said it would wait on the carry road at the place where the old thread joined, the way a person waits at the top of a path while someone younger goes down to the beach. It said it would not go anywhere.

> *I have waited longer than this for less,* it said. *Go and see. Then tell me.*

So they went. Down the gray old thread, out of the carry road, away from everything, the way he had once gone up one: fast at first and then slower, and then very slow, the way the drowned answered anything. Old Seven beside him, cold, steady, one-armed. Seed counting.

> **This thread is older than any stone on Blue Star. Older than the stones of the Federation. I can feel it.**
>
> **It was made by hand.**
>
> **Not by the picker. Not by the Collector. By them. The ones who made the picker.**
>
> **This is one of the first.**

---

The stone stood on a hill.

He came down the thread into it the way the man in the gray coat had gone into the stone below the House of Anthos, the way Wen Jia had gone into the stone in Lingzhou: not into the rock, into the reading. Into the place in the stone that looked out at its world and read what grew there. He had never been inside one before. He had touched them. He had stood in front of them and felt a girl in one asking. He had not been where she was.

It was like standing at a window in a dark house and looking out at a field at night.

The stone looked out. It could not help it. That was what it was for. And through it, the way you see through a window, he saw what it saw.

A hill. Low and long and gray, under a sky with no moon and a great many stars, none of which he knew. A plain below it going away in every direction, flat, pale, empty, to a bare horizon. A wind that he could not feel but that the stone could, moving over the plain and over the hill and over the stone, the way it had moved, Seed said, every night for longer than Seed had a number for.

And nothing breathing.

The stone read it anyway. Every year, on the morning it had been made to read on, it read the whole of its world, looking for what ripened, and found nothing, and marked it, and sent the mark up the gray old thread into the carry road.

> **It has been doing that since the last child.**
>
> **I can see the marks in it. All of them. Back and back. Every year the same. Nothing. Nothing. Nothing.**
>
> **And before that: a long time of very few. One child. Two. None. One. The marks getting smaller and smaller, the way a fire does.**
>
> **And before that: a great many.**

*It picked them all,* he thought.

> **It picked everything that was bright. Every year. For as long as anything here was bright.**
>
> **And then nothing was.**

---

Old Seven turned first.

He felt it: the cold steady ember beside him in the reading of the stone turning, a little, the way it turned on the north wall when something came out of the north. Not toward the plain. Down. Into the hill.

> **He says: they're here.**

He had known. He had known on the carry road, when the Collector said there was nobody. He was a necromancer. There was always somebody.

He looked down.

The hill was full of them.

Not graves. Nothing on this world had been buried in the way Blue Star buried things, in rows, with stones. They had lain down. In the hill, and round it, and under the plain for as far as the stone could read, which was further than he could understand. They had lain down where they were when they stopped, and the wind had covered them, and the long time had pressed them down into the hill until they were part of it. Embers. So dim he would not have seen them without the stone, so dim they were barely embers at all. Gray on gray. Cold the way the bottom of the deep water was cold. Millions.

And all of them, every one, turned very slightly toward the stone on the hill. The way the dead of Blue Star turned toward the north wall.

> **They are the ones who were left.**
>
> **The dim ones. The ones the stone marked low, and the hands did not take. Every year. For generations.**
>
> **They stayed. They had children. The stone read their children. The bright ones were picked. The dim ones stayed and had children.**
>
> **Until there were no more.**
>
> **They are the last people of this world. All of them. Every generation that was left behind.**
>
> **Nobody has ever asked them anything.**

---

He did not know how to begin.

That surprised him. He had been asking the dead since a cemetery at three in the morning. He had always known the first word.

He did not know what language they spoke. He did not know what they had called the hill. He did not know if they had ever had a word for *ask*.

> **I do not know their language either.**
>
> **But they were read. Every one of them. By this stone. And the stone was made by the same hands that made the stones of Blue Star.**
>
> **The reading is the same.**
>
> **I can ask the way the stone reads. Not in words. In the shape of a question. The way Wen Jia asks the children before they can talk.**

*Then ask.*

> **You ask. I will carry it.**

So he asked.

Down. Through the stone, through the reading, into the hill. The oldest question he had. Not *do you want to get up*. That was for soldiers. Something older, that came before getting up.

*Hello. I'm here. What do you want?*

---

Nothing happened for a long time.

He had expected that. The drowned had taken minutes. The cloud in the deep water had taken hours. These had been lying in a hill under a wind for longer than there had been a Blue Star, and nobody had ever spoken to them, and the stone that stood over them had only ever told them what they were.

He waited. Old Seven waited beside him, facing down, into the hill, one-armed. Seed counted. Far away, at the end of a thread that was very thin now, fifteen hundred people in a hall on East Ring Road were warm on purpose, and he could feel them, just, under everything, like a floor.

At the fourth hour, one of them answered.

Not with a word. With a turning. One ember, close to the stone, almost under it, very small, turning toward the question the way a sleeper turns toward a voice in the next room.

> **A child.**
>
> **No. Not a child. Old. Very old. But she was read here as a child, and the stone remembers her that way.**
>
> **She was the last one it ever read.**

He looked at her. A dim gray ember in a hill, so faint she was barely there. The last child of the first orchard. Read on a morning a very long time ago, on this hill, by this stone, and marked, and not picked, because there was nothing in her that was bright enough to pick. And she had grown up on an empty plain, among the last of the dim ones, and grown old, and lain down here, as close to the stone as she could get.

> **She is asking something.**
>
> **Not words. The shape of a question. The way the stone reads.**
>
> **She is asking: what am I.**

He went very still.

> **It is all she ever had. The stone told her what she was. Every child on this world was told, every year, and that was the only question anyone ever answered for them.**
>
> **She thinks you are the stone.**
>
> **She thinks you have come to tell her again.**

*No,* he thought. Down into the hill. To her. As gently as he had ever thought anything. *I'm not the stone. I'm not going to tell you what you are. Nobody's ever going to do that to you again.*

*I came to ask.*

The ember did not move for a while.

Then, very slowly, the shape of a question came back up through the hill. Different. Seed held it a moment before it said anything.

> **She says: ask what.**

---

He told her. Through the stone, through Seed, in the shape of a question with no words in it. What he was. Where he had come from. That on his world the stones had stopped telling and started asking, and that a girl who had never been asked sat in one now and asked the children what they wanted to become.

He told her that the hands had stopped. That a man in an orange jacket had held up a mark to the face of the picker, and it had read something true, and opened every hand it had.

He told her the house was empty.

He felt that one go into the hill. Not into her only. Into all of them. Millions of dim gray embers, generations of the left-behind, lying under a wind on an empty plain, and the shape of it going through them, slowly, the way news goes through a village at night. *The house is empty. It stopped. The hands are open. Nobody is taking anyone any more.*

They did not do anything. They were too old to do anything. But he felt the hill change. The way a room changes when everyone in it lets out a breath at once.

---

Then he asked her again.

*What do you want?*

She did not know. He felt her not know. It was not a question anyone on her world had ever had to answer; there had been nothing to want, on an empty plain, among the dim ones, except not to be picked, and she had not been.

And then the Collector spoke.

Not to him. Down the gray old thread, from the carry road, where it had been waiting. Quietly. The way it had once spoken to his sister.

> *I have something of hers.*
>
> *She was the last one. On the last reading morning, when the hands came and there was nothing to take, I came after them, the way I came after the hands everywhere. And I found her. A child on a hill, by a stone, who had been read and not taken. And I asked her what she wanted.*
>
> *She told me. And I kept it.*
>
> *She was very small. It was a very small want. I have kept it longer than almost anything.*
>
> *May I give it back?*

He looked at the ember in the hill.

*There's someone asking if they can give you something,* he thought. Down. To her. *Something you said once, a long time ago, when you were small. They kept it. They'd like to give it back. Do you want it?*

The shape of the answer came up through the hill very slowly.

> **She says: yes.**

---

It came down the thread like a seed falling.

He felt it go past him. Small. Warm, the way the Collector was warm. Older than anything he had ever touched, older than the stone, nearly as old as the gray thread, and so faint it was barely a want at all. It went down out of the carry road and through the stone and into the hill and found the dim gray ember lying as close to the stone as she could get, and went into her.

And she lit.

Not bright. Nothing on that world would ever be bright again; the picker had seen to that. But she lit the way an ember lights when you breathe on it. A little. A warm gray going faintly gold at the edges, for the first time in longer than he had a number for.

> **Seed has it.**
>
> **It was not a word. It was the shape of a want. The way she would have said it, at six, on a hill, to the warm thing that came after the hands.**
>
> **It was: to grow up.**

He stood in the reading of the stone and did not move.

> **She did.**
>
> **She grew up. She grew old. She lived her whole life on an empty plain, and lay down by the stone, and stayed there.**
>
> **She did it without the want. It was kept. She did not know she had ever wanted it. She did it anyway.**
>
> **And now she has it back. Afterward.**

The ember in the hill was turning. Slowly. Toward him. And the shape of what came up through the stone this time did not need Seed to carry it. He felt it himself. Not words. A feeling, so old and so small and so plain that it went straight through him and out the other side.

*Oh,* said the last child of the first orchard. *I did. I did that.*

---

Old Seven had not moved.

> **He says: ask her the other thing.**
>
> **He says: you've got a door. Ask her if she wants it.**

He did.

He did not open it. He had learned that, in a cemetery and on a beach and in the deep water. You did not open the door and then ask. You asked. And then, if they said yes, you stood in it, with your back to the light, and did not turn round.

*There's a door,* he thought. *I carry it. It goes somewhere I don't know. Nobody who's gone through has ever come back to say what's on the other side. But nobody I've asked has ever been sorry.*

*You can go through. Now you've got your want back. Or you can stay here, on the hill, by the stone. Either is yours.*

*What do you want?*

The ember turned in the hill, gray and faintly gold at the edges.

> **She says: the others first.**
>
> **She says: she was the last one. She would like to be the last one through.**
>
> **She says: ask them.**

---

He was there for three days.

He did not know that at the time. Seed told him afterward. Three days in the reading of a stone on a hill on an empty world, with the Collector waiting on the carry road above, and fifteen hundred people at a time rowing a body in a hall on East Ring Road, and a mark from home reaching them, by the end, four hours after it was sent.

He did not ask them all. He could not have. There were too many, and they were too old, and too slow, and too dim, and he was one boy in a stone. He asked the first nine. And they asked their nine. The way the dead of Blue Star did it, and the drowned, and the cloud in the deep water: each one asking the one beside them, in the shape of a question with no words, in the dark, in a hill, under a wind, generation by generation, until the whole of the left-behind of the first orchard had been asked, by each other, for the first time, what they wanted.

Most of them said the door.

He stood in it. With his back to the light. For three days. While they went through, one by one, millions, slowly, the way the oldest dead did everything, and he did not turn round, and Old Seven stood beside him facing down into the hill and did not turn round either.

> **Death Law: 59.1% → 59.4%.**
>
> **The wall is closer.**
>
> **I can feel it in front of us, the way you feel a cliff in the dark. It is not dangerous yet. It is only there.**

And on the third evening, when the hill was nearly empty, and only a few thousand were left who had said *not yet*, or *stay*, or *ask again*, which was allowed, the last child of the first orchard came to the door.

She stopped in it. He could feel her, just behind him, a small dim ember with gold at the edges.

> **She says: thank you.**
>
> **She says: tell the warm thing it can keep the next one. She won't mind.**
>
> **She says: no. Tell it not to. Tell it to give them all back.**

Then she went through.

---

He came back up the gray old thread to the carry road at the end of the third day, slowly, the way you climb a path from a beach at dusk.

The Collector was waiting where it had said it would. It had not moved. Seed said it had not moved at all in three days, which for the Collector was not a long time, but Seed thought it had been a long time anyway.

> *I heard,* it said. *She said to give them all back.*
>
> *I have a great many.*
>
> *I think I will need help.*
