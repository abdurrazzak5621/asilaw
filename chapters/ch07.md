## Chapter 7: The Black Tide Notebook

His room at Tianshu was on the twenty-second floor, and it had a window that wasn't real.

It was a screen set into the stone, showing a live picture of the mountain outside: pine trees black against a dark blue sky, the city glowing orange in the valley below, a few late cars crawling along the river road. It was so good that you forgot you were two hundred meters underground, until a bird flew across it and you remembered the bird was somewhere else.

At midnight, Yuanshu sat at the desk under that window with a single lamp on and the oilcloth bundle in front of him.

Old Seven stood by the door, spear grounded, the gray flame in his chest the only other light in the room.

He untied the string.

---

The notebook was bound in black leather that had cracked like dry mud. It smelled of smoke and old paper and, very faintly, of something metallic. The pages had gone the color of weak tea. The handwriting inside was small, quick and perfectly neat: the handwriting of a man who had been trained to write reports under fire and never lost the habit.

The first entries were ordinary.

A Necromancer's field journal from the first years after the Catastrophe. Patrol routes along the northern wall. Supply counts. Wind direction. A complaint about wet boots. A note that the Eastern Defense Line's cook had made dumplings for the whole company on New Year's Eve, and that Squad Leader Qi had eaten nineteen.

By the door, Old Seven did not move.

But something came down the thread between them. Faint and warm. Like a man pretending not to hear his own name across a room.

Yuanshu turned the page, and kept reading.

---

*Day 3,811.*

*The sky over the north has been wrong for a week. Not a rift. Rifts are wounds. This is a lens. Something up there is looking down. The beasts feel it too. They've stopped attacking the wall. They're sitting in the Wastes in rows, facing north, waiting.*

*Day 3,814.*

*Command doesn't believe me. A Necromancer saying the sky is watching is the kind of thing that gets you transferred to a desk. I told Qi. Qi believed me. Qi believes everything I say, which is either loyalty or terrible judgment, and with Qi it's always been both.*

*Day 3,816.*

*It came down tonight.*

*I don't have a better word for it than "man." It had the shape of a man. It had no face. Where the face should be there was something smooth that reflected nothing, not even the fires on the wall. It walked down out of the northern sky as if there were stairs there. Where it walked, the beasts knelt.*

*Then it turned and looked at the city, and the city started to die.*

*Not by fire. Not by claws. People just stopped. A soldier on the wall next to me sat down and didn't get up again. His eyes were open. He was breathing. But whatever made him* him *was gone. It had been taken. Lifted out, like water out of a bucket.*

*It was harvesting us. That's the only word I have. Like grain.*

*Command has ordered a retreat. There is nowhere to retreat to.*

*So I'm going to do the thing they always told us Necromancers must never do.*

---

The next three pages were stained dark and stiff, glued together at the edges. Yuanshu separated them with the tip of a knife, very slowly, one millimeter at a time.

> **Blood. Thirty-one years old.**
>
> **The Death residue on these pages is stronger than anywhere else in the book. He wrote them while channeling something far beyond his class.**

---

*Day 3,817. I think.*

*I raised them.*

*Not three. Not thirty. Everyone. Every dead body in three cities, all at once. I didn't ask them. There wasn't time to ask. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry. I will be sorry for the rest of my life, which won't be long now.*

*It worked. The faceless one couldn't harvest the dead. There was nothing left in them for it to take. The dead have nothing it wants. So they could walk right up to it, and it didn't even look at them.*

*They tore it apart.*

*It fell somewhere under Lingzhou. The Institute has it now. A young lieutenant from the Eastern Line, Lu, carried the containment order to me himself. He looked at me like I was the end of the world.*

*Maybe I am.*

*I can't put them down. There are too many and I'm too small a vessel. The dead are still walking and I can't make them stop. Ninety million, they're saying now. Most of them were already gone, emptied, before I ever raised them. But nobody will believe that, and I won't ask them to.*

*Qi is missing. He was holding the eastern end of the wall when it came down. I've looked everywhere I still can.*

---

Yuanshu stopped reading.

He looked up at the door.

Old Seven had not moved. But the gray flame in his chest had shrunk down to almost nothing, a tiny blue-gray bead, like a pilot light in a draft.

> **His echo is very quiet right now.**
>
> **I think he remembers that night. Not in words. In the shape of it.**
>
> **He held the line alone. And the man he trusted most was looking for him while he died.**

Yuanshu closed the notebook.

He got up, crossed the dark room, and stood next to the skeleton by the door. Shoulder to shoulder. Both of them facing the same way.

"He looked for you," he said quietly. "He didn't stop looking."

For a long time, Old Seven did not move.

Then, slowly, he lifted one bony hand and set it on Yuanshu's shoulder. It was cold, and very light. It stayed there for a second.

Then he took it away, and turned back to face the door, exactly as before. And the gray flame in his chest crept back up to its usual size.

---

The last page was different.

The handwriting was the same, but slower. Larger. As if the hand holding the pen had been very tired, and had needed to rest between words. It had no date.

*To the next one.*

*If you're reading this, the Stele finally gave one of us what we needed. I hope it gave you enough. It didn't give me enough.*

*They'll call you a monster. Let them. The dead don't care what the living call us, and the living will need you anyway.*

*Here's what I learned too late:*

*Ask them. Always ask them. They'll almost always say yes. The ones who held the line will always say yes. But you have to ask, or you become what they say you are.*

*The faceless one wasn't alone. It was a scout. Something sent it. Something will come back for what it marked.*

*I'm going north to die where the dead can't follow me. Under the white stone at the old survey marker, Grid 47-121, in the Northern Wastes. My staff is with me. Take it. It remembers more than I do.*

*Don't raise me. There won't be enough of me left to say yes.*

*— L.X.*

---

Yuanshu sat with the notebook open on his knees for a long time. The false window showed a car's headlights crawling along the river road, very small and very far away.

> **Grid 47-121 is three hundred and forty kilometers north of the Lingzhou wall. Inside the Northern Wastes.**
>
> **Beast density: extreme.**
>
> **And one more thing. Under the Death residue on these pages, there is a second law signature.**
>
> **I do not recognize it.**
>
> **I want to be exact. It is not that I don't know its name. It is that its structure does not match any of the three thousand Great Laws.**
>
> **In that way, it is like me.**
>
> **I do not like that.**

*The faceless one's law.*

> **91% probability. It touched these pages. Or it touched him while he was writing them.**

Yuanshu closed the book.

---

He found Su Mingli where he guessed she'd be.

At one in the morning, the observatory dome on top of the mountain was cold and dark and smelled of dust and pine. She was standing under the old brass telescope, the one that hadn't worked in forty years, with one eye pressed to the eyepiece, looking at stars through a lens that was too cloudy to show any.

He told her everything. The faceless one. The harvest. Lieutenant Lu. The scout. Grid 47-121.

She listened without interrupting once.

When he finished, she took her eye from the telescope and stood with her hand on the cold brass for a while.

"Your mother read that notebook," she said finally. "Twelve years ago. Grandpa Xu showed it to her. It's why she put sensors on the northern wall. It's why she started the project she called Seed." She turned around. Starlight from the open slit in the dome lay across her face. "And now you want to go north."

"Yes."

"You're Level 14. The ground around Grid 47 is Lord-grade. Worse, if the old stories are true." She folded her arms. "I could carry you there. Pick you up, fly you to the grave, set you down, carry you home. Like luggage."

"I'd learn nothing," he said. "Seed needs to see the north with my eyes. Not from your back."

The corner of her mouth moved.

"Then listen. The Wastes aren't a place you visit because a dead man asked you to. They're a place you survive. You'll go when you're strong enough to walk the last kilometer on your own feet, and not one day before." She held up a finger. "There's a dungeon forty kilometers east of the city. Bonefield Hollow. Grade two. You go in with no more than fifty undead. You come out at Level 20. That's the first step. Not the last."

"Deadline?"

"One week."

Yuanshu thought about it for exactly as long as it took Seed to finish a calculation.

> **Estimated time to Level 20 in a grade-two dungeon, with Old Seven and forty-nine undead: 2.6 days.**

"Three days," he said.

Su Mingli laughed out loud. The sound went up into the cold dark dome and came back down off the curved walls, again and again, like birds.

"Your mother," she said, "would have said two."
