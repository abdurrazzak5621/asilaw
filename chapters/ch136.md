## Chapter 136: Look at the Floor

Lu stopped.

Yuanshu saw it through the pane of law-glass across the chamber: the director's hands going still on the dead control board, his face going empty, his head beginning to turn, slowly, three degrees, toward the blank face that had found him. The way Orlov's had. The way a hundred thousand heads had turned in a vision twelve hundred years old.

Ren could not reach him. She was holding Orlov and the dome at once, and the alcove was on the far side of the chamber, and her face was white with it.

> **It has him.**
>
> **One slice. It is reading him. If it holds, it lifts him.**
>
> **Yuanshu, I cannot do anything. The gaps are too short.**

The gaps were too short for the living to move in.

The head was not.

He saw it the way he had seen the teeth in Kwan's flat gray line, all at once and already finished. Every slice, the head turned. Three degrees. Not the beam alone: the head. Hale's head had turned on its pillow because the beam was passing over him, but the faceless one's head turned because the beam came out of it. The head was the lighthouse lamp. It had to point the beam where it wanted to read.

You cannot read what you are not facing.

"*Mingli,*" he said. "*Its head. Hold its head. Make it look at the floor.*"

---

Su Mingli did not ask why.

She raised her hand, the one she had used to lift forty thousand tonnes over Tannery Square, and closed it.

The faceless one's head went down.

Not gently. Gravity came down on it like a hand on the back of a neck, from directly above, a weight so precise that it touched nothing else in the chamber. The smooth face tipped forward, past level, past forty-five degrees, until it was pointed straight down at the stone floor between its own feet, and stopped there, and held.

The slice of attention that had been holding on Lu Zhengtian swung down with it. Off the alcove. Off the director. Down onto the floor of the round chamber, where there was nothing to read but rock.

In the alcove, Lu gasped and caught himself on the control board with both hands.

> **It is reading the floor.**
>
> **The floor is stone. Stone is on the list of things that are allowed. There is nothing in it.**
>
> **It is not sweeping. It cannot sweep. Its lamp is pointed at the ground.**
>
> **Yuanshu, that was very good. That was better than anything I modeled.**

"*How long can you hold it?*" he said.

Su Mingli did not answer. Her arm was shaking. Blood had started at her nose again, a thin line, and she let it run.

"Not long," she said.

---

"*Spear Corps,*" said Adjutant Xu, at the foot of the platform, through the lattice. He did not need to shout. He asked the commanders. The commanders asked their nines. "*Go.*"

The dead went in.

Sixty spears, from every side, into the thing with its head held down. It could not read them. It never could. That was how it had lost the Black Tide: two million of the dead who had never been asked, tearing it apart in the dark because they were the one thing in the world it could not see.

It could still feel them.

The faceless one moved. Faster than anything that size should move. Its one arm swept out, and six of the Spear Corps went across the chamber and hit the wall and came apart, and inside the Field their bones slid back across the stone toward each other, and they stood up and went back in. Its body twisted. Gray crystal came out of its chest in spikes, through the seams, through the old repairs, branching, the way the sphere in Port Halvard had grown, and a spike went through a soldier's ribs and pinned him to the floor, and he took the spike in both bone hands and broke it off, and stood.

The spears found the seams. Old Seven had shown them how. Ankle, knee, hip, spine, shoulder, elbow, wrist. They drove for the seams in the gray body where thirty-one years ago the pieces had been fitted back together.

The points went in.

And stopped. Two centimeters deep. Against crystal.

> **It has grown under its skin.**
>
> **The seed has been plating it from inside. Every seam. Every repair. Two centimeters of Hunger crystal under the gray.**
>
> **The spears cannot go through. Not iron.**

"*Pale fire,*" said Yuanshu.

---

Lingyue was already moving.

She came across the chamber in a line of silver-gray, inside the Field, where her sword burned pale without the staff, and cut. Not at the faceless one. At the crystal coming out of its seams. Her blade went through a spike the way it had gone through the roots in Port Halvard, and where it passed, the gray went dull and frosted and crumbled.

But it was one sword. And the faceless one was three meters tall and plated in crystal from the inside, and its chest was growing spikes faster than one sword could cut them.

She looked back. Over her shoulder. At the west side of the chamber.

At her grandfather.

Shen Jinghong had not moved since the cylinder burst. He stood where he had been told to stand, inside the Field, beside where his granddaughter had been, with his hand on his sword. He had not drawn it.

Yuanshu understood. Eighty-four years of Light that did not care what it burned. Three hundred years of a family. A son who had burned a pass at the Northern Rift, and the rescue team in it.

"*Grandfather,*" said Lingyue. Not loudly. She did not stop cutting. "*It doesn't care what it burns. You do.*"

Shen Jinghong drew his sword.

---

It came out white.

A King's Light. Not a thread. Not a blade. A white so complete that every shadow in the round chamber vanished at once, and the walls and the floor and the dead and the Kings and the faceless one stood out in it like figures cut from paper. The Spear Corps on the platform turned their skulls away. Bai Lu, inside Ren's dome, put her hands over her eyes.

Yuanshu felt it hit the Field.

It hit the slope the way a river hits a dam. All that white, all that Light that had never once been told where not to burn, coming up against five kilometers of ground that ran downhill toward Death. And for a moment he did not know which would give. He felt the Field strain. He felt the slope bend under the weight of a King's Light the way the cobbles of Tannery Square had cracked under the Bridge-Breaker.

Then the white ran downhill.

From the hilt to the point, the way frost spreads across a window. White to white-gold to silver to the deep gray of the pale fire, a King's sword turning pale in an old man's hand, and the light in the chamber going with it, from blinding white to a cold soft silver that touched the dead and ran over their bones like water and did not leave a mark.

Shen Jinghong looked at his sword.

His face did not change at all. But his hand did. It loosened on the hilt, very slightly, the way a hand loosens when it has been gripping something for a very long time and has just been told it can stop.

Then he cut.

---

It was not a sword stroke. It was a weather.

A single sweep of pale fire across the whole back of the faceless one, from its shoulder to its heel, a King's width, and everywhere it passed, the gray crystal under the skin went dull and frosted and crumbled to sand. The faceless one's body arched. The spikes in its chest shattered. The seams split open where the plating had held them shut.

"*Now,*" said Adjutant Xu. "*Now, now, now.*"

The spears went in. All the way. Sixty of them, into the open seams, and the faceless one shuddered from head to foot, and its one arm swept and swept and the dead flew and stood up and went back in.

And Old Seven went up the front of it.

He climbed the way he had climbed the spire in the bay at Port Abiri. Bone on gray crystal. One hand with one finger and a thumb. Up the shuddering body to the chest, to the branching gray frost-crystal over the seed, to the gap the size of a coin where the color of a coal still showed.

It was smaller than a coin now.

It was closing while he climbed.

> **Ninety-eight percent.**
>
> **Ninety-nine.**
>
> **Yuanshu, it is closing faster. It is putting everything into closing.**

"*Mingli...*"

"*I know,*" said Su Mingli. She was on one knee. Her arm was still raised. Her face was gray. "*It's lifting. I can't...*"

The head began to come up.

---

Not far. A degree. Two. Against a King's gravity, the smooth blank face lifted off the floor, by degrees, by fractions, the way the hands of a clock move when you watch them long enough to see.

Toward level.

Toward the room. Toward the living.

And the gap in its chest, under Old Seven's hand, was a crack. A hair. A line of coal-color you could only see if you knew where to look.

On the far side of the chamber, in the alcove, behind the law-glass, Lu Zhengtian had taken his keycard off his lanyard.

Yuanshu saw him through the pane. Saw him look at the dead control board. Saw him look down, through the glass, at the platform in the center of the chamber, and at the low steel ring that ran around the edge of it, flush with the floor, that Yuanshu had stood on three times now and never once looked at.

The oldest array in the room. The first one. Built thirty-one years ago, to hold a cylinder upright on its pedestal, on its own breaker, on its own line, with its own switch beside it on the floor.

Not on the board.

Lu Zhengtian opened the door of the alcove and walked out into the round chamber.
