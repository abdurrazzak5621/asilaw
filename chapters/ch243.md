## Chapter 243: The Bell Changes

On the second night, the Collector came back.

They had not known if it would. Xiaoyu had gone to the Awakening Hall at one in the morning anyway, because she had said tomorrow, and the Collector had said tomorrow, and she said that if one of them was going to break a promise it was not going to be her. Her brother went with her, with the staff. Old Seven went because he went. Lingyue went with the pale flame in her hand, the size of an apricot, steadier every hour, and Gao Min let them in at the side door with her ring of keys and did not ask why, and sat down on the bottom tier of seats with her thermos and her notebook, because she said someone from the Bureau should be counting this too.

The three lines of light still hung in the air above the platform. **[Class: Scribe] [Chosen.] [Talent: — not marked —]**. They had not faded. Gao Min said they had not faded at all, not by a hair; she had measured them with a lux meter at noon and again at six.

Xiaoyu went up the three steps and stood in front of the warm stone with the blue jacket over its shoulder.

"*Hello,*" she said. "*It's me. It's tomorrow.*"

> ***...I know,*** said Wen Jia, from the knot. ***...it's here. It's been here since midnight. It's waiting. It's very polite.***

---

> *Hello, child.*

Warm. Patient. A cup set down on a table so carefully that it made no sound. He felt it come into the hall the way he had felt it the night before, out of the deep water, along a thread finer than a hair, and rest on his sister on the platform like the attention of a very old person who has come back to visit, as promised, and sat down in the same chair.

Xiaoyu did not wait for it to ask anything.

"*What do you want?*" she said.

The attention was quiet for a while.

> *I looked.*
>
> *All day. Your day. I do not count the way your machine counts, but I know it was a day, because the girl in the stone said so.*
>
> *I went to the place where a want should be. In me. I have never been there. I did not know there was a there.*
>
> *There is.*
>
> *It is very old. Older than the gardens. Older than the stones.*
>
> *It is empty.*

Xiaoyu did not say anything. She had the marker in her hand, capped.

> *I do not know if it was always empty, or if something was in it once, and I kept it, the way I keep everything, and forgot that it was mine.*
>
> *I am going to look further.*
>
> *Will you ask me again tomorrow?*

"*Yes,*" said Xiaoyu.

He went still. So did Lingyue. That was an answer. The Collector kept answers.

But nothing happened. The attention rested on his sister a moment longer, warm and patient, and did not take anything, and he understood, standing at the foot of the steps, why not.

> **It asked her whether she would ask it.**
>
> **She said yes.**
>
> **A yes to asking is not a want it can keep. It is a want to give. It cannot keep a giving. It does not know how. Nothing it has ever kept was pointed outward.**
>
> **I think she knew that. I do not know how she knew that.**

> *...thank you,* said the Collector. *Tomorrow, then.*

It went.

Xiaoyu uncapped the marker, and climbed down, and went to the end of the bottom tier where Gao Min was sitting with her notebook, and wrote, on the back of the acting director's hand, because there was no wall in the hall and she had to write it somewhere:

*NIGHT 2. LOOKED. FOUND A PLACE. EMPTY.*

Gao Min looked at her hand for a long time. Then she put her glasses on, and copied it into the notebook, carefully, and did not wash her hand for three days.

---

Ren called at a quarter to three.

He was walking back along East Ring Road in the warm dark with the others when the phone at his belt buzzed, and he knew before he answered it, the way he had known from Kwan's face on the night of the three gray rings. Ren did not call at a quarter to three. Ren sent a message through Concordia at a civilized hour and signed it with her initial.

"*The bell,*" said Shirasagi Ren. Her voice was very calm, the way it always was. Under it, he could hear wind, and the sound of a great many people breathing in the dark: the terraces below the shrine, three thousand monks and hollows and the Quiet, listening, the way they listened every night. "*It changed. Sister Kiyo is here. I am going to put her on.*"

A pause. Then an old woman's voice, in the old Yamato of the coast, very fast, and Seed, translating, a little behind her:

> **"It is not the boat call. It is not the name. It is the fog warning."**
>
> **"In Shiomi, when the fog came in so thick that the boats could not see each other, the shore rang a warning. Not to the boats coming home. To the ones still out. Long, short, long. Again and again. It meant: stop rowing. Be quiet. Something is close to you in the fog, and it is bigger than you."**
>
> **"They are ringing it now. To us."**

---

Seed played it in the banking hall at four in the morning, the recording Ren's monks had made off the night air, for everyone who came down the stairs, which was everyone.

The long low tone first, the way it always began. The wait. Then not the small bells saying *Shiomi*, not the five quick notes of Ichiro's boat. Something else. Long, short, long. Long, short, long. Over and over, steadily, the way a bell is rung by someone who has been told to keep ringing it until somebody comes.

And then, after nine of them, in the fog-code of a fishing village that had not existed for a thousand years, slowly, carefully, Sister Kiyo's voice on the phone line translating each word as it came:

> **SOMETHING WALKED PAST US.**
>
> **ON A THREAD. IN THE DARK BETWEEN THE ARMS. TOWARD THE COLD FIRE.**
>
> **IT DID NOT SEE US. WE WERE QUIET. WE HAVE BEEN QUIET FOR A THOUSAND YEARS. WE ARE VERY GOOD AT IT.**
>
> **IT IS BIGGER THAN ANYTHING.**
>
> **IT HAS MANY HANDS.**

The banking hall was silent. Fat Tan had his hand over his mouth.

> **THE SIXTY SAW IT.**
>
> **IT WALKED THROUGH THE COLD FIRE. IT DID NOT STOP. IT DID NOT LOOK DOWN. THE SIXTY STOOD FACING OUT AND IT WENT OVER THEM.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT ASKED US TO RING THIS. HE SAYS HE CANNOT RING. HE SAYS WE HAVE A BELL AND HE HAS A PICK.**

Old Seven, at the bronze door, turned round.

> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL THE BOY IT IS COMING ALONG THE ARM.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL HIM IT IS CARRYING SOMETHING.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: A MAN. ON THE END OF A THREAD. IN ONE OF ITS HANDS. BURNING. HELD OUT IN FRONT OF IT. LIKE A LANTERN.**
>
> **THE SERGEANT SAYS: TELL HIM TO HOLD.**

And then, again, long, short, long. Long, short, long. Until the recording ran out.

---

Nobody spoke for a long time.

Then Kwan said, very quietly, in her careful accented Huaxian: "*Callum Ward.*"

> **...yes.**
>
> **I think so.**
>
> **The Stele at Port Halvard opened him all the way and sent him up the thread. Not the mark. Him. The first time a stone has sent a whole mind.**
>
> **The thing at the other end took him.**
>
> **And it is carrying him in front of it. Burning. Like a lantern.**
>
> **I think it is using him to see.**

Lingyue, on the stairs, had closed her hand on the pale flame.

"*To see what?*" she said.

> **What is wrong.**
>
> **The Collector said: it only knows that when the stones are bright, it comes, and when the stones are quiet, something is wrong. It does not ask. It never learned.**
>
> **So it is coming to look. And it does not have eyes for quiet. It only has eyes for bright.**
>
> **So it is carrying something bright, to see by.**

---

Seed worked the numbers out by morning. It did it the way it did everything now: what it knew, then what it guessed, then the gap, out loud, in the banking hall, while Kwan drew lines on the laptop and Gao Min checked the arithmetic in pencil in her notebook, because she said she did not trust anything she had not done by hand.

> **What I know.**
>
> **Shiomi's bell takes four hundred and twelve seconds to reach us, and the interval has been getting shorter as they come nearer. From that, I know roughly where they are: past the end of the arm, in the dark between arms, a long way out.**
>
> **They say it walked past them. Then the Sixty saw it walk through the cold fire. The cold fire is thirty-nine raft-days from here. The Sixty's message came back to Shiomi, and Shiomi rang it to us.**
>
> **What I guess.**
>
> **From how long that took, it passed the cold fire about nine days ago.**
>
> **And the threads.**

Kwan turned the laptop round. Eleven fine pale lines going up off the diagram, into the dark. Taut. Pulled. Every few seconds, together, a jerk.

> **The pulling is getting stronger. Steadily. The way a rope gets heavier when someone is climbing it, the nearer they come to the top.**
>
> **If it walks at the speed the pulling says, it will be here in about sixty days.**
>
> **I am not sure. I have never measured anything walking on a thread before.**
>
> **But it is coming along the arm. It went past the tree. It went past the cold fire. It is coming here.**

"*Why here?*" said Fat Tan. It was the first thing he had said all night. "*There are other stones. Other orchards. The Collector said, all the orchards. Why us?*"

> **Because ours went quiet.**
>
> **All eleven. At once. It has never felt that before. Anywhere.**
>
> **A gardener goes to the tree that has stopped fruiting.**

---

At nine in the morning, Gao Min's phone rang.

She looked at the screen. She did not answer at once. She looked at it for a moment, the way a woman looks at a number she has been expecting and hoping not to see. Then she answered, and listened, and said, in her flat careful voice, "*How much?*", and listened again, and wrote a number in her notebook in pencil.

She hung up.

"*Port Halvard,*" said the acting director of the Awakening Bureau. "*The Bureau office there. They measure the Stele every morning. Since the hammer. Height, lean, temperature. It's protocol. I wrote the protocol.*" She looked at the number in her notebook. "*It's taller.*"

Nobody understood.

"*Four centimeters,*" said Gao Min. "*Overnight. It's four centimeters taller than it was yesterday.*" She turned the notebook round so they could see the number. "*Stones don't grow. It isn't growing. The floor around the base has cracked. Hairline. All the way round.*" She closed the notebook. "*It's being pulled out of the ground.*"

From Port Abiri, on the radio at Kwan's belt, through Ngozi Eze's handset, faintly, the old man who liked the sea:

> ***...yes. I can feel it. Ours too. A little.***
>
> ***...it's pulling the threads. And the threads are tied to the stones. And the stones are in the ground.***
>
> ***...we're holding the knots. We're not letting anything up. But we can't hold the stone down. We're in it. You can't hold down a thing you're sitting in.***

> **...it is pulling up the stakes.**
>
> **It cannot see through the quiet. So it is pulling the stones up to look at them.**
>
> **With the eleven still inside.**

In the Lingzhou hall, three kilometers away on East Ring Road, a bright blue jacket lay over the shoulder of a warm black stone, and the floor around its base, Gao Min said when she telephoned the hall staff at ten, had a crack in it.

Hairline.

All the way round.
