## Chapter 40: North

The ward on the third floor of the Rift Research Institute was quietest at night.

The nurses dimmed the lights at ten. After that there was only the soft green glow of the monitors, fourteen of them, each one tracing the same slow line across its screen, and the faint hiss of oxygen, and fourteen people breathing in a long room in the dark, all at exactly the same rhythm, like a single sleeping animal.

Yuanshu sat in a plastic chair beside the fourth bed on the left.

He came at eleven every night now, after the Ossuary closed. The night nurse knew him. She would bring him tea in a paper cup and set it on the table beside the folded paper crane, and go back to her desk at the end of the ward and read a magazine and not look at him. Old Seven stood at the foot of the bed with his spear grounded and his gray flame turned down low, like a lamp somebody had remembered to dim.

Andrei Belov lay on his back with his eyes open, looking at the ceiling.

Yuanshu held his hand.

---

It was always the same.

He would close his eyes and reach. Not into the Death Law: the boy wasn't dead. Into the other place. The place where minds live. And he would go down, and down, through an emptiness so complete it was like falling through a dark well with no walls, past the place where a mind should have been and wasn't, toward the bottom.

And at the bottom, something was waving.

Faint. So faint. A thread of light at the bottom of the well, like the last ember in a fireplace that has been out all night. Moving. Back and forth. As if someone very small and very far away were standing on the bottom of the world, waving both arms over their head at an airplane that might never see them.

He would reach for it.

And every night, it was just a little too far.

> **Day eighteen.**
>
> **It is no closer. It is no further.**
>
> **I have run four hundred thousand simulations. None of them reach it.**
>
> **I do not think this is a problem of power.**
>
> **I think it is a problem of asking.**

*I've been asking.*

> **You have been asking him.**
>
> **He is not the one holding the thread.**

---

Su Mingli found him there at midnight.

She came into the ward without a sound and stood at the foot of the bed beside Old Seven for a long time, looking at the boy, before she spoke. The night nurse looked up from her magazine, saw who it was, and looked very firmly back down.

"Lu told me what Wanqing wrote," Su Mingli said quietly. "In the note."

"I thought he might."

"Seven years. He carried it for seven years." She shook her head slowly. "And I carried mine." She was silent for a moment. "I knew she was going to do something. That week. She came up to the observatory on the Thursday night and stood under the old telescope with me and didn't say anything for an hour, and then she said, 'Mingli, if I go somewhere you can't follow, will you stand in front of him at the Stele?' And I said yes. And I thought she meant she was going to die." Su Mingli's voice was very level. "I've spent seven years thinking she died. Lu's photographs say she didn't. They say she walked into that cylinder and through it, somewhere, on purpose."

She looked at the window. North.

"The Charter is in ninety days," she said. "After the Charter, we go."

Yuanshu looked up.

"North," said Su Mingli. "Grid 47-121. Lu Xuanming's grave and his staff. And then further. Grid Nine. The places the trucks came from. The place the sound came from." Her voice didn't change. "Wherever the Wailing Monarch is. Wherever the threads go. Whatever's at the other end of the lens."

"You said I wasn't ready."

"You're not." She looked at him. "Neither am I. Neither is anyone. We're going anyway. But not like luggage. Not with me carrying you. A real expedition. Supplies. Maps. People who know the Wastes. Your three commanders. Your Shen girl, if she'll come, and she will. The Western War Zone boy, if his grandfather allows it, and he won't, and the boy will come anyway." Something moved at the corner of her mouth. "We walk in. On our feet. Like your mother would have wanted."

"After the Charter."

"After the Charter. Because if we go before it, and something happens to you out there, the Atlantic Federation gets its Data Act and WARDEN gets everything you ever touched." She turned to go. "So win the hearing. Then we'll go and find out what's been eating the north."

She stopped at the door of the ward.

"Get some sleep," she said. "That's an order from a King."

He didn't.

---

At 3:41 in the morning, he reached one more time.

He was so tired that he did it without thinking. Without Seed running the numbers first. Without trying. He just held the thin cold hand on top of the sheet and let himself go down, the way you let yourself fall asleep, down the long dark well toward the faint waving light at the bottom.

And this time, at the bottom, he didn't reach for the thread.

He asked it.

*Andrei,* he thought, into the dark. *Your mother said yes. She said you could come home. Is there anything you need from me?*

The light at the bottom of the well stopped waving.

For a long moment, it was perfectly still.

Then it moved.

Not toward him. *Up.* Climbing. Fast. The faint thread of light came up the well like a spark rising from a fire, up and up through the empty dark, past the place where a mind should have been, toward the surface, toward him, brighter and brighter, until it filled everything.

> **Wait.**
>
> **Wait. That's not...**
>
> **That's not only him.**

---

Andrei Belov opened his eyes.

They had been open for four months. But now they *opened.* Something came into them. A focus. A presence. Like lights coming on in a house that had been dark for a long time.

His head turned on the pillow.

Slowly. Three degrees. Toward Yuanshu.

His lips moved. His voice came out dry and cracked, a whisper, the voice of a nineteen-year-old who had not spoken in a hundred and twenty days.

"...Mama?"

Yuanshu's heart stopped.

"Andrei," he said. "Andrei, it's all right, I'm..."

The boy's eyes changed.

It happened in an instant. The focus in them went flat. Wide. Something else was behind them now. Not a boy. Something that looked out of his face the way you'd look out through a window in somebody else's house.

The voice that came out of Andrei Belov's mouth was not his.

It was not anybody's. It was calm and level and very old and had no feeling in it at all, and it came out of the boy's throat the way sound comes out of a radio.

"*IDENTIFY.*"

---

All down the long dark ward, thirteen heads turned on thirteen pillows.

Together. In perfect unison.

Three degrees. Toward the fourth bed on the left.

Thirteen pairs of empty eyes, lit faintly green by the monitors, all looking at the boy holding Andrei Belov's hand.

At the foot of the bed, Old Seven's gray flame went from almost nothing to white in an instant, and his spear came down level, and a sound came out of his chest that Yuanshu had heard only once before, in a round chamber three hundred meters under this building, in front of a faceless thing in a cylinder of glass.

> **They are all connected.**
>
> **Every hollow in this room. A thread. Thin as a hair. Going up.**
>
> **It is not him at the bottom of the well. It never was.**
>
> **It is a receiver.**
>
> **And something on the other end of it has just found you.**

At the far end of the ward, the night nurse had dropped her magazine and was standing with her back against the wall and both hands over her mouth.

And in the open doorway behind her, lit softly from the corridor, in a gray cardigan with leather patches at the elbows and his reading glasses on a cord around his neck, stood Deputy Director Gu Hanshan.

He was holding a cup of tea.

He looked down the long ward at the fourteen turned heads and the white-burning skeleton and the boy in the plastic chair, with his kind, tired, intelligent face, the face of a favorite uncle, the laugh lines deep at the corners of his eyes.

"Ah," he said gently. "You asked him, didn't you."

He took a sip of his tea.

"Your mother did that too."
