## Chapter 188: Eleven

The keeper counted them for him at three in the morning, the long way, through the Elder, and its voice shook.

> ***...ELEVEN.***

The Elder stood in the middle of the old banking hall in the lamplight with the gold thread running through her, and her silver eyes were wide open, and her long hands were not pressed together. They were at her sides. That was twice now, in a year, that he had seen her hands at her sides.

> ***...AT THE EDGE OF MY FIELD. WHERE THE ONE WALKED AWAY INTO THE HAZE.***
>
> ***...THEY CAME OUT OF THE HAZE TONIGHT. ONE AT A TIME. I COUNTED.***
>
> ***...ELEVEN.***

"*Walkers,*" said Yuanshu.

> ***...YES.***

"*Coming for you?*"

The gold thread trembled the whole length of the Elder, from the crown of her head to her feet.

> ***...NO.***
>
> ***...THEY ARE NOT WALKING INTO THE FIELD. THEY ARE STANDING AT THE EDGE OF IT. IN A LINE. ALL ELEVEN.***
>
> ***...THEY ARE NOT LOOKING AT ME. THEY ARE NOT LOOKING AT THE FEW WHO STAYED.***
>
> ***...THEY ARE LOOKING DOWN.***

He was very still on the vault steps.

> ***...AT YOUR WORLD.***
>
> ***...AT THE NORTH. AT THE WASTES. AT THE PLACE WHERE THE ONE THIRTY-ONE YEARS AGO WALKED DOWN THE SKY.***
>
> ***...AT YOU.***

---

Nobody in the banking hall said anything for a long time.

Fat Tan, at his desk under the window, had put down his pen. Kwan had stopped typing. Xiaoyu was on the stairs in her pajamas, where she had come down at the first word without anyone waking her, with her hand on the banister. Old Seven stood by the bronze door. Adjutant Xu sat on his chair beside it, with his cane.

> **The scout's message. Thirty-one years after it came down. FIELD. EMPTIED. KEEPER TURNED. CAUSE: THE ONE THAT ASKS. DEAD WHO STAND IN A SLOPE.**
>
> **The walker that went away. What it saw: a girl who throws light uphill, a boy with a white staff, and a thing made of the old stuff that stands in the open and says: look at me.**
>
> **The gardener has decided.**
>
> **It is not coming itself. It is sending walkers. Eleven. Down the sky. The way it sent one, thirty-one years ago.**
>
> **To look at the cause.**

"*How long?*" said Yuanshu.

> ***...THEY ARE SLOW. THEY ARE ALWAYS SLOW.***
>
> ***...THE ONE THIRTY-ONE YEARS AGO TOOK NINE OF YOUR DAYS FROM THE EDGE OF MY FIELD TO YOUR GROUND.***
>
> ***...NINE DAYS.***

---

Lu Zhengtian had been twenty-two on the night of the Black Tide.

Yuanshu thought about that, sitting on the vault steps at three in the morning with the gold thread humming through the Elder and the long white wall of names going away into the dark. A lieutenant running along the base of a burning wall with a containment case under his arm, toward an Institute that had seen a lens in the north three days before. One walker. Walking down the sky. And in its wake, the rifts, all across the north at once, and the beasts pouring out of them, and a necromancer on a wall who raised the dead of three cities in a single night to hold them back, because there was nothing else, and could not put them down again.

Ninety million.

One walker.

> **The beasts came out of the rifts because the walker came down through them. I think. The scout opened the way, the way a ship opens a wake. Everything behind it came through.**
>
> **I do not know if that is what happened. Nobody knows. Lu Xuanming did not know. Lu Zhengtian spent his life trying to find out.**
>
> **But if one walker coming down the sky made a Black Tide.**
>
> **Eleven.**

He did not let it finish.

"*Keeper,*" he said. "*Thank you for telling me. I'm going to tell everyone. In the next hour. I'm not going to wait.*"

> ***...THE COUNCIL.***

"*The Council. Concordia. All five. Article Eleven. If there's six hours, we meet in six minutes.*" He stood up. "*And then I'm going to ask the dead.*"

> ***...I WILL WATCH THEM.***
>
> ***...I WILL TELL YOU EVERY STEP.***
>
> ***...I AM AFRAID.***

"*I know,*" said Yuanshu. "*Me too.*"

---

Concordia met in fifty-three minutes. On screens. Five delegations and Chair Adaeze Okafor in her dressing gown in the crater under floodlights, and Ren on a sixth screen from the step of the broken hall in Yamato, and Ngozi Eze for the Southern Compact, and General Han Yizhou beside the Huaxia delegate, in uniform, at four in the morning, as if he had been waiting for this call for thirty years.

He told them all of it. Eleven. Nine days. The scout thirty-one years ago. The Black Tide.

When he finished, nobody spoke.

Then Marshal Orlov, on his screen in the Northern Union, in his greatcoat, took off his gloves. One finger at a time. And folded them, and laid them on the table in front of him.

"I was on the wall at Kirovsk," he said. "Thirty-one years ago. I was a hundred and sixty-nine. I had been a King for ninety years. I thought I had seen everything." He looked at his bare hands. "I had not. I had not seen what came through the rifts behind it. I had not seen a necromancer raise ninety million dead because there was nothing else, and weep on a wall because he could not stop them." He lifted his head. "Madam Chair. The Northern Union does not wait for nine days. We go today. To Lingzhou. I and Sokolova and the Second Ice Division." He looked at Yuanshu. "If Mister Zhou will have us."

"*Yes,*" said Yuanshu.

"Good," said Orlov, and put his gloves back on.

---

It went round the table like that.

Elias Brandt, putting his glasses on and taking them off: the Atlantic Federation would send whatever it had, and it did not have much that could fight things above the sky, but it had aircraft and medics and eleven hundred volunteers from the cities on the eastern coast who had stood on the beaches at Port Halvard when the sphere came up through the floor of Hearth One, and they would like to be asked.

Ngozi Eze: the Southern Compact had no King. It had a coast, and fishing boats, and four months ago it had moved sixty thousand people in sixty hours on a single road. If Lingzhou needed to be emptied, the Compact knew how. She would send the people who knew.

Ren, on the step, with her hand on the wood: "*I'm coming. Tonight.*" A pause. "*The Abbot's sweeping. He asked me where I was going. I told him. He said: then go.*"

General Han said nothing. He sat beside the Huaxia delegate in his dark green uniform and listened to all of it, and when it came to Huaxia, the delegate turned and looked at him, and he leaned forward.

"The Northern War Zone has nine divisions on the wall at Heishui," he said. "They are not awakeners. Most of them. They have rifles and artillery and thirty years of watching the north." He folded his hands. "Rifles do not work on things above the sky. I am told. I would like to find out."

---

"*Mister Zhou,*" said Okafor. "*What do you need?*"

He sat on the vault steps with the phone on the marble beside him and the staff across his knees, and thought about it. Not quickly. He had learned that.

"*I don't know yet,*" he said. "*I don't know what they'll do. One came down thirty-one years ago and was torn apart and caged under a city, and spent thirty-one years reading everyone in it three degrees at a time. These might do that. Or they might do something else.*" He was quiet. "*I know three things.*"

"*Go on.*"

"*They can't see the dead.*"

He felt the banking hall go still around him. Old Seven at the bronze door. Adjutant Xu on his chair.

"*They can see the living. Every mind they pass over, they read. If they hold on one, it's taken.*" He looked at the long white wall. At the column from the voyage. At the one from Sixty Seconds, when his mother had said *Lanterns* into a microphone on the battlements of Sector Nine and three million people had closed their eyes. "*And they can't read a quiet mind. Not one that's really quiet. The Sleepers knew that twelve hundred years ago. The cutting couldn't find the monks.*"

"*Lingzhou learned to go quiet,*" said Okafor slowly. "*For the Low Fire. Sixty seconds.*"

"*Sixty seconds,*" said Yuanshu. "*These walk slowly. They'll be over the city for hours. Maybe days.*" He looked at his sister, on the stairs, in her pajamas, with her hand on the banister. "*Nobody can be quiet for days.*"

"*Then what?*"

He did not answer at once.

On the stairs, Xiaoyu had gone very still. She was looking at him. She was looking at Mo Shi, who had come down behind her at some point without anyone noticing and was sitting on the bottom step with his eyes shut. She was looking at the stopwatch that still hung from a hook by the bronze door, where she had hung it after Sixty Seconds and never taken it down.

"*You don't have to be quiet for days,*" said Zhou Xiaoyu.

Everyone turned and looked at her.

"*You only have to be quiet when they're looking,*" she said. "*Three degrees at a time. Shi can see it coming. Three seconds ahead.*" She came down one step. "*It's the pocket. It's the same thing. Only it's not six people. It's the whole city.*" She came down another step. "*The lamps sweep. When the beam's coming over your street, your street goes quiet. When it's passed, you breathe. And the next street goes quiet.*" She stopped on the bottom step beside Mo Shi. "*Like a wave. Going round. Following the beams. Street by street. All night.*"

Nobody on any of the screens said anything.

"*Someone has to call it,*" said Xiaoyu. "*Every street. Every second. For hours.*" She lifted her chin. "*I did Sixty Seconds. I've got a whistle.*"

> **...she is right.**
>
> **It is the pocket. Inverted. Not moving six people into the gap. Moving the gap through three million people.**
>
> **I did not think of it.**
>
> **I am going to stop saying that.**

On his screen in the crater, General Han Yizhou looked at a sixteen-year-old girl in pajamas standing on the bottom step of an old bank, and for the first time since Yuanshu had known him, something moved at the corner of his mouth.

"*Madam Chair,*" he said. "*I would like it recorded that Huaxia's plan for the defense of Lingzhou was proposed by a schoolgirl at four in the morning.*" He folded his hands. "*And that I agree with it.*"
