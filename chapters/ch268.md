## Chapter 268: The Choir-Ship

They heard it before they saw it, and then everyone on Blue Star heard it, and nobody could say afterward what it had sounded like except that it was the most beautiful thing they had ever heard and they had wanted it to stop.

It came on the ninth night of the new year.

Tam woke the Ossuary at two in the morning. He did not knock on the bronze door; Old Seven was in it, and stood aside for him without being asked, and the boy came across the marble floor of the banking hall in the dark, barefoot, in his coat, with his face turned up toward the ceiling and past it, and said only, in the Huaxian he had been learning for six months at the school in the old customs house:

"*Now. It's here. It's here now.*"

And then everyone heard it.

---

Not with their ears. Not the way you hear a bell, or a voice, or the wind. The way you hear a thing you have always known and forgotten. Rising up out of the floor of the banking hall, and the stones of the city, and the river, and the sky. Low at first. A single tone, so low it was more a feeling in the chest than a sound, the way the branch had groaned over Lingzhou. And then another tone over it, higher. And another. And another. Until the whole night was full of it.

A choir.

Not human. Nothing human had ever sung like that. Thousands of voices, tens of thousands, each one many-throated, so that a single singer sang a chord, and the chords of all of them wove together and apart and together again, slowly, endlessly, the way water moves in a deep pool. It was not in any language anyone on Blue Star knew. It did not need to be. It was the sound of something singing to a thing it was afraid of, to keep it calm. Over and over. The same song. The way a mother sings at night to a child who will not sleep, except that the child was enormous, and the mother had been singing for ten thousand years, and did not know any other song.

Up and down the old quarter of Lingzhou, lights came on in windows. People came out into the street in their nightclothes and stood looking up. On the north wall, the soldiers of the night watch had taken off their helmets.

In the back room of the Ossuary, Lingyue had sat up in bed with both hands on her stomach and the whole room gone pale around her like the inside of a shell, brighter than it had ever been.

"*It's kicking,*" she said. "*It's kicking like it wants to get out.*"

> **A hundred and seventy-one.**
>
> **It has never been a hundred and seventy-one.**
>
> **It can hear it too.**

---

They went up to the north wall. Everyone did. Half the city was already there.

And over the north of Lingzhou, low over the old quarter and the river and the long curved roof of the Awakening Hall on East Ring Road, where nothing had been an hour before, there was a shell.

That was the only word for it. A vast spiral shell, pale, translucent, like the inside of a conch held up to a lamp, turning slowly in the dark a few kilometers above the city. Kilometers across. Lit from within by a soft shifting light that moved through its chambers as the song moved, brightening and dimming, so that the whole thing seemed to breathe. And from every opening along its spiral, every throat of every chamber, the song poured out over Lingzhou, and the night was full of it.

> **It came down our thread.**
>
> **The one from the Awakening Hall. The last one the one who picks read before it stopped. The one with your sister's writing in it.**
>
> **It followed the song up its own stone's thread to the knot in the deep water. It found the one who picks sitting still with its hands open. It read the last thing it read.**
>
> **And it came here.**

Beside him on the battlement, Ichiro's bell was ringing.

Not Ichiro's. Seed's recording of it, played through the staff, because Ren had called from Yamato ten minutes before and said that every bell in Shiomi was ringing in the fog of the bay at once, all the small bells, the boat calls and the headman's and the warning, not in any order, the way bells ring when something enormous is going overhead and they are trying to answer it and do not know what to say.

> **Ichiro says: it is music.**
>
> **He says: the fog-code is music. Notes and intervals. The Singers are music too. A different kind. But music.**
>
> **He says: he can hear words in it. Not words. Shapes. The way he heard the quiet ones in the deep water.**
>
> **He says: give him an hour. He talks to fish. He can talk to this.**

---

It took two hours, not one, and Ichiro complained the whole time, and Sister Kiyo, from the end of the breakwater at Shiomi with the lamp in her lap, said dryly through Ren that he had always underestimated how long things took, and had once spent an entire night rowing toward a lamp that turned out to be the moon.

But at four in the morning, Seed said:

> **I have it.**
>
> **Not all of it. Enough. With Ichiro's help. He says to tell you it was mostly his.**
>
> **It is a language. It is all one sentence. It has been one sentence for ten thousand years. They sing it every age, when they send up the tithe. They have been singing it since they left their world, nine months ago, along the thread.**
>
> **I will tell you what it says.**

He stood on the battlement in his old place with the staff and Lingyue beside him, in a coat over her nightclothes, with her hands on her stomach and her face set the way it set when she was about to fight something.

> **It says:**
>
> **We see you. You who are hungry. You who come along the threads.**
>
> **We are small. We are not bright. We are not worth taking.**
>
> **But we have brought you the bright ones. The chosen. The best of us. Dressed in light. Singing.**
>
> **Take them. And pass us by.**
>
> **Take them. And pass us by.**
>
> **Take them.**
>
> **And pass us by.**

---

Nobody on the north wall said anything.

The song went on. Over and over. Over the city, over the river, over three thousand faces on the battlements turned up to a vast pale shell turning in the dark. *Take them. And pass us by.* The most beautiful thing any of them had ever heard. And they wanted it to stop.

"*It's to the one who picks,*" said Lingyue. Her voice was flat. "*They're singing it to the one who picks. It's stopped, and they don't know, and they followed the last thread it read, and they're singing it to us.*"

> **No.**
>
> **I am sorry. I thought that too. For an hour.**
>
> **They know it has stopped. They found it. In the deep water, with its hands open.**
>
> **They are not singing to it.**

He went very still.

> **Ichiro found the shape in the middle of the sentence. The part that changes. The part that says who it is sung to.**
>
> **It used to be the shape for the one who picks. For ten thousand years.**
>
> **It has been changed.**
>
> **It is the shape for the one who ate it.**

On the battlement, Lingyue turned her head and looked at him.

> **They found it stopped. They read the last thing it read. A girl's writing, true, tied by a child of the world that asks and held up by a man nobody picked. They did not understand it. They understood that something here had made the thing they had sung to for ten thousand years stop.**
>
> **They think we ate it.**
>
> **They think we are hungrier.**
>
> **They have come to sing the tithe to you.**

---

At dawn, the song changed.

Not the words. The same sentence, over and over, *take them, and pass us by*. But one voice came out of it. One. Alone. Out of the tens of thousands, the way a single bird sings out of a dawn chorus. Many-throated, a chord in one throat. Old. Very old. And it sang, not to the city, not to the sky.

Down.

To the north wall. To the Sector Nine battlement. To a boy in his old place with a white staff in his hand and a harvest sack behind his eyes and a dead captain at the top of the stairs.

> **It is the Cantor.**
>
> **Ichiro says the word means: the one who sings first. The one who chooses the tithe and dresses them in light and sings them up. Every age. Their priest. Their queen. He is not sure. He says it is all the same word.**
>
> **She is singing to you.**
>
> **She says...**

Seed stopped. Then went on, slowly, the way it went on when it was carrying something it did not want to carry.

> **She says: you who ate the one who picks.**
>
> **She says: we have come a long way. We are tired. We have sung for nine months to reach you.**
>
> **She says: we have brought you one thousand one hundred and eleven. The chosen of this age. The brightest of the Hollow Bell. Dressed in light. They have been burning since we left, waiting to be taken. Nothing has taken them.**
>
> **She says: they are in the heart of the ship. They are singing too. Can you hear them?**

He could.

Under the choir, under the tens of thousands, in the center of the vast pale shell turning over the city, where the light inside it was brightest: another sound. Smaller. Higher. A thousand voices and more, singing the same sentence, *take us, take us*, burning as they sang. The way Callum Ward had burned in a hand in the deep water. The way the Sixth had burned in the eyes of the Mouth. The best thing they had ever felt, and being used up by it.

> **She says: take them.**
>
> **She says: please. Take them, and pass us by.**
>
> **She says: we do not know what you are. We do not know what you want. We only know how to give.**
>
> **She says: it is all we have ever known how to do.**

---

He stood on the battlement in the dawn with the staff in his hand.

The song poured down over Lingzhou. The shell turned. The light in its heart burned, a thousand and more, waiting.

Beside him, Lingyue had both hands flat on her stomach. The light was coming off her, pale, all round her, the way it came off her in the mornings now. She did not seem to notice. She was looking up at the shell.

"*They've been burning for nine months,*" she said. Very quietly. "*Nine months.*" Her hands pressed flatter. "*Like Callum. Longer than Callum.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*Nobody took them.*"

"*No.*"

"*And now they want you to.*" She did not look at him. "*Because they don't know how to do anything else. Because nobody ever asked them anything except whether they'd be chosen.*"

He did not answer.

Up in the heart of the shell, a thousand one hundred and eleven of the brightest of a world he had never heard of were singing *take us* in voices that were beginning, Seed said, very slightly, to crack.

And Seed said, very quietly, in the place a little above his eyes:

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **I asked one of them. Just one. I am sorry. I did not ask you first.**
>
> **I asked what she wanted.**
>
> **She said: to be chosen.**
>
> **She said it the way you would say: to breathe.**
