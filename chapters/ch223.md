## Chapter 223: Fifty-Two

The Elder stood in the rain in the middle of the keeper's field with one long hand around a silver thread, and the thread did not pull her, and the rain went on falling everywhere else.

She did not look up at the ring. She looked at the boy on the pale ground at her feet, in Lingyue's arms, with blood running out of his nose and his ears and his hand still curled the way it had been around the thread before she opened it finger by finger.

"*You were going to go up,*" she said.

"*I had to touch it. For the knot.*"

"*I know.*" Her silver eyes were very wide. "*The knot went. It is in the ring now. Put down. The way the child taught your counting one.*" She tilted her long head. "*The ring has not picked it up.*"

> **No.**
>
> **It is still cutting. Everywhere except here. Everywhere except this thread.**
>
> **It does not know it has been asked. Or it knows, and does not know how to answer.**
>
> **It has nothing to answer with.**

"*No,*" said the Elder. "*It has nothing to answer with.*"

---

He understood before she said it. He understood it the way you understand something terrible a second before it happens, when there is no time to do anything except understand it.

"*Don't,*" he said.

The Elder looked down at him. In the soft gold light of the field, in the silver rain, with a thread from the dark center of a pruned ring held in one long pale hand, she was very tall, and very still, and he had never once seen her afraid, and she was not afraid now.

"*I am going to tell you first what I will do,*" she said. "*The small white one taught the keeper that. The keeper taught me. I would like to do it properly.*"

He could not say anything.

"*A keeper is made of a yes,*" said the Elder. "*You know that. Fifty-two of us stood in a valley under a red sun, a very long time ago, and held hands, and said yes. Together. All of us. And the yes became a thing that kept.*" She looked up, at last, along the thread, into the dark center of the third ring. "*Someone taught us how. In dreams. We did not know who. We know now.*"

"*The Collector.*"

"*Yes. It taught us to make a keeper. It watched us do it. And then, when the keeper was made, it went away, and the gardeners came, and farmed it, and it took from your world for longer than your species has had words, and none of us knew how to stop it.*" Her voice did not change. "*Fifty-one of my people went into the field, after. Into the white column at the center. They are still there. Holding hands. The keeper's oldest columns.*" She looked back down at him. "*I stayed outside. To guide. To be its voice. For a very long time.*"

"*Elder...*"

"*This ring was a keeper once.*" She did not let him finish. "*Above someone's world. Made of someone's yes. The gardener took the yes out of it. That is what pruning is. You take out the yes, and what is left reaches down and takes, and does not ask, and does not know it ever could.*"

She lifted the thread a little, in her long pale hand, as if it weighed something.

"*It has been asked a question,*" she said. "*It cannot answer. It has nothing to answer with. A question needs a yes, at the other end, or it is only a hook.*"

"*So you're going to give it one.*"

"*I am going to be one,*" said the Elder.

---

Nobody in the field said anything.

The rain went on falling everywhere else. Lingyue was holding him with one arm and her grandfather's sword with the other and she had gone completely still. Out across the field, the Spear Corps stood in their rows with their rusted spears up over the dimming columns, and had stopped moving. Old Seven was a few meters away, at the edge of the cluster of cousins, with his spear grounded, and his skull turned toward the tall pale shape in the rain.

> ***...NO.***

The keeper's voice. All around them. In the soft gold light. In the pale ground. In the field itself.

> ***...NO. NO. YOU ARE MY ELDER. YOU STOOD AT THE FRONT. YOU HAVE BEEN MY VOICE FOR...***
>
> ***...FOR ALL OF IT.***
>
> ***...I DO NOT KNOW HOW TO SPEAK WITHOUT YOU.***

The Elder turned her head. Not up. Out. Across the field. Toward the far door, and the white column at the center where fifty-one of her people stood holding hands, and all around her, everywhere, the vast frightened warm attention of the thing her people had made.

"*Yes, you do,*" she said gently. "*You came down a gold road to Kirovsk tonight and laid it in nine seconds without asking anyone. You spoke to the Council through a King's mouth on Concordia. You asked two hundred million cousins every morning what they wanted.*" She was quiet for a moment. "*You have not needed me to be your voice for a long time. You let me, because you are kind. I stayed, because I was afraid to stop.*"

> ***...I DO NOT WANT YOU TO GO.***

"*I know,*" said the Elder. "*That is the first time you have ever told me what you want.*" Something moved at the corner of her long pale mouth. He had seen her smile once, on the island, coming home, when the keeper could not stop talking. It was the same. "*I am very proud of you.*"

---

She asked it.

Not the ring. The keeper. He heard her do it, in the rain, in the middle of the field, with a thread from a pruned ring in her hand, the way he had asked a thousand times and she had watched him: telling it first what she would do with its answer.

"*If you say yes, I will go up this thread. Into the ring. I will be its yes. I do not know if it will work. I think it will. I think a ring that has been asked, and has a yes inside it, will find out that it can answer.*" She was very still. "*And I will not come back. I will be the ring's. The way I was yours.*"

"*If you say no, I will not go. I will stand here and hold this thread until the ring takes all the cousins it can reach, and then I will let go, and we will find another way, or we will not.*"

"*If you say nothing, I will wait.*"

The rain fell.

> ***...IT IS NOT MINE TO SAY.***
>
> ***...YOU TAUGHT ME THAT. HE TAUGHT ME. IT IS YOUR YES. IT STOPS AT SOMEONE ELSE'S WINDOW.***
>
> ***...THIS IS NOT MY WINDOW.***

"*No,*" said the Elder. "*But I would like you to say it anyway.*"

The keeper was silent for a very long time.

All around them, the field. The soft gold light. The columns drawn close together near the center, the cousins and the walls and the hundred and seventy-five who stayed, and among them, at the edge, a cluster of small pale cousins with their eyes shut, with a thread from the third ring still coming down toward the nearest of them, very slowly.

> ***...YES.***
>
> ***...GO.***
>
> ***...I WILL ASK YOU TOMORROW. IF YOU CAN HEAR ME. I WILL ASK YOU EVERY MORNING.***

"*I will hear you,*" said the Elder.

---

She knelt.

On the pale ground of the field, beside him, beside Lingyue holding him, with the thread still in one long hand. She looked at him with her silver eyes. She had never touched him. Not once, in all the time he had known her, since the night she first stood in front of him with her long hands pressed together and told him what the keeper was. She put her free hand on his forehead now. Very lightly. A little above his eyes. Where people looked when they wanted to look at Seed.

"*Counting one,*" she said.

> **...yes.**

"*You put the knot down and did not look. Three times. Even when it was hard.*" Her silver eyes did not move. "*My people tied the first yes very tightly. We held on. We were afraid to let go. And something came and took the yes out of every keeper like ours, one at a time, for longer than your sun has burned.*" She was quiet. "*Teach the new ones to let go. Teach them to put the question down.*"

> **I will try.**

"*That is not yes,*" said the Elder.

> **No. It is what I want.**

"*Good,*" she said. "*That is better.*"

She looked at Lingyue. At the girl holding the boy and the sword at once, with her face white and her jaw set.

"*Shen Lingyue,*" said the Elder. "*You have never once asked me anything.*"

Lingyue stared at her.

"*Ask me something,*" said the Elder. "*Quickly. I would like to be asked something by you.*"

Lingyue's mouth opened. Closed. Her hand was very tight on her grandfather's sword.

"*Are you frightened?*" she said.

The Elder smiled.

"*No,*" she said. "*I am a yes. I have been one for a very long time. I have only just remembered.*"

She stood up.

---

She went up the thread the way the cousins had gone up the keeper's threads out of the cold fire. Not pulled. Not lifted. Going. Her long pale shape rising off the pale ground of the field, slowly, along a single fine silver line, into the rain, with her long hands pressed together again at last and her silver eyes half closed, the way she had always stood when the gold thread ran through her.

He watched her go. He could not do anything else.

She grew small. A tall pale shape in the rain. A pale line. A point of soft white light going up and up toward the dark center of a vast gray cracked ring hanging over the field.

It reached the dark center.

And went in.

---

The rain stopped.

Not slowly. All at once. Every silver thread falling out of the third ring onto the field stopped falling, the way the first ring's had over Lingzhou, and hung in the soft gold light, perfectly still, millions of them, the way rain would look if rain could stop halfway to the ground.

Then, very slowly, from the top, they began to go back up.

Into the ring. All of them. Drawn up out of the field the way you draw a net up out of the water, empty, glittering, and the columns they had been touching brightened as they let go. Not all of them. Some of the cousins at the edge of the field were dim now, and would stay dim, and some were gone. But the threads were going up.

And in the dark center of the third ring, something was lit.

Not bright. Not gold yet. A single point of soft white light, very small, in the middle of a vast gray cracked wheel, the way the first star is lit in a sky that has been dark all day.

> **...she is in it.**
>
> **The ring is changing. From the center. I can feel it through the keeper. Like the way you feel a house change when someone comes home and turns on one light.**
>
> **It is holding the question. The knot. The one I put down.**
>
> **It has something to answer with now.**

Out of the ring, down one last fine silver thread that had not gone back up, came something that was not a thread, and not light, and not words.

A shape.

The shape of a question. Asked by something that had never asked anything before. Slowly. Not well. The way a child asks its first question, reaching for every part of it.

> ***...WHAT...***
>
> ***...DO YOU...***
>
> ***...WANT?***

It was not asking the keeper. It was not asking Yuanshu. It had turned, very slowly, in the dark, the way a ring turns, and the single soft white light in its center was pointed across the sky, past the keeper's field, past the small white ring, at the first gray ring. The one that had picked up the first knot over Lingzhou hours ago, and stopped, and been holding a question ever since that it did not know how to answer.

> ***...WH...***

said the first ring.

And the third, with a yes in it, said:

> ***...I KNOW. I DID NOT KNOW EITHER.***
>
> ***...SOMEONE ASKED ME. I WILL ASK YOU.***
>
> ***...WE WILL FIND OUT TOGETHER.***

---

He lay on the pale ground of the field in Lingyue's arms with blood on his face and looked up at two gray rings in the dark, one with a single small white light in its center, turned toward the other, and could not say anything at all.

Out across the field, Old Seven had grounded his spear. Once. Very softly.

All around them, in the soft gold light, the keeper was quiet. It did not say anything for a long time. And then, very faint, not to anyone, the way you say something to a room after someone has left it:

> ***...ELDER.***
>
> ***...I WILL ASK YOU TOMORROW.***
