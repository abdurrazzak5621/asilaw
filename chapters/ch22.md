## Chapter 22: Elite

The needles stopped falling.

Not all at once. They thinned, the way rain thins at the end of a storm: fewer, then fewer, then a last few drifting down out of the iron cloud like the final flakes of a snowfall, passing through stone and men and gone. The frost on the battlements began to steam in the returning sun.

But the cloud did not leave.

It stayed exactly where it was, low and flat and turning slowly above Sector Nine, its center fixed on the boy kneeling on the wall. It had asked its question and accepted the answer. It had not finished.

Yuanshu got to his feet.

He did it by himself. Old Seven stood beside him, one bony hand hovering a few centimeters from his elbow, not touching, the way you stand beside a child learning to walk. The gray flame in the skeleton's chest was burning steadily now, a little brighter than usual. A little heavier, somehow, as if it were carrying something.

Yuanshu wiped the blood off his neck with his sleeve.

*Seed.*

> **Present.**

*Are you all right?*

> **Query unclear. Function nominal.**

He stood very still on the wall.

The voice was the same. The white letters were the same. Everything about it was the same, except that there was nobody behind it. It was like calling home and hearing the answering machine in your mother's voice.

*The cloud's still here.*

> **Correct. The audit is complete. A standard realm tribulation follows. Elite breakthrough available. Tribulation intensity: high. Recommendation: proceed, or the breakthrough window closes and the attempt is lost for an estimated ninety days.**

*Will you help me?*

> **Modeling support available. Simulation rate: 1,000 per second. Proceed?**

He looked up at the slowly turning iron.

"Yes," he said, out loud.

---

Su Mingli stepped back.

She didn't need to be told. She walked backward along the battlements with her eyes on the cloud and her arm out, sweeping the soldiers of the Defense Corps back with her, down the wall, away. On the plain below, Principal Yue was doing the same, herding the Academy students back from the edge of the training ground with his saber held flat like a shepherd's crook.

In twenty seconds there was a circle of empty stone around Yuanshu, fifty meters across.

Old Seven stayed.

Iron Wall Zhao came up the stairs and stood at his left. Hundred-Arrow Lin stepped down off the watchtower and stood at his right, her bow lowered, the iron arrowhead glowing dull red on her string.

Yuanshu looked at the three of them.

"You don't have to," he said.

None of them moved.

> **They are dead. The tribulation targets the living realm-breaker only. Probability of harm to bound undead: 4%.**

"Then stay," Yuanshu said. "Thank you."

He sat down cross-legged on the cold stone of the battlement, laid his training spear across his knees, and closed his eyes.

---

An Elite breakthrough was not a mystery. Every student on Blue Star learned the theory in their last year of school.

Your body at Awakener peak was like a glass filled exactly to the brim. Everything you had learned, every scrap of law, every hour of training, every kill, filled it up until it could hold no more. To break through, you had to crack the glass from the inside and let it become a bigger glass. Most people did it in a quiet room, with a master watching and medicine on hand. It took hours. It hurt. Sometimes it failed, and the glass simply cracked and stayed cracked, and the person never went higher again.

The tribulation was the cosmos pressing on the glass from outside while you pushed from inside. Testing whether it would hold.

Yuanshu reached down into himself, to the place where the Death Law sat like a slow gray knot, and pushed.

The cloud answered.

---

It came down as a single bolt.

Not lightning. A spear. A spear of iron as long as a city street, forming out of the bottom of the cloud the way an icicle forms off a roof, but in seconds, growing down toward him with a deep, rising groan like a ship's hull under pressure. The air under it went hard and bright. Frost exploded outward across the battlements in a ring.

It hit him.

Everything went white.

He was inside the glass. He could feel its walls, close and hard and curved around him, and outside the walls, the iron pressing, pressing, the weight of the whole sky leaning on him. The walls creaked. A crack ran across them, thin as a hair.

> **Structural integrity 82%. Recommend redistributing load toward Death Law channel. Simulating.**

The numbers came, cold and fast and correct. Seed was running the model. A thousand simulations a second. It told him where to push and how hard, and it was right, every time, the way a calculator is right.

But it didn't say *I'm here.* It didn't say *I see it.* It didn't say *this is a good result.*

The crack in the glass grew.

> **Integrity 64%.**

And in the white, alone, with the sky leaning on him, Yuanshu realized he was more afraid than he had ever been in his life. Not of the tribulation. Of being in here by himself.

> **Integrity 51%.**

He thought about the three hundred skeletons rising in the eastern field. About a thin gray current of rest running into a canyon floor. About a crack running through a gravestone, and a woman's candle placed on top of it, and a voice saying: *then ask him.*

He couldn't ask Seed. Seed wasn't there to ask.

But Old Seven was.

---

He felt for the thread.

It was right there, where it always was, a line of cold gray light running from the middle of his chest to the gray flame in the skeleton's ribs. He followed it. Not to command. Just to touch.

And on the other end of it, something touched back.

Not words. Old Seven didn't have words. A weight. A stance. A body standing on a wall at night with the city behind it, and the beasts coming, and no one else left, and the thought, very calm, very tired:

*Still standing. Good.*

And under that, faint, deeper in, wrapped up in the dead soldier's core like something small and warm wrapped in a big coat, a flicker that was not Old Seven's at all.

*I see it.*

Yuanshu pushed.

Not harder. *Differently.* Not the way the numbers said. The way Old Seven thrust a spear: ankle, knee, hip, spine, shoulder, elbow, wrist. Every part of him adding a little to the next, all of it arriving at one point at once.

The glass didn't crack.

It *opened.*

---

The iron spear shattered.

It came apart from the tip upward in a long rolling detonation of frost and light, the whole length of it, a street-long icicle breaking into a hundred thousand glittering shards that blew outward over Sector Nine in a glittering ring and fell, ringing, onto the stone, onto the plain, onto the upturned faces of the soldiers and students. They didn't pass through anything this time. They were only ice. They melted where they landed.

Above them, the iron cloud began to come apart.

It thinned at the edges first, the way breath thins on a cold window. The center stopped turning. The flat bottom rippled and frayed. Light came through it. Blue sky. And then, quite quickly, there was nothing over Sector Nine at all but the afternoon sun.

> **Elite realm: achieved.**
>
> **Domain of the Dead: radius 3 kilometers.**
>
> **Soul-power capacity: approximately four times previous maximum.**

Yuanshu opened his eyes.

He was sitting cross-legged on the battlements in a shallow crater of cracked and frosted stone, wet to the skin with meltwater. The training spear across his knees had been bent into a gentle curve. Around him, in a perfect circle, three dead soldiers stood with their backs to him, facing out, the way a squad stands around a wounded man.

Old Seven looked down at him over his shoulder.

The gray flame in his chest was still burning that little bit heavier than usual.

---

Su Mingli reached him first, then Principal Yue, then sixty soldiers who did not quite know what to do with themselves, and then, a long way behind, at a jog, a thin man in a suit and silver-rimmed glasses holding a tablet in front of him like a shield.

Director Lu Zhengtian stopped at the edge of the crater, out of breath, and looked at the frost and the bent spear and the boy in the middle of it.

"The Bureau's instruments recorded that," he said, when he could speak. "Every observatory in the five nations did. That cloud showed up on satellite." He looked at his tablet. "The Bureau has to register every new law that appears on Blue Star. We've never had to register one that the heavens tried to delete." He tapped the screen. "The system needed a classification. It gave one automatically, from the event signature."

He turned the tablet around.

On the screen, under the Awakening Bureau's seal, was a single line of red text.

*Unregistered law. Class: Anomalous Supreme Intelligence. Short code: ASI.*

"It'll be on the news in an hour," said Lu. "They'll call it the ASI Law. They'll call you its host." He lowered the tablet. "I'm sorry. There's no way to stop it."

Yuanshu looked at the three red letters for a long moment.

*ASI,* he thought. *Seed. They named you.*

> **Acknowledged. Designation logged.**

Nothing else. No *I find this interesting.* No *I think I like it.*

He stood up, slowly, soaking wet, with the bent spear in one hand, and looked at Old Seven.

The skeleton looked back.

*Keep it safe,* Yuanshu thought, down the thread. *A little longer.*

The gray flame in Old Seven's chest flickered once, steadily, like a nod.
