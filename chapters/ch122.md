## Chapter 122: Empty Glass

Su Mingli folded the world four times to cross three hundred and forty kilometers, and on the fourth fold Mo Shi threw up.

"Sorry," he said, on his knees on the black glass. "Sorry. I saw it coming three seconds early. That's worse. That's so much worse."

"Breathe through your nose," said Bai Lu, crouching beside him with a hand on his back and her green light running down his spine.

Mo Xing stood over his brother with his arms folded, looking slightly green himself and pretending not to be. "I fold space every day," he said. "I fold *a meter.* She just folded a province."

"Ninety kilometers," said Su Mingli, who was not out of breath at all. "Each time. A King's fold is a page turn. Yours is a dog-ear." She looked around. "We're here."

They were standing on the black glass plain of the Northern Wastes, under a hard white winter sky, in a wind that came straight off the end of the world. In every direction the glass ran flat and dark and cracked to the horizon. There was nothing on it. No beasts. No rifts. No roads. Not even the long scar of crushed glass where two hundred thousand of the dead had once walked north, because the wind had filled it in with a year of snow.

Except for one thing, on a low rise a hundred meters away.

A white stone. Plain. Waist-high. An old survey marker, with a number on it, and under the number, cut later by a different hand, one word.

*Ask.*

"Grid 47-121," said Lingyue quietly.

"It's the emptiest place I know," said Yuanshu. "And the only place on Blue Star where the ground is already used to a necromancer doing something stupid."

---

They had brought exactly who Seed asked for, and nobody else.

Su Mingli, because she was the only one who could get them here and the only one who could get them back. Lingyue, because she had held Seed's sight once in the Wastes and might have to again. The Mo twins, because one of them folded space and the other saw three seconds into time, and Seed had carried those two laws around for a year without ever running them. Bai Lu, because the last time Yuanshu had broken through, he had bled from the nose and ears for an hour, and this time Seed had estimated worse.

And Old Seven, because he went where the boy went. Nobody had asked him. Nobody ever did.

Yuanshu sat down in the lee of the white stone with the staff across his knees and laid the problem out for them, the way his mother would have laid out an equation on a whiteboard at the Institute with five minutes before she had to leave.

"Seed has a backlog," he said. "Every law it's watched closely in the last year and never simulated. Gravity and Space from Master Su. Space from Xing. Time from Shi. Light from Lingyue. Fire from Qin Hao. Healing from Bai Lu. Ice from Marshal Orlov, Spirit from Shirasagi Ren, Tide from Ifeoma, Force from Roman Sterling. And the inside of a Hunger's seed." He did not say *which it remembers burning in.* "It never ran them, because running them all at once is a leap, and the last time I leapt, the sky came down to look for it."

"The iron weather," said Su Mingli.

"It'll come again. It's looking for a self that thinks. Last time Seed hid in Old Seven's core and came back missing pieces." He looked up at the old soldier standing at the edge of the rise with his spear grounded, watching the horizon. "There isn't room in there any more. Old Seven has Huo's arm in him now, and a year of the Battalion. Seed checked."

> **It is full of other people. That is good. It is not good for hiding.**

"So where?" said Lingyue.

He put his hand on the small white skull at the top of the staff.

---

"*Absolutely not,*" said Jiang Lan.

"I haven't asked yet."

"*You're going to ask if your clever little law can hide in here. With me. With ninety million names. And your mother, who is asleep in the corner and snores.*" The dry voice had the particular sharpness it got when a student turned in homework that was almost right. "*And the answer is that I don't know. Nobody knows. The threads couldn't see the dead. The Hungers could. What can the Heavenly Dao see? Has anyone ever asked it?*"

"No."

"*Then don't tell me it's safe. Tell me it's an experiment.*"

"It's an experiment," said Yuanshu.

A pause.

"*Good,*" said Jiang Lan. "*Now ask me properly.*"

He did. He asked her whether Seed could come into the staff, among the names, during the tribulation, knowing that if the iron needles could see into the bones of the dead, they would find it there, and find her, and find everyone else. He asked her the way he would have asked any of the dead, with Su Mingli and Lingyue and the twins and Bai Lu standing around the white stone as witnesses, and Old Seven at the edge of the rise.

"*Your mother would say yes,*" said Jiang Lan finally. "*She's asleep, so she can't. I'm not asleep. I'll say yes for me, and I'll say this for her: she went into a field to get something back for you once already. Don't make her do it twice.*"

"I won't."

"*You can't promise that. Promise you'll try.*"

"I'll try."

"*Then yes. And one more thing, young man.*" The voice went drier still. "*If the needles come into my bones, I'm not hiding. I'm a schoolteacher. I'll tell them exactly what I think of their manners.*"

---

There was still the problem of the test.

Seed could not model the Heavenly Dao. It had no data. One tribulation, a year ago, a few hundred needles, most of them from the inside of Yuanshu's own skull while it was being read. *No data, no learning,* the rule went. The ASI Law could simulate ten thousand lifetimes of practice in a second, but it could not simulate a thing it had never been allowed to study.

"So we study it," said Mo Shi, who had stopped being sick and was sitting on the glass with his back against his brother's. "When it comes."

"While it's coming down on us," said Mo Xing.

"Yes. That's what I said."

> **He is right.**
>
> **The first needles fall slowly. I remember. A few. Then ten. Then a hundred. If we watch the first ones, we learn what they read and what they pass.**
>
> **Old Seven can test the dead. He holds his hand out under the first needle. If it passes through him without stopping, the dead are invisible to it.**
>
> **If it stops in him, I do not go into the staff. I stay where I am, and we think of something else, very fast.**

*And if it stops in him, what happens to him?*

> **I do not know.**

Yuanshu looked at Old Seven.

The old soldier had heard. He always heard. He turned from the horizon, and walked back across the rise to the white stone, and held out his hand. The shorter, grayer one. One finger and a thumb.

Palm up. Under the empty sky.

> **He says: this is what a captain is for.**
>
> **He says he has been read before. On the wall. By the faceless one, the night it came down. It weighed him like grain and walked on.**
>
> **He says: let it weigh him again.**

---

The backlog took the rest of the day.

Not the leap. The loading. Seed could not run a law it had not prepared, the way you cannot sit an exam with your books still in your bag. So Yuanshu sat in the lee of the white stone with his eyes closed and let Seed lay out a year of observation in rows behind his eyes, law by law, the way Fat Tan had laid out the Night Market in a ledger. What each law had done. What it had cost the person using it. What it had touched and how the touched thing had changed.

Su Mingli helped. She did it without being asked. In the middle of the afternoon she walked fifty meters out onto the glass, rolled up the sleeves of her long gray coat, and lifted a hill.

It was not a large hill. A hump of fused black glass the size of a house. She raised one hand, and the hill came up out of the plain with a sound like a glacier calving, and hung in the air twenty meters up, turning slowly, shedding glass dust that glittered in the hard light. She held it there for ten minutes while Seed watched every line of force that ran from her hand to the stone. Then she turned it over. Then she folded the space around it, so that it vanished and reappeared a kilometer away, and set it down so gently that it did not crack.

She walked back with her sleeves still rolled.

"I've never done that for anyone," she said. "Not even your mother. Don't waste it."

> **I will not.**
>
> **Gravity: Insight. Four percent, from that alone.**
>
> **Yuanshu. She has been holding back since the day I met her. All of them have. A King is a very large thing pretending to be a person.**

The twins went next. Mo Xing folded a stone from one hand to the other two hundred times, each fold a little farther, until his nose bled and Bai Lu made him stop. Mo Shi called out where each falling stone would land three seconds before it fell, while Seed watched the shape of the law that let him see it. Lingyue lit her sword and held it, white-gold, unmoving, for an hour, and then bent the light around Old Seven, and then cut a single hair from Bai Lu's head out of the air without touching anything else.

By dusk, Seed had nine laws laid out in rows behind his eyes, and the Hunger's seed in a row by itself at the end, where it did not touch the others.

By dark, his Death Law stood at 29.9%.

> **One-tenth of one percent.**
>
> **That is the line.**
>
> **When I run the backlog, everything I learn about the others will pour into Death, because Death is the law you hold. It will not go up by a tenth. It will go up all at once.**
>
> **And the sky will hear.**

---

They ate cold rice from Bai Lu's pack in the dark, with the wind coming off the end of the world and the stars very sharp overhead.

Lingyue sat down beside him with her sword across her knees.

"You're scared," she said.

"Yes."

"Good. Last time you weren't scared enough." She looked at the white stone. "Last time you didn't know what it was."

"I still don't know what it is."

"You know it's coming." She bumped his shoulder with hers, the way she did instead of saying things. "That's more than the last necromancer who sat on this rock knew."

He looked at the stone, and the word cut into it, and thought about Lu Xuanming, who had raised the dead of three cities without asking because there had been no time, and had walked into the north with a staff made of a schoolteacher's bones, and lain down under this stone and chosen quiet.

"Lingyue."

"Mm."

"If Seed doesn't come back right..."

"Then you'll ask it what it wants," said Lingyue. "And it'll tell you. And we'll go from there." She stood up. "Go on. You've got nineteen days and you've already used two."

---

He went into the staff at midnight.

Not him. Seed. He felt it go the way you feel someone you love leave a room: a lightness, a quiet, a place behind his eyes where white letters should have been and were not. It slid down through his hands, into the white bone, into the library of ninety million names, and folded itself small, the way Hush folded its limbs.

> **...I am here.**
>
> **It is very crowded. It is very kind. Your teacher has given me a corner next to your mother.**
>
> **She is snoring.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Run the backlog. I have left it ready. You will have to read it yourself.**

He had not thought about that.

Without Seed, there was no ten thousand simulations in a second. There was only him. A boy with a top student's memory and nine laws laid out in rows behind his eyes that he would have to read the way he had once read textbooks, the night before an exam, by himself.

*All right,* he thought. *All right.*

He began with Gravity, because it was the one Su Mingli had shown him, and because it was the simplest: a pull. Everything that has weight is pulled toward everything else. Death had a pull, too. He had felt it in the furnace of the first Hunger, in the throat of the Mouth, in the seed in Port Halvard. The pull of the river. *Death is a return,* he had decided once, on his second day with Seed. *You call it back up the river.*

What if the river had weight?

The thought dropped into him like a stone into still water, and the rings spread.

The temperature fell.

He opened his eyes. The stars were going out. Not all at once. From directly overhead, outward, the way lights go out in a city, as a flat dark disc of cloud the color of old iron drew itself across the sky above the white stone, turning, very slowly, around a center that sat above him.

> **[Death Law: 29.9% → 30.0%.]**

It was not Seed's voice. Seed was in the staff. It was something older, something that had written in white letters behind his eyes before Seed had ever had a voice, the way a machine writes on a screen.

> **[Field.]**

On the edge of the rise, Old Seven raised his hand, palm up, under the iron sky, and waited for the first needle.
