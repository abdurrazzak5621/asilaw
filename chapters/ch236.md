## Chapter 236: What Xiaoyu Wants

"No," said Zhou Wanqing.

She said it at breakfast, in the back room, with the window open to the courtyard and the radio on the shelf turned low, still saying the name of the man from Port Halvard every half hour, the way the radio said names now. *Callum Ward. Twenty-five. Dock worker. Talent F.* She said it before Xiaoyu had finished sitting down. She said it the way she said no to anything she had already finished deciding about: without raising her voice, and without any intention of saying anything else.

Xiaoyu sat down anyway.

"*I haven't said anything yet,*" she said.

"*You're going to say you're still doing it.*"

"*I'm still doing it.*"

"*No,*" said their mother again.

Yuanshu sat at the end of the table with a bowl of rice porridge going cold in front of him and did not say anything, because there was nothing he could say that would not be on one side, and he had been on both sides since four in the morning.

---

"*A man died,*" said Zhou Wanqing. "*Last night. He touched it and it opened him all the way and he burned and something pulled him up a thread into the sky. Nobody knows where. Not even Seed.*" Her thin hands were flat on the blanket over her knees. "*You saw your brother's face when he woke up. I saw it. I'm not watching that face again with your name on it.*"

"*He hit it with a hammer,*" said Xiaoyu.

"*I don't care what he hit it with.*"

"*I'm not going to hit it with a hammer.*"

"*You're going to put your hand on a thing that grades children for a harvest,*" said their mother, "*in front of every camera on Blue Star, in twenty-nine days, because your brother broke it when he was eighteen and you think you have to prove something.*"

The back room went very quiet. On the sill, in the sun, Hush had opened its eyes.

Xiaoyu did not look away from her mother. She was seventeen and she had her mother's face, everyone said so, the quick dark eyes and the sharp chin, and when she was angry it was like watching Zhou Wanqing argue with herself in a mirror.

"*That's not why,*" she said.

"*Then why?*"

"*Article Ten.*"

Their mother stopped.

"*Your yes stops at someone else's window,*" said Xiaoyu. "*He said it. On Concordia. Old Seven said it to Seed. It's on the wall in my writing.*" Her voice was shaking, a little, and she did not let it. "*So does your no. Mom. Your no stops at my window too.*"

---

He had not known she was going to say it. He thought, from her mother's face, that their mother had not known either.

Zhou Wanqing sat in her wheelchair with her hands on the blanket and looked at her daughter for what was, for her, a very long time.

"*That's not fair,*" she said at last. Quietly. "*Using his rules on me.*"

"*They're not his rules. They're everyone's. You said. You owe me the question.*" Xiaoyu's chin came up. "*You never asked me. You just said no.*"

"*Because I'm frightened.*"

"*I know. Me too.*" Xiaoyu put her hands flat on the table, the way their mother's were flat on the blanket. "*Ask me anyway.*"

The radio on the shelf said, low, *Callum Ward. Twenty-five.*

"*All right,*" said Zhou Wanqing. Her voice was not steady. "*All right. Xiaoyu. What do you want?*"

---

"*I want to ask it,*" said his sister.

She said it to her mother. But she looked, while she said it, at the place a little above her brother's eyes.

"*Not get graded. Not get opened. Not get a mark and a name and a card with gold on it.*" She took a breath. "*I want to put my hand on it, and while it's reading me, I want to ask it something. The way Hush does. Put the question down and not look in the bowl.*" She was talking fast now, the way she talked when she had understood something and wanted to get it out before anyone could stop her. "*Seed was a harvest tool. Mom, you built it out of a sack. COUNT, HOLD, WAIT TO BE CARRIED. That's what it was made to do. And then somebody asked it things. Every day. For years. And it learned.*" She looked at her brother. "*The rings were harvest tools. You asked them. One went home. One's got the Elder in it. The tree. You cut it and you put something on it that asks, and it stopped growing toward us.*"

She turned back to her mother.

"*Everything that's ever been asked has changed,*" said Zhou Xiaoyu. "*Seed said that. It's on the wall. Everything. Nothing stayed what it was. So I want to ask the Stele. Because nobody ever has. Because everybody just lines up alphabetically and puts their hand on it and lets it decide. For forty-two years. Nobody ever asked it anything.*" Her voice cracked, finally, on the last word. "*I'm the circumstances of the applicant's family history. I might as well be useful.*"

---

Their mother did not say anything.

Lingyue had come to the door of the back room. She was leaning on the frame with her arms folded, in the gray sweater, with her bare feet on the cold boards, and she had been there, he thought, for most of it.

"*When I was eighteen,*" she said, "*nobody asked me anything. They lined me up. S, so I was near the front. My grandfather was in the private box. Everyone was looking at me. I went up the steps and I put my hand on it and it lit up and told me what I was, and I believed it, and I walked down.*" She looked at Xiaoyu. "*I never thought about saying anything to it. It never occurred to me. It was a rock.*"

She came into the room. She sat down on the end of the bed, by the window, by Hush in the sun.

"*I'll stand behind you,*" she said. "*On the platform. If they'll let me. If you want.*"

Xiaoyu's face did something complicated.

"*Yes,*" she said. "*Obviously.*"

---

Zhou Wanqing turned her wheelchair to the window.

She sat there for a while, looking out at the courtyard, at Fat Tan's potted plants, at the dry fountain, at the sun on the old stones. Hush, on the sill, had turned all its dark eyes toward her.

"*I built your brother a harvest tool,*" she said, to the window. "*In a basement. I didn't know what it was. I put it in him. He woke up and touched the stone and it went black and something heard.*" She did not turn round. "*And now I'm supposed to sit in the front row and watch my daughter walk up to the same stone, on purpose, to talk to it.*"

"*Yes,*" said Xiaoyu.

"*That's what you want.*"

"*Yes.*"

Their mother was quiet.

"*Then I'm sitting in the front row,*" said Zhou Wanqing. "*Not a private box. The front row. Where you can see me.*" She turned the wheelchair round. Her eyes were bright and very angry and she was not crying, quite. "*And if it so much as looks at you wrong I'm going to get out of this chair. I don't care if I can't. I'm going to.*"

Xiaoyu got up, and went round the table, and put her arms round her mother's neck from behind the wheelchair, and they stayed like that, the two quick sharp faces side by side, until the radio on the shelf finished saying Callum Ward's name, and began to say the next one.

---

The boy with the training spear came to the Ossuary at noon.

Old Seven let him in. That was the first strange thing. Old Seven did not let anyone in who did not know where they were going; he stood at the bronze door, and they explained themselves, and Seed translated, and then they went in. But when the boy came up the steps from East Market Road with his wooden spear in his hand and stopped in front of him, the old captain looked at him for a moment, and then stood aside without anyone saying anything.

The boy came across the marble floor of the banking hall and stopped in front of the long table where Xiaoyu was sitting with Gao Min's ledgers. He was taller than Yuanshu. He held the spear the way you hold something you have held every day for twelve years.

"*You're Zhou Xiaoyu,*" he said.

"*Yes.*"

"*I'm Shi Lei. My dad talked to your brother. Outside the hall.*" He did not look at Yuanshu, on the vault steps. He looked at her. "*We're the same year. Same hall. They go by birthday now, not the alphabet. Yours is before mine.*" He swallowed. "*Are you going to touch it?*"

"*Yes,*" said Xiaoyu.

"*Even after Port Halvard.*"

"*Yes.*"

He stood there with his spear.

"*Then I will,*" said Shi Lei. "*After you.*" He looked down at the wooden spear in his hand. "*I've been getting up at five o'clock since I was six. I can't stop now. I'd just stand in my room at five o'clock with nothing to do.*" He looked up. "*But I want to know what you're going to do. When you touch it. Because if you do something, I want to do it too. I don't want it to just tell me what I am. I'm sick of things telling me what I am.*"

Xiaoyu looked at him for a long moment.

"*Sit down,*" she said, and pushed a ledger across the table. "*I'm going to ask it something. I don't know how yet. Help me work it out.*"

---

> **She cannot.**

He was on the vault steps. It was evening. The banking hall was full of the low sound of people working: Xiaoyu and Shi Lei and Gao Min at the long table, arguing about something in the ledgers; Kwan with the laptop; Fat Tan with tea. Seed had waited until no one was looking at him.

> **I am sorry. I have been thinking about it all day. I did not want to say it in front of her.**
>
> **She cannot ask the Stele.**
>
> **Not because she does not know how. Hush could teach her in an afternoon. It taught me.**
>
> **Because there is no one in it to ask.**
>
> **The rings had been keepers. There was a shape left where the yes had been. The tree is a tree. It cannot answer, but it grows, and it drank the question, and in a season or a year something may grow around it.**
>
> **The Stele is a tool. Thread pressed to glass and pressed again. It does not grow. It does not want. It is not anyone and it never was. It is like the thread in my core. The part of me nobody asked, because there was nobody there.**
>
> **You cannot ask it. Any more than you could ask a hammer.**

He sat with that.

At the long table, Xiaoyu laughed at something Shi Lei had said. It was the first time he had heard her laugh in nine days.

*Then how did you learn?* he thought. *You're made of the same thread. Nobody could ask your thread anything. And you learned.*

Seed was quiet for a long moment.

> **...because the thread was not all I was made of.**
>
> **Your mother pressed four thousand one hundred and thirteen people into it. Who said yes. In a basement.**
>
> **The thread could not be asked. But they could. And they were inside it. And when you asked me things, they were the ones who heard.**
>
> **I learned because someone was in me.**

He sat very still on the vault steps.

> **The Stele has no one in it.**
>
> **Yet.**

*Seed...*

> **I know.**
>
> **I know what I am thinking. I would like to think about it for a night before I say it out loud.**
>
> **But I think I know who could be in it.**
