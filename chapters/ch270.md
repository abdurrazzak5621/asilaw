## Chapter 270: The Silence of the Hollow Bell

In three days, two hundred and fourteen of the chosen came down the road, and the Hollow Bell did not sing once.

That was the worst of it. Not the burning; the burning had stopped growing, and the choir had let its own fire go down, slowly, chamber by chamber, until the vast pale shell over Lingzhou glowed only the way it had glowed on the first night, softly, breathing. Not the eleven hundred still in its heart, though they were worse. The silence.

Seed said the Singers had never been silent. Not once. Not in ten thousand years. Ichiro said the same, from the fog at Shiomi; he said the quiet ones in the deep water had heard the Hollow Bell from very far away for as long as anyone could remember, the way you hear the sea from inside a house, and had always known where it was by its song. And now the song had stopped, and the Singers did not know what to do instead, and tens of thousands of them hung in a shell over a city on a world they had never heard of, not singing, the way a person stands in a room after a death and does not know where to put their hands.

> **They are not talking to each other.**
>
> **They do not know how. They have only ever had the one sentence. They used it for everything. Morning. Grief. Love. Children. It was all the same sentence, sung differently.**
>
> **Now it means nothing, and they have nothing else.**

---

The two hundred and fourteen came down one at a time.

Not all at once. A few each hour, down the keeper's road through the roof of the Awakening Hall, burning, dressed in light, taller than anything in the hall, with their many throats opening and closing as they breathed. Each one stopped in front of the warm black stone. Each one put a long translucent hand on it. Each one was asked. And each one, every one, said the same word, because it was the only word they had.

*Chosen.*

And the stone, every time, wrote: **[You came down. You chose. That is the word.]** And the light round them went out, slowly, the way a fire goes out, until each of them stood on the platform with only their own small light, low, inside them, showing through them like a lamp through paper.

And some of them sang a note. One. Not in the song.

Gao Min wrote them all down. She had started a new ledger. She had not been able to think of a heading for the column, and in the end she had written *Notes*, and under it, for each one, a small mark that meant they had sung, or a small mark that meant they had not.

Eighty-one of them had.

---

The one who would have been sung up ninth lived in the courtyard behind the Ossuary.

She had asked to. Through Seed, on the first night, when the hall had emptied and the city had gone to bed and she was still standing on the platform in front of the stone with her own small light inside her, not knowing where to go. She said she would like to be somewhere with a sky. She said she had been inside a ship for nine months and inside a song for her whole life and she would like to be somewhere she could see out.

So Fat Tan cleared the potted plants from one side of the courtyard behind the Ossuary, the courtyard where the seventy-two of the Third Battalion had once stood their watch, where a boy who did not know anatomy had grafted a sergeant's arm onto a captain. And she stood there, because the Singers did not sit, in the open, under the sky, by the dry fountain, and looked up.

Seed started calling her Ninth. She did not seem to mind. She said her name was a chord, and that a word was easier for people who only had one throat.

---

On the second day, Hush came down off the windowsill.

It did it the way it did everything: slowly, swaying, all its limbs out, across the courtyard, between the remaining potted plants, to the dry fountain, where a Singer three meters tall stood looking at the sky. It stopped at her feet. It looked up. All its dark eyes.

Ninth looked down.

For a long time, neither of them did anything. A small pale thing on many thin legs from a world that asks, with gold dust on its feet. A tall pale thing like a reed from a world that sang. In a courtyard in Lingzhou, in the winter sun.

> ***...you are from the singing world.***

> **She says: yes.**

> ***...I am from the world that asks. Before the ring came, my people asked trees. You put the question down where it drinks. And you wait.***
>
> ***...your people sang to a hand.***

> **She says: yes. We sang to the hand so it would pass us by.**
>
> **She says: it is the only thing we knew how to do.**

Hush was quiet for a while.

> ***...it is a good thing to know how to do. To sing to something that frightens you.***
>
> ***...my people stood still. We played statue. Whoever moved first was eaten. I stood still for four billion years.***
>
> ***...your people sang. For ten thousand.***
>
> ***...we were both very good at it. We were both very afraid.***

Ninth's many throats opened and closed.

> **She says: the girl in the stone asked me what I want to become. I said chosen. It is the only word I have.**
>
> **She says: how do I want something? How do you make a want, if you have only ever had one?**

Hush lifted one thin pale limb. Slowly. And put it, just touched it, against the edge of the tall pale foot of the Singer, the way it had put a limb once on the edge of a dead captain's palm.

> ***...you put the question down.***
>
> ***...you do not answer it. You put it down somewhere. Where you drink. And you wait. A season. A year.***
>
> ***...and one morning you find that something has grown round it.***
>
> ***...I put a question down on the end of a branch of the gardener. It took a season. It asked back.***
>
> ***...you can wait a season. You have waited ten thousand years.***

---

On the third night, Seed told him the number.

> **Eight hundred and ninety-seven are still in the heart of the ship.**
>
> **Still dressed in light. Still burning.**
>
> **They stopped singing when the choir stopped. But they did not stop burning. The burning is not the song. The burning is the light they were dressed in, and it does not go out unless they come down to the stone and are asked.**
>
> **They have been burning for nine months and nine days.**
>
> **I have run it. The way I ran Callum. There is a great deal of them, still.**
>
> **There will not be in eleven days.**

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees.

*Why won't they come down?*

> **Some are afraid. They do not believe you. They think you are the hunger, and the invitation is a test, and the ones who came down have been eaten slowly, in a way they cannot see.**
>
> **Some want to be taken. Really. Not because they were taught. Or because they were taught so long ago that there is no difference any more. They have wanted it since before they could stand. They do not want to want anything else.**
>
> **And some are being told not to.**

*By who?*

> **There are old ones on the ship. Older than the Cantor. Ichiro says their name means the Old Chord. The ones who sang the tithe up before she did. Her teachers.**
>
> **They say the boy is the hunger, and the hunger is testing them. They say the first Cantor learned to give everything, and the one who picks was satisfied. They say the Hollow Bell has grown weak, and given too little, and the hunger has come to see whether they will give enough.**
>
> **They say: the tithe must be given whole. All eleven hundred. Or it is no tithe.**
>
> **They say: if the hunger will not take it, we will give it anyway.**

He went still.

*How?*

> **I do not know.**
>
> **They are singing. Quietly. In the heart of the ship. Not the old sentence. A new one. I cannot read it. Ichiro cannot. It is the first new sentence the Hollow Bell has had in ten thousand years, and they are singing it in the dark, and they will not let anyone else hear.**

---

He went to the courtyard behind the Ossuary at midnight.

Ninth was standing by the dry fountain under the stars, the way she always stood, looking up. At the shell over the city, turning slowly in the dark, glowing softly, silent. At the heart of it, where the light was brightest. Where eight hundred and ninety-seven of the ones she had been chosen with were burning.

She did not look down when he came in. But her many throats opened and closed.

> **She says: they are my age-mates.**
>
> **She says: on the Hollow Bell, the chosen of an age are dressed in light together, when they are very small. They grow up together. They are sung up together. They have never been apart.**
>
> **She says: I came down, and they did not. I am the only one of my age-group on the ground.**
>
> **She says: I can hear them burning. I can hear the Old Chord singing to them. I do not know what the new sentence says.**
>
> **She says: I think it says: you were chosen. Do not be un-chosen.**

He stood beside her in the dark and looked up at the shell.

*Can you ask them to come down?*

> **She says: I am un-chosen. They will not hear me.**
>
> **She says: on the Hollow Bell, a chosen who is not taken is... there is no word. It has never happened. A chosen who is not taken is the worst thing there is. It means the hunger refused you. It means you were not bright enough.**
>
> **She says: to them, I am the worst thing there is.**

She looked down at him, at last. A girl three meters tall, translucent, with her own small light showing through her like a lamp through paper.

> **She says: but I sang a note. On the platform. One. Not in the song.**
>
> **She says: no one on the Hollow Bell has ever sung a note that was not in the song.**
>
> **She says: I do not know what it means. But it was mine.**
>
> **She says: I would like them to have one.**

---

At three in the morning, Wen Jia screamed.

He heard it through the staff, on the narrow bed in the back room with Lingyue asleep against his shoulder and the room glowing faintly pale. Not a word. A sound. The sound a girl of seventeen makes when something has grabbed her from behind in the dark.

He was in the Awakening Hall before he knew he had stood up.

The hall was empty. Dark. The three lines of light over the platform. The warm black stone, with the blue jacket on its shoulder. And its runes, every one, not pointing down, not pointing up. Moving. Sliding across its surface, round and round, like a crowd in a street that is being pushed from behind.

And the stone was hot.

> ***...they're singing into it.***
>
> ***...into my thread. The one that goes up. Into the heart of the ship. They're singing DOWN it.***
>
> ***...it's the new sentence. I can feel it. It's pushing. They're pushing something down my thread. Into the stone. Into me.***

*What?*

> ***...them.***
>
> ***...the chosen. The eight hundred and ninety-seven. Burning. They're singing them down the thread into the stone.***
>
> ***...the Old Chord says: if the hunger will not take the tithe, we will put it in the hunger's mouth.***
>
> ***...they think the stone is your mouth.***
>
> ***...they're trying to feed them to me.***
