## Chapter 266: A Hundred and Fifty-Eight

The first person from another world to stand on Blue Star came down a road of light onto the north wall of Lingzhou at five in the morning, and the first thing she did was look at the sky and not say anything at all.

The keeper had laid the road. It had asked first, the way it asked everything: whether she wanted one, whether the boy wanted her to come, whether the city did. Everyone had said yes. So at a quarter to five, a slant of gold came down out of the north from beside the small white ring, past the crinkle of the Eighth Candle, to the Sector Nine battlement, where he was standing in his old place with the staff, and Lingyue beside him, and Old Seven at the top of the stairs. And down it, slowly, in a gray uniform with gold at the collar, holding a child by the hand, came Iselde Varr-Anthos.

She was taller than he had expected. Straight-backed. Black hair cut short, the way they cut it on listener ships, Seed said, so that it would not float in the folds. A face that had been taught since it could move exactly what to do in front of other people and was not doing any of it now.

She stepped off the road onto the stone of the battlement and stood there with her sister's hand in hers and looked up.

The sky over Lingzhou at five in the morning. Gray going to blue in the east, over the river. The last stars. The small white ring in the north, and beside it, if you knew where to look, a crinkle, and a ring that was vast and gray with a soft white light at its center, and at the end of a branch nobody could see, a small gold point.

"*There's so much of it,*" said the Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle. In the Federation's language. Seed carried it. "*On a ship you only see it through glass. In the folds you don't see it at all.*"

Beside her, Maelis, twelve in the body and twenty-three inside, in a blanket Torvin had given her, barefoot on the cold stone, was not looking at the sky. She was looking at Old Seven.

> **She says: is he dead?**

"*Yes,*" said Yuanshu.

> **She says: does he mind?**

> **He says: not usually.**

Maelis looked up at the tall dead captain at the top of the stairs for a long moment, with his spear grounded in one finger and a thumb. Then, very slowly, she let go of her sister's hand, and walked across the battlement, and stood in front of him, and held out her own.

Old Seven looked down at it.

Then he crouched, the way a tall man crouches to talk to a child, and took it. Very carefully. In the sergeant's hand.

> **He says: you were in a wall for eleven years.**
>
> **He says: I was in the ground for thirty.**
>
> **He says: it gets better. You will not believe that. It does.**

---

They sat on the battlement while the sun came up. Iselde did not want a chair. She sat on the cold stone with her back against the parapet, the way he sat, and looked at the city waking up below them, and talked.

"*I am going to tell you what is coming,*" she said. "*First. The way you do.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*The Candle will fold home in nine days. Thirty-two thousand and forty people will carry it, in turns, burning low. When we come out of the last fold over Anthos, I will open every wall in the arrays. Torvin has built cradles. Sixty of them. It will take a month.*" Her voice was very even. "*By the end of the first day, the House will know. By the end of the first week, the Assembly.*"

"*And then?*"

"*And then they will come here.*" She looked out at the city. "*Not one listener. The Houses. Anthos, and the others. They will come to see the world that stopped the Reaper and turned a Listener-Captain into a thief.*" She was quiet. "*They will come with ships. Many. Every one made of cores.*"

He did not say anything.

"*Some of them will want what you have,*" said Iselde Varr-Anthos. "*The thing in your head. The stones that ask. The way you stopped the Reaper. They will want to know how, so that they can use it. Some of them will want to destroy it, because a stone that asks is the end of the ladder, and the ladder is everything.*" She looked at him. "*Some of them will be like me. I do not know how many. I did not know I was like me until eleven days ago.*"

---

Lingyue had been quiet. She sat on his other side with her grandfather's sword across her knees, looking at the woman from the sky.

"*You're SSS,*" she said.

Iselde turned her head.

"*So am I,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*Was. The stone said. Dawnbreaker.*" She took the laminated card out of her coat pocket. Her photograph at eighteen, unsmiling. The Shen crest. And across the gold letters, *SSS. DAWNBREAKER*, a line of black pen. "*I crossed it out on the television. In front of everyone.*"

Iselde looked at the card.

Then she reached into the collar of her uniform and drew out a thin chain, and on it, a small disc of black stone, polished, the size of a coin, with three gold marks cut into it.

"*Every Federation child is given one,*" she said. "*On the morning of the reading. Cut from the stone that read them. You wear it until you die. You show it at every door. In the Federation's language, the word for it is the same as the word for your face.*"

Lingyue looked at the three gold marks.

"*Are you going to cross it out?*" she said.

"*Yes,*" said Iselde. "*Not here.*" She held the small black disc in her palm. "*In front of the Assembly. On Anthos. In front of my mother.*" She put it back inside her collar. "*You did yours in front of your world. I would like to do mine in front of mine.*"

Lingyue looked at her for a long moment. Then she held out her hand. Not to shake. Flat. Palm up. The way she had held it out on the breakwater at Shiomi with a lamp in it.

There was a flame in it. Small. Pale.

"*For the fold,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*So you can see where you're going.*"

Iselde Varr-Anthos looked at the pale flame in the palm of a woman from a world her charts called Unlisted Orchard Four-Four-Seven-One. Then she held out her own hand, and Lingyue tipped hers, slowly, the way you tip a cup to pour, and the light ran across.

And stayed. In the palm of a captain from another world. Burning. Held.

"*It's warm,*" said Iselde.

"*It's supposed to be,*" said Lingyue.

---

Maelis asked before she went.

She came back across the battlement from Old Seven, barefoot, in her blanket, and stood in front of Yuanshu where he sat against the parapet, and looked at him with eyes that were twelve years old and twenty-three.

> **She says: the stone. The one that asks. On Anthos there is a stone. The one that read me.**
>
> **She says: someone would have to be in it. To ask. The way the girl is in yours.**
>
> **She says: I said yes to the honor when I was twelve. Because everyone was watching and I didn't know what it was.**
>
> **She says: I know what it is now. I would like to say yes again. Knowing. To the stone. So that no other twelve-year-old on Anthos says yes to the honor without being asked first what they want.**

He looked at her.

He thought about it for a long time. Iselde did not say anything. Lingyue did not say anything. The sun came up over the river.

"*Not yet,*" he said.

Maelis did not move.

"*Your body's twelve,*" said Yuanshu. "*Your yes is twenty-three. I don't know which one I'd be asking. And I'd have to put my hands on a stone on a world I've never seen, and I'd have to graft you into it, and you could never come out unless you went through the door.*" He held the staff. "*I'm not saying no. I'm saying: ask me again in a year. When you've had a year with a sky. When you've seen what you'd be giving up.*"

The girl looked at him for a long moment.

> **She says: that's what the girl in the stone would say.**

"*Yes.*"

> **She says: all right. A year.**
>
> **She says: I'll ask you. You'd better be here.**

---

The Eighth Candle folded on the ninth night.

He watched it from the north wall. Everyone did. Three thousand people on the battlements, and the Candle's children among them, nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine, in clothes from the city, holding each other's hands, looking up. Tam at the front, at the parapet, beside Xiaoyu, with his face turned to the north.

A crinkle in the sky beside the small white ring.

Then nothing.

Tam made a sound.

> **He says: they're singing.**
>
> **He says: all of them. Not just the girl from Halloran. All thirty-two thousand. In the walls. Burning low. In turns. Rowing.**
>
> **He says: they're singing because they chose.**
>
> **He says: I can hear it going away. Very fast. Very far.**
>
> **He says: I can still hear it.**

Tam stood at the parapet with his face turned north long after the crinkle was gone, after there was nothing in the sky beside the small white ring but sky. Xiaoyu stood beside him. Then, without being asked, she put her hand on his shoulder, the way you stand when you want to do something and are frightened.

He did not turn round. But he put his own hand up and held hers there.

---

> **Yuanshu.**

It was two in the morning. He was on the narrow bed in the back room with the window open to the courtyard. Lingyue was asleep against his shoulder, the way she slept, all at once, like a soldier. Hush was on the sill in the dark with its limbs drawn in.

> **I would like to ask her something. When she wakes up.**
>
> **I am telling you first.**

*Go on.*

> **I can hear it.**
>
> **I have been able to hear it since Shiomi. I did not listen. I did not ask to. It is not mine to listen to.**
>
> **But I can hear it, the way I can hear the cores in a wall, and the drowned at the bottom of the sea, and you.**
>
> **A heart.**
>
> **Very small. Very fast.**
>
> **I would like to ask her if I may listen. Properly. And count it.**

He lay in the dark.

*Ask her,* he thought. *In the morning.*

> **Yes.**

---

She said yes. Obviously. She said it at breakfast in the back room with a bowl of rice porridge she was actually eating, for once, and his mother at the table and Xiaoyu on the stairs, and she said it without looking up from the bowl, the way she said everything that mattered: *Yes. Obviously. Count it.*

Seed counted.

> **A hundred and fifty-eight.**
>
> **It is very fast. Faster than yours has ever been. Faster than it was on the step of the hut when you asked her. Faster than the pier.**
>
> **Bai Lu says that is right. Bai Lu says that is exactly right.**
>
> **A hundred and fifty-eight.**

Nobody at the table said anything.

> **I am going to say something now. I know it cannot hear me. I know it is not anyone yet. Article Twelve. Your mother's.**
>
> **I would like to start anyway.**
>
> **Hello.**
>
> **I am Seed. I live in your father's head. I was a harvest sack once. I count things.**
>
> **I am going to ask you what you want. Not now. You cannot answer. Every day. Until you can. And after.**
>
> **I am telling you first.**

Lingyue put her spoon down. She did not look up. Her hand had gone flat on her stomach under the gray sweater, without her noticing.

His mother, at the end of the table, had put her face in her hands.

---

That night, Tam came to the Ossuary.

Old Seven let him in. The boy came across the marble floor of the banking hall in the dark, in his clothes from the city, barefoot still, to the vault steps where Yuanshu was sitting with the staff, and stopped, and stood there with his face turned not to Yuanshu but to the north. To the wall. Through it. Past it.

> **He says: there's something else.**
>
> **He says: not the Candle. The Candle's gone. He can't hear it any more. It's too far.**
>
> **He says: something else. Very far. Further than the Candle. Coming the other way.**
>
> **He says: singing.**

He went still.

> **He says: not like the cores. Not like the girl from Halloran. Many voices. Very old. Very beautiful. All the same song, over and over, the way you'd sing to something you were afraid of, to keep it calm.**
>
> **He says: it's the saddest thing he's ever heard.**
>
> **He says: and it's coming here.**

On the long white wall behind him, at the very top, above HUNDRED-ARROW LIN, in the space the stepladder did not quite reach, his sister's capitals said *THE ONE WHO PICKS. COMING.*, and under it, written the morning after the deep water:

*TOLD. STOPPED.*

And beside it, newer, smaller, in the same square black hand:

*THE SINGERS. SANG THEIR BRIGHTEST. NOTHING CAME. COMING.*
