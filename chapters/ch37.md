## Chapter 37: Lu's Confession

Director Lu Zhengtian lived alone in a small apartment on the ninth floor of an old building by the river, and it looked like nobody lived there at all.

White walls. A gray sofa with the plastic still on one arm. A kitchen so clean it looked like a photograph of a kitchen. No pictures on the walls. No books on the shelves, except a row of thick binders with the Awakening Bureau's seal on their spines. The only thing in the whole apartment that looked like it belonged to a person was on the windowsill: a single small plant in a clay pot, a twisted little juniper no taller than a hand, very old, very carefully pruned.

He saw Yuanshu looking at it.

"Hanshan gave it to me," he said. "Seven years ago. The week after." He didn't say after what. "He said a man who lives alone should have something to keep alive."

He poured two glasses of something brown from a bottle that had never been opened, and set one in front of Su Mingli, and kept one for himself, and didn't offer one to Yuanshu. He sat down on the gray sofa on the side without the plastic.

Out the window, the river was black and slow. The lights of the far bank shook on it.

"Ask," said Lu.

---

"My mother's last week," said Yuanshu. "All of it."

Lu looked at the glass in his hand for a long time.

"She came to my office on a Monday," he said. "Late. After everyone had gone home. She was never in my office. She thought the Bureau was a tool for frightened men, and she said so, in meetings. To my face." Something moved at the corner of his mouth. "She was right, mostly."

He drank.

"She closed the door and she said, 'Zhengtian, someone in this building is talking to the sky.'"

The river moved past the window.

"She'd put sensors on the north wall twelve years ago. You know that. For the lens. Waiting for it to open again. And it never did. Not once. Five years of nothing." Lu set his glass down. "Then she turned the sensors around."

"Around?"

"Pointed them at the city instead of the sky. She said: 'If something up there is listening, maybe something down here is talking.' And for two years she found nothing. And then, that spring, she found a pulse. Very faint. Once a month. Going *up.* From somewhere in the north of Lingzhou. She couldn't narrow it down further than a few blocks." He looked at Yuanshu. "Those few blocks had the Institute in them."

> **The weather mast.**
>
> **Eleven years old.**
>
> **She was hunting it. Four years before it melted.**

---

"I didn't believe her," said Lu quietly. "That's the first thing I have to confess. I thought she was overworked. I thought she'd been staring at that faceless thing in the basement too long. I told her to take a holiday." He laughed, once, without any sound. "She looked at me the way you'd look at a dog that had just eaten your homework, and she left."

He was quiet for a moment.

"On Wednesday night, at 2:14 in the morning, the scout turned its head."

"Toward her lab."

"Toward her lab." Lu nodded. "She was in it. She was always in it. She called me at 2:20. She wasn't frightened. She was..." He searched for the word. "Excited. Like a child. She said, 'Zhengtian, it's not looking at me. It's *answering* me.' I asked her what she'd said to it. She said, 'Nothing yet. I've only been thinking very loudly.'" He rubbed his eyes under his glasses. "And she hung up."

Su Mingli had not touched her glass. She was sitting very still in the corner of the room with her coat still on.

"You never told me any of this," she said. Her voice was perfectly level.

"No."

"Seven years."

"I know." Lu didn't look at her. "That's the second thing I have to confess."

---

"Friday," said Yuanshu.

Lu got up. He went to the shelf of binders, took one down, and opened it on the low table between them. Inside, in a plastic sleeve, was a sequence of photographs. Stills from a security camera. Grainy, green, timestamped in the corner.

*Friday. 23:41.*

The round chamber at the bottom of the shaft. The blue rings of the containment arrays. The cylinder of law-glass with the faceless shape inside it.

And a woman, walking across the floor toward it.

She had her back to the camera. Hair tied up carelessly. A gray coat too big for her. In one hand, a slim metal case. She walked without hurrying, the way you walk into your own kitchen.

*23:43.* She stood in front of the cylinder. She set the case down on the floor beside her and opened it. The camera couldn't see what was in it.

*23:44.* She put her palm flat on the law-glass.

*23:44.* The next photograph was white. Pure, flat white, as if the camera had been pointed at the sun.

*23:55.* The chamber again. The blue rings. The cylinder. The faceless shape inside it, its head turned. The case on the floor, open, empty.

And no one.

"Eleven minutes," said Lu. "Every camera in the building went white for eleven minutes. Every instrument. When they came back, she was gone. No body. No trace. The doors of the shaft never opened. The elevator never moved." He closed the binder. "There's no way out of that room except the way in. She didn't use it."

Yuanshu looked at the closed binder for a long time.

> **She went somewhere.**
>
> **"I had to go somewhere you can't follow yet."**
>
> **I do not have the recording anymore. But I remember that I had it. I remember that sentence.**

---

"There's a third thing," said Lu.

He reached into the inside pocket of his jacket, the jacket he wore every day, the same dark suit. He took out a small square of paper, folded many times, soft and gray at the creases from being handled.

"I found this in my coat pocket on Saturday morning," he said. "I have no idea when she put it there. I had been nowhere near her that week. I'd been in my office." He held it out. "I've read it every day for seven years."

Yuanshu unfolded it.

Quick slanting handwriting. The same as the whiteboards. The same as every note on the refrigerator of his childhood.

*Zhengtian,*

*Don't let them raise it. No matter who asks. No matter how good the reason.*

*When my son awakens, they'll come for him with collars and contracts. Let the world see what he does before anyone gets to decide for him. Do it badly, if you have to. He'll forgive you. I won't, but I won't be there.*

*Keep him away from the thing in the basement until he can ask it a question without being read.*

*And watch the people who are kindest to you. The thing that is talking to the sky has been here a very long time, and it has learned how to be loved.*

*— W.*

---

Nobody spoke for a long time.

The river ran past the window. Somewhere on the far bank, a ferry sounded its horn, long and low.

"The lockout," said Yuanshu finally. "On my first night. Forty minutes. An old man in a lodge."

"I did it badly," said Lu. "She said I could." He picked up his glass and looked at it and put it down without drinking. "I needed the world to see you before the Atlantic Federation could get a collar on you. Before the Council could decide you were a weapon. I needed footage of you choosing, on your own, with nobody telling you what to do." He took off his glasses. His eyes without them looked older and smaller. "So I gave you a rift and a reason, and I stood in my office and watched, and if you had run, or killed, or lost control, I would have had to watch that too."

"And Grandpa Xu."

"Was never going to die. Master Su was in the clouds the whole time. I knew she would be. I've known her for twenty years." He put his glasses back on. "That's not an excuse. It's just the truth."

Su Mingli made a small sound in the corner. It might have been a laugh. It might have been something else.

---

"The note," said Yuanshu. "The last line."

"*Watch the people who are kindest to you.*" Lu nodded slowly. "I have. For seven years. Every person in that building who ever brought me tea or asked about my weekend. I've checked their files and their phones and their bank accounts. I've watched them all." He turned his head, and looked across the room at the windowsill. At the small twisted juniper in its clay pot, very old, very carefully pruned. "Every single one of them except the one who held me together after she left."

He didn't say anything else.

He didn't have to.

> **"It has learned how to be loved."**
>
> **One percent.**
>
> **Exactly the right size to be too small to be sure.**

Yuanshu folded the note along its old soft creases and held it out.

Lu shook his head.

"Keep it," he said. "It was always yours. I just held it for you." He looked out at the black river. "That's the other reason I chose Quiet in your Ledger, you know. Not because I don't want to be raised." He was silent for a moment. "Because I didn't want you to be able to read this week out of me. I didn't want you to see how long I didn't believe her."

"I'd have asked first," said Yuanshu.

"I know," said Director Lu. "That's what made it worse."
