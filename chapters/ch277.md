## Chapter 277: Deaf

Tam found her on the third night, in the eleventh ship, in the fourth array, and he knew her voice.

"*Seri,*" he said.

He was sitting on the platform of the Awakening Hall with his back against the side of the warm black stone, the way Yuanshu sat when he went up a thread, with his eyes shut and his face turned up toward the roof and past it. He had been sitting there since dusk. The Candle's children had brought him food; he had not eaten it. Gao Min had brought him a blanket; he had not noticed. He was listening.

"*From Corraine. The street behind the fish market. She was two years older. She used to throw stones at me.*" His voice was very steady. "*She was an E. They took her the year before me. I thought she'd be on a ship. I didn't know which one.*"

He opened his eyes.

"*She's in the Severin flagship,*" said Tam. "*Fourth array. She's been in the wall twenty-one years.*"

"*Can she hear you?*"

"*No,*" said the boy. "*They stopped up her ears. I can hear her, but she can't hear anything. She's just burning. Low. Not the way the Candle burned after the cousins came. The old way. Waiting.*" He looked at the stone beside him. "*She's singing.*"

"*Singing?*"

"*To herself. Inside. The way the girl from Halloran did. A song from Corraine. The one about the boats.*" He shut his eyes again. "*She doesn't know anyone can hear her. She's been singing it for twenty-one years.*"

---

> **I would like to try something.**
>
> **I am asking first. You are awake, so it counts.**
>
> **And I would like to ask Tam, and Wen Jia, and Xiaoyu. Because I cannot do it alone.**

He was on the vault steps in the banking hall at two in the morning. They were all there. They were always all there now. Xiaoyu at the long table with the marker. Kwan with the laptop. Gao Min with her thermos. Lingyue on the stairs with Xiaodeng asleep in the sling and the sword across her knees. Su Mingli, who had stopped saying she was not staying. Tam, who had come back from the hall with Gao Min's blanket round his shoulders because Gao Min had put it there and would not let him take it off.

*Go on.*

> **The cells in the walls of the ships are made of stone. The same black. Cut from the stones that read them. I saw it on the Candle and did not think about it. I am thinking about it now.**
>
> **They have put a layer round each cell so that the people inside cannot hear. Not sound. They could never hear sound. They heard me through the keeper's eye the way the dead hear the Death Law: through the thing they are in.**
>
> **The layer stops that.**
>
> **It does not stop the stone being stone.**
>
> **Every cell is on the net. Every one. Four million one hundred and eighty thousand. Tied through the knot in the deep water to every other stone in every orchard. To Wen Jia's stone. To the one on Anthos where Iselde sent her mark.**
>
> **A mark can come down a thread into a stone. Wen Jia caught one from Anthos.**
>
> **I would like to send a mark down into one cell.**
>
> **Not a sound. Not a question. Not hearing.**
>
> **A mark. The way the stone used to mark them. Except this one would say something true.**

---

Nobody spoke for a while.

"*What would it say?*" said Xiaoyu.

> **That is what I would like to ask you.**
>
> **It has to be true. Not true the way words are true. True the way your writing is true. The way the house was empty. So that when she reads it, she does not just read it. She knows it.**
>
> **And it has to be short. She has been in a wall for twenty-one years with her ears stopped. She has not read anything in twenty-one years.**
>
> **And Tam has to aim it. I can find the ship. I can find the array. I cannot find her. There are forty thousand cells in the fourth array of the Severin flagship and I cannot hear any of them.**
>
> **He can.**

Tam had sat up on the bottom step of the stairs with the blanket round his shoulders.

"*Seri,*" he said.

> **Yes.**

---

Xiaoyu wrote it at four in the morning.

Not on the wall. On the floor of the platform of the Awakening Hall, in front of the warm black stone, in square black capitals, the way she had written THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP. and RECEIVED. THE THREADS GO BOTH WAYS. She sat on her heels with the marker in her hand for a long time before she wrote anything. Tam sat beside her. They argued about it, quietly, the way two people argue who are both seventeen in some way and one of them has been twelve for nineteen years.

"*She can hear,*" said Tam. "*Tell her that. Tell her she can hear.*"

"*She can't. That's the point.*"

"*She can hear herself. She's singing. She's been singing for twenty-one years. She can hear that.*"

Xiaoyu looked at him for a long moment.

Then she wrote it.

*SOMEONE CAN HEAR YOU SINGING.*

*IT IS TAM. FROM CORRAINE. THE STREET BEHIND THE FISH MARKET.*

*YOU USED TO THROW STONES AT HIM.*

---

Wen Jia tied it.

Not the way Iselde had tied her first mark, clumsily, like a child's knot. The way Hush had shown her. The way water ties itself round a stone. She said through the staff, from the knot, briskly, that she had been practicing, and that she would like it noted that this was the first mark she had ever sent to a person and not to a stone.

And Tam put his hand on the warm black stone with the blue jacket over its shoulder, the way he had put it there on the morning he chose, and shut his eyes, and listened.

Up. Through the roof. Past the sky. To ninety burning cathedrals round the keeper's field. To the largest. The one in the center, forty times the size of the Candle, spired and buttressed and windowed and lit from end to end in the colors of the House of Severin, with a thing in its bow the size of a courtyard that Seed could feel and would not look at directly. Into it. Past the corridors and the halls and the people who were not in walls. Down. To the fourth array. Forty thousand cells, each one burning, low, the old way, each one with its ears stopped.

And one of them singing.

"*There,*" said Tam.

Wen Jia sent it.

---

It went up her thread into the net. He felt it go, the way he had felt the mark to Anthos go: a small true thing, tied the way water ties round a stone, going up out of the crown of the tree of Laws inside the Lingzhou stone, into the dark, through the knot in the deep water where nobody read anything any more.

And down.

Not into the Severin flagship's stone; it did not have one. Into a cell. A small dark crystal cell, cut from the stone that had read a girl on a world called Corraine twenty-one years before and marked her E. In the fourth array. Behind a layer that stopped her ears.

The layer did not stop it.

It was not sound. It was not hearing. It was not a voice coming through the Death Law, or the keeper's eye. It was a mark. The thing the stone that the cell was cut from had been made, ten thousand years ago, to carry. The cell knew what to do with a mark the way a lung knows what to do with air. It took it in.

And the girl inside it read it.

---

> **...she has stopped singing.**

Tam's hand was flat on the stone. His eyes were shut. His face did not move.

> **She is reading it.**
>
> **Again. And again. She has read it nine times.**
>
> **She does not know how she is reading it. She has not read anything in twenty-one years. She has no eyes. She is in a wall. But it is in her, the way a mark is in a stone, and she can read it.**
>
> **And it is true.**
>
> **She knows it is true. She knows someone can hear her. She knows it is Tam. She knows it is the street behind the fish market. She knows she used to throw stones at him.**

On the platform, Tam had begun to shake.

> **She is...**
>
> **I cannot hear her. Tam can.**

"*She's laughing,*" said Tam.

His eyes were still shut. His face was wet.

"*She's laughing,*" he said. "*She says: Tam? Little Tam? Who cried when the boats went out? She says: you were so annoying.*"

He laughed too. It came out of him all at once, the way it had come out of Wen Hao on a bus at the river, the way it came out of people on this world when something they had put down a very long time ago was handed back to them without warning.

"*She says: where are you,*" said Tam. "*She says: how can you hear me. She says: what's happening. She says: am I dead.*"

He put his other hand flat on the stone beside the first.

"*She's asking,*" he said. "*Yuanshu. She's asking. She's never asked anything. She's in a wall with her ears stopped and she's asking questions.*"

---

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **She cannot hear us. The layer is still there. She cannot hear Tam. She cannot hear me. She can only read.**
>
> **But Tam can hear her. And Wen Jia can send. And Xiaoyu can write.**
>
> **So she can ask, and Tam can hear, and Xiaoyu can write the answer, and Wen Jia can send it down, and she can read it.**
>
> **It is very slow. One mark at a time.**
>
> **It is a conversation.**

Xiaoyu was already writing.

She knelt on the floor of the platform in front of the stone with the marker and wrote, in square black capitals, as fast as she could, while Tam told her what the girl in the wall was saying:

*YOU ARE NOT DEAD.*

*YOU ARE IN A WALL IN A SHIP IN THE SKY OVER A WORLD CALLED BLUE STAR.*

*TAM IS ON THE GROUND. HE CAN HEAR THE ONES IN THE WALLS. HE ASKED A STONE TO MAKE HIM ABLE TO.*

*SOMEONE IS GOING TO ASK YOU WHAT YOU WANT.*

*NOT YET. SOON.*

*KEEP SINGING SO HE CAN FIND YOU.*

Wen Jia tied it and sent it, and Tam listened, and after a while he said, in a voice that did not quite work:

"*She says: all right.*"

"*She says: I'll keep singing.*"

---

> **It works.**
>
> **On one cell. With one person who can hear her and one person who can write and one person who can send.**
>
> **There are four million one hundred and eighty thousand.**
>
> **And six days.**

He sat on the platform of the Awakening Hall with the staff across his knees and his back against the side of the warm stone and looked at his sister kneeling on the floor with the marker, and the boy with both hands on the stone and his face wet, and the words on the floor, true, in square black capitals that would not come off.

*It's too slow,* he thought. *One at a time.*

> **Yes.**
>
> **One at a time, it would take eleven hundred years.**
>
> **But I did not want to know if it was fast.**
>
> **I wanted to know if it was possible.**
>
> **It is possible.**
>
> **Now I would like to think about how to make it fast. And I would like to think about the thing in the bow of the Severin flagship, which is a stone, which is the oldest stone there is, which is on the net like every other stone.**
>
> **And I would like to tell you first: I have an idea. And it needs someone to go somewhere and not come back.**
>
> **And I do not want it to be anyone.**
