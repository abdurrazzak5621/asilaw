## Chapter 158: Twelve

Seed got Kesse wrong too, but this time it found out in time, and the reason was a woman forty kilometers away sitting on a jetty with her eyes shut.

> **Yuanshu. The Kesse trunk is growing.**
>
> **It should not be. There is no tide now. It is the middle of the morning. The land breeze has stopped. The deep water should be still.**
>
> **It is not still. It is rising. Slowly. All along the coast between here and Sandu.**

They were in the back of a Southern Compact army truck on the coast road, twenty minutes out of Port Abiri, going south. Yuanshu sat on the bench with the staff across his knees and the Field moving with him down the coast like a lamp carried along a corridor. Lingyue was asleep against his shoulder, still wet to the waist, with both swords in her arms. Adjutant Xu sat across from them with his cane. The twins were in the cab with the driver, arguing about the radio.

"Why?" said Yuanshu.

> **Ifeoma.**
>
> **She is holding the Sandu upwelling down. Ten kilometers of deep water that wants to come up, and she is not letting it.**
>
> **But the water has to go somewhere. Water is lazy. If it cannot come up at Sandu, it goes sideways along the bottom to the nearest place where it can.**
>
> **The nearest place is Kesse.**

He understood.

> **She is pushing the Sandu tide north into the Kesse trunk. Not on purpose. She cannot help it. It is what water does.**
>
> **New surfacing time for Kesse: 11:50. Not 15:20.**
>
> **It is 09:12.**
>
> **I did not count this. I should have. It is the same water. I modeled the three trunks as three separate things, and they are not separate. They are one sea.**

"Can she stop?"

> **If she stops, the Sandu trunk gets its tide, and surfaces this afternoon, with eleven thousand people still on the river road.**

"Then she can't stop."

> **No.**

"And we've got two hours and thirty-eight minutes."

> **Yes.**

---

The drowned were walking.

He could feel them through the Field, three kilometers down, on the seabed at the foot of the shelf: two thousand seven hundred and forty-two banked embers from Port Abiri, moving south along the bottom of the sea toward Kesse, twenty kilometers away, in the cold and the dark. They had not been asked to. Nobody had been able to ask them anything since one in the morning. But they had heard the trunk die, the way you hear a tree fall in a forest, and they had felt the Field move away from them down the coast, and fishermen know which way a boat is going.

Emeka was at the front. Seed said so. A man who had gone over the side of a white fishing boat a hundred and forty years ago, walking south across the bottom of the sea with a lit fishing knife, toward his wife.

Behind them, slower, eight hundred meters up the slope of the shelf, came the Spear Corps. Three hundred dead soldiers of the Eastern Line, who had walked into the sea off the beach at Port Abiri at midnight in their rows with their rusted spears, because Old Seven had asked them to, and had been walking downhill in the dark ever since.

Old Seven himself was in the truck, sitting on the floor at Yuanshu's feet with his spear across his knees, because the truck was faster.

> **He says he does not like being in a truck while his men walk.**
>
> **He says he likes it less than he likes drowning, and he has never drowned.**

---

At Kesse, the long beach was empty.

Thirty-one thousand people had lived in the town at the top of it. The buses had taken most of them. The last few hundred were going up the dirt track over the hills behind the town in a long slow line of trucks and handcarts and people on foot, and the beach below them, where a year ago their brothers and cousins and grandparents had stood with lanterns all night listening to the dead of Port Abiri speak out of the air, was a long empty curve of pale sand in the late morning sun.

Out past the surf, two kilometers offshore, the sea was steaming.

> **The top of the trunk is thirty meters down. Rising.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **It has closed.**

He felt it the moment the Field reached it.

The trunk off Kesse was the same as the one off Port Abiri. Three kilometers of cracked gray crystal standing up out of a heap of sand on the seabed, riding the rising water. But at the top, where the Port Abiri trunk had been open, a hollow throat going down to the seed, this one was sealed. A cap. A thick blunt dome of crystal, grown over the top of the throat in the last three hours, since the trunk at Port Abiri died.

> **They talk to each other. The trunks. Through the sand. It is one heap, really, under thirty-eight kilometers of sea.**
>
> **This one felt what Lingyue did to the other one. It has shut its mouth.**

Lingyue was awake. She was standing at the edge of the surf with her own sword in her hand, looking out at the steam.

"Can I go through the cap?"

> **No. Not from here. It is too thick. Your light would spread over the top of it like rain on a roof.**

She did not say anything. She lit her sword anyway, and let a drop of pale fire fall off the point into the sea, and he felt it go down two kilometers through the water and land on the dome at the top of the trunk, and spread, and go out.

She looked at the place where it had gone in.

"Then I'll drop it on the drowned again," she said. "When they get here. They can dig."

> **The seed is under three hundred meters of sand. They dug at Port Abiri for thirteen minutes and got forty meters.**
>
> **The trunk surfaces in two hours.**

---

The drowned reached Kesse at 10:40.

And the drowned of Kesse were waiting for them.

He had not asked them. He had not been able to. They lay at the Kesse end of the long smear of sand, outside the Field's reach until the truck came down the coast, nineteen hundred embers he had never spoken to, being eaten very slowly in the cold. When the Port Abiri drowned came walking across the bottom of the sea out of the north, the Kesse drowned did not move. They did not know what was coming. Nobody had ever told them anything.

> **I cannot ask them. Not from here. No pipe.**
>
> **And there is no time to make one. Ifeoma is at Sandu.**

Yuanshu stood at the edge of the surf with the staff in his hands and the warm water breaking around his shins, and looked at the steam.

Then he looked at Adjutant Xu.

The old man was standing on the sand a little way up the beach with his cane, the way he had stood on the end of the harbor wall at Port Abiri two nights ago, humming a question down a pipe of still water. His empty eye sockets were turned toward the sea.

*The Lattice*, Yuanshu thought.

> **...yes.**
>
> **Yes. Yuanshu. Yes.**

The Asking Lattice did not need him to reach the bottom. It had never needed him. It needed the dead to reach each other. Each soldier asks its nine. And a question that scattered in three kilometers of layered water between the surface and the seabed did not scatter in the three meters between one drowned fisherman and the next one, standing beside him on the sand at the bottom of the sea.

*They can ask each other.*

> **The Port Abiri drowned already have the question. You asked them two nights ago. They remember it. Every word.**
>
> **If Adjutant Xu sends one note down, just one, it does not need to arrive clearly. It only needs to say: now.**
>
> **And they will turn to the ones beside them and ask.**

---

Adjutant Xu hummed one note into the sea.

It went down three kilometers and arrived as a blur, the way a voice arrives through a closed door. But two thousand seven hundred and forty-two of the drowned of Port Abiri were waiting for a blur. They had been told, two nights ago, by a fisherman named Yaw who had been asked first and told the others, that when the old man's note came, it meant *ask.*

They turned to the nineteen hundred drowned of Kesse, lying in the cold sand around them, and asked.

Not in Yuanshu's words. In their own. Fishermen asking fishermen, in the language of the coast, at the bottom of the sea. *There's a boy at the top. He's sorry nobody came before. This thing's been eating us. It's going up to bite Kesse. Do you want to help? You can say no. You can say you want to sleep.*

And the drowned of Kesse answered the people beside them. Each one to each one. Nine at a time. Up the Lattice, through the Port Abiri drowned who had been asked properly, through Yaw and Emeka and a girl who had been a very good swimmer, to Adjutant Xu standing on the beach with his cane, and through him, to Seed.

It took eleven minutes.

> **Yes: one thousand four hundred and six.**
>
> **Quiet: three hundred and twelve.**
>
> **Only to speak: two hundred and two.**
>
> **They asked each other, Yuanshu. Nobody told them what to say. They asked.**

"Grandpa Xu," said Yuanshu.

> **He says he knows. He says he's been waiting sixty years for a net to do that by itself.**
>
> **He says: don't make a fuss.**

---

That left the cap.

Four thousand one hundred and forty-eight of the drowned stood in a ring on the seabed around the heap off Kesse, with Lingyue's pale fire falling on their hooks and knives out of the dark above them like rain, digging. And the trunk above them, sealed at the top, riding the rising water toward the surface, pulled its roots in tight around the heap and held.

> **11:14. Thirty-six minutes.**
>
> **They are at sixty meters. The seed is at three hundred.**
>
> **They will not reach it.**

"The roots," said Yuanshu.

He had been watching them through the Field for twenty minutes. Thousands of them. Gray, branching, running out of the heap through the sand in every direction, the way the roots at the Western Pass had run through the mountain to forty-one thousand graves. Going out to every drowned ember the trunk could find. Coming back.

And every one of them ran back into the heap. To the center. To the seed. The way every root of a tree runs back to the trunk.

"The cap closed the top," he said. "But the roots are still open. They have to be. That's how it eats." He looked down at the staff in his hands. "If one of the drowned catches a root, with pale fire on his hands, and holds it, the fire runs down the root. Downhill. Into the heap. To the seed. The same way it ran down the throat at Port Abiri."

> **...yes.**
>
> **And the root touches him.**

"Yes."

> **And he burns.**

"Yes," said Yuanshu.

He was quiet for a moment.

"How many?"

> **One root will not be enough. Twelve, at once, from all sides, so the fire arrives at the seed together.**
>
> **Twelve who will catch a root and hold on.**
>
> **Probability that any of them come back: very small. I will not give you a number. It would be a lie in either direction.**

---

He asked them.

Through Adjutant Xu. One note down the sea for *now*, and then the question, carried down the Lattice from the dead who had been asked properly to the dead who had only just been asked, each one to their nine. He did not dress it up. He did not make it brave.

*I need twelve. To catch a root with fire on their hands and hold it until it reaches the seed. If you do, it'll touch you, and you'll burn, and I don't think you'll come back. You can say no. Nobody will think less of you. Nobody will ask you twice.*

Seed counted them as they came up the Lattice.

Not all of them. Not even most. A great many said nothing at all. A great many said no, quietly, the way you say no to someone you like. That was right. That was what the question was for.

> **Three hundred and eight say yes.**
>
> **I need twelve.**
>
> **They are choosing among themselves. I am not doing it. Neither are you.**

They chose.

Twelve. Yaw, from Kesse, who had gone over the side in a storm forty-one years ago, who had answered first. An engineer from a northern cargo ship who did not speak the language of the coast and had been asked twice. Three of a Kesse crew who had gone down together in the year before the Steles fell. A ferryman. A boy of seventeen. And a bride and her groom, from a wedding party sixty years ago, who said they had gone down holding hands and would rather go out the same way.

Emeka was not one of them. He had asked to be. The others had said no. *Your wife is at Sandu*, they said. *Go and stand where she can feel you.*

---

At 11:31, on Adjutant Xu's single note, twelve of the drowned of the Southern Compact coast reached out in the dark at the bottom of the sea, from twelve sides of a heap of waking sand, with pale fire on their hands, and took hold of twelve gray roots, and did not let go.

He felt them burn.

He felt the pale fire go down the roots, twelve thin threads of silver-gray running downhill through the sand toward the center, faster and faster, while the twelve who held them flared and went white at the edges and held on. Yaw held on. The engineer held on. The bride and her groom held on to the same root, together, with their four hands.

The fire reached the seed.

The seed cracked.

Above it, three kilometers of sealed gray crystal, nine meters below the surface of the sea off the long empty beach at Kesse, began to come apart. From the bottom. Into sand.

> **[The Kesse trunk is dead.]**
>
> **11:33. Seventeen minutes.**
>
> **The twelve are gone. I have their names. I have what they said when they were asked, two nights ago or eleven minutes ago.**
>
> **The bride said: tell my mother I wasn't frightened. She will be a hundred and four now, if she is alive. I am going to find out.**

On the beach, Yuanshu sat down in the warm surf with the staff across his knees.

Old Seven came and stood beside him and did not say anything. After a while the tall skeleton bent down, slowly, and took a handful of wet sand from the edge of the water, and held it in his one-fingered hand, and let it run out between the bones.

> **He says that is twelve.**
>
> **He says they are his now. In his book. With the others.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **Ifeoma has been holding for five hours and fourteen minutes. She was supposed to hold for three.**
>
> **She has not stopped.**
