## Chapter 256: Ask Them All

> **Two hours and fifty-eight minutes.**
>
> **I have counted what I can.**
>
> **I cannot count them. I can count how long it would take to ask them.**
>
> **It is longer than we have.**

He stood on the slack thread in the deep water and looked at the cloud.

It went on in every direction. Small dim lights, burning low, drifting out of the open hands of the stopped thing at the center of the web, slowly, the way snow drifts when there is no wind at all. Some were so old they had almost gone out. Some were bright still, new, picked from some world in some orchard only days ago and carried in a curled gray hand along a cable toward a house where nobody lived. None of them were going anywhere.

Nobody had told them they could.

*How long?*

> **If the dead ask them one at a time. Twelve thousand and sixty dead. Each asking one, and then the next.**
>
> **Eleven hundred years.**

He did not say anything.

> **If we do it the way we did the walls of the Mouth. Each asks its nine. And each one who answers is asked to ask nine more.**
>
> **Fourteen rounds. Perhaps fifteen. Each round takes as long as the slowest answer.**
>
> **The walls of the Mouth took twelve rounds and nineteen hours. These are further apart. More of them. Older. Some of them have not been asked anything since before your sun.**
>
> **I do not know how long a round will be.**
>
> **I know there are two hours and fifty-seven minutes.**

---

He did it anyway.

He had not known he was going to, and then he had always known. He stood on the thread with twelve thousand embers round him and sixty more and a burning man gone warm and quiet, held between a captain and a sergeant, and went down into the Death Law. All of it. Fifty point six percent. To the bottom, to the cool dark place where the door was, a little open, the way it was always a little open now.

And opened it.

Not all the way. Not yet. Enough. The way you open the door of a house at night when you hear people outside in the cold, and stand in it with the light behind you, so that they can see there is a door.

"*Ask them,*" he said. "*Each asks its nine. Tell them first. Tell them there's a door, and that it's open, and where it goes. Tell them the threads they came down still go home, if they want home. Tell them they can stay where they are, if they want that. Tell them nobody's going to carry them anywhere ever again unless they ask to be carried.*"

He looked at the cloud.

"*And tell them,*" he said, "*when they've answered, to ask nine more. And tell them that first too.*"

The dead went out.

---

He would remember it as the strangest two hours of his life, and the most beautiful, and he would never be able to say why, and Xiaoyu would write *HE COULDN'T SAY* twice on the wall, and Seed would say that was correct.

Twelve thousand and sixty cold dim sparks going out from the slack thread into a cloud of small lights that went on forever, each one stopping in front of one light, and then another, and then another. Nine. Asking. The Spear Corps, in a knot, going out together, the way they stood round stone No. 7. The First Legion in their rows of nine, which were rows of nine even here. Grid Nine, who had never seen the sea and had now seen the deep water, asking, one of them told Seed, *very politely, because we don't know what language they speak.* The Third Battalion, behind their sergeant, who asked each light he stopped in front of whether it had eaten. The Watch of the Shelf, the color of deep water, slow, the drowned of the coast off Kesse, who asked the coldest and oldest and slowest of the lights, because, Nana Ampofo said through Seed, *they're used to waiting for an answer at the bottom of something.*

And the lights answered.

Not in words. Seed could not translate most of them. Languages from worlds that had been gone since before Blue Star had a sun. Shapes that had never had sounds. But it did not need words. It was not a hard question. *There is a door. There is the way home. There is staying. What do you want?* And the lights answered the way the walls of the Mouth had answered: some of them at once, the way a crowd answers when it has been standing in the cold for a very long time waiting for someone to ask. Some slowly. Some not at all, not yet.

And then the ones who had answered turned, and asked nine more.

---

The first round took nineteen minutes.

He felt it go out through the cloud, nine by nine, the way frost goes across a window: from the slack thread outward, a widening ring of the asked, and at the edge of it, the asked asking. He felt the door take them. The ones who said *door*. Most of them. Most of the old ones. He felt them come in out of the drifting cloud toward him, toward the cool dark place at the bottom of the Death Law, small lights burning low, and go past him on the threshold, one after another, the way the walls of the Mouth had gone past him at the cold fire. Through. Where things went when they were finished.

He felt the ones who said *home*. Fewer. The new ones, mostly. Bright still, picked days ago or a season ago. He felt them turn in the dark, each one, toward a slack thread going out from the stopped web in some direction, and go down it. Not carried. Going. The way a soldier's want had gone along a river to a ferry, when the thing that held it let go. Home. To some world in some orchard where a stone that had marked them stood, quiet now, with nothing at the other end of its thread.

He did not know what was waiting for them there. He hoped something was.

And he felt the ones who said *stay*. A few. Lights that had drifted out of a hand into the deep water and found, for the first time since they were picked, that nobody was carrying them, and wanted to stay exactly where they were, being nobody's, for a while.

*Ask us again,* they said. Seed translated that one. It was the same in every language. *Ask us again tomorrow.*

> **Round two. Twenty-two minutes.**
>
> **Death Law: 50.6% → 51.9%.**

---

Round three. Round four. The ring of the asked going out through the cloud, nine by nine by nine. The door taking them. He stood in it. He did not move. He held it open the way you hold a door when the people coming through it are old and slow and there are a great many of them, and you have told them it is open, and you are not going to let it close in their faces.

Round six. Round seven.

His nose was bleeding. He knew it the way he knew things about his body from a very long way off. On a platform in the Awakening Hall in Lingzhou, a body was lying against a warm black stone with its nose bleeding onto a bright blue jacket, and a hand was flat on its chest, and fifteen hundred people were holding on to the shoulders in front of them so that it would stay where it was.

> **Death Law: 53.2%.**
>
> **One hour and nine minutes.**

Round nine. Round ten.

The rounds were getting longer. Seed had said they would. Further out, the lights were older. Slower. Some of them had been carried for so long that when they were asked, they did not understand the question for a long time, and the dead had to wait in front of them, the way the Watch waited, at the bottom of something, until the answer came up.

> **Round eleven. Thirty-four minutes.**
>
> **Death Law: 54.8%.**
>
> **Twenty-one minutes.**

---

He knew at round twelve that they would not finish.

Seed did not have to tell him. He could feel it: the ring of the asked going out through the cloud, nine by nine, wide now, wider than anything, and beyond it, still, the edge of the cloud. Small dim lights, drifting. Not asked yet. Hundreds of thousands. Millions. He did not know. Seed did not know.

> **Two more rounds. Perhaps three.**
>
> **Each one longer than the last.**
>
> **Twelve minutes.**

*I can hold it longer.*

> **No.**
>
> **You said nine hours. I said nine hours. Your body said nine hours. Lingyue said she would pull.**
>
> **I am not going to let you hold the door open in the deep water until there is nothing on the platform for her to pull back.**

*There's people out there nobody's asked.*

> **I know.**
>
> **I know. I am sorry. I have run it. Nine hours and one minute is the number where there is still a body. Nine hours and four minutes is the number where there is not.**

He stood in the door at the bottom of the Death Law in the deep water, with the ring of the asked going out through the cloud and the edge of the cloud beyond it, and did not know what to do, and for the first time in a very long time could not think of anything at all.

---

> **Huo says...**

Seed stopped. Then went on, slowly.

> **No. Not Huo.**
>
> **Nana Ampofo says.**

He turned his head. Not his head; the shape of it. Out across the deep water, at the far edge of the ring of the asked, where the oldest and slowest of the lights were, a cluster of cold dim embers the color of deep water had stopped asking, and turned, and were looking back at him along the slack thread.

The Watch of the Shelf. Eleven hundred and forty-six of the drowned of the coast off Kesse.

> **Nana Ampofo says: we'll stay.**
>
> **She says: we live at the bottom of the sea. We have for forty years. Some of us for a hundred. The deep water isn't the sea, but it's close enough. It's dark and it's cold and nothing pushes back and you have to wait a long time for anything to answer.**
>
> **She says: we're good at that.**
>
> **She says: we'll keep asking. Nine by nine. The ones at the edge. As long as it takes.**
>
> **She says: you can't hold the door open from here. We know. But it's always a little open now, isn't it? You said. The ones who want it will find it. We'll show them where.**
>
> **She says: ask us again in a year.**

He stood on the thread.

*Nana,* he thought. *It's not your border. It's not even your sea.*

> **She says: Yaw asked first. In the vote about the top. Why does there have to be a top. Remember.**
>
> **She says: this is the same. Why does there have to be a shore.**
>
> **She says: that's not a question, boy. Go home. Your wife's counting.**

---

> **Four minutes.**

He did the only thing he could. He asked them. All eleven hundred and forty-six. Through the net, the slow way, nine by nine, the way they asked everything: whether they wanted this. Whether they were sure. He told them what it would be. The deep water, forever, or until they asked to stop. The cold. The dark. The waiting. Nobody to sing the names to them at night, the way Nana Ampofo sang them at the bottom of the sea off Kesse.

And the answer came back up the net in under a minute, which was faster than the Watch of the Shelf had ever answered anything.

> **Yes.**
>
> **Eleven hundred and forty-six.**
>
> **She says: we'll sing them to each other.**

> **Two minutes.**
>
> **Death Law: 55.9%.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**

*Callum,* he thought. *The captain's got him?*

> **He says: got him. Going down. The sergeant's on the other arm. We'll be slower than you. Go.**

He let the door go back to a little open.

He felt it: the threshold, the cool dark place at the bottom of everything, going back to the way it was always going to be now, ajar, the way a door is left ajar in a house where someone is expected. And out at the edge of the cloud, eleven hundred and forty-six cold dim embers the color of deep water turning back to the lights they had not asked yet, and beginning, very slowly, nine by nine, to ask.

> **One minute.**

---

And a hand on his chest pulled.

He felt it the way he had felt it on the end of the Port Halvard thread: warm, very far away, very strong, a hand flat on a chest a very long way down, closing, and pulling, the way you pull someone out of water. He did not fight it. He let go of the thread, and fell.

Back along it. Through the slack web. Past the stopped knot at the center, where the oldest thing anyone had ever made sat still for the first time since before the gardens, its thousands of hands hanging open on its cables like gloves on a line. Down the cable, past the place where the threads from other orchards came in and ran alongside, slack, quiet. Out of the deep water. Past the end of the arm and the empty edge of the cold fire. Past the branch with its gold bud, which turned. Past the keeper, warm, a third smaller, saying very rough and close *THERE YOU ARE*. A docker's rough hand. An old man who liked the sea. A girl's voice, brisk: *got you, you idiot, got you*.

And down.

---

He opened his eyes on the platform of the Awakening Hall in Lingzhou with blood in his mouth and his wife's face over his.

Her hand was still on his chest. Flat. Pressing. The pale flame in her other palm was blazing the size of a lamp and she did not seem to know it was there. Behind her, Qin Hao, white to the lips, his hands open, the white fire gone out of them because it had all gone up the staff. Su Mingli at the corner of the platform with her arms not folded. Xiaoyu at the foot of the steps with the marker in her hand, uncapped.

And on the bottom tier of seats, Gao Min, with an old mechanical stopwatch from the Bureau in her hand, holding it up so he could see.

*9:00:00.*

"*You said nine hours,*" said Shen Lingyue. Her voice was shaking. She was not crying. Not quite. "*I said I'd pull. I pulled.*"

"*You pulled,*" he said.

"*Did you finish?*"

He lay on the platform against the warm stone with the blue jacket over its shoulder and fifteen hundred people holding each other's shoulders up the aisle and out of the door, and thought about the edge of the cloud, and eleven hundred and forty-six cold dim embers the color of deep water, asking, nine by nine, in the dark, for as long as it took.

"*No,*" he said. "*Somebody else is finishing.*"
