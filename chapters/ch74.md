## Chapter 74: The Stalk

The foot of the stalk was a whirlpool.

He came to it in the last hour before dawn, out of the haze, with the dead behind him, and stopped at the edge of the pale ground, and stared.

Threads. Every thread on Blue Star. They came in out of the gray from every direction, billions of them, silver and fine, drifting low over the pale ground like mist over a lake. And here, in a vast round hollow perhaps two kilometers across, they met. And began to turn.

Slowly at first, at the edges. A great slow spiral, the way water turns at the top of a drain. Then faster, further in. Tighter. Brighter. The threads winding around each other, twisting together, strand on strand on strand, until at the center, the spiral rose up off the ground in a single column of silver light so bright he could not look straight at it. Two kilometers wide at the base. Rising. Narrowing. Straight up into the dark sky, up and up and up, until it touched the center of the vast pale ring that filled half the heavens.

It made no sound.

That was the strangest thing. Billions of threads, turning, twisting, rising, and not a whisper. Only the soft, even, silver light, falling across the pale ground and across the faces of seventy-two dead soldiers and an old spear captain and a boy with a white staff, standing at the edge of the whirlpool at the bottom of the world.

> **Every listening ear on Blue Star.**
>
> **Every hollow. Every turned head.**
>
> **All going up. Into this.**

"*Well,*" said Jiang Lan, very quietly, from the staff. "*There it is. The door.*"

---

"How do I go in?"

"*You don't go in. You go up.*" The dry voice was brisk. "*Put me in the light. Hold on. Be quiet. That's all. I'll do the rest.*" A pause. "*And young man. Once we start, don't stop. Don't look down. Don't think about anything you love. It's all going up these threads, everything anyone loves, and if you think about yours, it'll hear you in the noise.*"

"I understand."

"*No, you don't. Nobody does until they're in it.*" Something softened in her voice. "*Just keep climbing. I'll tell you when we're there.*"

He walked to the edge of the spiral.

The threads flowed past his boots, across the pale ground, silver, turning. He took one more step, and they flowed over his boots. Then around his ankles. Cool. Faintly tingling. Like stepping into a stream.

Behind him, Old Seven stepped in. Then Iron Wall Zhao. Then Hundred-Arrow Lin, a drift of ash in the silver light. Then Sergeant Huo, and seventy-one soldiers of Third Battalion behind him, walking into the whirlpool as if it were nothing. The threads passed through their bones without stopping.

He walked toward the center.

The threads rose as he went. Knee-deep. Waist-deep. Chest. Turning around him faster and faster, brighter and brighter, until he was walking through a storm of silver light, unable to see anything but the staff in his hand and the gray-white glow of the small skull at its top.

Then the ground was gone.

---

He was going up.

Not climbing. Not flying. Rising. The way a leaf rises in a draft. The silver light had closed around him on every side and was carrying him, and he could feel the staff in his hand, warm, pulling, like a dog on a leash pulling its owner up a hill. Below him, gone. Above him, light. All around him, threads, so close now that he could see each one, each separate silver strand turning past his face.

And he could hear them.

Not with his ears. With the other thing. Every thread carried something. Everything its hollow heard. Everything near it. And here, in the stalk, wound together, it was all of it at once.

A woman singing to a baby in a language he didn't know.

Rain on a tin roof.

A man at a kitchen table saying, *I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I didn't mean it.*

Children shouting in a playground.

A nurse, very tired, reading aloud to someone in a hospital bed. *Chapter four. The ship came into the harbor at dawn.*

A teahouse. Silence. Fourteen candles. And then, very soft, a man in a delivery uniform whispering to the boy in the fourth bed on the left, *It's all right. I'm here. You're not alone.*

> **Do not listen.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Do not listen to them.**
>
> **Think about your feet. You do not have feet right now. Think about them anyway.**

He thought about his feet.

He thought about the ice. About the frozen glass of the Wastes. About nineteen days of walking. Left. Right. He let the voices go past him like wind going past a stone.

And he rose.

---

He did not know how long it took.

Minutes. Hours. There was no time inside the stalk. Only the light, and the voices, and the warm pull of the staff, and the dead.

He could see them. Above him and below him and on every side, in the silver storm. Old Seven, rising with his spear grounded against nothing, his flame steady. Iron Wall Zhao, turning slowly, end over end, like a great iron leaf. Hundred-Arrow Lin, a streak of ash. And all around, seventy-two gray shapes rising through the light in a loose formation, like a flock of birds, like the bones of a ship's rigging.

Sergeant Huo, rising beside him, turned his skull toward him once, and lifted his three-fingered hand.

Yuanshu lifted his.

And then, without warning, the silver light around them thinned.

---

He could see out.

Below him. Far, far below.

The stalk ran down beneath him like a rope of light dropped from a great height, narrowing to a silver thread, to a hair, to a point. And around the point, spread out under him, enormous, curving away on every side, was Blue Star.

He was so high.

It was night below. The whole curve of the world was dark, a vast black shape against the blacker sky. And on it, scattered across it like coals in the ashes of a fire, were lights. Cities. Clusters of gold. Lines of gold along coasts. A great bright smudge in the south that must have been the Southern Compact's capital. A long gold necklace far to the east that could only be the coast of Yamato.

And there. On the southern edge of the black emptiness of the Wastes. A thin bright line, sixty kilometers long, glittering. A wall. Lit from end to end with candles.

Lingzhou.

> **Do not.**
>
> **Yuanshu, I know. I know. Do not.**

He didn't. He looked at it for one second, and then he looked away, and he did not think of the girl in the hoodie with the mended flask, or the old woman at the bottom of the teahouse stairs, or the boy in the fourth bed, or a kitchen with a rice cooker on the counter.

He thought about his feet.

The silver light closed over him again. And he rose.

---

"*Nearly there,*" said Jiang Lan.

Her voice was strained. The staff in his hand had gone hot. Not warm. Hot. The gray-white glow of the small skull had brightened until it hurt to look at, and he could feel her, the dry brisk teacher in the bones, pulling with everything she had.

"*It's heavy, carrying a living one. I'd forgotten. Xuanming was skinnier.*" A sound that might have been a laugh. "*Keep quiet. Keep climbing. We're at the edge.*"

The light changed.

It was not silver anymore. It was white. Then a hard pale white, like frost on glass. And then, all at once, there was no light around him at all.

He was in darkness. Total. Cold. The threads were still there, he could feel them, all around, rising past him. But they no longer glowed. And there was a sound now, for the first time since he had entered the stalk. A vast, low, slow sound, felt rather than heard. Like breathing. Like a heartbeat. Like something unimaginably huge, asleep.

> **We are in the lens.**
>
> **Inside it.**
>
> **This is the black water. The pupil of the eye.**
>
> **It is not looking at us. It is looking at the ridge. At Grid Nine. At three Monarchs waiting in the dark for a sound.**
>
> **It does not know we are here.**

The pulse came.

He felt it pass through him, a great slow throb going down through the black water and down the stalk and out along every thread on Blue Star at once. Every hollow. Every turned head. *Thump.*

And in the stillness after it, the staff pulled him up, one last time, hard. And the darkness tore open above him like the surface of a lake.

---

He came up into the field.

The same field. Flat and endless under a sky with no sun, lit by a soft even light from nowhere. Smooth pale ground, faintly warm. And growing out of it in long straight rows that ran away to every horizon, the soft gold columns of light. Billions of them. Each one the size of a person. Each one a kept mind, standing perfectly still, eyes closed. Calm as pressed flowers.

He was standing on the pale ground. He had feet again. He could feel the warmth through his boots.

Behind him, one after another, the dead came up out of the ground the way swimmers come up out of the sea. Old Seven. Iron Wall Zhao. Hundred-Arrow Lin. Sergeant Huo. Seventy-one soldiers of the Third Battalion. They stood up on the pale ground among the gold columns, in rows, and looked around.

The staff in his hand had gone cool again. The glow of the small skull had dimmed to almost nothing.

"*There,*" whispered Jiang Lan. Her dry voice was very faint. "*Told you I'd tell you.*"

"Are you all right?"

"*I'm a staff. I'm always all right.*" A pause. "*I need to rest. A little. Don't open the other door while I'm napping.*"

"I won't."

"*I know.*"

The glow went down to a single faint point, like a pilot light. And went quiet.

---

He stood in the field.

It did not know he was there. He could feel that. The vast attention of the thing was everywhere around him, in the light and the ground and the endless rows, the way the sea is everywhere around a fish. But it was turned away. Downward. Toward a ridge at the bottom of the world.

He was a stone in its river. A quiet place it had not noticed.

> **Billions.**
>
> **We cannot search them one by one, Yuanshu. Not in sixty days. Not in sixty years.**

*She's here.*

> **She is here. Somewhere.**
>
> **But last time, she found you.**
>
> **This time, we have to find her.**

He looked out over the endless field. The rows ran away to every horizon, gold and still and silent.

And then Old Seven, beside him, did something strange.

The old spear captain raised his skull, and turned it, slowly, the way a dog turns its head toward a sound too high for people to hear. And held it there. Pointing. Not straight ahead. Off to the left, and far away, across a thousand rows.

His gray flame flickered once.

> **He hears something.**
>
> **Not a mind. Not a voice.**
>
> **Yuanshu, the dead can hear what the dead hear.**
>
> **Somewhere over there, someone is very, very quiet.**
>
> **So quiet that only the dead could notice.**
