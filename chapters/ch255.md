## Chapter 255: Told

The light was the best thing he had ever felt, and he went into it dim.

He had thought he knew how. He had held the door in his chest closed for a week, on a hotel bed, on a gold road over the sea, in a banking hall at three in the morning. He had thought that was holding. It was not. That had been holding a door shut in a quiet room. This was holding it shut inside a fire.

Callum Ward's light came through him like weather. White and gold and every color, everything at once, the way it had come through him on the end of the north pier when the docker opened him; except that then the light had been his own, and now it was another man's, burning, being used up, and it poured over him and round him and against the door in his chest from the outside, the way a flood pushes against a door from the street.

*Let me in,* it said. Not in words. In the shape of wanting. *Open. Burn with me. It's the best thing there is.*

> **I am here.**
>
> **I am in the door. With my back to it.**
>
> **It is very bright on my back.**
>
> **Keep going.**

He kept going.

---

Inside the fire, it was quiet.

He had not expected that. Outside, the roar of it, the light, the vast slow attention of the thing behind it looking. Inside, at the center, where a man was burning with his arms out, it was quiet. The way it is quiet at the center of a bonfire, if you could stand there: all the noise going outward.

And a man. Not a shape in the fire. A man, as near as anything was a man out here. Young. Broad in the shoulders. A dock worker's arms, held up over his head, still, after six weeks, as if there were a hammer in them.

He turned his head.

*...there you are,* said Callum Ward.

*Here I am.*

*...you're not burning.*

*No.*

*...how? I can feel it. It's all over you. It wants you to.*

*I know.* He held on. *Somebody's holding the door for me. And I keep thinking what I'd be burning for.*

Callum looked at him. Up close, out of the fire, his face was not a face, quite. But it was enough of one.

*...yeah,* he said. *...yeah. I've had six weeks to think about that.*

---

He held out his right hand.

He did not have a hand. Neither of them did. But he had the shape of one, the way the dead have the shape of a pick or a spear or an arm with one finger and a thumb. And in it, closed, the small gray knot the size of a fingernail that a girl of seventeen had written along in square black capitals and a small pale thing with all its limbs out had tied the way water ties itself round a stone.

*It's true,* he said. *My sister wrote it. What she writes is true. That's what she asked for. Nobody knows how it works.*

Callum looked at it.

*...what's it say?*

*The house is empty. You can stop.*

The man in the fire did not say anything for a moment.

*...the house is empty,* he said slowly. *...that's it? That's the thing? That's what it doesn't know?*

*It's been carrying what it picks to an empty house since before the gardens. Before anything. The ones who made it stopped, and nobody told it. There was nobody in it to tell.* He held out the knot. *You're the only thing it's ever looked at.*

Callum Ward took the knot.

---

He felt the moment it went from one hand to the other, the way you feel a weight change hands on a ferry, the way Huo had felt his arm go to his captain in a courtyard among potted plants. Then it was Callum's. Closed in the shape of a dock worker's hand, inside a fire, held up in front of the face of the oldest thing in the deep water.

*...it's warm,* said Callum.

*Yes.*

*...it's not bright. It's warm. Like a cup of tea. Like Mum's kitchen.* He looked at his closed hand. *...that's what true feels like? I never knew.*

Behind him, beyond the fire, the attention of the thing at the center of the web had not moved. Vast. Patient. Not warm, like the Collector's. Not anything. The attention of a thing with nobody in it, looking, for six days, at the only thing it had ever looked at, and not knowing what it was looking at.

*Callum,* said Yuanshu. *Whenever you want.*

---

Callum Ward turned round in the fire.

Slowly. The way a man turns round on a dock when someone has shouted his name from the other end of the quay. Out of the center of the light, toward the outside of it, toward the vast slow knot where ten thousand cables came together and every mark from every stone in every orchard in the deep water was read. Toward the face.

He was the brightest thing there had ever been in front of it. He did not try not to be. He turned round in his own light and faced it, an F from the docks at Port Halvard, twenty-five, who had come in through a window at night with a sledgehammer because a stone had told him what he was.

He lifted his arms.

The way he had lifted them in the square hall with the hammer in them. Over his head. Except that this time there was no hammer. There was a small gray knot the size of a fingernail, closed in the shape of his hand, held up in the light.

*Oi,* said Callum Ward.

To the oldest thing anyone had ever made.

*Oi. You. Look at this.*

He opened his hand.

---

It looked.

He felt it. Everyone felt it. Twelve thousand embers on the cable at the edge of the busy dark and sixty more and a boy who was not an ember with a door in his chest held shut by a sack: they all felt the moment the attention of the thing at the center of the web, which had glanced at a billion marks a second since before the gardens, stopped glancing.

And read.

Not a glance. Not the way it read the marks coming up the threads from the stones, one after another, a finger running down a column. The way you read a letter that has your name on the outside, that you did not expect, in a hand you have never seen. All of it. Slowly. Once.

THE HOUSE IS EMPTY.

YOU CAN STOP.

And it was true. That was the thing. It was not words on a thread. A girl had written it so that nobody could say afterwards it was not there, and a small pale thing from the world that asks had tied it the way you tie something to a branch for a tree that is too old to answer, and a man nobody had ever picked had carried it in his hand and held it up. It was true the way the wall was true when Seed read it. It was being told by someone who was there.

The thing with nobody in it read it, and for the first time since before the gardens, it knew something.

---

The web stopped.

Not slowly. All at once. He felt it go through the busy dark the way a held breath goes out of a room. Every mark going up every thread in every cable stopped arriving, because the thing they were arriving at had stopped reading them. Ten thousand cables. Ten thousand threads in each. Every stone in every orchard in the deep water sending a mark up a thread, and nothing at the other end. The hum that had been everywhere, under everything, for five hours, the traffic of marks, the city at night, went quiet.

And the hands stopped.

All of them. Every vast gray hand on every cable in the deep water, the ones going out with their fingers open and the ones coming back with their fingers curled round something shining. They stopped where they were. On the threads. Still. The way a man stops halfway across a room when he has suddenly remembered that the person he was carrying something to is not there.

> **...it understood.**
>
> **I do not know how. There is nobody in it. But it read something true, and it understood.**
>
> **The house is empty.**
>
> **Nobody is waiting for what it carries.**

And then, all over the deep water, everywhere, on every cable, at once, the hands opened.

---

He would try to tell Xiaoyu afterward, for the wall, and would not be able to, and she would write *HE COULDN'T SAY* in small capitals under it, and that would be truer than anything he could have said.

Lights.

Out of every hand in the deep water that had been coming home full. Out of the palms, as the fingers uncurled. Small lights, dim, burning low, the way the cousins had burned in the Low Fire. Picked. From worlds he did not know the names of, carried for longer than he could understand toward a house where nobody lived. Hundreds from one hand. Thousands from another. And there were thousands of hands.

They came out into the dark like seeds out of a burst pod. Like sparks going up a chimney. Like the lanterns the people of Lingzhou had lit in their windows on the night the island left, that someone very far away might have seen and thought were stars.

They drifted.

Out of the open hands into the busy dark, which was not busy any more, slowly, a great slow cloud of small dim lights coming apart in every direction across the deep water, not going anywhere. Not being carried. For the first time since they were picked, nobody's.

> **I cannot count them.**
>
> **I am trying. I cannot.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I cannot count them.**

It was the first time Seed had ever said that.

---

And in front of him, the hand that held the lantern opened too.

He saw it from inside the fire. The vast gray fingers round the burning man, the ones that had held him up to the face of the thing for six days, uncurling. Slowly. Letting go. The way you open your hand to let a moth out of a window.

Callum Ward fell.

Out of the hand. Out of the front of the face of the thing that had stopped. Still burning, still white and gold and every color, the brightest thing in the deep water, falling away from the knot at the center of the web into the dark, with his arms still up over his head and his hand open, empty now, the mark gone out of it into the thing that had read it.

And the dark caught him.

Not the dark. The dead. Twelve thousand embers had gone out along the cable and onto the fingers of the hand while it held the lantern, the way Old Seven had said, a line of the dead standing on the fingers of a glove that could not feel them, waiting for the moment to open it. And it had opened itself. And they were there, under it, when the lantern fell.

Two of them first. A cold steady ember he would have known among a million, and a short square one with the shape of a pick on its shoulder. Side by side, the way they had stood on the north wall in the Black Tide. They caught the burning man between them. One by each arm. The way two soldiers carry a third off a wall.

> **He says: got him.**
>
> **Huo says: he's hot, sir.**
>
> **He says: he's been burning for six weeks, sergeant. Of course he's hot.**

---

The fire went out.

Not all at once. The way a fire goes out when the thing that was feeding it stops: slowly, from the outside in, the white going gold and the gold going orange and the orange going red, and the light that had been the only light in the deep water for six weeks dimming, and dimming, and then just a man. Held between two dead soldiers on a thread in the dark. Not burning. Warm. Like a cup of tea.

*...oh,* said Callum Ward.

His voice was not at the bottom of a well any more. It was just a voice. Small. Tired. Surprised.

*...oh, it's stopped. It's stopped. I'm not... I'm not bright.*

*No.*

*...it doesn't feel like anything.* A long pause. *...that's good. I think. I think that's good.* He was quiet. *...I'm so tired.*

*I know. Rest. They've got you.*

*...is Mum still holding?*

*Yes.*

*...tell her I told it,* said Callum Ward. *...tell her I held it up and I said oi and it looked. Tell her I told it what it was.*

*I'll tell her.*

*...tell her it's...* He stopped. *...tell her I want to go home.*

---

He came out of the light.

He had not noticed he was still in it. The fire had gone out round him and he was standing on a thread in the deep water, dim, a drop of water on a string, with the door in his chest still shut and a sack standing in it with its back to where the light had been, and he realized that he could feel the sack shaking.

> **...it is gone.**
>
> **The light.**
>
> **I did not turn round.**
>
> **I would like you to know that I did not turn round.**

*I know,* he thought. *Thank you.*

> **You are welcome.**
>
> **I would like to sit down now. In the quiet place. On the first page. Where the hundred and thirty-eight were, and the eleven.**
>
> **Just for a minute.**

*Go on.*

He stood on the thread in the dark and looked out at the deep water.

The busy dark was not busy any more. The web was still. Ten thousand cables going out in every direction from the vast slow knot at the center, slack, quiet, nothing going up them. Thousands of vast gray hands hanging open on them, still, like gloves left on a line. And everywhere, between them, in every direction as far as there was any direction, drifting, a cloud of small dim lights.

Picked. Carried. Let go.

Nobody's.

And somewhere very far away along one of the slack threads, the long way, faint, he thought he felt something. Warm. Patient. An attention he knew, resting for a moment on the place where the oldest thing anyone had ever made had stopped.

> *...thank you,* said the Collector. *I saw.*

Then it was gone.

> **Six hours and two minutes.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **They are all still out there. Every one. Drifting.**
>
> **Nobody has asked them anything.**
