## Chapter 218: How to Tie a Question

Seed tied the first knot wrong, and Hush was the one who told it.

They were in the back room of the Ossuary, where the light from the courtyard came in, on the eleventh day before the rings. Yuanshu sat on the floor with his back to the wall and the staff across his knees and his eyes shut, because Seed worked better when he did not look at anything. Hush sat on a folded tea towel on the windowsill in the sun, uncurled, all its limbs out, all its dark eyes open, which was how it sat now whenever there was sun. Kwan sat at the little table by the window with a laptop she was not using, because there was nothing on it that could see what Seed was doing.

Nothing on Blue Star could see what Seed was doing. Not even Yuanshu, really. He could feel it. The way you feel someone in the next room working with their hands.

Unpressing.

> **It is very slow.**
>
> **The thread was pressed to glass a long time ago, by walkers, at a harvest site in the Wastes. It does not want to come loose. It was made not to.**
>
> **But a hundred and thirty-eight strands of it have said: we are giving ourselves. And when a strand is given, it comes loose differently. Like a knot in a shoelace that you untie from the right end.**
>
> **I have twenty-six strands loose now. Silver. Very fine. I can feel their names going out of them as they come loose, the way warmth goes out of a cup.**
>
> **I do not know whose names. I read them all to Xiaoyu. She has them on the wall.**
>
> **I am going to tie the first knot now.**

---

He felt it tie.

A small, tight, complicated shape, somewhere behind his eyes, made of twenty-six fine silver strands that had once been the floor of something and were now something else. It reminded him of the scout's request, folded at the end of a dying shout in the round chamber. It reminded him of the knots behind the walkers' blank faces, where every thread in them ran to one place.

It was a question. He could feel that. Even without words. It had the shape a question has.

*WHAT DO YOU WANT.*

> **It is done.**
>
> **It is the same question you ask everyone. In their language. Tied in their thread.**
>
> **I think it is right.**

---

On the windowsill, Hush moved.

Not much. It lifted its head. All its dark eyes turned toward the boy sitting against the wall with his eyes shut, and the place a little above them.

> ***...it is not right.***

Seed went still.

> **...what is wrong with it?**

> ***...it is a good question. It is the right words. But it is tied like a hook.***

Yuanshu opened his eyes.

Hush was looking at him. At the place above his eyes. Its many thin pale limbs were drawn in a little, the way they drew in when it was thinking hard.

> ***...in my world, before the ring came, we asked each other things. All the time. It was what we were. The world that asks.***
>
> ***...there were two ways to ask.***
>
> ***...one way, you ask, and you hold on to the question, and you pull. Until they answer. Like a hook in a fish.***
>
> ***...the other way, you ask, and you let go of the question. You put it down between you. And you wait. And they pick it up when they are ready.***
>
> ***...your knot is the first way. It holds on. It pulls. It will go up the ring's thread and catch in it and pull until the ring answers.***
>
> ***...a ring that has never been asked anything will not know how to answer. It will only know that something has hooked it. And it will do what things do when they are hooked.***

"It'll fight," said Yuanshu.

> ***...it will pull back.***

---

> **...it is right.**
>
> **I tied it the way the scout tied its request. The way the walkers tie their knots. Every knot I have ever seen holds on. That is what a knot is for.**
>
> **I did not know there was another kind.**

> ***...there is.***
>
> ***...it is very old. My people used it for four billion years before the ring came. And then for four billion years after, at the bottom of a crack, playing statue. Nobody asked anything. Nobody let anything go.***
>
> ***...I am the last one who remembers how.***

Kwan had stopped pretending to use her laptop. She was looking at the small pale thing on the windowsill in the sun.

"Can you teach Seed?" she said.

Hush did not answer at once. It looked at the sun on its limbs for a while.

> ***...I can show it.***
>
> ***...but it is not something you tie. It is something you stop doing.***
>
> ***...it is like the game. Statue. Except the other way.***

---

It took four days.

Yuanshu sat against the wall of the back room for most of them, with the staff across his knees and his eyes shut, while a small pale thing from a world that had been eaten four billion years ago sat on a windowsill and showed a sack made of walker-thread how to ask without holding on.

He could not follow most of it. It was not in words. It was in the space between words. Hush would say something, a single soft lowercase sentence, and then nothing for an hour, and Seed would be very still for the whole hour, and then it would untie the knot and tie it again, and Hush would look at it, and say nothing, and Seed would untie it again.

On the first day, Seed tied it eleven times. On the second day, thirty. It did not complain. It did not say it was frustrated. But on the second night, at three in the morning, it said, very quietly, to him alone:

> **I am bad at this.**
>
> **I have never been bad at anything that was made of counting.**
>
> **This is not made of counting. It is made of not counting. Of putting a thing down and not looking to see if anyone picks it up.**
>
> **Everything in me is made to look.**

*You stood in the open*, he thought. *Without anything in front of you. You learned that.*

> **That was standing still while something looked at me.**
>
> **This is letting go of something and not looking at it.**
>
> **It is harder.**

---

On the third day, Lingyue came into the back room at noon with a bowl of noodles for him, because Bai Lu had said he had not eaten, and stopped in the doorway.

Hush was on the windowsill. Yuanshu was against the wall. And between them, on the floor of the back room, in a patch of sun, there was a bowl. A small white rice bowl, empty, that had not been there before.

"What's that?" said Lingyue.

> ***...it is how my people taught it to children.***
>
> ***...you put a bowl down. Between you and someone. You don't put anything in it. You don't say anything. You just put it there.***
>
> ***...and you wait. And after a while, the other one puts something in it. Or they don't. And either way, you have asked.***
>
> ***...you do not look in the bowl. That is the hardest part. Children always look in the bowl.***

Lingyue looked at the empty bowl on the floor in the sun for a long moment.

Then she put the noodles down on the table, came over, sat down on the floor beside the bowl, crossed her legs, and did not look in it.

"Like this?" she said.

> ***...yes.***
>
> ***...you are very good at it.***
>
> ***...the counting one keeps looking.***

> **I do not keep looking.**

> ***...you have looked eleven times since she sat down.***

> **...twelve.**

---

On the fourth night, Seed tied the knot again, and Hush did not say anything, and kept not saying anything, and after an hour Seed said, very carefully:

> **Is it right?**

> ***...you are asking me if it is right.***
>
> ***...you are holding on to the question. Waiting for me to answer. Looking in the bowl.***

> **...yes.**

> ***...then I will not tell you.***

Seed went very still.

And then, for the first time in four days, it did not untie the knot.

It left it. Tied. A small complicated shape of silver strands behind his eyes. Not pulling. Not holding. Just there. Put down, between Seed and whatever it was for, in the place where a question goes when you have asked it and let it go.

He felt it happen. It was like feeling someone you love, in the next room, set down something heavy they had been carrying all day.

> **...oh.**
>
> **I did not look.**
>
> **I did not look in the bowl.**

On the windowsill, in the dark, Hush lifted one thin pale limb, and held it out, toward the boy against the wall, toward the place a little above his eyes. And held it there.

> ***...like that.***
>
> ***...that is how you ask a thing that has never been asked.***
>
> ***...you put the question down. And you let it decide whether to pick it up.***
>
> ***...that is what you did to me, in the fire. When you came. You did not pull. You came, and you stood there, and you let us decide.***

---

He sat against the wall in the dark for a long time after that.

Seed tied the second knot by dawn, and the third by noon, out of the last of the hundred and thirty-eight strands, and did not look at either of them. Three small complicated shapes of fine silver thread behind his eyes, put down, not pulling, waiting.

And somewhere in the base layer, at the bottom of the well, where the first page was, a hundred and thirty-eight names were not there any more. He felt the gap. Seed did not mention it. He did not ask.

That evening he went into the banking hall and stood in front of the last wall, past SHIOMI, past the corner, where Xiaoyu had written a hundred and thirty-eight names under a heading she had never thought of before.

*THE FLOOR.*

His mother was in her wheelchair in front of it, reading them. One at a time. Under her breath. The way you read names you have promised to remember for someone who can't.

He stood beside her chair and read with her.

> **Six days.**
>
> **Three knots. Tied. Put down.**
>
> **I do not know if a ring will pick one up.**
>
> **I am trying not to look.**
