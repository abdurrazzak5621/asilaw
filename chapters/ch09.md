## Chapter 9: Bonefield Hollow (Part 2)

The canyon stood up.

There was no other way to say it. At the narrow end of Bonefield Hollow, where the walls rose highest, the bones began to come loose from the cliffs. Ribs. Spines. Skulls. Whole fused slabs of skeleton the size of buildings. They didn't fall. They *flowed,* downward and inward, grinding against each other with a long, deep roar like a glacier sliding into the sea. The ground shook. Dabao's fire jumped and scattered sparks.

And in the center of the canyon, the bones gathered into a shape.

A body the size of a hill. Seven necks rising out of it, each built from a hundred spines locked end to end, swaying like reeds. Seven heads at the top of the necks, and each head a different skull: a wolf's, a lizard's, a long-beaked thing, a crocodile's, things with no name, their jaws hinged together with fused ribs. Bone dust poured off them in white streams.

In the center of its chest, behind a cage of ribs, something pulsed.

A crystal the size of a car, glowing a sick, bruised violet. It beat. Slowly. Like a heart.

> **Grade-two dungeon boss. Bone Hydra. Equivalent to Level 30.**
>
> **It is not alive. It was never alive.**
>
> **It is a rift core, using the dead of this canyon as a body.**
>
> **It is doing what you do. Badly.**

The seven heads swung toward the camp. Together. In perfect unison.

"Bro," said Dabao, very quietly, getting to his feet and reaching for his shield. "That's a lot of heads."

"Seven," said Shen Lingyue, drawing her sword.

"I was counting them emotionally."

---

The first head came down like a crane falling off a building.

Dabao threw himself underneath it with the shield braced on his shoulder. The impact drove him into the gravel up to his knees. The sound was enormous, a crack and a boom together, and white dust exploded outward in a ring.

He didn't move back an inch.

"*UNBROKEN!*" he screamed, mostly at himself.

Shen Lingyue went up the second neck as if it were a staircase. Three steps, four, running up the slope of bone with her sword trailing a ribbon of light, and at the top she cut. The lizard skull came off in a shower of splinters and tumbled down the hydra's side.

The neck grew a new one in four seconds.

The bones flowed up out of the canyon wall, across the gap, and locked together into a new skull, jaws already snapping.

> **It regenerates from the canyon. Every bone in Bonefield Hollow is available to it.**
>
> **Approximately one hundred and forty million fragments.**
>
> **You cannot win a fight of attrition here.**

*Then I won't fight it with attrition.*

In the middle of the chaos, Yuanshu stood still.

And closed his eyes.

---

He could feel the core's grip on the bones.

It was like seeing the strings on a puppet. Millions of thin violet threads ran out from the beating crystal in the hydra's chest, into every bone of its body, and out further, into the canyon walls, into the floor, into everything. Every thread carried the same command, over and over, hammering, like a scream on a loop.

*MOVE.*

*MOVE.*

*MOVE.*

> **Analyzing the core.**
>
> **It does not use the Law of Death. It uses force. It drives a command into remains that have no will to refuse it.**
>
> **Your Calamity Raise is different. You call to the echo. The echo answers. It is a conversation.**
>
> **This is a shout.**

*Can I take them away from it?*

> **Not by shouting louder. That core has far more raw power than you.**
>
> **These are beast remains. They have echoes, but no wills. They cannot say yes or no.**
>
> **But they have the shape of death. Every echo wants to go home. Back to rest.**
>
> **The core is holding them back from that.**
>
> **If you offered them rest instead of a command...**

Yuanshu understood.

He didn't reach for the bones. He reached past them, into the gray knot of the Death Law that Seed had shown him in the dark. The river that wanted to run back to its source.

And he opened a door.

> **Simulating. Forty thousand runs.**
>
> **Death Law: 18.6% → 20.3%.**
>
> **There. I see it.**

A thin current of gray light ran out of him and down into the canyon floor.

It did not command anything. It didn't push. It simply *was.* The shape of rest. The shape of lying down at the end of a long day. The bones nearest to his feet felt it the way a tired body feels a bed.

And they let go.

---

One of the hydra's seven necks sagged.

Not broken. Abandoned. The spines that made it simply stopped holding onto one another. They slid apart, gently, and fell into a heap on the canyon floor, and lay still. The violet thread that had been driving them snapped like a wire cut with pliers.

The hydra screamed with its six remaining mouths. The sound shook dust off the walls.

> **It's working.**
>
> **They chose rest over command. Every time.**

"Lingyue," Yuanshu said, without raising his voice. "The core. Ten seconds."

She didn't ask what he meant. She was already running.

He pushed the gray current outward, wider.

The second neck fell. The third. With each one, the hydra shrank, and the walls of the canyon stopped feeding it. The fourth head struck at him, desperate, jaws wide, and Old Seven stepped into its path, drove his spear up through its eye socket, and *held* it there, braced, while Dabao came in from the side and slammed his shield into its jaw with his whole weight.

The fifth neck fell.

The rib cage over the chest swung open like a gate, and the violet crystal lay bare.

Shen Lingyue came down out of the sky.

She had gone up the last neck before it collapsed and jumped from the top of it, and now she fell, both hands on her sword, point down, hair streaming, like a star falling out of a clear night.

The whole canyon went white.

*Dawnbreaker.*

The rift core shattered.

---

Every violet thread in Bonefield Hollow snapped at the same instant.

The hydra's body came apart in a long rolling avalanche of bone that thundered down the canyon floor for half a minute, roaring, throwing up a wall of white dust that rolled over all of them and blotted out the sky.

Then it settled.

And then it was very quiet.

---

They stood in the dust.

Dabao sat down where he was, shield across his knees, breathing like a blacksmith's bellows. Shen Lingyue landed lightly on a fallen rib and lowered her sword. She was trembling, very slightly, all over, from the effort. She pretended she wasn't.

> **Level 18 → Level 22.**
>
> **Death Law: 20.3%.**
>
> **New understanding: Return. The dead that someone else controls by force can be released.**
>
> **This matters. Any Necromancer, any rift core, any beast that raises the dead by force: you can take its army apart without fighting it.**

Yuanshu walked through the settling dust to the center of the bone pile, where the core had been.

Something glinted in the fragments. He crouched and picked it up.

A shard of the shattered crystal, no bigger than a coin. Still faintly warm. And burned into its flat face was a mark: a ring of tiny open mouths, dozens of them, all screaming.

> **That mark was not made by the core.**
>
> **It was made by something else. Something that branded it.**
>
> **The core was not wild. It was placed here.**

He closed his hand around it and put it in his pocket.

---

On the long walk out, Shen Lingyue walked beside him for a while without saying anything.

At the mouth of the canyon, in the gold light of the second morning, she stopped.

"You're not what the news says," she said.

"What does the news say?"

"That you're a monster." She looked at him sideways. "You're worse." She considered the word. "You're *careful.*"

She walked off toward the road before he could answer.

Dabao caught up with him, beaming through a face full of bone dust. "Bro. Bro. Did you hear that? I think she likes you."

"She said I'm worse than a monster."

"Exactly," said Dabao, with total confidence. "That's how the Shen family flirts."

---

Su Mingli was waiting at the mouth of the canyon, sitting on the hood of a black car with a thermos of tea.

She looked at the dust still rising off the far end of the hollow, where a hydra the size of a hill had stood that morning. Then she looked at her watch.

"Two days," she said. "And four hours."

Yuanshu held up his panel. *Level 22.*

Then he took the shard out of his pocket and dropped it into her open hand, and watched her face change as she saw the mark.

"Your mother," Su Mingli said slowly, turning the shard in the light, "would have been insufferable about the time." She closed her fingers over it. "And she would have wanted to know who's been planting cores in our canyons."
