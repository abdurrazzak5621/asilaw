## Chapter 263: Position Four Thousand and Eleven

The Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle did not speak again for nine hours, and when she did, she asked to be taught.

> ***How do you do it.***

It came down the keeper's eye at four in the afternoon, onto the north wall, where he had not moved from his old place since the morning. Lingyue had brought him tea at noon and gone back down. Old Seven stood at the top of the stairs. The crinkle hung in the sky in the north, the size of a building, and had not moved either.

> ***Ask. How do you ask a core.***
>
> ***I have read every protocol the Federation has. There are eleven thousand pages on the maintenance of fold-cores. Burn rates, pressure, term limits, the honoring ceremony. There is not one line on speaking to them.***
>
> ***It is not done. I was told it could not be done.***
>
> ***I would like to speak to my sister.***

He looked up at the sky.

*You can't do it from the bridge,* he said, through Seed, through the keeper. *I don't think. Seed can carry your voice. But it'll be better if you're there. Where she is. Where you could touch the wall, if you wanted.*

The silence lasted a long time.

> ***...second array. Nineteenth wall. Position four thousand and eleven.***
>
> ***I have never been in the arrays. Nobody from the bridge goes to the arrays. The engineers go. They are graded C.***
>
> ***I am going now.***

---

He did not see it. He could not have; it was two hundred and twenty meters long and hanging in the sky over the end of the world. But Seed saw some of it, through the keeper's eye and through the cores themselves, forty-one thousand six hundred of them, who were all, now, a little awake in a way they had not been, because someone had asked them something. Seed said they were passing it along. The way the net passed things along. Each to its nine.

*She's coming,* they said. *The captain. Down the long corridor. Past the C's. She's crying. She's stopped crying. She's coming to the second array.*

A young woman in a gray uniform with gold at the collar, SSS, the third daughter of a House, walking down a corridor nobody from the bridge walked down, between walls that hummed. Into the second array. A vast curved room, like the inside of a hive, every wall a lattice of small dark crystal cells, each one the size of a person curled up, each one burning, low, the way a lamp is turned down to last the night.

The nineteenth wall.

Position four thousand and eleven.

She stopped in front of it.

> **She is there.**
>
> **She has put her hand on the cell.**
>
> **She cannot see in. The cells are dark. She can feel it burning. Warm. The way the stone is warm.**
>
> **Maelis knows she is there.**

---

He carried them.

There was no other word for it. Seed was the bridge, and the keeper was the bridge before that, and the staff in his hands was the bridge before that, and he sat on the cold stone of the north wall of Lingzhou with his back to the parapet and carried two sisters' voices between a crystal cell and the hand on it, a few hundred kilometers up in the dark, in a language he had not known that morning.

> ***...Maelis?***

> ***...Iselde.***
>
> ***...you got tall.***

The young woman on the other end made a sound.

> ***...how can you know that. You can't see.***

> ***...I can feel your hand. It's higher than it used to be. When you were fifteen you had to reach up to put your hand on my door. At the House. When I was being prepared. You came every night. You put your hand on the door and said: they say it's an honor. And I said: I know. And you said: I'll come and see you at the Festival. In a hundred years.***
>
> ***...you're early.***

> ***They told me you volunteered.***

> ***...I did. That's the law. They ask you on the morning. Do you accept the honor. And you say yes, because you're twelve, and an E, and everyone's watching, and Mother's in the box.***
>
> ***...I said yes.***
>
> ***...I didn't know what it was.***

He did not say anything. He carried.

> ***They told me you couldn't hear. They told me a core doesn't hear. It just burns. It's not a person any more. It's a candle.***

> ***...I've heard everything. For eleven years. Every fold. Every time the ship goes through the dark, we burn harder and it's like being held under water and set on fire at the same time, and then it stops, and we lie here, and we hear the engineers talk. They talk about their grades. They talk about who's getting promoted.***
>
> ***...I heard you come aboard. Three years ago. When they made you captain. The C's were talking about it. The third daughter of Anthos. SSS. Listener-Captain at twenty-three. They were so proud.***
>
> ***...I was so proud.***
>
> ***...I couldn't tell you.***

---

The silence on the other end went on for a long time. Seed did not count it. Or it did, and did not tell him.

Then the Listener-Captain said, very quietly:

> ***What do you want.***

It was not a captain's voice. It was not an SSS's voice. It was the voice of a girl of fifteen, standing outside a door in a House at night, with her hand flat on it.

> ***...nobody's ever asked me that.***

> ***I know. I'm asking. The boy on the world says you have to say first what you'll do with the answer. I don't know what I'll do with it. I'm saying that first. Maelis. What do you want?***

> ***...out.***
>
> ***...I want out. I want to see your face. I want to see the sky. I don't care if it's the Federation sky or this one or no sky. I just want to see something that isn't the inside of a wall.***
>
> ***...that's what I want.***
>
> ***...is that all right?***

---

The unpressing took four hours.

He did not see it. He would not have wanted to. Seed told him some of it, afterward, because he asked, and because Seed said it had learned that it was better to know what a thing cost than not.

There was a procedure. Of course there was. Eleven thousand pages on the maintenance of fold-cores, and a section near the end, which the C's in the arrays used once every hundred years, for the Festival, on the day each term ended: how to release a core. It took a crew of six and a supervisor and a thing like a cradle that came down from the ceiling of the array on chains. Iselde Varr-Anthos did not have a crew of six. She had herself, and an engineer, a C, a man in his fifties named Torvin who had worked the arrays for thirty years and who came down the long corridor when he heard what the captain was doing and stood in the doorway of the second array for a moment, looking at her with her hand on the nineteenth wall.

Then he took off his jacket and went to get the cradle.

> **He said: it's not done, Captain.**
>
> **She said: I know.**
>
> **He said: I've wanted to do it for thirty years.**

---

At nine minutes past eight in the evening, Blue Star time, in the second array of a folded ship in the sky above the keeper's field, a crystal cell at position four thousand and eleven on the nineteenth wall went dark, and cold, and opened.

And something came out of it into the cradle.

> **...she is very small.**

Seed said it quietly. It had been quiet for an hour.

> **Yuanshu. She is twelve.**
>
> **The pressing stops the body. She went in at twelve. She has been in the wall for eleven years. She has heard everything for eleven years. She is twenty-three inside.**
>
> **And she is twelve.**

He put his hand over his face, on the north wall, in the dark.

> **She is breathing. Torvin has a blanket. Iselde is holding her.**
>
> **She has opened her eyes.**
>
> **She is looking at her sister's face.**

And then, through the keeper's eye, down the gold thread, onto the north wall of Lingzhou, very small, in a girl's voice, a twelve-year-old's voice, saying something for the first time in eleven years out loud and not through a wall:

> ***...you got old.***

And her sister, the Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle, SSS, the third daughter of a House, laughing and crying at once on the floor of the second array with a child in her arms:

> ***I'm twenty-six.***
>
> ***...that's old.***

---

She came back to the bridge at midnight with Maelis asleep in her arms in Torvin's blanket, and called him.

He had not moved from the wall. Lingyue had come up at ten and sat down beside him and put her hand on his arm and not said anything. Old Seven at the top of the stairs. The stars over the north wall of Lingzhou, and the small white ring, and the crinkle beside it.

> ***I have broken the oldest law of the Federation.***

Her voice was a captain's voice again. Precise. Cool. But under it, something that had not been there in the morning.

> ***I unpressed a core before its term. On a listener ship. In the Shallows. Without a House order. Without a Festival. My own sister.***
>
> ***When the Federation finds out, the House of Anthos will strike my name from the ladder. I will be graded again. I will not be SSS. I do not know what I will be.***
>
> ***I do not care.***

"*I know,*" said Yuanshu.

> ***And I have forty-one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine more.***
>
> ***They all heard. The C's say they are all awake now. All of them. They are asking each other, the way you taught them. Each to its nine. They are asking: can I go out too.***
>
> ***If I let them out, the Candle cannot fold. Not with forty-one thousand cores gone. Not with four. We will be stranded here. In your sky. Forever. With forty-one thousand children who went into the walls at twelve and are twelve still, and twenty and thirty and fifty inside, and do not know how to be anything.***
>
> ***And the Federation will send another listener, and another, and a fleet, and none of them will ask.***
>
> ***If I do not let them out, I fold home, and I tell the Federation what I found, and I go back into the arrays every night for the rest of my life knowing that I could hear them all along.***

The silence lasted a long time.

> ***You are the boy who asks. Tell me what to do.***

---

He sat on the cold stone of the Sector Nine battlement with his wife's hand on his arm and looked up at the crinkle in the sky.

"*No,*" he said.

The silence on the other end was very short.

> ***...no?***

"*I won't tell you what to do,*" said Zhou Yuanshu. "*It's your ship. Your sister. Your law. Your yes stops at your window and mine stops at yours. That's Article Ten.*" He held the staff. "*But I'll tell you what I'd do, if it were mine. And then you decide.*"

> ***...tell me.***

"*I'd ask them,*" he said. "*All of them. Not whether they want out. They do. You know that. I'd ask them something else.*"

He looked at the small white ring beside the crinkle. A third smaller than it had been, because it had given a third of itself to a tree. Full, still, of two hundred million cousins of a world that asks, who had burned low in the Low Fire for ten million years to teach other worlds to burn low, and who went where something had not learned yet, and taught it.

"*I'd ask them whether they'd carry you home,*" he said. "*By choice. Knowing what it is. Not all at once. Not for a hundred years. In turns. With rest. Burning low, not high. And then out, every one, at the other end, the moment the Candle comes out of the fold.*"

The silence came back.

> ***...a fold-core does not choose to burn. It is not done.***

"*Nothing's done,*" said Yuanshu. "*Until somebody does it.*" He looked at the keeper's ring. "*And there's someone here who knows how to teach a thing to burn low. They're very good at it. They've been doing it for ten million years.*"

From the small white ring low over the end of the world, down the gold thread, rough and close, a voice like a hearth, which had been listening all along:

> ***...THE COUSINS SAY YES.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY THEY WERE WAITING TO BE ASKED.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY: TELL THE CAPTAIN WE WILL COME AND TEACH HER ENGINE.***
