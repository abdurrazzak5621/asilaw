## Chapter 220: The Road to Kirovsk

The keeper laid the road in nine seconds, and it was the first time it had ever done anything without being asked.

> ***...GO.***
>
> ***...I DID NOT ASK. I AM SORRY. THERE IS NO TIME TO ASK.***
>
> ***...YOU CAN SAY NO AT THE TOP.***

Gold. Out of the small white ring over the end of the world, not down to the wall this time but west, across the dark, in a long low arc over the Wastes and the snowfields and the frozen rivers of the Northern Union, four thousand kilometers in a single bridge of warm light laid across the night, from the battlements of Sector Nine to a long gray wall in the snow.

He did not say no at the top.

He went up it running, with the staff in his hand and Lingyue beside him with her grandfather's sword already lit, and Old Seven ahead of them, and behind them the Spear Corps, two hundred and seventy-two, and behind the Spear Corps, because Ma Lianhua had asked them through the Lattice in the nine seconds it took the keeper to lay the road and they had said yes in eleven, three thousand of the First Legion.

Qin Hao came. With his bandaged arms and his ordinary fire. He did not ask anyone. He was on the road before Yuanshu noticed he was there.

The road was not like the roads to the field. Those had gone up. This went along. Fast. The gold under his feet moved, the way the island had moved, carrying them, and the night went past on either side, snowfields and frozen rivers and the lights of small towns far below, and ahead, coming up out of the dark, a long gray wall in the snow with a city behind it, and above the city, in the sky to the north, the second gray ring.

And between the ring and the city, rain.

---

> **It is cutting.**
>
> **Not reading. Not three degrees. The threads come down and touch and the head goes straight up and something comes up the thread.**
>
> **The way it was twelve hundred years ago, in Yamato. The way the monks saw. The cutting.**
>
> **Kirovsk did not have the Lanterns long enough. The Quiet came here last. Orlov's people learned the game four months ago. They are good soldiers. They are not good at sitting still in the dark.**
>
> **It found the loud ones first. It is finding more.**

"*How many?*" said Yuanshu.

> **I cannot count from here. Too far. Too fast.**
>
> **Too many.**

---

They came down onto the wall at Kirovsk at 22:08, and the cold hit him like a door.

He had never been this far north. Nobody from Lingzhou had. The Northern Union's great wall ran along the edge of the polar Wastes for two thousand kilometers, gray stone and black iron, with fortresses every fifty kilometers and the city of Kirovsk behind the largest of them, and the snow on the battlements was knee-deep and blue under the floodlights, and the air was so cold that the breath froze on his lips.

And above it, rain.

Silver. Millions of threads, falling out of the dark center of a gray ring low in the northern sky, over the wall, over the city. Not slowly. Not listening. Coming down and touching, and where they touched, heads turned. Straight up. And held.

He saw it on the battlements. Soldiers of the Northern Union in white winter fatigues, at their posts along the wall, standing perfectly still in the snow with their faces turned straight up at the sky, and a fine silver thread touching the crown of each head, and up each thread, faint, gold, flickering, the way he had seen it in the dead monk's vision: light. Going up. A face. A meal. A song. A first word. A dead parent's hands.

All of it going up the thread into the dark, in a long single breath.

On the battlement nearest him, a young soldier with frost in his eyebrows stood with his face turned up and his rifle hanging from its strap, and the light going up out of him, and his mouth slightly open, as if he had been about to say something.

---

Lingyue did not wait.

She went past him down the battlement with her grandfather's sword lit pale, silver-gray, in the snow, and cut the thread over the young soldier's head. One stroke. The way she had cut a thread over Bai Lu on the black glass a long time ago. A flash of silver-gray, and a sound like a violin string snapping, and the thread recoiled, smoking, back up into the dark.

The young soldier's head came down.

He blinked. He looked at her. He did not know where he was.

But he was there. Behind his eyes. Seed felt it. Not hollow. Not quite. Something had gone up the thread before she cut it; something had not.

> **She cut it in time. Most of him is still there.**
>
> **Lingyue. Every thread you cut in time, someone keeps most of what they are.**
>
> **There are a great many threads.**

"*I know,*" said Shen Lingyue, and went down the wall into the rain.

---

He had never seen her fight like that. Not on the north wall, not in the field, not in the cold fire. Not raining light on eighteen thousand edges or throwing it uphill across a kilometer and a half. She did not throw anything. She did not rain anything. She ran.

Down the battlements of Kirovsk in the knee-deep snow, under the falling silver, with a King's sword in both hands, cutting. Every thread she passed. Every head she reached. A flash of silver-gray, a sound like a string snapping, a soldier's face coming down out of the sky, blinking, and she was already past, at the next one, and the next. She did not stop to look at any of them.

And behind her, the Spear Corps.

They could not see the threads. They did not need to. The dead were invisible to threads, and threads were invisible to the dead, but Old Seven had been on a wall in the Black Tide when the lens in the north reached down and cut, and he knew what a cut soldier looked like from the outside. Two hundred and seventy-two of the Spear Corps went down the wall in their rows, through the snow, and wherever a living soldier of the Northern Union stood with his face turned straight up at the sky, a dead soldier of the Eastern Line put his rusted spear up over that soldier's head, lit pale by a drop of Lingyue's light, and the thread touched the spear before it touched the man, and snapped.

"*Shields,*" said Yuanshu. "*They're using their spears as shields.*"

> **Old Seven did not tell them to.**
>
> **He says: a captain does not need to tell soldiers how to cover a man. He only needs to tell them which man.**
>
> **He is telling them which man.**

---

Marshal Orlov was at the center of the wall, on the great fortress above the main gate of Kirovsk, in his greatcoat, with his gloves off and frost all the way up both his arms to the shoulder.

He was not cutting threads. He could not. He was a King of Ice, two hundred years old, and threads were not something ice could touch. He was doing the only thing he could. He had frozen the air.

Over the whole city. A layer of cold so hard and so still that the falling threads slowed in it, the way a spoon slows in honey. They still came down. They still touched. But slowly. Seconds slower. And seconds were what a girl with a sword and two hundred and seventy-two dead spears needed.

He turned his head when Yuanshu came up the stairs of the fortress, and his face was white with frost and something else.

"*They came at nine,*" said the Marshal of the Northern Union, in his formal old-fashioned Huaxian, very calmly. "*We said Lanterns. We have practiced it four months. My soldiers are very bad at it.*" The frost on his arms cracked as he lifted one hand toward the city. "*Kirovsk has nine hundred thousand people. It has three Quiet chapters. They have been sitting with the loud ones since nine o'clock. There are not enough of them.*"

"*How many?*"

Orlov did not answer.

> **Forty-one thousand.**
>
> **Taken. Before we came. Hollow.**
>
> **And it has not stopped.**

---

Yuanshu stood on the fortress above the main gate of Kirovsk in the snow with the staff in his hands and looked up at the second ring.

Gray. Vast. Cracked. Low in the northern sky over the polar Wastes, with a dark center like the pupil of an eye, and out of it, the rain. Not looking for him. Not looking for anything. Cutting. A keeper that had been pruned, with nothing left in it that had ever asked, doing the only thing a ring knew how to do.

He opened.

All of it. The way he had on the north wall of Lingzhou with six lamps in front of him. The Field, nine point eight kilometers of it, over the city and the wall and the frozen dark beyond. The staff. The Death Law, forty-four percent, down to the door that had not closed properly since the cold fire. Every name. Every answer.

He lit up like a lantern in the dark over Kirovsk.

And above him, in the north, the second ring turned.

He felt it. The rain did not stop. But something in it changed. A single thread, out of millions, coming down out of the dark center toward the fortress above the main gate. Slowly. The way a spider comes down on one strand.

Toward the brightest thing in the north.

> **It has found me.**
>
> **It was not looking. But you are very bright.**
>
> **I am ready.**
>
> **I am not going to look.**

The thread touched him.

His head turned. Three degrees. Then all the way. Straight up.

And the second ring pulled.

---

It was harder than the first.

He felt that at once. The first ring had pulled the way a fisherman pulls, steady, cold, curious. This one pulled the way a net pulls when it is full and the boat is turning. It had been cutting for an hour. It was full of forty-one thousand people's lives going up its threads in long single breaths. It did not want to stop and look at one more thing on the end of a line.

It pulled anyway. Because it was a ring, and he was the vessel, and somewhere in what was left of it, the gardener's instruction said: *collect.*

> **I am putting the second knot down.**

He felt Seed put it down. A small complicated shape of silver strands, fifty-six names nobody remembered except a girl with a marker and a woman in a wheelchair, set down on the thread the way you set an empty bowl down between you and someone.

It went up.

Fast. With everything else going up. With the faces and the meals and the songs and the dead parents' hands of forty-one thousand people. Up the thread into the dark center of the second ring, and gone.

> **I am not looking.**

The ring did not stop pulling.

He felt it go on. Cold. Steady. Enormous. The place behind his eyes going colder, and colder, the way your hand goes cold in snow, and Seed in it, very small, not looking.

Ten seconds. Twenty. Forty, which was how long the first ring had taken.

Sixty.

The ring did not stop.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is not picking it up.**
>
> **It is so full. It has so much going up its threads. I think the knot is in there with everything else, and it cannot find it.**
>
> **I think it does not know it has been asked.**

Ninety seconds.

His knees were in the snow. He did not know when that had happened. Lingyue was somewhere below on the wall, cutting. Orlov was beside him with his bare hands held out over the city, holding the air still. Old Seven was halfway down the battlements with his spear over a living soldier's head.

Nobody was holding his hand.

> **I am still not looking.**
>
> **It is very hard.**
>
> **Yuanshu. It has started to lift.**
