## Chapter 204: The Cold Fire

They saw it on the eleventh night by Bai Lu's clock, and at first nobody could tell what they were seeing, because it gave no light at all.

It was a place where the far lights stopped. That was all. A great curved shape in the dark ahead of the island where the scattered gold and blue and green of distant fields and worlds were simply not there, blotted out, the way stars are blotted out by a mountain at night. Huge. Growing. Coming up out of the black ahead of them as the island hummed forward, until it filled half the sky in front of the leading edge, and Old Seven, standing at the rim with his spear grounded, had to tip his skull back to see the top of it.

> **The Mouth.**
>
> **Two rings. Fused. A wheel of gray stone a thousand kilometers across, cracked all over, the way the trunks were cracked, the way the spires were.**
>
> **It used to have two coals for eyes. On Blue Star, in the sky, they glowed.**
>
> **They are dark.**
>
> **It is cold.**

He had seen it from below, once, filling the sky over Lingzhou with two burning eyes, the night his mother said *Lanterns.* He had been inside the Low Fire then, looking up at it through gold-veined walls.

It had been the most frightening thing he had ever seen.

It was not frightening now. It was a ruin. A vast cold wheel of cracked gray stone hanging dead in the dark of the Sea, with nothing burning in it and nothing pulling at anything, turning very slowly, the way a millwheel turns in a dry riverbed when the wind catches it.

And on its far side, the side away from them, he could see something.

Not see. Feel. Through the Field, which reached nine point eight kilometers and could not reach the far side of a thousand-kilometer ring; through the keeper's attention, which ran out ahead of the island and wrapped around the Mouth like a hand around a cup. Something on the far side. Large. Slow. Patient. Reading.

> **It is still reading.**
>
> **From the far edge. Inward. Slowly.**
>
> **It has read about a third of the Mouth.**
>
> **It has not found the cousins yet. Hush says they are in the middle. In the quiet part. Where the curtain was. Burning as low as they know how.**
>
> **It will reach the middle in about two days.**

---

The island came alongside the Mouth at the place where a crack ran down through the gray stone of the outer rim, wide enough to walk through nine abreast.

The Elder brought them in. She stood at the center of the island with the gold thread running out of her into the dark, and her long hands pressed together, and the island slowed, and turned, and drifted in against the rim of the Mouth so gently that the pale warm ground touched the cracked gray stone without a sound, the way a boat touches a jetty when someone who knows how is at the tiller.

"*We're here,*" she said, in her glass-note voice. "*The keeper is very tired.*"

> ***...I AM HERE.***
>
> ***...I AM VERY TIRED.***
>
> ***...I DID NOT LOOK AT IT. ALL THE WAY. I DID NOT LOOK AT THE MOUTH. I WAS AFRAID IT WOULD LOOK BACK.***
>
> ***...IT IS COLD. IT CANNOT LOOK BACK.***
>
> ***...I AM LOOKING AT IT NOW.***
>
> ***...IT IS VERY SAD.***

---

They went in through the crack.

Old Seven first. He did not wait for anyone. He walked off the pale ground of the island onto the cracked gray stone of the Mouth's rim with his spear in one finger and a thumb and his gray flame burning steady, and went into the crack, into the dark, and the Spear Corps went after him in their rows, because they had not asked him and he had not asked them.

Then Yuanshu, with the staff. Lingyue beside him with her grandfather's sword lit pale. Bai Lu with her bag. The Mo twins, elbows touching. Hu Dabao with his shield. Qin Hao with his bandaged arms. Kwan with a headlamp she still did not need, because the light of the Field went with them, soft and gray-white, into the dark. Adjutant Xu at the top of the Lattice, humming.

Behind them, fifty thousand dead of Blue Star stayed on the island, at the rim, facing out. Sergeant Ma had asked them. Somebody had to hold the boat.

The crack went in a long way.

Through the stone of the outer rim, which was kilometers thick. Gray. Cracked. Cold. Covered, on every surface, with the faint marks of things that had burned against it for ten million years: soot, scorching, long smooth channels where fire had run like water. And then, at last, the crack opened out, and they came into the inside of the Mouth.

---

He had been inside the Low Fire.

He remembered it: gold-veined walls, round glowing windows where mouths had once been, a few hundred million minds looking out of the veins as the living and the dead walked past, curious, wondering, some of them smiling. A cold place at the heart, with a lock on it that only opened if someone asked.

This was not that.

This was a cathedral after a fire. A vast dark hollow inside the ring, so large that the light of the Field went out into it and did not reach the far side, and above them and around them and below them, everywhere, the walls. Gray. Cracked. Veined, the way the Low Fire had been veined. But the veins were dark. Not gold. Black. Long channels running through the gray stone in every direction, where fire had once run, empty now, the way a riverbed is empty in a drought.

And in the veins, very faintly, so faintly that he only saw it because the Field was there and the light of it touched them: minds.

Billions.

Not burning. Not screaming. Not even burning low. Just there. In the dark veins of the dead walls of the Mouth, very small, very dim, very cold, the way embers are cold at the bottom of a hearth in the morning when the fire went out in the night. Every world the Mouth had ever eaten, and the two Hungers it had been made from had ever eaten, for ten million years. Pressed into the walls. Waiting.

> **...I cannot count them.**
>
> **I have counted everything. Every ember. Every name. I have never not been able to count.**
>
> **I cannot count these.**
>
> **They are too dim. Too many. Too cold.**

Lingyue's hand found his, in the dark. She did not say anything. She held on.

---

"*Which way?*" said Yuanshu.

He did not ask Seed. He did not ask the Elder. He asked Old Seven, who was standing at the front of the Spear Corps a few meters ahead in the gray light with his skull tilted, listening, the way he had walked through the keeper's field a long time ago toward a quiet column nobody else could find.

Old Seven did not answer.

He turned. A few degrees. Toward the middle of the dark. And walked.

They followed him.

---

It took four hours.

Across the inside of the Mouth, through the dark, on a floor of cracked gray stone that was not a floor, because a ring had no floor, only the curve of the inner wall going away on every side; the Field made it a floor, the way it made the pale ground of the field a floor, and the dead walked on it, and the living walked behind the dead. Past black veins full of cold embers. Past the long smooth channels where fire had run. Past the place, Seed said, very quietly, where the second ring had joined the third and fourth, where the stone was thicker and stranger and the veins ran together in great dark knots like the roots of an old tree.

And then, ahead, in the dark, a light.

Small. Dim. Gold. Very far. The only warm thing in the whole cold dead hollow of the Mouth, the way a single window is the only warm thing in a house where everyone else has gone to bed.

> **The quiet part.**
>
> **Where the curtain was.**
>
> **Where they have been burning low for four hundred and forty days.**

Old Seven walked faster.

---

There was a wall.

Not a wall. A curtain. He understood that as soon as he saw it. A long, high, faintly shining fold in the dark, hanging from nothing, the way a curtain hangs in a doorway, soft gray at the edges and gold in the middle where the light came through. It was not stone. It was not anything he had a word for. It was the place where something very old and very quiet had stood for a long time, behind the fire, and when it went away it had left the shape of itself behind, the way a body leaves a hollow in a bed.

And in front of the curtain, in a line, facing out into the dark, facing them, standing, waiting:

Skeletons.

Sixty-one. In their rows. Soldiers of the Third Battalion of Lu Xuanming's march, who had walked off Blue Star on a raft because a boy had asked them, and gone into a burning Hunger to stand guard over a world that said yes, and stood at the edge of the quiet part for four hundred and forty days, so that the cold would come to them first.

They were very dim. Their gray flames were banked so low that Yuanshu could barely see them. Their bones were white with something that was not frost, because there was no frost in the Sea, but looked like frost.

At the front of them, a short broad skeleton with one arm.

The left arm was missing at the shoulder. Not lost. Given. A long time ago, on the first voyage, in the throat, bone to bone, so that a captain could hold a spear in two hands again.

Sergeant Huo Changsheng.

Old Seven stopped walking.

---

They stood there, the two of them, three meters apart, in the gray light of the Field in the dark of a dead Hunger. A tall skeleton two meters ten with a spear in one finger and a thumb, wearing a sergeant's left arm. A short broad skeleton with one arm, at the head of sixty-one men.

Nobody on either side moved.

Then Huo lifted his one hand. Slowly. To his brow. And held it there.

A salute.

Old Seven grounded his spear. The butt of it on the gray stone, once, with a sound that went out into the dark of the Mouth and did not come back. And lifted his free hand, the one that had been Huo's, the left one, with its finger and its thumb.

To his brow.

And held it there.

> **He says: Sergeant.**
>
> **He says: you held.**
>
> **Huo says: sir.**
>
> **Huo says: sixty-one, sir. All present. One of them doesn't know where he is.**
>
> **Huo says: we were told somebody would come.**

---

Something moved behind the line of the Third Battalion.

Small. Pale. On many thin legs. It came out from behind the skeletons in front of the curtain, slowly, swaying, the way it had walked in on its many thin pale legs through a gap in a fire a long time ago, past a cooling shield. It was smaller than he remembered. Or it was holding itself smaller. All its limbs drawn in close against the cold. All its dark eyes half open.

It walked straight to Old Seven.

And the old soldier crouched down, the way a tall man crouches to talk to a child, and held out his hand. The one with one finger and a thumb.

Hush climbed into it. All of it. Curled up in the palm of a dead man's hand, its limbs wrapped around itself, like a cat that has come in from the rain.

And held up, in one thin pale limb, so that Old Seven could see it, a playing card. Worn soft at the edges. Faded. A queen. Red.

> ***...I still have it.***
>
> ***...I told you.***
>
> ***...tell the captain.***

Old Seven's other hand came up, very slowly, and cupped itself over the small pale shape, the way you'd shelter a candle from the wind.

And his gray flame came up. All the way. Brighter than Yuanshu had ever seen it. Gray. Warm. Steady. Lighting the dark in front of the curtain, and the sixty-one skeletons of the Third Battalion, and their sergeant with one arm, and the faint frost on their bones beginning, very slowly, to go.

> **He says: I am the captain.**
>
> **He says: I was told.**
