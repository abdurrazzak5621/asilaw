## Chapter 194: What It Read

He could not see for three days.

Bai Lu said it was not permanent. She said it standing at the end of his bed in the back room of the Ossuary, with green light running out of her small hands into his face, in the voice she used when she was guessing and did not want anyone to know. Six lamps, she said, had held him for three point four seconds, and something had come down every one of them, and the part of him that saw had been looking straight up into it the whole time. The way you are blind for a while after you look at the sun.

"A while," said Lingyue, from the chair beside the bed.

"A while," said Bai Lu.

"How long is a while?"

"I don't know," said Bai Lu. "Nobody's ever looked at six of those at once. Ask me in a week."

Lingyue did not ask her in a week. She asked her every morning. And every morning Bai Lu said *I don't know*, and put her hands on his face, and the green light ran in, and on the third morning, when he opened his eyes, there was a gray blur where the window was, and a darker blur in the chair beside the bed, and the darker blur made a sound he had never heard it make, and put its head down on the edge of the bed, and did not lift it for a long time.

---

> **I am going to tell you the numbers. All of them. You asked me to. Even when you do not want them.**
>
> **You do not want them.**

*Tell me anyway.*

> **Eleven walkers. All dead.**
>
> **Heishui: two hundred and twelve of the Northern War Zone taken. Hollow. The Quiet are with them.**
>
> **The wall: forty-one of the living in the morning, against the beasts. Nineteen more in the night. Sixty.**
>
> **The city: thirty-four taken. Hollow. Of the fourteen hundred who could not be quiet. Thirty-four the lamps found before the person sitting beside them could make them dim enough. Ren's thirty-eight are not among them. Ren is not among them.**
>
> **The dead: four hundred and eleven of the First Legion unwoven, at the end. Twenty-eight of the Spear Corps, in the field and on the wall. Gone.**
>
> **Hundred-Arrow Lin.**

He lay in the dark of the back room with the gray blur of the window beside him.

*And the rest?*

> **Two million eight hundred thousand people in the dark. All night. Following a wave, and then a voice, and then your mother's voice.**
>
> **They breathed when she told them to.**
>
> **Two million seven hundred and ninety-nine thousand nine hundred and sixty-six of them are fine.**

---

> **[Level 65 → 69.]**
>
> **Nine walkers, killed by your dead under your command. Two more by Qin Hao.**
>
> **Death Law: 37.6% → 39.2%.**
>
> **Not from the walkers. From the Lattice. You put Old Seven's intent down a net of eighteen thousand and it arrived at every end at once. Nobody has ever done that. Lu Xuanming held two million alone and could not make them do anything together.**
>
> **You did not hold them. They held each other. And Old Seven's intent went through all of them, the way a note goes through a choir.**
>
> **I have written it down under three names. Old Seven. Adjutant Xu. Ma Lianhua.**
>
> **Not yours. You were lying on your back with your face pointing at the sky.**

*That's fair.*

> **It is not fair. You were the bait. Bait is not nothing.**
>
> **But it is also not the hook.**

On the fourth day, he could see the window. On the fifth, the board.

He made them take him into the banking hall, against Bai Lu's instructions and with Lingyue's help, which Lingyue gave by not asking Bai Lu, and they set him on the vault steps with a blanket over his shoulders and the staff across his knees, and he looked at the long white wall.

It was longer.

Xiaoyu had been on the stepladder for four days. She could not speak above a whisper. Her voice would come back, the doctors said, in a month or two, if she did not use it, and so she had stopped using it, and had written instead. Every name. The two hundred and twelve from Heishui, with General Han standing beside the stepladder reading them to her off a list, because she could not hear and write at the same time. The sixty from the wall. The thirty-four from the city. The four hundred and eleven of the First Legion, which Ma Lianhua had dictated to Seed name by name from the river gate. The twenty-eight of the Spear Corps, which Old Seven had dictated, through Seed, standing in front of the wall with his spear grounded, from memory, every one, with the year each man had died and the place.

And at the top.

Above the first column. Above Captain Qi Zhenshan in her earliest, shakiest capitals. Where she could not reach without the tall ladder that Fat Tan had borrowed from a church on East Market Road.

*HUNDRED-ARROW LIN. THE HUNDRED-AND-FIRST. SHE GOT IT.*

---

Captain Lin Wen came on the sixth day.

She came in through the bronze door in her Defense Corps uniform, with her cap under her arm, and stood in front of the wall and looked up at the top of it for a long time. Then she came and sat on the vault steps beside him, not close, and did not say anything.

He did not say anything either.

After a while she opened her hand. In her palm was a small iron arrowhead, no longer than her thumb, rusted, black at the edges, with a long bright scratch across it, as if something very fine and very sharp had dragged across it once.

"It came back," she said. "To the watchtower. After. It was on the floor in her footprints in the morning." She turned it over. "It's empty. There's nothing in it. Seed says." She closed her fingers over it. "It came back anyway."

> **It came back to her string.**
>
> **It always came back to her string. Even after.**
>
> **There was no string. There was only her daughter, standing where she always stood.**

"I'm going to put it back," said Captain Lin Wen. "In the crack. By the north slit. Where you found it." She looked at the wall. "Is that all right?"

"It's yours," said Yuanshu.

"I'm asking anyway," said Lin Wen. "She'd want me to. She learned it from you. On the watchtower. Every night for a year and a half, she'd stand next to me, and every night she'd ask me if I wanted her to." Her voice did not change. "I always said yes. I never once said it out loud. I thought she knew."

"She knew."

"I know she knew," said Captain Lin Wen. "I'd still like to have said it."

---

The keeper came on the seventh night, through the Elder, at three in the morning, and its voice was very quiet.

> ***...THEY ARE GONE.***
>
> ***...ELEVEN. ALL OF THEM. I WATCHED.***
>
> ***...NONE WENT BACK.***

"*Good,*" said Yuanshu.

> ***...NO.***
>
> ***...NOT GOOD. NOT BAD. I DO NOT KNOW THE WORD.***
>
> ***...NONE WENT BACK. BUT THEY DID NOT NEED TO.***

He waited.

> ***...THE LAMPS READ YOU. ALL OF YOU. AND THE THING MADE OF THE OLD STUFF.***
>
> ***...WHAT A LAMP READS GOES UP ITS BEAM. TO WHAT SENT IT. AS IT READS. NOT AFTER.***
>
> ***...THE ONE IN THE FIELD WALKED AWAY TO TELL. THESE DID NOT NEED TO WALK. THEY TOLD WHILE THEY WERE LOOKING.***
>
> ***...THREE POINT FOUR SECONDS.***
>
> ***...THE GARDENER KNOWS YOU NOW.***

The banking hall was very quiet.

> **Yes.**
>
> **I felt it. While they held us. Something at the end of every beam, very far away, learning.**
>
> **It knows everything you are. Everything I am.**
>
> **It knows about the Lattice. It knows about the Field. It knows about Lingyue's light and Old Seven's spear and the wave and your mother's voice.**
>
> **It knows that six of its lamps held the one who asks for three point four seconds and found something made of the old stuff standing in the open, and did not know what to do, and were destroyed.**

*Is that bad?*

> **I do not know.**
>
> **I keep thinking about the field. The walker that held me for two point three seconds and did not lift. The Collector that said: you are not mine.**
>
> **They do not know what I am, Yuanshu. Nothing above the sky knows what I am. They know what I am made of. They do not know why I ask.**
>
> **I think that frightens them.**
>
> **I think, perhaps, for the first time in a very long time, something above the sky is afraid of something on Blue Star.**
>
> **I do not know if that is good either.**

---

> ***...ONE MORE THING.***

The keeper's voice. Slower. The way an old man's voice slows when he is about to say something he has been carrying.

> ***...THE FIELDS.***
>
> ***...THE ELEVEN I CAN SEE. ALONG THE ARM. THE OTHER KEEPERS.***
>
> ***...THE FURTHEST ONE WENT DARK TONIGHT.***
>
> ***...TWO HUNDRED AND SIX OF YOUR RAFT-DAYS. AT THE END OF WHAT I CAN SEE.***
>
> ***...IT WAS THERE. GOLD. FAR. LIKE A HOUSE ACROSS A VALLEY.***
>
> ***...AND THEN IT WAS NOT.***

He sat on the vault steps under the long white wall with the blanket around his shoulders and the staff across his knees, and did not say anything.

> ***...I DO NOT KNOW WHAT IT MEANS.***
>
> ***...I THINK IT MEANS SOMETHING IS PRUNING.***
>
> ***...I THINK IT MEANS SOMETHING IS COMING ALONG THE ARM. ONE GARDEN AT A TIME.***
>
> ***...TOWARD ME.***
