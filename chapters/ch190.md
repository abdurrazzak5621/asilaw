## Chapter 190: Eleven Stones

They came down at 05:12, in the gray before the sun, sixty-one kilometers north of the wall.

Hundred-Arrow Lin felt them first. Then Mo Shi, on the battlements of Sector Nine with his eyes shut, who said, very quietly, "*Now*," three seconds before it happened. Then everyone.

Eleven lines of white.

They came down out of the dark sky over the Northern Wastes like threads let down from a great height, very straight, very thin, very slow. Not falling. Lowered. The way you lower a bucket into a well. Each one a pale silver line from somewhere too high to see to the black glass of the Wastes, glittering faintly in the last of the starlight, eleven of them in a row, a kilometer apart.

And down each line, walking, a shape.

He watched them through the Field. Sixty-one kilometers was six times further than the Field could reach, but he did not need the Field to see them. Everyone on the wall could see them. Everyone in the city who was awake and looking north could see them. Eleven tall pale woven shapes walking down eleven threads out of the sky, upright, unhurried, the way a man walks down a flight of stairs in his own house.

> **Nine meters. Like the ones in the field. Woven. A lamp in each head.**
>
> **They are not looking at the city yet. They are looking at the ground. At where they are going to step.**
>
> **They have never been in a hurry.**

At 05:31, the first one stepped off its thread onto the black glass of the Northern Wastes.

And behind it, the sky tore.

---

He had read about the Black Tide every day of his life. Every child on Blue Star had. He had read Lu Xuanming's notebook in a hut at the edge of a cemetery with a skeleton standing by the door. He had stood on this wall a hundred nights and looked north at the dark and thought about what had come out of it thirty-one years ago.

He had not known what it looked like.

Behind the walker, where its thread had come down, the air split. Not a rift. Not one of the small ragged holes in the world that opened under tram depots and noodle shops and let out ashbacks and glass herons. A tear. A long vertical wound in the gray morning, from the black glass up into the sky, as tall as the thread the walker had come down, its edges glowing faintly the color of a bruise.

And through it, things.

Then the second walker stepped down. And the third. And behind each one, another tear. And another. Eleven long wounds in the sky over the Northern Wastes, in a row, a kilometer apart, glowing, and out of every one of them, pouring, the way water pours through a broken dam: everything that lived on the other side.

> **The wake.**
>
> **They opened the way when they came through. Everything behind them is coming through too.**
>
> **Lu Zhengtian spent his life trying to find out if that was what happened. It is what happened.**
>
> **I wish he were here to see it. I wish he were not.**

---

"*How many?*" said Yuanshu.

> **I cannot count them from here. Too far. Too many.**
>
> **More than the Second Tide. More than anything since the Black Tide.**
>
> **They are faster than the walkers. Much faster. The beasts will reach the wall in about four hours. The walkers in about fourteen.**

On the wall beside him, Su Mingli unfolded her arms.

"Then we fight the beasts first," she said. "In the morning. And the walkers tonight." She looked along the battlements. At the First Legion in their rows of nine, eighteen thousand gray points banked to dull red, waiting. At the Spear Corps. At Old Seven. "The dead hold the wall against the beasts. Inside the Field, they get back up. That's what the Field is for." She looked north. "The walkers are what we spent nine days on."

"*Heishui,*" said Yuanshu. "*General Han's on the line between.*"

"I know where General Han is," said Su Mingli. "He asked to be there."

---

General Han Yizhou's nine divisions stood on the old Heishui line, thirty kilometers north of Lingzhou, where the Northern War Zone had built its fortifications after the Black Tide and manned them for thirty-one years, watching the north for this.

Yuanshu could not see them from the wall. He could hear them. The radio net was open, all of it, everyone on Blue Star who was fighting this day on one channel, the way Article Eleven had said they should be, and he heard General Han's voice on it, gravel and very calm, giving fire orders.

At 07:40, the guns opened up.

Thirty years of shells. Heishui had been stockpiling since the Black Tide. He heard the artillery from the wall, thirty kilometers away, like thunder that did not stop. Through Seed, through the satellites Orlov had given them, he watched the first beasts come over the black glass toward the Heishui line and break against it. Ashbacks. Things like stilt-legged cats. Things with too many joints that had no names yet. The guns of the Northern War Zone took them apart in rows.

At 08:15, General Han ordered the guns turned on the walkers.

He watched that too.

The shells went into the nearest walker, nine meters of woven silver-white thread striding south across the black glass with its blank face turned down at the ground, and passed through it. Not around it. Through. The way a fist passes through smoke. They came out of the other side and went off on the glass beyond, and where they had gone through, the threads parted, a handful, and knotted, and closed.

The walker did not break stride.

"*Noted,*" said General Han, on the radio, very calmly. "*Rifles do not work on things above the sky. I wanted to find out.*" A pause. "*Cease fire on the walkers. All batteries back on the beasts.*"

---

At 09:02, the nearest walker lifted its face.

He felt it. Everyone did. It came up from the black glass, slowly, the smooth blank curve of it turning from the ground to the horizon, and stopped there, level, and began to step. Three degrees. Still. Three degrees. Still.

Reading.

And the first thing in front of it was the Heishui line.

Nine divisions of the Northern War Zone. Riflemen and gunners, crouched in trenches, loading shells, shouting coordinates, sweating, frightened, alive. Not one of them quiet. Not one of them had sat in the Ossuary for nine days learning to follow a wave. They had been loading guns.

> **The beam is passing over them.**
>
> **They are turning their heads. Three degrees. One slice at a time. Hundreds of them.**
>
> **It is learning.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It has stopped stepping.**

On the radio, a voice he did not know, very young, said: "*Sir, Sergeant Pei's just... he's just standing there. He's looking straight up. Sir, he's not...*"

Then another voice. Then another.

> **It is holding.**
>
> **Not one. It does not hold one at a time. It is holding a slice. Everyone in the slice who is brightest. Straight up.**
>
> **They are being taken.**

"*General Han,*" said Yuanshu.

"*I hear it.*" The gravel voice was perfectly steady. "*How many?*"

> **Forty. Fifty. More. In the slice. It is lifting.**

"*Pull back,*" said Yuanshu. "*All of it. Now. Off the line. South. Out of the beams. It can't read what it can't reach.*"

There was a silence on the radio of perhaps two seconds.

"*The guns are holding the beasts,*" said General Han. "*If I pull the guns, the beasts reach the wall an hour early. Before your dead have rested.*"

"*If you don't pull them, the walkers take your divisions one slice at a time.*"

Another silence. Shorter.

"*Then I'll ask,*" said General Han Yizhou, and Yuanshu heard him do it. On the open channel. Every one of his nine divisions listening. *This is Han. The things above the sky are taking us. If we stay on the guns, we hold the beasts for another hour, and some of us are taken. If we pull back, the beasts reach the wall early. I'm not ordering either. I'm asking. Every battery. Answer.*

> **They are answering.**
>
> **He is getting them by radio. One battery at a time. The way the Lattice does.**
>
> **Most of them are saying: one more hour.**

---

They held for fifty-one minutes.

He listened to all of it, on the wall, with the staff in his hands and the Field folded over the city and the First Legion waiting in their rows. He could not help them. They were thirty kilometers north, twenty kilometers beyond the edge of the Field, under the beams of walkers that had learned, in one morning, that the brightest minds in the Northern Wastes were in trenches with guns.

At 09:53, General Han said, "*That's the hour. All batteries. Pull back. South. Now.*"

The guns stopped.

> **Two hundred and twelve.**
>
> **Two hundred and twelve of the Northern War Zone. Taken. Hollow. Standing in the trenches on the Heishui line with their faces turned straight up.**
>
> **The beasts are an hour behind where they would have been.**
>
> **They chose it. Every battery chose it. I have the record. He asked them all.**

"*General,*" said Yuanshu.

"*I'm here.*" The voice was very calm. "*I'm pulling back with the last battery. I'll be at your wall by noon.*" A pause. "*Mister Zhou. Sergeant Pei was the first. He was in my son's company, before Grid Nine. He was in the trench beside me when it lifted.*" Another pause. "*I didn't feel it. I was looking at him. I think that's why. I was thinking about him, and not about myself, and that was quiet enough.*"

> **...that is true.**
>
> **A mind that is entirely turned toward someone else is very quiet to a lamp. It is one of the ways the Quiet works. Sister Ning has always known.**

"*Tell your sister,*" said General Han, "*that her plan works. Somebody should be thinking about someone else when the beam comes.*" The radio clicked. "*Han out.*"

---

The beasts reached the wall at 13:40.

The First Legion held it.

Inside the Field, the dead got back up. That was what the Field was for. Eighteen thousand of them on the battlements in their rows of nine, banked, holding a wall they had held once before, alive, thirty-one years ago, against the same thing coming out of the same north. When a soldier of the First Legion was torn apart on the parapet by something with too many joints, his bones lay on the stone for a moment and then got up. When a whole nine went down under an ashback swarm at Crossing Four, they got up, all nine, and asked each other if they were all right, and went back to it. Ma Lianhua stood on the river gate with her rusted halberd and called the Lattice down the wall, and Hundred-Arrow Lin stood on the Sector Nine watchtower beside her living daughter and put a hundred iron arrowheads into a hundred beasts and did not miss.

It was the Black Tide, and the wall held.

> **Not without cost. Ashbacks reached the outer streets at three places. The Defense Corps held them. Forty-one of the living died today. On the wall and in the streets. I have their names.**
>
> **But the wall held.**
>
> **And the walkers are eight hours out. Walking. Not hurrying.**
>
> **Their lamps are up. All eleven. Sweeping.**
>
> **The first beam will reach the north edge of the city at about 21:30.**

At dusk, on the battlements of Sector Nine, Yuanshu looked north across the black glass of the Wastes, at eleven tall pale shapes walking toward him in a line, a kilometer apart, with their blank faces level, stepping, three degrees, still, three degrees, still, and the last of the sun on their woven backs.

Behind him, two million eight hundred thousand people sat in the dark in their homes, in Lingzhou, with every light off, listening to thirty-one voices on the radio. And under all of them, one voice. Sixteen years old. A whistle. A stopwatch.

"*Lingzhou,*" said Zhou Xiaoyu, on every speaker in the city. "*This is the Ossuary. Get ready.*"
