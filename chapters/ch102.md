## Chapter 102: Two Rafts

"How long," said Yuanshu, "until the other two reach Blue Star?"

> **"The keeper has been watching them since they turned,"** said the Elder. **"They are not like the first. They are younger. Hungrier. Faster."**
>
> **"One hundred and nine days."**

Nobody spoke.

The raft hung over the Lantern sea. Above them, the dead ring turned. Somewhere very far away, a chorus of burning voices that had been fighting a Hunger from inside for ten million years was waiting for an answer. Somewhere else, very far away in the other direction, a small blue world with a wall full of candles was one hundred and nine days from two rings that did not ask anything at all.

"Sixty days to the Sixth," said Lingyue slowly. "Seventy, maybe. And then however long it takes. And then home." She looked at the Elder. "How long from the Sixth to Blue Star?"

> **"On the currents? Ninety days. Perhaps more."**

"So if we go to the Sixth," said Lingyue, "we don't get home in time."

"And if we go home," said Qin Hao, "we leave them in the fire."

---

Nobody spoke for a long time.

Hush had curled up in Old Seven's hand again, very tight, all its limbs wrapped around itself.

> ***...ten million years,*** it said, very faintly. ***...they have been playing the game in the fire for ten million years.***
>
> ***...if no one comes, they will play it for ten million more.***

"I know," said Yuanshu quietly.

> ***...and your world. Your sister. Your mother. The wall.***

"I know."

He looked down at the white staff across his knees. At the small skull with its silver reading glasses. At the brass key on its red string around his neck.

He thought about a folding table in the old banking hall of the Ossuary, and a girl with a clipboard, and a question read out exactly as written.

*Do you want to go?*

*I don't want to. I'm going anyway.*

---

"Then we don't choose," said Yuanshu. "We split."

Everyone looked at him.

"The keeper's thread goes all the way home," he said. "In a straight line. Not along the currents. Straight." He looked at the Elder. "Could it pull a raft home along the thread? Faster than the currents?"

The Elder was quiet for a long moment. Listening. To the thread. To something at the far end of it.

> **"...it says yes."**
>
> **"It says it has never pulled anything toward itself. Only lifted. Only kept."**
>
> **"It says it would like to try."**
>
> **"It says: twenty days. Perhaps less. If the ones on the raft are patient."**

"Then some of us go home," said Yuanshu. "Now. To the wall. To help them get ready. And some of us go to the Sixth."

"And how do we decide who?" said Sokolova.

Yuanshu looked at her.

"Same way we always do," he said.

---

Sokolova got out Fat Tan's ledger and turned to a new page.

She did not write a heading. She looked at it for a long moment, and then she wrote, in careful Huaxian, *Where do you want to go?* And then, underneath, two columns. *HOME.* *THE SIXTH.*

And then she asked them.

---

Qin Hao said home.

He said it immediately, and then sat very still, with his jaw tight, looking at the shining sea.

"My grandfather is eighty-two," he said at last. "He's going to stand on the Western Pass when those things come, with one arm, in front of forty-one thousand graves. Because that's what he does." He touched the colonel's star pinned over his heart. "Someone has to stand next to him. And I'm the only one who can set things on fire." He looked at Yuanshu. "I'm sorry."

"Don't be," said Yuanshu. "That's the right answer."

---

Sokolova said home.

She wrote it in her own column, in her own hand, very neatly, and then she sat looking at it.

"Marshal Orlov is two hundred years old," she said. "He has fought at seven wars. He has never once in his life asked anyone to come and stand beside him." She closed the ledger on her finger. "Last week, on the thread, he asked me if I would come home." She looked out at the sea. "I would like to be asked again. I would like to say yes."

---

Hundred-Arrow Lin did not speak. She never spoke.

But she drifted to the center of the raft, a shape of ash and dark air with an iron arrowhead for a heart, and stood in front of Yuanshu. And the iron arrowhead in her chest turned. Slowly. Pointing.

Back along the gold thread. Home.

> **She says the wall.**
>
> **She says that when you first raised her, the first thing she asked was whether her daughter made it to the shelter. And her daughter had.**
>
> **She says her daughter is a captain on that wall now. Lin Wen. Defense Corps.**
>
> **She says: someone should stand on the wall beside her.**

"Yes," said Yuanshu. "Someone should."

---

The others said the Sixth.

Lingyue without looking up from her sword. "Obviously," she said. "Someone has to make you come back."

The Mo twins together, the same word at the same time, and then a brief argument about who had said it first, which Mo Shi won, because he had seen his brother about to say it.

Bai Lu, very quietly. "They've been burning for ten million years," she said. "I'd like to be there when it stops hurting."

Sergeant Huo, for the Third Battalion. Sixty-two of them. *Where the captain goes.*

Old Seven, who did not need to be asked.

Hush, curled in Old Seven's hand. ***...the Sixth. I want to meet them. They played the game too.***

The Elder. ***"The Sixth. You will need someone who knows the currents."***

And the staff.

"*Don't look at me,*" said Jiang Lan. "*I'm a staff. I go where I'm carried.*" A pause. "*The Sixth. Obviously. Somebody has to keep track of all these names.*"

---

The keeper split the raft at dawn.

Not dawn. There was no dawn. When Bai Lu's brass clock said dawn.

It happened very gently. A line of soft gold light ran across the pale ground of the raft, from rim to rim, straight through the middle, like a seam. And then, slowly, the two halves drew apart.

Not breaking. Parting. The way a door opens.

On one half: Qin Hao, with his colonel's star. Sokolova, with Fat Tan's ledger, which Yuanshu had given her, because she would need it more. Hundred-Arrow Lin, a drift of ash with an iron arrowhead turned toward home.

On the other: everyone else. And at its rim, a dead captain standing in a doorway, leaning on his shield, facing out.

And between them, widening, a strip of dark.

The gold thread that ran home split too. Two threads now. One running from each half of the raft, back along the same line, side by side, into the black.

---

They did not say goodbye properly. There was no time for it. And there was a gap of dark between them, and it was growing.

Qin Hao stood at the edge of his half and saluted. Not Yuanshu. Old Seven. The way he had saluted at Sunday dinner in the Academy dining hall, a long time ago, with dust on his shoulders.

Old Seven returned it.

Sokolova did not salute. She stood at the edge with the ledger against her chest and looked across the widening gap at Yuanshu, and her ice-gray eyes were very steady.

"I will tell the Marshal," she said, "that you are coming back alive. And that this is an order. And that you will not obey it." Something moved at the corner of her mouth that might, on someone else, have been a smile. "And that I am going to make you."

"I'll try," said Yuanshu.

"Do not try," said Vera Sokolova. "Do."

Then the gold thread pulled, and her half of the raft began to move. Away. Faster. Along the thread. Back toward a small white ring and a patched field and a world with a wall full of candles. Until it was a pale disc in the dark, and then a speck, and then a faint gold line, and then nothing.

---

That night, on what was left of the raft, the Elder called the Architects.

They had left eleven days before. Seven ancient figures in gray, riding a current away from the Lantern world toward the first husk on their long list. A world the first Hunger had eaten before Hush's world. They had gone to stand there and say they were sorry, out loud.

They had not gone far.

> **"They heard,"** said the Elder. **"About the Sixth. The keeper told them."**
>
> **"They are coming back."**
>
> **"They say: there is a cold place at the center of every Hunger. Seven chambers. We built all four."**
>
> **"They say: the cold place in the second ring is empty. Its builders were burned a long time ago."**
>
> **"They say: the seal needs a builder's hand."**
>
> **"They say: we owe them that much."**

---

Yuanshu lay on his back on the half-raft that night, looking up.

Lingyue lay beside him, not asleep. The twins back to back. Bai Lu with her clock. Sixty-two soldiers at the rim. A dead captain in a doorway. Old Seven standing guard with a small pale shape curled in his hand.

Above them, the dead ring turned.

Beyond it, very far, past the far small lights, a voice was waiting for an answer.

> **I sent it.**
>
> **While you were sleeping. Like you asked.**
>
> **Just one word.**

*What did they say?*

> **They have not answered yet.**
>
> **But Yuanshu.**
>
> **The second ring.**
>
> **It has started moving.**
>
> **Toward us.**
>
> **Very slowly.**
>
> **As if it were trying to meet us halfway.**
