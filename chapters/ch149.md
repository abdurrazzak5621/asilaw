## Chapter 149: The Fourth Company

He asked the Western Pass what it wanted, and the Western Pass said: to stay.

Not all of it. But most. Thirty thousand of the dead of the mountain stood in their rows on the terraces in the cold afternoon, banked, dim, their roots crumbled to sand in the graves behind them, and through the staff and the Lattice and Adjutant Xu's Relay, he asked them the question he had not had time to ask before they went down into the inner gate. *It's over. The seeds are dead. What do you want now?*

> **Rest: 4,107. They are tired. They want to lie back down. They want to be asked again in a year.**
>
> **Speak and rest: 812. They want to say something first.**
>
> **Stay: 25,695.**
>
> **They say this is their post. They say they held it alive. They say they would like to hold it dead.**
>
> **They say: not for you. For the pass.**

"For the pass," said General Qin.

He was standing on the first terrace beside Yuanshu, in his greatcoat, with his one hand behind his back. He looked up at twenty-five thousand of his soldiers standing among the graves in the cold.

"That's the right answer," he said. "I'd have been worried if they said it was for you."

---

The four thousand who wanted to rest lay down before dusk.

He did it the way he had done it for the fifty-three of the Third Battalion in the valley a long time ago, and for the people in WARDEN's core, and for the minds in the crystal under the Old Ferry Bridge: gently, one at a time, through the staff, opening the Death Law under each of them like a cool dark door. Not *Return.* There was nothing forcing them to stand. Only rest.

One by one, in the last light, four thousand one hundred and seven soldiers of the Western Pass folded down onto their own graves and lay still. The ones who wanted to speak first spoke. Seed wrote down every word. Most of them said a name. Some said where something was hidden: a letter in a boot, a ring in a tin under a floorboard, a debt that should be forgiven. One said that the cook in the officers' mess had been stealing sugar for thirty years and someone should tell him it was all right, because the sugar had gone into the soldiers' tea.

The general wrote that one down himself.

---

The twenty-five thousand who stayed needed someone to hold them when he left.

That was the problem. Seed laid it out that night in the gatehouse, the way it laid out everything now: plainly, with the numbers, and with a line at the end about what it did not know.

> **When you leave the mountain, the Field leaves with you.**
>
> **Without the Field, they stand only on the Lattice. Each one asks its nine. Adjutant Xu holds the top.**
>
> **But Adjutant Xu is going with you. He goes where Old Seven goes. And the Relay is in him.**
>
> **So they need a knot of their own. Here. On the mountain. Someone they trust, at the top of their own Lattice, who stays.**

*A Law Core.*

> **Yes.**
>
> **And I know who.**

---

His name was Ma Dashan.

He was the sergeant of the Fourth Company, the colonel's company, the one who had counted out loud in a mountain accent all the way down the inner gate and all the way through eighty meters of cracked rock and all the way into the cavern and out again. He had died eight years ago in the inner gate, beside his colonel, in the fourth hour, when the rift finally came through. He had been a miner before he was a soldier. He had joined the Western War Zone at nineteen because the mine closed.

He stood in the gatehouse at midnight, a broad, short skeleton with a sergeant's chevrons still sewn to what was left of a sleeve, and listened to Yuanshu ask him.

> **He says he is not an officer.**
>
> **He says the colonel was the officer.**
>
> **He says the colonel said ONLY TO SPEAK, and spoke, and lay down, and the sergeant has been standing in the inner gate ever since, in his head, waiting for an order that is not coming.**

"I'm not asking you to be the colonel," said Yuanshu. "I'm asking you to be the knot. The top of the net. Every soldier on this mountain asks their nine. Somebody has to ask the commanders. Somebody they'd answer."

The sergeant did not say anything for a while.

> **He says: they would answer him. He has been counting them for eight years. In the gate. Every one.**
>
> **He says: yes.**
>
> **He says: but he wants to ask the colonel first.**

---

They went up to row two hundred and twelve at one in the morning.

The general came. He walked up the terraces in the dark with his one hand on Qin Hao's shoulder, slowly, stopping every fifty rows, the way he had a hundred times in the last month. Old Seven came. Adjutant Xu came, with his cane. Sergeant Ma Dashan walked at the front.

At the colonel's grave, the sergeant knelt.

He did not touch the stone. He knelt in front of it, on the frozen ground, in the cold, a dead miner in a sergeant's chevrons in front of the grave of the officer he had died beside, and bowed his skull.

Colonel Qin Zhaolin had said ONLY TO SPEAK. He had spoken. He had lain back down. Yuanshu did not reach for him. Nobody did. That was the rule.

But the sergeant spoke to him anyway. Through the Lattice, because that was the only voice he had now, and the Lattice was asking.

> **He says: Colonel. They want me to hold the gate.**
>
> **He says: the whole mountain this time. Not just the inner gate.**
>
> **He says: I'm not an officer. I'm a miner who can count.**
>
> **He says: if you don't want me to, I won't.**

The grave did not answer. It could not. Its owner had said what he had to say, a year ago, about a man in a gray cardigan who brought tea, and gone back to rest.

General Qin Zhenyue put his hand on his son's stone.

"He'd say yes," said the old man roughly. "He used to say you were the only sergeant in the Western War Zone who could find his way out of a mine in the dark." He did not take his hand off the stone. "He'd say: *Ma, stop asking and hold the damn gate.*"

The sergeant's skull came up.

> **He says: yes, sir.**

---

They built the core before dawn, in the gatehouse, with the general as witness.

A miner's Earth Law, eighteen percent, the kind that tells you where the rock is weak before it falls, and which way the air is moving in a dark tunnel, and how to count men by the sound of their feet. Eight years of counting in the inner gate. A colonel's company, and a colonel's last order, which had been *hold.* Seed held the shape. Yuanshu held the door. Sergeant Ma Dashan told them when they had it wrong, in a mountain accent so thick that Seed had to ask him to repeat himself four times, which made him laugh, which made Adjutant Xu laugh, which made Sister Ning, on the phone from Lingzhou, ask what on earth was going on.

> **[Law Core forged: The Inner Gate.]**
>
> **[Earth Law 18% + Death Law 35.1%.]**
>
> **[Bound: Sergeant Ma Dashan. Commander. Permanent.]**
>
> **He is the knot at the top of the Western Pass Lattice. He holds twenty-five thousand six hundred and ninety-five, and they hold each other.**
>
> **You hold him. Through the Relay. From anywhere.**
>
> **Load, for the whole Western Legion, from a thousand kilometers away: nine percent.**

*Nine.*

> **The Lattice is very good, Yuanshu. They hold each other. You only hold the top of the top.**
>
> **Grandpa Xu would be very pleased. He is pretending not to be.**

---

At dawn, the Western Legion stood on the mountain in the frost.

The frost had come back. That was the first thing Yuanshu saw when he came out of the gatehouse. In the night, with the seeds dead and the roots crumbled and the graves cooling, the cold had come back up the mountain from the pass, and every one of forty-one thousand three hundred and six small gray stones was white with it. The light coming over the eastern ridge caught them and turned them, for a moment, gold.

Among them, in their rows, twenty-five thousand of the dead of the Western Pass, banked to steady gray, holding their post.

At the gate, Sergeant Ma Dashan stood with a pick over his shoulder and a colonel's company behind him, counting.

General Qin came and stood beside Yuanshu.

"You'll go," he said. "Today. South."

"Yes."

"Good." The old man looked up at his mountain. "Go and do the next thing. That's what you're for." He did not look at Yuanshu. "Come back. That's what I'm asking. Not for them. They've got their sergeant. For me. I'm eighty-two. I'd like to be asked a question by somebody under sixty before I die."

"I'll come back."

"That's not yes," said General Qin. "That's what you want."

"I know," said Yuanshu. "I'm learning the difference."
