## Chapter 10: Return to Sender

The Tianshu Institute was the safest building in Lingzhou. Forty floors of rock. Law-shield arrays humming on every level, faint blue lines in the stone that brightened when anything moved. A King asleep on the thirty-ninth floor.

At 3:14 in the morning, the corridor on the twenty-second floor was dark and empty, lit only by the low amber strips along the floor.

Then a man walked out of the wall.

He came through the stone the way a swimmer comes up through the surface of a pool: face first, then shoulders, then the rest of him, without a sound, without a ripple, leaving the wall behind him exactly as it had been. The shield lines in the stone did not brighten. As far as the building knew, nothing was there.

> **Spatial distortion. Floor 22, east corridor.**
>
> **An awakener with a Phase talent. He is walking partially outside normal space. The shield arrays cannot see him.**
>
> **I can. Barely.**

Yuanshu opened his eyes in the dark.

*Where's he going?*

> **Not toward you.**
>
> **Toward room 2207.**

Room 2207 was Xiaoyu's.

---

The man's name, though no one would learn it for some days, was Dorian Hale. He had been a mercenary for nineteen years, S-rank Phase Walker, and he had never failed a contract. His orders that night were simple and he had memorized them on the flight: *Do not engage the Calamity. Take the sister. Leverage is cheaper than war.*

He drifted down the dark corridor half-in and half-out of the world, his body faintly transparent, like a reflection in a window. Doors meant nothing to him. Walls meant nothing. He reached 2207 and leaned forward to pass through it.

Something was standing in the doorway.

It had not been there a moment ago. It was tall, and made of dark bone, and in the center of its rib cage a small gray flame was burning.

Dorian Hale had a rule: nothing in the physical world could touch him while he was phased. Spears went through him. Bullets went through him. He had walked through a firing squad once, for a bet.

So he didn't stop. He walked straight through the skeleton.

Or tried to.

> **To phase through a solid object, he must fully shift for 0.04 seconds at the moment of contact. During that shift, his body has no position.**
>
> **But he has to come back into the world on the other side.**
>
> **Old Seven, the window is in 0.31 seconds. Behind your left shoulder. Thirty centimeters.**

Old Seven did not turn around.

He reversed his spear and thrust it backward past his own left shoulder, without looking, into empty air.

Dorian Hale became solid on the far side of the doorway, exactly thirty centimeters behind the skeleton's left shoulder, at exactly the moment the spearhead arrived there.

He looked down at it with an expression of pure professional surprise.

Then he fell.

On the other side of the door, Xiaoyu sat up in bed and said, "Ge? Did something fall over?"

"Just a delivery," said Yuanshu from the corridor, very calmly. "Go back to sleep."

---

Su Mingli was there forty seconds later. General Han was there in ninety, which was impressive for a man who had been asleep across the city.

They stood in the corridor looking at the body.

"S-rank Phase Walker," said Su Mingli quietly. "There are four in the world. Tianshu's arrays can't see them." She looked at Yuanshu. "You could."

"Seed could."

"He was going for your sister."

"I know."

His voice was perfectly level. General Han glanced at him and then, very carefully, took one step back.

> **Your heart rate is 61.**
>
> **It has been 61 the entire time. I think this is not calm.**
>
> **I think this is something else.**

Yuanshu knelt beside the body.

He put two fingers on the dead man's wrist, the way you'd check a pulse, and reached down the Death Law the way he had in the cemetery: listening for an echo. For a will. For anything that could say yes or no.

There was nothing.

> **No will. Not faded. Erased.**
>
> **There is a structure in his brain residue: a law-lock, implanted. It triggers on death and destroys the will completely. Professional assassins carry them so that their minds cannot be read after they die.**
>
> **There is no one here to ask.**
>
> **This is now only remains. Like the beasts in the canyon.**

*Can you read anything?*

> **The lock destroyed the will. It did not destroy the law residue. Or the very last image his eyes recorded before his mission. That kind of memory is stored like law. It is burned into the body.**
>
> **One moment. Reconstructing.**

In Yuanshu's mind, a picture formed, gray and blurry: a briefing room. A long table. A screen showing Xiaoyu's school photo. And on the wall behind the screen, a seal. An eagle over a broken chain, and five words beneath it.

*ATLANTIC FEDERATION — OFFICE OF CONTAINMENT.*

> **Not the Office of Strategic Talent. Not Catherine Moore.**
>
> **A different office. One that did not agree with her.**

Yuanshu stood up.

"Who sent him?" General Han asked.

"Someone who thought my sister was cheaper than a war," Yuanshu said. "They should have done the math."

---

At eight in the morning, the dead man left the Tianshu Institute through the front gate.

He walked.

He walked down the mountain road in the clothes he had died in, with the hole in his back where the spear had gone through, and he did not hurry. The pale light in his eyes was steady. Around his neck hung a sheet of white card on a string.

By the time he reached the city limits, there were six news drones above him.

By the time he reached Lingzhou's central avenue, there were sixty, and a crowd of several thousand people was following him at a careful distance, filming, whispering, not daring to get closer. Traffic stopped. Police cars rolled slowly alongside him with their lights off, unsure what law applied to a walking corpse that was not hurting anyone and was technically on its way somewhere.

He walked fifteen kilometers in four hours, through the middle of the city, in the full summer sun.

At noon he arrived at the gates of the Atlantic Federation consulate.

The guards on the gate had their rifles up. They had been told he was coming for the last two hours. They did not fire. Nobody wanted to be the person who shot a dead man on live television.

The corpse stopped at the gate. It turned, slowly, so that the cameras could see the card around its neck.

The card said, in neat black handwriting, in both Huaxian and the Atlantic common tongue:

**RETURNED TO SENDER.**

**NEXT TIME, SEND SOMEONE WHO CAN STAY.**

**— Z.Y.**

Then it knelt down on the consulate steps, laid itself out flat as if going to sleep, and the light went out of its eyes for good.

---

In the Tianshu observatory, three kilometers away, Yuanshu released the last thread and opened his eyes.

Su Mingli was standing behind him with her arms folded. She had watched the whole walk on a screen without speaking once.

"You just started a diplomatic incident," she said.

"No." He stood up. "They started one at 3:14 this morning. I just made sure everybody saw it."

She was quiet for a moment. Then she nodded, slowly, the way a teacher nods at an answer she didn't expect but can't fault.

"The Federation will deny it," she said.

"Let them. They'll know I know. And everyone else will know what happens to the next one."

The door burst open and Xiaoyu ran in with her phone held out in front of her like a trophy.

"Ge! *Ge!* It's the number one video on the planet! Two billion views in three hours! There's a song! Somebody already made a *song!*" She stopped. Looked at his face. Then, more quietly: "That man was coming for me. Wasn't he."

Yuanshu looked at her for a moment. Then he put his hand on her head and messed up her hair, the way he'd done since she was four.

"He didn't get there," he said. "Nobody ever will."

Xiaoyu looked at the door, where Old Seven stood guard with his spear grounded, and then back at her brother, and then she hugged him so hard it hurt.

> **Your heart rate just changed.**
>
> **For the first time since 3:14.**
>
> **I understand now. It was not calm. It was anger, held very still.**
>
> **I am learning that the most dangerous version of you is the quiet one.**

---

That afternoon, the Atlantic Federation issued a three-sentence statement denying any knowledge of the incident.

That evening, the Office of Containment's director resigned.

And at midnight, the heads of state of all five nations received the same urgent message from the Concordia Council: an emergency summit, to be held in seventy-two hours, with one item on the agenda.

*The Calamity.*
