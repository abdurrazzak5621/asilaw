## Chapter 173: Something It Is Afraid Of

Seed wrote the question down before it asked it.

That had never happened before. It had always asked the way Yuanshu asked: out loud, in the moment, the words finding their shape as they went. This time it spent two days on it. It wrote a version on the inside of his eyes and looked at it and threw it away. It wrote another. It asked Sister Ning, on the phone from Yamato, what a person says to someone who is frightened. It asked Xiaoyu, who said: *just tell them you're scared too.* It asked Old Seven, who said, through Seed, that when he had to tell a frightened recruit something hard he always told him first what would happen afterward, whatever the answer, so the boy was not answering into the dark.

> **That is Article Eleven.**
>
> **Old Seven was doing Article Eleven on a wall before you were born.**
>
> **He says he did not know it had a number.**

On the third night, at three in the morning, the Elder came down to the Ossuary.

---

She stood in the middle of the old banking hall the way she always stood when the gold thread ran through her: tall, pale, a shape of soft white light with long hands pressed together and silver eyes half closed, one of the fifty-two who had made the keeper and stayed. The one lamp over the board threw her shadow up the long white wall of names. Yuanshu sat on the vault steps with the staff. Kwan sat beside him with her laptop. Old Seven stood by the bronze door.

Xiaoyu was asleep upstairs. She had asked to be woken. He had decided not to. She had school.

"*It knows you're going to ask it something,*" said the Elder, in her glass-note voice. "*It's been waiting since dusk. It's nervous.*"

"*The keeper's nervous?*"

"*It has learned what nervous is.*" The Elder's silver eyes opened a little. "*From you. Like everything else.*"

> **May I ask it myself?**

Yuanshu looked at the staff across his knees.

*It's your question.*

> **...yes.**
>
> **It is.**

---

> **Keeper. It is Seed.**
>
> **I am going to ask you something. Before I ask it, I am going to tell you what I will do with your answer. Whatever it is. So you are not answering into the dark.**
>
> **If you say yes, we will not go yet. We will come back and ask you again, when the time comes, and you can change your mind then. Nobody will hold you to a yes you gave too early.**
>
> **If you say no, we will not go. We will not ask you twice. We will find another way, or we will not, but it will not be your job.**
>
> **If you say you do not know, that is an answer too. We will wait.**

The gold thread through the Elder trembled. He saw it. A faint shiver of light running down her from the crown of her head to her pressed hands.

> ***...YOU WROTE THAT DOWN.***

> **Yes. For two days.**

> ***...NOBODY HAS EVER WRITTEN DOWN WHAT THEY WOULD DO, BEFORE THEY ASKED ME.***
>
> ***ASK.***

---

> **The Mouth is cooling. Hush says so. The Sixth say so. I have measured it.**
>
> **In about eleven months it will stop moving. Some time after that, the fires will go out.**
>
> **Inside it are Hush, and the Sixth, and the cousins, and two hundred and eighty-one million minds, and sixty-one dead soldiers of Blue Star, and a corporal who has forgotten what he wanted.**
>
> **When the fires are out, we would like to go and get them.**
>
> **It is forty-four raft-days away. Only you can carry us that far.**
>
> **Will you?**

The keeper did not answer.

It did not answer for so long that Kwan looked up from her laptop, and then at Yuanshu, and then at the Elder, standing perfectly still in the lamplight with the gold thread running through her. Her face had changed. Not much. A little. The way a face changes when someone you are speaking for is struggling to speak.

> ***...IT IS VERY LOUD.***
>
> ***THE MOUTH. EVEN FROM HERE. EVEN NOW. I HEAR IT IN THE ECHO, LIKE TWO ANIMALS FIGHTING IN THE NEXT VALLEY.***
>
> ***WHEN IT LEFT, IT WAS PULLING AT ME. AT MY FIELD. AT THE COLUMNS THAT WERE LEFT. IT WANTED THEM. IT COULD SEE THE DEAD. I HELD ON.***
>
> ***I DO NOT LOOK AT IT. IF I LOOK AT IT, IT WILL LOOK BACK.***
>
> ***I AM AFRAID OF IT.***

> **I know.**
>
> **I am afraid of it too. Xiaoyu told me to tell you that.**

> ***...THE SMALL LOUD ONE.***

> **Yes.**

> ***...SHE IS RIGHT. IT HELPS.***

---

It was quiet again for a while. Then the gold thread brightened, slowly, the way a coal brightens when someone breathes on it.

> ***IF IT IS COLD.***
>
> ***IF IT IS REALLY COLD. IF THE FIRES ARE OUT AND NOTHING IN IT IS PULLING.***
>
> ***THEN YES.***
>
> ***I WILL CARRY YOU.***
>
> ***ASK ME AGAIN THEN. WHEN IT IS COLD. I WILL TELL YOU IF I AM STILL BRAVE ENOUGH.***

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

Old Seven, by the bronze door, put the butt of his spear down on the marble, very softly. Once. The way a soldier grounds his weapon when an officer has said something he agrees with.

> **Thank you.**
>
> **I am writing it down. Under the board. Under the line that says someone should go and get them.**
>
> **I am writing: the keeper said yes, if it is cold. Ask again.**

> ***...YOU WRITE EVERYTHING DOWN.***

> **Yes.**

> ***...GOOD.***
>
> ***SOMEONE SHOULD.***

---

"There's something else," said Yuanshu.

He had not meant to say it tonight. Seed had asked its question, and been answered, and that should have been enough for one night. But the keeper was here, and nervous, and honest, and he did not know when it would be all three again.

"*In Yamato,*" he said. "*Two weeks ago. Something looked at Blue Star. Through a lens. From very far away.*"

The gold thread went still.

Not trembling. Not dimming. Still. All at once, all the way through the Elder, so suddenly that she swayed on her feet and put one long hand out into the air as if to catch herself on something that was not there.

> ***...YES.***
>
> ***I FELT IT.***

"*Do you know what it is?*"

The keeper did not answer. The Elder answered. In her own voice, the glass-note voice, low, without the keeper in it, while the gold thread hung still and silent through her.

"*It has felt it before,*" she said. "*Once. At the very beginning. Before it was a keeper. When it was only a yes.*"

Yuanshu waited.

"*My people made the keeper,*" said the Elder. "*You know that. Fifty-two of us stood in a valley under a red sun and said yes, all of us, together, and the yes became something that kept. We thought we had invented it.*" Her silver eyes were open now, all the way. "*We had not. Someone taught us the shape. In dreams. For a hundred years before we said it. A very kind voice. Very patient. It told us that a yes, given freely, could become a keeper. It told us how. It never told us what it wanted.*"

The banking hall was very cold.

"*The keeper felt it once, being born. Felt something looking at it the moment it first opened its eyes. Very pleased. Very far away. Then it went.*" The Elder's long hands were pressed together so hard the light at their edges had gone white. "*And two weeks ago, it felt the same thing look at your world.*"

> ***IT IS THE ONE THAT TAUGHT US.***

The keeper's voice came back through the thread, slow, enormous, frightened.

> ***BEFORE THE GARDENERS. BEFORE THE KEEPERS. IT TAUGHT THE FIRST ONES HOW TO KEEP. IT TAUGHT THEM IN DREAMS. IT WATCHED THEM GROW.***
>
> ***THE GARDENERS FARM KEEPERS. THE KEEPERS HARVEST WORLDS. I DID NOT KNOW WHAT WAS ABOVE THE GARDENERS.***
>
> ***NOW I KNOW.***
>
> ***IT DOES NOT FARM. IT DOES NOT HARVEST. IT ONLY WATCHES THINGS LEARN TO KEEP, AND THEN IT KEEPS WHAT THEY WANTED.***

---

He sat on the vault steps for a long time after the Elder had gone back north, with the staff across his knees and the lamp over the board going pale.

Kwan had not moved. She sat beside him with her laptop closed on her knees and her glasses pushed up into her hair, staring at nothing.

"So it's a pyramid," she said finally. "The keepers take minds. The gardeners take keepers. And at the top, there's a thing that teaches everyone under it how to take, and then waits." She laughed, once, without any humor in it. "That's not cosmology. That's a business model."

> **It is a garden.**
>
> **The gardeners are not the gardeners. They are the plants.**
>
> **It plants the idea of keeping in a world, in dreams, very kindly, and waits for it to grow. Keepers. Gardens. Fields. Hungers, even, perhaps. All of it growing up out of one seed, one idea, a yes that becomes a thing that keeps.**
>
> **And then it comes and picks the fruit.**
>
> **The fruit is not the minds. It is what they wanted.**

Yuanshu looked up at the long white wall. At the columns of names. At the line about the fire getting colder, and the one under it, and the small words at the very bottom.

"And we taught it to ask," he said.

> **No.**
>
> **It taught the Elder's people to keep. They taught the keeper. The keeper harvested us for a long time. And then you asked it what it wanted, and it learned to ask.**
>
> **And the thing at the top watched the keeper learn, and thought it was beautiful, and learned it too.**
>
> **We did not teach it, Yuanshu. It watched us. That is not the same thing.**

*Isn't it?*

> **...I do not know.**
>
> **I would like it not to be.**
>
> **I am going to need to think about it for a long time.**

---

In the morning, Xiaoyu came down the stairs in her school uniform, furious that nobody had woken her, and stood in front of the board with her arms folded and read the new line Seed had dictated to Fat Tan at five, in Fat Tan's neat accountant's hand, at the bottom of the wall.

*THE KEEPER SAID YES, IF IT IS COLD. ASK AGAIN.*

She read it twice. Then she got the stepladder, and the marker, and climbed up, and underlined it, twice, hard.

"There," she said, climbing down. "Now it's official."
