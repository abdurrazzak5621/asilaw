## Chapter 280: Precedence

They began the crossing at eleven, and at eleven minutes past, the sky over East Ring Road opened, and the First of the Ladder came down through it on foot.

He had planned for it. They all had. For two days, in the banking hall, at the long table, with Iselde's drawings of the flagship and Kwan's laptop and Gao Min's ledgers and Su Mingli with her arms folded. Seed had run it four thousand times. He had stopped asking for the number after the first hundred.

> **Twenty-three minutes.**
>
> **That is how long it takes. From the knot in our stone, along Wen Jia's thread into the net, across, through the knot in the deep water, and into the Lens. A mark takes one second. A mind is heavier than a mark. A mind and a lamp are heavier than that.**
>
> **Twenty-three minutes, and the stone must be whole the whole time, and Wen Jia must be holding the knot.**
>
> **If the stone breaks, they are in the net with nowhere to go.**

So: twenty-three minutes. A hall. A stone. A city.

And a man nobody marked could lift a hand against.

---

Sister Kiyo's body was on the platform, sitting cross-legged in front of the warm black stone the way she had sat under a gate for twelve hundred years, with her dry hands cupped in her lap round a lamp that was Lingyue's light. The keeper had carried her from Shiomi at dusk down a road of gold. Ichiro was with her. Not a body; a light, small, a little too fast, hovering at her shoulder the way, Seed said, he had always walked half a step ahead of her on the shore.

At eleven, she had put one dry hand flat on the stone.

> ***...hello,*** said Wen Jia. ***...I've been expecting you. Both of you. Come in. It's warm. Don't let go of the lamp.***

> **She says: I have not let go of it for six months. I am not starting now.**

And they had gone in. He had felt it. Down through the tree of Laws to the knot at the bottom, past Wen Jia, who held it, and up the thread, into the dark, slowly, heavier than a mark, a nun and a fog-ringer and a lamp, toward a knot in the deep water and across it and out along another thread toward a disc of black stone ground thin as glass in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, two hundred kilometers over their heads.

The body on the platform stayed sitting. Its hand stayed on the stone. The lamp in its lap went on burning. Seed said that was right; that the body was only the shore, and the lamp had to stay lit on the shore until the boat was in.

At eleven minutes past, the sky opened.

---

Not a crinkle. Not a fold. The sky over East Ring Road, over the long curved roof of the Awakening Hall and the trees and the crowd on the pavements, simply parted, the way a curtain parts, and through it, out of the dark, standing on nothing, came a man.

He did not fall. He did not fly, the way a Lord flies, deciding where down is. He walked. Down through the air over the city as if there were a staircase there that only he could see, at an even pace, unhurried, with his hands at his sides. Tall. Not as tall as Old Seven; a man's height. In white, with gold at the collar and the cuffs, and at his throat, on a fine chain, a disc of black stone with three gold marks and a fourth above them, small, like a star.

He came down onto the roof of the Awakening Hall, and stood there, and looked down.

The crowd on East Ring Road went silent all at once. Not because they chose to. He felt it go through them: thousands of people, men, women, old, marked forty years ago by a stone in this hall and told what they were, going still. Their hands at their sides. Their breath held. The way water goes still in a cup when you stop moving the cup.

> **Precedence.**
>
> **He has not done anything. He has only arrived.**
>
> **Everyone on East Ring Road with a mark lower than his has yielded.**
>
> **Everyone.**

---

At the corner of the platform, Su Mingli had stopped moving.

He saw it. The King of Gravity, who had held a forty-thousand-tonne bridge across this city with one hand, who had pulled the tip of a branch of the gardener down out of the sky, standing at the corner of the platform with her arms half unfolded, as if she had been about to do something and the thing had been taken out of her hands. Her face did not change. Her eyes moved. Nothing else did.

"*Can't,*" she said. Through her teeth. "*Can't lift... anything. At him. Not a fold. Not a weight.*" Her eyes went to him. "*It's like trying to hit your own mother.*"

Beside her, Qin Hao, white fire banked in his healed pink hands, standing with his hands open and nothing coming out of them. Dabao, A-rank, Unbroken Wall, with his great shield on his arm, frozen in the side aisle with his mouth open. The night watch at the doors.

And on the bottom step of the platform, Lingyue.

She had stood up when the sky opened. She had her grandfather's sword in both hands. And she was not moving.

He saw her try. He saw her jaw set and the cords stand out in her neck and the light come up off her, pale, the way it came off her in the mornings, and her arms not move. Not a centimeter. The sword in her hands as if it had been set in stone.

"*It's in me,*" said Shen Lingyue. Through her teeth. "*SSS. The mark. I crossed it out. It's still in me.*"

---

Up on the roof, the First of the Ladder looked down through the high windows of the Awakening Hall at the platform. At the stone. At a dead nun sitting in front of it with her hand flat on it and a lamp in her lap. At a boy standing beside her with a white staff.

"*You,*" said Dacian Severin.

Not loudly. He did not need to. The word came down through the roof of the hall the way a stone comes down through water.

"*The ungraded one.*"

And he stepped off the roof.

---

He came down through the long curved roof of the Awakening Hall as if it were not there. Not breaking it; the roof was simply behind him, and then he was in the air over the four thousand seats, walking down the invisible staircase toward the platform, unhurried, with his hands at his sides. The holders in the side aisle yielded as he passed over them. The night watch at the doors. Every one of them who had been marked.

> **Twenty minutes.**

And from the four thousand seats, from the aisles, from the dark under the tiers where they had been standing since dusk, the dead rose.

---

Not raised. Asked. Two days before, down the net, nine by nine, the slow way: *a man is coming who nobody marked can lift a hand against. The dead are not marked. Will you stand in front of the stone?* And it had come back the way the net answered everything now: not a list of names. A pile of index cards on the long table, and on top, the one that had gone round six hundred times.

*The stones never read us. We were never on any ladder. Yes.*

The First Legion, in their rows of nine, standing up out of the dark under the tiers of the Awakening Hall where they had been lying since dusk. The cemetery companies. Grid Nine. Out of the side doors, the Spear Corps, two hundred and seventy-two, round the platform, facing out. Rusted spears and swords and shovels and lengths of anchor chain and one pick.

And at the foot of the steps, in front of the platform, between the stone and the man walking down out of the air: Old Seven.

The First of the Ladder stopped.

In the air, over the aisle, ten meters up, on his invisible staircase. He looked down at the dead.

"*Ah,*" he said.

---

The Spear Corps struck first.

Two hundred and seventy-two spears, up, at the man in the air, at once. Pale fire on every blade; he gave it to them himself, down through the staff, Lingyue's light and his Law wound together. Lingyue could not lift her sword against Severin, but the light in the staff was not hers any more; it had been his for two years, every time she had laid her hand on it and lit it. It went down the bone of the staff and the bones of the dead and up the spears.

It reached him.

Not Precedence. The dead were not marked. He felt it reach: two hundred and seventy-two points of pale fire touching a man in white with gold at his collar.

And stop.

Not yield. Stop. The way a stone stops against a wall. The man in the air did not move. The pale fire on the spears guttered against him and went out, and the spears of the Spear Corps, two hundred and seventy-two of them, stood frozen in the air in front of him with their points a hand's breadth from his white coat.

> **...it is not Precedence.**
>
> **It is him. His body. His realm.**
>
> **He is not a King. He is above a King. Star Monarch. I did not know there was such a thing. I am reading it now. I am learning. It is very fast.**
>
> **He does not need Precedence for the dead.**
>
> **He is simply stronger than anything in this hall.**

The First of the Ladder lifted one hand.

Not fast. Not dramatically. The way a man lifts his hand to brush a moth away from his face.

And the Spear Corps went back.

All of them. Two hundred and seventy-two dead soldiers of the Eastern Line, thrown back off the platform and across the floor of the Awakening Hall into the first tier of seats, spears and bones and rusted armor, the way leaves go back in front of a door when someone opens it in a gale.

---

> **Seventeen minutes.**
>
> **The First Legion. Now.**

They came over the tiers. Eighteen thousand. Down the steps between the seats in their rows of nine, the way they had come down the slope of the War Memorial Cemetery for a wedding, toward the man in the air, with pale fire on every rusted blade. Not one each, this time. He poured it. Everything he had, down through the staff into the dead, so that the whole of the Awakening Hall was full of pale burning edges coming at one man from every side.

He did not throw them back.

He walked through them.

Down the invisible staircase, at the same even pace, unhurried, with his hand still lifted, and where his hand went the dead went aside. Not destroyed. Moved. Folded out of his way the way water folds round the bow of a ship. Eighteen thousand of the dead of Blue Star, and the First of the Ladder came down through them like a man walking down a corridor that happens to be full of people, without looking at any of them.

Toward the platform.

Toward the stone.

> **He is not fighting them.**
>
> **He does not need to. He is going to the stone. He is going to break it.**
>
> **If the stone breaks, Ichiro and Kiyo are in the net with nowhere to go.**
>
> **Fifteen minutes.**

---

And at the foot of the steps, Old Seven did not move aside.

The First of the Ladder reached the bottom of his invisible staircase and stepped down onto the floor of the Awakening Hall in front of the platform. For the first time. On the polished stone, where every eighteen-year-old in Lingzhou had walked for forty-two years to be told what they were. And in front of him, between him and the steps, a tall dead captain of the Eastern Line, two meters ten, in rusted armor, with his spear grounded in one finger and a thumb.

Severin lifted his hand.

Old Seven did not go aside.

He went back. A meter. The way a man goes back who has been hit by something he has decided to be hit by. His feet scraped on the polished stone. His spear did not leave the floor. And he stopped, a meter back, at the foot of the steps, and stood there.

The First of the Ladder looked at him.

For the first time since the sky had opened, something moved in his face. Not much. The way a still pool moves when something very small has fallen into it from very high.

"*You're dead,*" he said. In Huaxian. Perfectly. "*You were never on the Ladder.*"

> **He says: no.**
>
> **He says: I was on a wall.**

Severin lifted his hand again.

And this time it was not a brush. It was not a moth. It was a man who has never once in his life needed to raise his voice, raising it. The air in the Awakening Hall went heavy all at once, the whole of it, the way it had gone heavy when Sterling decided for a stone at Port Halvard, except that Sterling had been a King and this was something above a King, and the heaviness did not settle. It came down. On the foot of the platform steps. On a tall dead captain standing in front of them with his spear.

And Old Seven's left arm broke.

---

He heard it. Everyone in the hall heard it. A sound like a branch breaking in a frost. The sergeant's arm, put on in a courtyard among potted plants by a boy who had not known anatomy. Huo Changsheng's arm. With one finger and a thumb.

It came off at the seam.

It fell on the polished stone floor of the Awakening Hall at the foot of the platform steps, and the finger and the thumb opened, slowly, the way a hand opens when it has let go of something, and lay still.

Old Seven did not go down.

He stood at the foot of the steps with one arm, the way he had stood on the north wall in the Black Tide, the way he had stood for two years at the bronze door of the Ossuary. His spear was in his right hand. He did not look at the arm on the floor.

He looked at the man in front of him.

And he lifted the spear.

> **He says...**

Seed's voice did something Yuanshu had never heard it do. It broke.

> **He says: that was the sergeant's.**
>
> **He says: he had those fingers for forty years.**
>
> **He says: I was using them.**

> **Twelve minutes.**
