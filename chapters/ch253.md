## Chapter 253: Up

Hush tied the knot.

Nobody asked it to. It came down off the windowsill in the back room of the Ossuary on the last afternoon, slowly, swaying, all its limbs out, across the marble floor of the banking hall to the long table, where Xiaoyu was sitting with a single silver-gray strand of thread in front of her on a sheet of white paper, and a marker, and no idea what to do next.

The strand was from Fat Tan's box. The walker-thread. The silk the six had left on the north wall when the First Legion struck, which he had swept up two years ago and would not throw away, and kept in the vault beside the queens of hearts. Hush had taken two strands from it once, for two rings. Xiaoyu had taken one more that morning, for the mark.

"*I can write on it,*" she said. "*I tried. It stays. But it's just a thread with words on it. It's not a mark. A mark has to go up and be read. Seed says it has to be tied.*"

Hush looked at the strand for a long time.

> ***...it is a question-knot, but backward.***
>
> ***...a question-knot is a thing held out and let go of. So that what is asked can answer, or not.***
>
> ***...a mark is a thing held up. So that what looks at it sees.***
>
> ***...in my world, before the ring, we tied them for the trees. When a tree was too old to answer and we needed it to know something. We tied what it needed to know, and hung it on a branch where the tree would grow round it.***
>
> ***...I can tie it.***

Xiaoyu looked at the small pale thing on the table beside her elbow, with gold dust still on its thin legs from the cut.

"*Will you write it with me?*" she said.

> ***...yes.***

She wrote it. In square black capitals, very small, along the silver-gray strand, the way you write along the edge of a ruler: *THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP.* And Hush, with four thin pale limbs, slowly, while she held the ends, tied it. Not the way a person ties a knot. The way water ties itself round a stone. When it was done, it lay on the white paper, a small gray knot the size of a fingernail, with the words gone inside it, and when Seed looked at it through Yuanshu's eyes, it said:

> **...it is true.**
>
> **I cannot read it. It is tied. But I can feel that it is true. The way I can feel the wall.**

Hush sat back on its legs.

> ***...I moved first once. At the cut. That was enough.***
>
> ***...I will not come up the thread. I will sit in the sun, on the windowsill, and wait.***
>
> ***...that is also a thing someone has to do.***

---

Xiaoyu wrote the other mark that evening, on the floor of the Awakening Hall, in front of the warm stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder, in letters as tall as her arm. *THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP.* And stood back.

> ***...ready?*** said Wen Jia, from the knot.

"*Ready.*"

> ***...I've never sent one. Not like this. Not one that's true.***
>
> ***...here goes.***

The runes on the black face of the stone, which had pointed down into it since the night before Awakening Day, turned. All of them. Up. And the letters on the floor in front of the stone went faint, not gone, faint, as if something had read them all at once and carried what it read up the fine thread out of the crown and into the dark.

On Kwan's laptop, eleven pale lines going up off eleven diagrams of eleven stones jumped. Once. Together. A glance.

And went still again.

> **It read it.**
>
> **It glanced. It moved on. The way it reads everything.**
>
> **Wen Jia was right. A glance is not enough.**

"*Then we take it the rest of the way,*" said Yuanshu.

---

They went up at midnight.

The hall was full again. Not with the stones rising; with people who had heard. Fifteen hundred of them, in a line up the aisle and out of the side door and along the pavement of East Ring Road under the trees, with their hands on each other's shoulders, and the one at the front with her hand on the warm black stone. Wen Hao. He had parked the Number Nine at the curb at eleven and turned the engine off and come in and asked to be at the front, and nobody had argued.

Seed had said nine hours. Not one minute more. A Lord's body could hold empty for nine hours with fifteen hundred people anchoring it through the stone, and the stone through the floor, and the floor through the earth. After that, the anchor would hold, but there would be nothing left in the body for the anchor to hold to.

He lay down on the platform with his back against the side of the stone, under the bright blue jacket. Lingyue knelt beside him with her hand flat on his chest and her grandfather's sword on the floor beside her and the pale flame in her other palm. Qin Hao knelt on his other side with his hands open and the white fire banked in them. Su Mingli at the corner of the platform, arms folded. Gao Min on the bottom tier with her notebook and a stopwatch she had brought from the Bureau, an old mechanical one, because she did not trust the phone. Sister Ning on her folding stool with her candle lit. Xiaoyu at the foot of the steps with the marker.

And in his right hand, closed, a small gray knot the size of a fingernail.

Old Seven was not at the foot of the steps. He was lying down. Beside the platform, on the polished stone floor of the Awakening Hall, on his back, with his spear across his chest and his one finger and his thumb closed round it, the way the dead lie in the ground. Yuanshu had never seen him lie down. Not once in two years.

> **He says: a captain goes out ahead of the line.**
>
> **He says: but he goes out on his feet, and he has never gone up a thread, and Seed says the dead go lighter if they are lying down. So.**
>
> **He says: if anyone tells the Spear Corps he lay down, he will deny it.**

"*Gao Min,*" said Yuanshu.

"*Ready,*" said the acting director of the Awakening Bureau, and pressed the stopwatch.

He closed his eyes.

---

Up.

The way he had gone before, through the staff, across the meter of air into the warm black stone, and up the crown of the tree of Laws inside it, and onto the thread. A drop of water on a string in the rain, sliding. Fast. Out through the roof of the hall and the sky over Lingzhou and the blue and then the black.

But not alone.

Behind him, beside him, all round him, coming up out of the War Memorial Cemetery and the slope by the river and stone No. 7 and a nameless valley cemetery in the Northern Wastes and the bottom of the sea off Kesse, along the net of the dead, nine by nine by nine, and up into the eleven stones, sideways, the way the eleven had gone, and up the threads: the dead.

Not bodies. Embers. Cold, dim, ash-gray, the color of the dead when they are banked, each one a small spark going up a thread in the dark. Thousands. Ten thousand. Twelve. Like the sparks that go up a chimney from a fire someone has just banked for the night.

The Spear Corps. Two hundred and seventy-two, in a knot of sparks round him, close, the way they stood round stone No. 7. Grid Nine, forty, a little way off, looking at everything. The First Legion in their rows of nine, which were rows of nine even as sparks, because that was who they were. The Watch of the Shelf, eleven hundred and forty-six, cold, slow, the color of deep water.

And ahead of him, on the thread, leading, the way he always led, a single ember, cold and steady and dim, that he would have known among a million.

> **He says: keep up, boy.**

---

The dock worker's rough hand caught his on the way up, as it had before. The old man who liked the sea said *there you are*. Wen Jia, from the bottom, briskly: *don't let go of the knot. Either knot.* And then the keeper's field went by, gold and vast and warm, a third smaller, and from it, as they passed, very rough, very close, a voice like a hearth:

> ***...GO CAREFULLY.***
>
> ***...COME BACK.***
>
> ***...I WILL ASK YOU WHAT YOU WANT WHEN YOU DO.***

And the branch, a darkness where the far lights bent, with its small gold bud at the end. It turned as they went past. He was sure of it this time. A very small turning. The way a bud turns on a branch toward something going by.

> **Twelve minutes.**
>
> **Eight hours and forty-eight to go.**

Along the arm. Past the place where the third field had been pruned and gone dark from one side like a page burning. Past the place where the Mouth had been.

And down below the thread, in the dark, very cold, a great stillness, and at the center of it, small, a line of embers, standing, facing out.

---

The cold fire.

He had been there before. Once, in his body, on an island that was getting smaller every night. He had stood on the edge of the quiet part in front of a curtain and held a door open for nineteen hours for the walls of a Hunger, and the Sixth had burned in the eyes of the Mouth for thirty-one hours, singing, and sixty soldiers of the Third Battalion had stood at the curtain and said *we can stand here a little longer*, and the island had gone home without them.

The Mouth was gone now. The cold fire was what was left: a vast cold stillness in the dark, the shape of a place where a great deal of burning had once been, and had stopped. And at its edge, where the curtain had been, sixty embers. Standing. In a line. Facing out.

The thread to the deep water went right over their heads.

The river of sparks going up it slowed.

Not because he told it to. Because the ember at the front did. Old Seven stopped on the thread, a cold dim spark in the dark, and turned, and looked down.

And from the line of sixty below, one ember came up off the edge of the cold fire. Slowly. A short one. Square in the shoulders, if a spark can have shoulders. Holding something over its shoulder that, in the shape the dead keep even when they are only embers, was very clearly a pick.

It came up onto the thread and stopped in front of Old Seven.

> **Huo says...**

Seed stopped.

He felt it stop. The way it had stopped on the battlement when an old adjutant said *message delivered*. The way it stopped when something was too big to carry through all at once and it had to put it down and pick it up again in pieces.

> **Huo says: sir.**
>
> **Huo says: you lay down.**
>
> **Huo says: I can tell. You're going light. You only go light when you're lying down. I saw you lie down once. In the Black Tide. On the wall. When the boy who lost his squad wouldn't take your hand. You lay down next to him so he wasn't the only one lying down.**
>
> **Huo says: nobody else saw. I did. I've never told anyone.**

Old Seven did not say anything. Embers do not say anything. But he did not move off the thread.

> **Huo says: show me the arm.**

---

He did not know how a spark could show anything. He watched it happen anyway.

The ember that was Old Seven, cold and steady and dim, lifted something. Not an arm; there was no arm, only the shape the dead keep. But the shape of an arm. The left. A sergeant's arm, put on in a courtyard, among potted plants, by a boy who had not known anatomy. With the shape of a hand at the end of it.

One finger. And a thumb.

The ember that was Huo looked at it for a long moment.

> **Huo says: you lost two of my fingers.**
>
> **He says: in the round chamber. Both at once.**
>
> **Huo says: those were good fingers. I had those fingers for forty years.**
>
> **He says: I know.**
>
> **Huo says...**

And then something came through the staff and the thread and the place a little above his eyes that Seed did not translate, because it did not need to, because it was not words. It was the sound, in the shape the dead keep, of a short square sergeant of the Third Battalion who had stood at the edge of a cold fire for months facing out, laughing.

> **Huo says: good. That means you were using them.**

---

The sergeant turned to him then. The ember with the pick. To the boy on the thread, who was not an ember, who was a drop of water on a string with a small gray knot in his right hand and a white staff that was not there and the warmth of his wife's hand on a chest a very long way down.

He had said this name every day. Every morning. Out loud. To nobody. To the room. On a riverbank in the spring. On the north wall. In the banking hall. In Port Halvard, in a hotel, at three in the morning.

He had never said it to his face.

"*Huo Changsheng,*" said Zhou Yuanshu. "*Sergeant. Third Battalion.*"

He found he could not say the rest. He had said it every day for months, and now that the man was in front of him, he could not.

The ember waited.

"*You held,*" he said.

The sergeant of the Third Battalion stood on the thread in the dark over the cold fire, with the shape of a pick on his shoulder, and looked at him.

> **Huo says: I know. You told me. Every day. I heard you.**
>
> **Huo says: you held too. The door. Nineteen hours. I was there. I watched you.**
>
> **Huo says: so we're even.**
>
> **Huo says: now. Where's this man who wants to go home.**

---

The Sixty came up off the cold fire.

All of them. Fifty-nine embers in a line behind their sergeant, up from the edge of the stillness where they had stood since the island went, onto the thread, into the river of sparks. The Spear Corps made room for them. The First Legion made room. Nobody told anyone to. The Third Battalion of the Eastern Line came into the middle of it, behind its captain, where it had always marched.

And below them, the cold fire, empty now. For the first time since the Mouth went, nobody standing at its edge, facing out.

> **Huo says: it's all right. There's nobody left in there to keep the cold off. The last ones went through the door months ago. We were just standing there because nobody told us we could stop.**

He looked down at the empty edge of the cold fire.

Then ahead. Along the thread. Past the end of the arm. Into the deep water between arms where there was no map at all, and the thread ran on into the dark, still, held, and somewhere at the far end of it, something enormous and patient and complicated was sitting on eleven threads in the dark, holding a burning man up to its face, looking at him.

> **Forty-one minutes.**
>
> **Eight hours and nineteen to go.**
>
> **He says: forward.**

They went into the deep water.
