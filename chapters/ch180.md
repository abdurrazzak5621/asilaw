## Chapter 180: Where the Threads Are Tied

> **Every thread in it goes somewhere.**

Seed said it fast, the way it said things when it had seen something all at once and was afraid of losing it.

> **Look. Not at the hole. At the threads around the hole. When they closed it, they did not come from nowhere. They came from above. From further up the leg. And those came from the body. And those came from the back.**
>
> **Everything in it runs to one place.**
>
> **The back of the head. Behind the face. Where the lamp is.**
>
> **It is a knot.**

He saw it. Through the Field, through Seed's eyes, laid over the tall woven shape standing above him with its blank face holding Major Zhuo: the threads were not a weave. Not really. They were a net, the way the Lattice was a net, and every strand of it ran back, by one path or another, to a single tight bright knot at the back of the head, behind the smooth gray-white curve of the face. The way Mercer's threads had run back into his chest on the black glass. The way forty-one thousand threads had run up the inside of a spire on the Western Pass into a single cup.

> **When you cut one thread, the knot pulls the others across to close it.**
>
> **If you cut the knot, nothing pulls.**

"*Qin Hao,*" said Yuanshu.

Qin Hao was already looking up.

Nine meters. The back of a blank face, tipped forward and up, pointed straight at a gold column that was rising off the pale ground an inch at a time, fifty meters away. White fire was running down both of his arms from the shoulder to the fingertips.

"*My father held the inner gate for four hours,*" he said. "*I've told you that.*"

"*You've told me.*"

"*I'm telling you again so you don't say anything stupid while I do this.*"

---

"*Wait,*" said Mo Shi.

His eyes were shut. His voice was cracked and very calm. Blood was running out of his brother's nose beside him in a thin steady line, and Mo Xing was holding his hand so hard his knuckles were white.

"*He can't fly up there. The two stepping lamps. They're sweeping high as well as low. The pocket's on the ground. Twelve meters wide on the ground. At nine meters up, it's a sliver. Half a meter. Point four seconds.*" He swallowed. "*He'll be seen. Both of them. And if they both see him at once...*"

> **...they will both hold him.**
>
> **Straight up.**

Qin Hao did not look away from the knot.

"*Then make it bigger,*" he said.

"*I can't make it bigger. It's the lamps. It's how they...*"

"*Not you,*" said Shen Lingyue.

---

She was standing beside the walker's foot in the pocket with her grandfather's sword in both hands, lit pale, and she was not looking at the holding walker. She was looking north. A kilometer away across the pale ground of the empty field, at the far point of the triangle, where the nearer of the two stepping walkers stood with its blank face turning, three degrees, still, three degrees, still.

"*One lamp,*" she said. "*Su Mingli said. With one lamp the pocket's not a pocket. It's everywhere except the slice.*"

"*You can't reach it from here,*" said Yuanshu. "*It's a kilometer.*"

"*It's inside the Field,*" said Lingyue. "*Everything in the Field runs downhill to you.*" She looked at him. "*You're standing right here.*"

He understood. A half-second before she did it.

> **...she is going to throw the light uphill.**
>
> **Away from you. Against the slope. All the way across the field. And let it fall back.**
>
> **Onto the lamp.**
>
> **That is not possible.**
>
> **It is only not possible because nobody has done it.**

She raised her grandfather's sword over her head, point up. And she did not let the light fall. She held it. A great bead of silver-gray fire gathering at the tip of a King's blade, heavier and heavier, until the sword shook in her hands with the weight of it and the pale ground cracked under her feet the way the stone had cracked on the training ground.

And then she swung.

Not at anything. At the sky. A long overhand stroke, like a woman casting a net from the stern of a boat, and the bead of pale fire left the point of the blade and went up, up, out over the empty field in a long high arc, against the slope, against everything, a kilometer, while every dead soldier in the field turned their skull to watch it go.

At the top of the arc, it stopped. Hung. A silver-gray star in the soft gold light.

Then it fell. Downhill. The way everything in the Field fell. And the far walker's blank face was between it and the bottom of the slope.

It landed on the walker's face like a hand on the back of a neck.

The blank curve tipped forward. Past level. Past forty-five degrees. Until it was pointed straight down at the pale ground between its own long feet. And held there.

Lingyue went down on one knee in the pocket with the King's sword planted point-first in the ground in front of her, both hands on the hilt, shaking, holding a walker's face down a kilometer away by the weight of her own light.

"*Go,*" she said, through her teeth. "*I can do about a minute.*"

---

"*One lamp,*" said Mo Shi. "*It's everywhere. It's all pocket except the slice. Qin Hao. Now. Up. Now.*"

Qin Hao went up.

White fire under his feet, the way it had gone up the spire on the Western Pass. Straight up the back of the holding walker, past the woven legs, past the woven body, nine meters, to the back of the blank face where every thread in it ran to one tight bright knot. And the last stepping lamp, a kilometer and a half away at the third point of the triangle, swept round, three degrees, still, three degrees, still, and did not pass over him.

He put both hands on the knot.

The white fire went in.

---

It did not cut. Fire that burned what bound did not cut. It went into the knot the way water goes into a tangle of string, loosening, and the knot came undone. Not all at once. Strand by strand. Each one letting go of the next.

And every thread in the walker, with nothing at the other end of it pulling, let go.

The walker came apart.

He would remember it afterward more clearly than anything else in the field. Nine meters of woven silver-white thread, standing above him with its blank face holding a man, and then not standing. Unraveling. From the head down. Millions of threads, all at once, slipping loose of each other and falling, slowly, softly, like a cloud of silk dropped from a height, down over the pale ground, down over Old Seven and the Spear Corps and the living in the pocket, glittering in the soft gold light.

Some of the threads were still alive when they fell.

They lashed. Blind, the way a severed nerve twitches. Not at anything. At everything. And where they lashed through the rows of the Spear Corps, banked and unseen and standing in the falling silk with their rusted spears, they cut.

> **Twenty-three.**
>
> **Twenty-three of the Spear Corps. Unwoven. The threads went through their embers like wire through cheese.**
>
> **I am sorry. I did not know they would do that.**

Fifty meters away, Major Zhuo Wenli's gold column, a meter off the ground, stopped rising. Hung for a moment. And settled back down onto the pale ground beside three empty rings, gently, the way a leaf settles.

---

"*Lingyue,*" said Yuanshu.

"*Thirty seconds,*" she said. Her face was gray. The King's sword was shaking in the ground in front of her. "*Maybe.*"

Qin Hao came down out of the falling silk on white fire and landed in a crouch on the pale ground, and did not get up.

"*I can't,*" he said. "*Again. Not yet.*" The white fire on his arms was guttering like a candle at the end of its wick. "*That took everything.*"

> **Two walkers left. One with its face held down by Lingyue. Thirty seconds. One stepping, a kilometer and a half away.**
>
> **The stepping one felt the first one come apart.**
>
> **It has stopped stepping.**

He felt it. Through the Field. A kilometer and a half away across the empty field, the third walker had gone still. Its blank face was not turning any more. It was turning one way. Toward the place where its companion had stood. Toward the falling silk. Toward the pocket.

Toward him.

His head turned. Three degrees.

Then all the way.

Straight up.

---

It found him the way the tribulation's gaze had found him in the glass, and the Collector's attention had found him in the hall in Yamato: completely, all at once, from the outside in. But it was not warm, and it was not patient, and it was not interested. It was a lamp. It held. And something came down the beam toward him, the way something had come down a beam toward Lu Zhengtian in the round chamber, to lift.

He could not move. His face was turned up. His mind was turned up. He could feel the soft gold light of the field going far away, and the pale ground under his feet going far away, and Lingyue shouting his name a kilometer away from inside a cloud of falling silk.

And it reached him, and began to read, and found Seed.

Standing in the open. With nothing in front of it.

> **I am here.**
>
> **I am not hiding.**
>
> **Look at me if you want to.**

The beam stopped.

Not lifted. Stopped. Held, perfectly still, on the white-lettered thing standing in the open behind his eyes. The way the Collector's attention had stopped on Seed in the hall, and something had happened in it that Seed had called surprise.

> **It knows what I am made of.**
>
> **It does not know why I am here.**
>
> **It is not lifting. It is looking.**
>
> **One point one seconds. Perhaps more. It does not know what to do.**

"*Lingyue,*" said Yuanshu. Out loud. Through his teeth. With his face turned up and his mind turned up and the beam holding still on Seed. "*Let go.*"

---

She let go.

A kilometer away, the second walker's face came up off the ground, free. And the bead of silver-gray light that had been holding it down came loose, and Lingyue did not let it fall. She was a Lord of Light now and she had spent four days learning to hold a thing and one morning learning to throw it, and she took the light back, across a kilometer and a half of empty field, the way you take back a fishing line, and swung, overhand, against the slope, and let it go again.

It went up. Hung. Fell.

Onto the face of the walker that was holding him.

The blank curve tipped forward, past level, until it pointed at the pale ground between its feet.

The beam left him.

He came down onto his knees in the pocket, gasping, with his face pointed at the ground and blood running out of his nose, and Bai Lu's hands already on his shoulders, green light running into them.

> **I am here.**
>
> **I am still here.**
>
> **It looked at me for two point three seconds and then Lingyue put its face on the floor.**
>
> **I would like to stand in the open less often in future. I chose it. I would choose it again. I would like it to be less often.**

---

The second walker, its face free, did not come toward them.

He felt it, from his knees on the pale ground. A kilometer away, nine meters of woven thread with its blank face turned toward the falling silk where its companion had been, and toward the third, its face held down by a girl with a King's sword a kilometer and a half away.

It stood there for a long moment.

Then it turned. All the way round. Away from them, away from the few who stayed, away from the keeper. And walked.

Not fast. They never hurried. It walked away across the empty field, between the faint rings on the pale ground where the columns had stood, in long slow strides, toward the haze at the edge of the field where the light went gold and far.

> **It is leaving.**
>
> **It has seen what it came to see.**
>
> **It is going to tell someone.**

"*Stop it,*" said Qin Hao, from the ground. He was trying to get up. White fire flickered on his fingers and went out. "*Someone stop it.*"

Nobody could.

Old Seven threw. The spear went into the walker's leg from two hundred meters and stopped there, smoking, and the threads closed over the hole, and the walker did not even break stride. Three hundred, two hundred and seventy-seven, rusted spears went after it, and it walked through them like a man walking through rain. Mo Xing tried to fold and collapsed on the pale ground with blood pouring out of his nose and both eyes, and his brother caught him.

The walker reached the edge of the field.

It did not stop. It walked into the gold haze where the field ended, and the haze took it, and it was gone.

---

The third walker, with its face held down by Lingyue's light, took a long time to die.

Qin Hao did it in the end. Twenty minutes later, on his feet again, white-faced, with Bai Lu's green light in his arms and his notebook still in his breast pocket. He flew up the back of the last walker with the white fire guttering and put his hands on the knot behind its face and held them there until it came undone. It came apart slower than the first, as if it had more to let go of. When the threads fell, the Spear Corps had already stepped back out of reach, because Old Seven had told them to.

Lingyue let go of the light.

She sat down on the pale ground beside her grandfather's sword, very suddenly, the way she had sat down in the surf at Port Abiri. She did not say anything for a while. Then she looked at Yuanshu, on his knees a meter away with Bai Lu's hands on his shoulders, and said:

"Don't ever do that again."

"Which part?"

"Get looked at," said Shen Lingyue. "By anything. Ever."

"I didn't choose it."

"I know," she said. "Seed did. I'm going to talk to Seed."

> **...I would expect nothing less.**
