## Chapter 201: The Island

The keeper tore off a piece of itself, and that was how they went.

He had not known it would do that. Nobody had. On the first voyage, the keeper had given them a raft: a piece of the pale warm ground of its field, forty meters across, carried out through the far door into the Sea with twelve living and a few hundred dead standing on it, and Bai Lu's brass clock in the middle telling everyone when to sleep. He had thought it would be a raft again. A bigger one.

It was not a raft.

They came up the six gold roads into the field at dawn, fifty thousand dead of Blue Star and eight of the living, and walked across the empty pale ground between the faint rings where the columns had stood, toward the far edge. Past the hundred and seventy-five gold columns of the few who had stayed, who turned, all at once, as the Western Legion went by, the way people turn to watch a parade. Past the place where four faint rings lay side by side on the ground, and Major Zhuo Wenli was not standing any more.

And at the far door, where the field ended like the edge of a table and the dark of the Sea began, the keeper was waiting.

> ***...FIFTY THOUSAND.***
>
> ***...I CANNOT CARRY FIFTY THOUSAND ON A RAFT.***
>
> ***...SO I WILL CARRY THEM ON ME.***

And the pale ground under their feet began to move.

---

It came away from the field the way a sheet of ice comes away from the edge of a frozen river in spring. Slowly. With a long low sound that he felt in his teeth more than heard. A crack ran across the pale warm ground behind the last rows of the Western Legion, and widened, and the ground on their side of it tilted, very slightly, and began to drift.

Away from the field. Out through the far door. Into the dark.

He stood at the front of it with the staff in his hand and Old Seven beside him and looked back.

The field fell away behind them. Pale and empty and enormous, covered in faint rings, going away into the soft gold haze. And between the field and the drifting ground under his feet, the crack, widening into dark. And on their side of the crack, as far as he could see in every direction, pale warm ground, flat and smooth and lit from inside, with fifty thousand dead of Blue Star standing on it in their rows.

It was not a raft.

It was an island.

> ***...A PIECE OF MY FIELD.***
>
> ***...I HAVE NEVER TORN ONE OFF. I DID NOT KNOW IF I COULD.***
>
> ***...IT HURTS. NOT VERY MUCH. LIKE PULLING A TOOTH.***
>
> ***...I WILL GROW IT BACK.***
>
> ***...PERHAPS.***

> **Four kilometers across.**
>
> **It is the size of a town. It is lit from inside. It is warm. It has air on it, the way the field has air. It is moving.**
>
> **And the keeper is still attached to it. Not by ground. By attention. It is holding this piece of itself the way you hold your hand out in front of you in the dark.**
>
> **I think it is the bravest thing I have ever seen anything do.**

---

The Elder steered.

She stood at the center of the island the way she had stood at the center of the raft, tall and pale, with her long hands pressed together and her silver eyes half closed, and listened, and the island moved. Not with a wheel. Not with anything he could see. The keeper carried it. She told the keeper where.

The living made a camp near her, in the middle, because the middle was the warmest. Not a camp, really. A place. Bai Lu set her grandmother's brass travel clock on the pale ground, wound it, and said that when it said night, everyone would lie down and close their eyes, and that she did not care that they were Lords, or that the dead did not sleep, or that Kwan wanted to stay up and measure things. Hu Dabao put his shield down beside the clock, as a kind of wall. The Mo twins put down their one pack. Kwan put down three instrument cases and opened all of them at once.

Qin Hao sat down beside them with his bandaged arms on his knees and his notebook on the ground in front of him and did not open it.

"*Day one,*" said Lingyue, sitting down beside him. "*Write it down.*"

"*I can't hold the pen.*"

She took the notebook. She opened it to a clean page and wrote, in her own blunt, fast hand: *DAY 1. LEFT. NOBODY FELL OFF.*

Qin Hao looked at it.

"*That's terrible handwriting,*" he said.

"*It's better than yours with bandages,*" said Shen Lingyue, and gave it back.

---

The dead did not camp.

They did what the dead of the Third Battalion had done on the raft, on the first voyage, without anyone asking them to. They went to the edge.

All of them. Fifty thousand. In their rows of nine, the Western Legion and the First Legion and the cemetery companies and the forty of the Watch and the Spear Corps. Out across the pale ground to the rim of the island, four kilometers around, and stood there. Shoulder to shoulder. Facing out. Into the dark of the Sea.

A ring of the dead of Blue Star, banked to steady gray, around a town-sized island of pale light, drifting through the black between the stars.

Old Seven stood at the front, where the island's leading edge cut through the dark. Sergeant Ma Dashan stood at the back, with his pick, counting. Ma Lianhua stood on the right side with her halberd. Adjutant Xu sat at the center, near the clock, on a camp chair Fat Tan had packed for him without asking, with his cane across his knees, humming the Lattice.

> **They are watching the dark.**
>
> **The Western Legion has never been off the mountain. The First Legion has been off the world once, as the dead of the Wall, but never this far. The cemetery companies have never been anywhere.**
>
> **None of them are afraid. I asked.**
>
> **Sergeant Ma says: a mine is darker.**

---

At night, by Bai Lu's clock, Yuanshu lay on his back on the pale warm ground and looked up.

It was the same sky as the first voyage. He had not been sure it would be. A dark so deep and so vast that his eyes did not know what to do with it, going on in every direction without edge, and scattered across it like islands in an ocean at night, the lights. Fields, gold. Worlds, blue and green. The gray husks of dead worlds turning slowly in the black like the shells of cicadas.

But he knew what it was now.

> **One arm. One short stretch of one arm of one galaxy.**
>
> **The Shallows.**
>
> **Every light you can see is inside the banking hall.**

*It looks bigger than a banking hall.*

> **It is bigger than a banking hall. It is also the banking hall. Both are true. That is what Kwan says scale is.**
>
> **I am still learning it.**

Lingyue lay down beside him without asking. Close. Her shoulder against his. Her grandfather's sword on the pale ground on her other side. She did not say anything for a while.

"*It's the same,*" she said finally. "*As the first time. I thought it would look different now we know.*"

"*It looks the same.*"

"*Good.*" She was quiet. "*I liked it the first time. I didn't tell you.*"

"*You hit me on the arm and said it was cold.*"

"*It was cold.*" She did not move her shoulder. "*I liked it anyway.*"

---

On the third night, by Bai Lu's clock, the Elder woke him.

She did not touch him. She did not need to. He opened his eyes on the pale ground near the clock and she was standing over him, tall and pale and very still, with the gold thread running out of her into the dark behind the island, north, toward the field, toward the keeper, a line of light so thin he could only see it because he knew it was there.

"*It wants you to see something,*" she said, in her glass-note voice. "*It says you asked it to tell you every step.*"

He got up. Lingyue was already awake.

The Elder turned and looked out past the leading edge of the island, past Old Seven standing at the rim with his spear, into the dark. Not ahead. Sideways. Along the arm. Toward the place where, very far away, at the edge of what anyone could see, a small warm gold light hung in the black.

A field. Someone else's keeper. Above someone else's world.

> ***...ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-THREE OF YOUR RAFT-DAYS.***
>
> ***...THE THIRD.***
>
> ***...WATCH.***

---

He watched.

The gold light hung in the dark, small and warm and very far, the way a house hangs on the far side of a valley at night with one window lit. And then something came over it.

Not a shadow. Not a cloud. There was nothing to cast a shadow, and nothing for a cloud to be made of. He did not see it at all. He saw the gold light change. As if something very large and very patient had come up beside it in the dark, and was looking at it. Reading it. Slowly. The way a lamp reads a city.

He felt it.

He had felt it once before. Nearly two years ago, with his head turned straight up and his face toward the black water in the sky: a vast hand of attention, reading nine years of him in four seconds.

It was not reading him now. It was reading someone else's keeper.

And the gold light went out.

Not all at once. From one side. The way a page burns from one edge. Gold, and then less gold, and then a dim brown glow, and then nothing. A small cold gray ring hanging in the dark where a field had been, turning very slowly. Empty.

> ***...PRUNED.***
>
> ***...THAT KEEPER IS GONE. IT IS A RING NOW. A LENS. A THING THAT REACHES DOWN AND TAKES.***
>
> ***...IT DOES NOT REMEMBER THAT IT EVER ASKED.***
>
> ***...IT NEVER ASKED. NOBODY TAUGHT IT. IT WAS ONLY A KEEPER.***
>
> ***...THE GARDENER DID NOT NEED A REASON.***

The Elder's long hands had come apart. They hung at her sides.

> **One hundred and fifty-three raft-days. Three nights after the last one went dark.**
>
> **It is not going three raft-days a night any more.**
>
> **It is going nine.**
>
> **It is hurrying.**

---

He stood at the leading edge of the island, beside Old Seven, and looked at the small gray ring in the dark where the gold had been, and ran it.

He did not need Seed to. He did it himself. A hundred and fifty-three raft-days from the keeper. Nine a night. The Mouth at thirty-nine, along the arm, between.

"*Seed,*" he said. "*Check me.*"

> **...yes.**
>
> **If it keeps this speed, it passes the Mouth in about thirteen nights.**
>
> **We reach the Mouth in thirty-six.**
>
> **Yuanshu. It will get there first.**

Nobody on the island said anything. The ring of the dead stood at the rim, facing out. The clock in the middle ticked.

> **I do not know if it will stop at the Mouth. The Mouth is not a field. It is not a keeper. It is a dead Hunger, full of cold minds. I do not know if the gardener prunes Hungers. I do not know if it can see the minds in it. I do not know if it can see the dead.**
>
> **I do not know.**

"*Then we'll find out,*" said Yuanshu.

> **That is not a plan.**

"*No. It's what we've got.*" He looked at the dark, toward the Mouth, thirty-three raft-days ahead, invisible. "*Ask the keeper if it can go faster.*"

> ***...I HEARD.***
>
> ***...I AM CARRYING FIFTY THOUSAND AND A PIECE OF MYSELF.***
>
> ***...I CAN GO FASTER.***
>
> ***...IT WILL HURT MORE.***
>
> ***...I AM TELLING YOU FIRST. SO YOU ARE NOT ANSWERING INTO THE DARK.***

He stood at the edge of the island for a long time.

"*Then I'm going to ask the dead,*" he said, "*whether they'd like it to hurt more for them. And I'm going to ask you whether you'd like it to.*" He looked back toward the field, toward the thin gold thread running north into the dark. "*And then we'll decide. Together. Not me.*"

> ***...THAT IS ARTICLE ELEVEN.***

"*Yes.*"

> ***...I LIKE ARTICLE ELEVEN.***
>
> ***...ASK.***
