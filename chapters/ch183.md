## Chapter 183: Eighty Days

General Han Yizhou came to the Ossuary on a Wednesday, alone, in plain clothes, and read the wall for four hours.

He did not ask anyone anything. He came in through the bronze door at ten in the morning with his cap under his arm and stood in front of the first column, the one from the cemetery, the one that began with Captain Qi Zhenshan in Xiaoyu's earliest, shakiest capitals, and read it. All of it. Then the next. Then the next. He did not skip. He did not hurry. Fat Tan brought him a chair at noon and he did not sit in it.

At two, he reached the column headed GRID NINE, and stood in front of it for a long time.

Then he reached up, very carefully, the way Old Seven reached up with his spear to straighten a line, and touched one name with two fingers, and took his hand away.

He came back the next month. And the month after that. Always on a Wednesday. Always alone. He never said anything to anyone except, once, to Xiaoyu, who was on the stepladder at the end of the wall adding names from the Watch of the Shelf: "Your capitals have improved."

"I've had a lot of practice," said Xiaoyu.

"Yes," said General Han, looking at the wall. "I can see that."

---

On the eleventh day after the field, Mo Xing tried to fold a pebble from one hand to the other, and it did not move.

On the twentieth, it did not move.

On the thirty-second, he sat on the floor of the old banking hall under the board with the pebble in his palm and his brother beside him, and stared at it for an hour, and it did not move, and he threw it across the marble floor so hard it bounced off the bronze door and Old Seven, standing beside the door, did not even turn his head.

"I'm done," said Mo Xing. "I burned it out. It's gone."

"Bai Lu said two months," said Mo Shi.

"Bai Lu said she was guessing."

"She's a good guesser." Mo Shi got up and walked across the marble floor and picked up the pebble from beside the bronze door, and came back, and put it in his brother's palm, and closed his brother's fingers over it. "Again."

On the forty-first day, at three in the morning, Yuanshu woke on the vault steps to the sound of someone crying very quietly on the floor under the board. He looked. Mo Xing was sitting cross-legged with his hands cupped in his lap, and in his right hand, where it had not been a second before, was a gray river pebble. His left hand was empty.

His brother was asleep against his shoulder.

Mo Xing did not wake him. He just sat there in the lamplight, looking at the pebble in his right hand, with tears running down his face, and after a while he moved it back to his left. And then to his right. And then to his left.

---

In Yamato, on the fifty-fourth day, a young man on the fourth terrace turned his head.

Seed heard about it from Ren, at midnight, on the phone, in a voice that was trying very hard to be calm and not managing it. The young man who had said *I know, I still want it* to Lingyue in the dark, and lifted his face, and gone hollow. A woman from the Quiet in Osaka had been sitting beside him for seven weeks saying his name. Tanaka Ryo. Tanaka Ryo. You're on a mountain. It's morning. Someone put a blanket on you.

On the fifty-fourth morning, when she said it, his head turned. Not three degrees. All the way. From straight up, down, to her.

He did not speak. He looked at her. And then he looked down at his hands, in his lap, as if he had never seen them before, and began to cry.

"*He doesn't know what he wanted,*" said Ren. "*He can't remember. He says there's a hole where it was. He says it's like reaching for a step in the dark and the step isn't there.*" Her voice cracked and steadied. "*But he's back. He knows his name. He asked for tea.*"

> **The want is gone. It is kept. Above the sky.**
>
> **The man is not.**
>
> **He will have to want something new. From the beginning. Like a child.**
>
> **I think that is possible. I do not know. Nobody has ever come back from being kept by that.**
>
> **He is the first.**

By the eightieth day there were eleven.

Hara Setsuko was not one of them. Neither was the Abbot. Ren sat with him every morning in what was left of the hall and said his name once, quietly, and then sat in silence for an hour. Twice, Seed thought it saw his head move. Once, it was sure. One degree. Toward the door.

Ren did not stop.

---

The messages from inside the Mouth came on Day 272 and Day 303.

Xiaoyu wrote them on the wall. Under them, small, at the end of each line, she wrote the seconds. Twenty-six. Thirty-one.

> ***Day 272. Worlds asked: 41. Burning low: 29.***
>
> ***The fires are quieter. They do not scream at each other now. They mutter.***
>
> ***Liang Fu can play the card game. He won once. He did not know he had won. The sergeant told him. He smiled. The sergeant says it is the first time.***
>
> ***I still have the queen of hearts. Tell the captain. — H.***

> ***Day 303. Worlds asked: 41. Burning low: 31.***
>
> ***We have stopped asking new worlds. The Mouth has stopped moving close enough to any. It drifts.***
>
> ***The Sixth say it is like being in a house where the stove is going out. You put on more coats. You sit closer together. You wait to see if anyone comes.***
>
> ***The sergeant says somebody will come. He says the captain would not leave his men.***
>
> ***Tell the captain. — H.***

Old Seven read them every month, from the beginning of the wall to the end. It took him most of a night now. When he reached the line from Day 303, he stood in front of it for a long time with his spear grounded, and then he turned around and walked across the marble floor to the vault steps where Yuanshu was sitting, and stood in front of him, and did not say anything.

> **He is not asking.**
>
> **He is standing there so that you know he read it.**

"I know," said Yuanshu. "Ten months. Maybe less."

> **He says: good.**
>
> **He says: I have marched further.**

---

He learned to fly sideways on the sixty-first day.

It was not dignified. Lingyue taught him, because Qin Hao refused on the grounds that watching was more fun, and because she said she had learned from a King of Gravity how weight worked and that he was doing it wrong. He was trying to push himself along, she said, like a man pushing a cart. A Lord did not push. A Lord decided where *down* was, and fell there.

"Do less," said Seed, out loud, through the speaker in the Ossuary wall, every time he crashed.

"I'm doing less."

"You are doing less hard," said Seed. "That is different."

He crashed into the Academy flagpole on the sixty-first day. On the sixty-second, into the roof of the dining hall. On the sixty-third, at dusk, he went out over the training ground a meter above the sand, sideways, slowly, very straight, like a man walking on a floor nobody else could see, and came down at the far end on his feet, and stood there.

Lingyue, on the steps, put her hands together. Twice. Very slowly. The way you applaud a child who has finally tied his shoes.

On the steps beside her, Qin Hao wrote something in his notebook, and turned it round so Yuanshu could see it from the far end of the training ground. It said, in very large letters: *SIXTY-THREE DAYS.*

---

On the seventy-third day, Ngozi Eze called from the Southern Compact.

She was the woman who had sat in the Compact's chair at Concordia because there was no King to send. A tall, serious, careful administrator from the capital who had been Ifeoma's deputy minister of the coast for nine years and had, she said, never once been allowed to make a decision without being told she was a child.

"*The Architects have written,*" she said.

He did not say anything for a moment.

"*Through the Council. A message. Very short. The Elder translated it.*" Ngozi Eze read it out, in her careful voice. "*Seven of us will come to the hill at Port Abiri on the day we said, one year after. We have been to forty-one worlds. There was no one on any of them. We said it anyway. We will say it again on the hill. We were told there will be an old woman there. We would like to say it to her first.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*They don't know,*" said Ngozi Eze. "*They don't know she's gone.*"

"No."

"*Someone has to tell them. Before they get there.*" She was quiet. "*I'm the Compact's chair. I suppose it's me.*" A pause. "*Mister Zhou. I've never spoken to anything that wasn't human. I've read the records. I've read what she said to them. I don't know how to tell seven things that built the Hungers that the woman they've come halfway across the Shallows to apologize to is at the bottom of the sea.*"

"Then don't tell them," said Yuanshu.

"*What?*"

"Let her tell them."

The line was quiet.

"*She's dead.*"

"She said only to speak. She said what she wanted to say. Seed has every word." He looked at the board. At the column with no heading, with *Ifeoma Adeyemi. She said not to be soft* by itself in the middle. "And Akosua Mensah has been waiting on that hill for a year, every morning, to make them say it for two hours. One for her, and one for Ifeoma." He was quiet. "Ifeoma told her she'd know if they cheated."

---

On the eightieth day, the night before he went south, Yuanshu sat on the vault steps of the Ossuary with the staff across his knees, and Seed played him the bell.

It did that sometimes now, at night. Eleven minutes from the edge of the keeper's eye. The long low tone, the wait, the small bells saying *Shiomi*, the longer wait. Over and over. Every four hundred and twelve seconds. The three hundred and seventy-two monks under the gates on the mountain in Yamato listened to it every night through Ren's phone, and wrote down anything that changed, and nothing ever had.

> **It changed.**

He sat up.

> **The interval. It was four hundred and twelve seconds for every recording since the first. Every one.**
>
> **The night after the field, it was four hundred and eleven. One second. I thought it was noise. I wrote it down and did not tell you, because I did not want to say something I was not sure of.**
>
> **It stayed at four hundred and eleven for seventy-three nights.**
>
> **Six nights ago, four hundred and ten. Five nights ago, four hundred and nine. Last night, four hundred and seven.**
>
> **I am sure now.**

"Someone's ringing it faster."

> **Yes.**
>
> **And there is something new in the wait. In the long silence after the name. Very faint. A second bell. Smaller. A different note. Once.**
>
> **The monks say it is not the night bell of Shiomi. They say it is the other bell. The one the village rang when a boat came in.**
>
> **The one that means: we see you.**

He sat very still on the vault steps under the long white wall.

"The night after the field," he said.

> **The night after the field. The night after something walked away across the keeper's field into the gold haze, to tell what sent it about a girl who throws light uphill and a boy with a white staff and a thing made of the old stuff that stands in the open and says: look at me.**
>
> **I do not know if the two things are the same thing.**
>
> **I do not know who else was listening.**
>
> **But someone in the deep water has started ringing faster. And someone has rung the bell that means: we see you.**
