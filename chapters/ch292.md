## Chapter 292: What the First Wanted

Dacian Severin walked into the Lens chamber with one arm hanging and stopped between ten Paragons of the Great Houses and the root of every face in the Federation, and the first thing he did was apologize.

Not to them. To Lingyue.

"*I'm sorry,*" he said. In Huaxian. Perfectly. "*I said the corridor was mine. I said anyone who came to the Lens would come past me. Ostrava came past me. And they came round.*" He did not look at the ten. He looked at the woman with the sword and the baby. "*You had to stand here. With a child. I'm sorry.*"

"*I'd have stood here anyway,*" said Shen Lingyue.

"*I know,*" said the man in the torn gray coat. "*I'm sorry anyway.*"

Then he turned round.

---

The ten Paragons on the edge of the broken wall did not move.

They had not moved since the woman with the sword said she was not moving. Ten of them, in the colors of their Houses, burning, the only people in the Federation still burning on purpose, with faces at their throats that were not their faces, standing on the edge of a hole in the wall of a ship in the orbit of Anthos, looking at the man who had been First.

He was bleeding. A great deal. Seed said so, quietly, in the place above his eyes. Ostrava Iron had come past him in the corridor, and she had not done it gently, and a Star Monarch did not bleed the way other people bled. The arm hanging at his side was broken in more places than Seed would count aloud. There was blood on his face and in his hair and down the front of the gray coat Halvarde had given him.

He stood in front of the Lens with his good hand at his side and looked at them.

"*Kael,*" he said. To the young one, in the blue of Teller. "*You're crying.*"

The young Paragon put his hand to his face.

"*You've been crying since the Assembly,*" said Dacian Severin. "*I saw you. In the eighth tier. When Haskel said he wanted to see his brother.*"

Kael Teller did not say anything.

"*Brannoch,*" said Severin, to the next. "*Iyra. Tolland.*" One at a time. Every name. Ten Paragons of the Great Houses, and he knew all of them, the way Halvarde knew all the rowers. "*You said yesterday that you wanted to be first. In front of everyone. I heard you. The boy said it back.*"

He took a breath. It did not sound good.

"*I wanted that too,*" said the former First of the Ladder. "*For two hundred years. I was. It was the only thing I ever wanted. The stone opened me that way when I was twelve and I never wanted anything else.*"

He reached into the pocket of the torn gray coat with his good hand.

"*And I'd like to show you what it's worth.*"

---

He took out the face.

The black disc. Three gold marks, and a fourth above them, small, like a star. Cut from the Lens itself. The face of the First of the Ladder, which the House of Severin had worn for nine hundred years. Cracked from edge to edge, on the platform of the Awakening Hall in Lingzhou, when a boy whose stone had gone black let it read him and a harvest sack read it back.

It had light in it again. Two lines. True.

He held it up so they could see.

"*This was cut from that,*" he said, and did not look behind him at the Lens. "*Nine hundred years ago. A piece of the root. My House wore it. It made us first. Everything with a mark yielded to it.*" He turned it in his fingers. "*It's not my face. A girl on an orchard wrote that this morning and I read it and it's true. It was never anyone's face. It was a piece of a stone that was made to read children and mark how bright.*"

He turned round. Slowly. With one arm.

And walked to the Lens.

"*Dacian,*" said one of the ten. Iyra, in green. Her voice had gone very high. "*What are you doing?*"

"*Giving it back,*" said Dacian Severin.

He pressed the cracked black disc flat against the surface of the Lens, the disc of black stone the width of the chamber, ground so thin you could see through it. Where the lamp was. Where a fog-ringer was talking.

And let go.

---

The disc went in.

Not broke. Not fell. Went in. The way water goes into water. A piece of black stone cut from the root nine hundred years ago, the size of a coin, going back into the thing it had been cut from, slowly, the edges first, until there was only a small dark place on the surface of the Lens where it had been, and then not even that.

The crack in it closed as it went.

> **...Ichiro says: oh.**
>
> **He says: it's back. The bit that was missing. He didn't know there was a bit missing. He says it's like a tooth coming back that you didn't know you'd lost.**
>
> **He says: the Lens is whole.**
>
> **He says: it's never been whole. Not since the House cut the First's face out of it nine hundred years ago.**
>
> **He says: everything's... clearer. He can see further. Every face. Every stone. Every orchard. He says he can see stones he didn't know were there. Out past the Federation. Past the Shallows. Thousands.**
>
> **He says: Kiyo says to tell the man in the gray coat thank you. And that he's bleeding on her floor.**

---

The First of the Ladder sat down.

Not because he chose to. His knees went, the way Wanqing said knees went, deciding for you. He sat down on the floor of the Lens chamber with his back against the Lens, the way he had sat against the wall of this room for ninety days, and his broken arm in his lap, and his face, his own face, the one that was not a disc, gray.

"*Without it,*" he said, "*I'm just a man with a broken arm.*" He looked up at the ten on the edge of the hole in the wall. "*That's all. That's what's under it. Under the First. A man.*"

"*You're dying,*" said Kael Teller.

"*Probably,*" said Dacian Severin. "*Ostrava doesn't do anything halfway.*" He almost smiled. "*She'll be along. She went to the arrays. She's going to try to stop the rowers.*"

> **She is in the first array.**
>
> **She is standing in front of Halvarde.**
>
> **Halvarde has not moved.**
>
> **Halvarde is asking her what she wants.**

---

Kael Teller came down off the edge of the broken wall.

Slowly. A hundred years old, young for a Paragon, in the blue of the House of Teller, with tears on his face he had stopped trying to wipe. He came across the floor of the Lens chamber, past the woman with the sword, who did not move and did not lift it, past the boy with the staff, to the man sitting with his back against the Lens with a broken arm in his lap.

He knelt.

Not the way people knelt to the First of the Ladder. Not on the quay at Corraine, two hundred thousand people going down on the shingle out of ten thousand years of habit. The way you kneel beside someone who is hurt.

"*What do you want?*" said Kael Teller.

He said it badly. Like a word in a book. With the grade in it, and then without.

The former First of the Ladder looked at him for a long time.

"*Nobody's asked me that,*" he said. "*Except a fog-ringer. And a sack. And a C.*" He breathed. It did not sound good at all now. "*You're the first Paragon who ever has.*"

"*I'm asking.*"

"*I know.*"

Dacian Severin turned his head, with difficulty, and looked at the boy from Blue Star.

"*You said,*" he said. "*In the Assembly. The Matriarch said she'd stand beside the stone on Anthos. On every reading morning. Until someone chooses to sit in it.*" He was quiet for a moment. "*Is that still true? That someone can choose?*"

Yuanshu knelt down on the other side of him.

"*Yes,*" he said.

"*Even someone who did what I did.*"

"*The Old Chord,*" said Yuanshu. "*Seven Singers. They sang their own children up a thread to a hand in the dark for longer than you've had a Federation. And forty-one burned in a thread because they pushed them. And they asked to go into the stones of their world when they stop, and ask every child.*" He held the staff. "*Nobody said it was enough. It isn't. They asked anyway.*"

The First of the Ladder closed his eyes.

"*Then that's what I want,*" said Dacian Severin. "*The stone on Anthos. Below the House. Where I was read at twelve, and my father stood up in the box.*" His good hand had gone flat against the floor. "*I'd like to sit in it. And ask them. Every child. On the morning. Before anything else.*"

"*Corvane will be standing beside it,*" said Yuanshu.

The man on the floor almost laughed.

"*Good,*" he said. "*She'll make sure I do it properly.*"

---

He died at nine minutes to one, on the floor of the Lens chamber, with his back against the root, with a Paragon of the House of Teller on one side of him and a boy from an orchard in the Shallows on the other.

Yuanshu asked first.

He went down into the Death Law, all fifty-seven point eight percent of it, to the cool dark place at the bottom where the door was, a little open, and found him there. Not burning. Not bright. A man's ember, very small, the way the dead are small, sitting on the threshold with his back against something that was not there.

*It's me,* he said. *The ungraded one. I'm asking. Do you want the door? Or the stone?*

> ***...the stone,*** said Dacian Severin. ***...I said.***
>
> ***...is she there? Corvane?***

> **She is in the reading-hall below the House of Anthos.**
>
> **Ichiro has told her. Through her face. She has her hand on the stone.**
>
> **She says: tell him I am here. Tell him I have been standing here since dawn. Tell him he is late.**

> ***...of course I am,*** said the ember on the threshold. ***...I've never been late for anything in my life. Tell her I'm making up for it.***

---

They did it the way they had done it at Corraine. Except that the Lens was the root, and Ichiro was in it, and Ichiro said that everything came through the root now, and that if the boy would hold the door at the bottom of the Death Law, and the old man at Port Abiri and the docker and the girl in Lingzhou would hold their knots, he would pass the First of the Ladder along the threads himself.

From the Lens. Down. Through the net. Not up to the knot in the deep water and across. Down. Because the Lens was the root, and every stone was cut from it, and the stone on Anthos was cut from stones cut from it, and a fog-ringer who had found that the root could hear everything found that it could carry, too.

Ninety seconds. Seed counted.

And in a reading-hall below the House of Anthos, beside a black stone ten meters high where every child of Anthos had been read for nine thousand years, a woman of sixty-one in deep red with her hand flat on the stone felt something come into it, through her palm, past her, down, the way a cup feels water poured into it.

And the runes on the stone of Anthos, all of them, turned like a crowd in a street, and pointed down.

> **Death Law: 57.8% → 58.3%.**
>
> **The stone on Anthos has someone in it.**
>
> **Corvane says the letters are forming. Over the stone. In the air.**
>
> **She says they say: [I'm here. I'm late. Ask me tomorrow.]**
>
> **She says: that is the first time in his life he has ever asked anyone to ask him tomorrow.**

---

Kael Teller was still kneeling on the floor of the Lens chamber beside a man in a torn gray coat who was not there any more.

He did not get up for a long time. Nobody asked him to. The other nine stood on the edge of the broken wall, in their colors, and had stopped burning. Seed said so. One at a time, while Severin died, every one of them had turned it down. Not out. Down. The way four thousand seats had been turned down in the Assembly that morning.

At last the young Paragon of Teller stood up.

He looked at the Lens. Whole now. With a lamp in it, burning, and a fog-ringer, and two lines of writing it had sent to every face in the Federation that morning that nobody could unread.

He took the face off his own throat.

He did not cross it out. He did not put it in the Lens. He held it out, on its fine chain, to the boy from Blue Star.

"*I don't know what to do with it,*" said Kael Teller. "*I've never not worn it.*"

Yuanshu looked at it.

"*Ask the stone,*" he said. "*The one on Teller. When there's someone in it.*" He did not take the disc. "*Until then, keep it in a box. Someone I know keeps everything in boxes. He says that way you can decide later.*"

---

Ostrava Iron came to the bow at half past one.

Alone. Walking. Down the long corridor where she had fought the First of the Ladder and come past him. In iron gray, with a face at her throat with four gold marks and two lines in it, true. She did not have a mark on her. Not a scratch. A Star Monarch who had come past another Star Monarch and not been touched.

She stopped in the door of the Lens chamber.

She looked at the floor where a man had been sitting. At the nine on the edge of the hole in the wall, turned down. At Kael Teller with his face in his hand. At a woman with a sword and a baby. At a boy with a staff.

At the Lens.

"*He's in the stone on Anthos,*" said Yuanshu. "*He asked to be. He'll ask every child before it reads them.*"

The Paragon of the House of Iron did not say anything.

"*Halvarde asked you what you wanted,*" said Yuanshu. "*In the first array. Seed says you didn't answer.*"

Ostrava Iron stood in the doorway for a long time.

"*She asked me eleven times,*" she said at last. In the Federation's language. With every grade in it. "*She asked me by name. Every time. Like I was a rower.*" Her voice did not change. "*I came to break the stone. I walked past two hundred and forty thousand people in walls, and a C asked me eleven times what I wanted, and I did not break anything.*"

She looked at the Lens.

"*I don't know why,*" said the Paragon of the House of Iron.

> **She does.**
>
> **She does not want to say.**
>
> **That is allowed.**

"*Ask me tomorrow,*" said Ostrava Iron. And turned round. And walked back up the long corridor, alone, at an even pace, unhurried, toward the arrays, where Seed said afterward she stood for the rest of the night beside a small old C with a voice like gravel, and listened to her ask two hundred and forty thousand people, one at a time, by name, whether they wanted to go on.
