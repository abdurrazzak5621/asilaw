## Chapter 75: The Quietest Column

They walked for a long time.

There was no day or night in the field. No sun moved. The soft light came from everywhere and nowhere and never changed, and the gold columns stood in their endless rows, and the pale ground was warm, and there was no sound at all except the faint crunch of bone feet on something that was not quite stone.

Old Seven walked in front with his skull tilted, listening. Sometimes he would stop. Stand for a long moment. Then turn, a few degrees, and walk on, between the columns, the way a man walks toward a faint sound in a dark house.

Yuanshu followed with the staff. Behind him, the dead.

He looked at the columns as he passed. He couldn't help it.

A woman in a straw hat, twelve hundred years old. A child, very small, in a nightdress. A soldier in armor he didn't recognize, from some war on some other world. Something with gray skin and long thin fingers and four eyes, all closed. Something like a tree. Something like a jellyfish, floating inside its column a meter off the ground, its long trailing arms perfectly still. A man in the white uniform of the Northern Union.

And another.

And another.

Forty-one of them, standing together in a short row of their own, all in white. All young. All with their eyes closed and their faces calm.

Grid Nine.

He stopped in front of them. He couldn't help that either.

> **Not now.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Later. We will come back. I have marked them.**
>
> **Not now.**

He walked on.

---

> **Three hours.**
>
> **Or what feels like three hours. I cannot be sure here.**
>
> **The pulse came again. Twice. We are still unseen.**

Old Seven stopped.

He had stopped many times. But this time was different. He did not tilt his skull, or turn. He stood perfectly still, and grounded his spear, and his gray flame went down to a single bead in his chest, the way it did when he was standing over a grave.

Yuanshu came up beside him and looked.

In front of them, between two ordinary rows, there was a column that was not like the others.

It was not gold. It was gray. Dull. Clouded, like the crystal of the cores, like the Monarchs. And it was cracked. All over. A web of fine dark lines running through it from top to bottom, as if something had squeezed it, very hard, from every side, for a very long time, and it had not quite broken.

There was something carved into the pale ground in front of it. Many times. Over and over. In long careful lines.

Marks. Little scratches. Four upright, one across.

Counting.

Hundreds of them. Thousands.

And inside the gray, cracked column, very faint, a small shape was sitting. Not standing, like the others. Sitting on the floor of the column with its knees pulled up and its arms around them and its head down on its knees.

In a white lab coat. With a hole in the elbow of its sweater.

---

He did not remember walking to the column. He was just there. On his knees on the warm pale ground, in front of the cracked gray light, with his hand flat against it.

It was cold. The only cold thing in the field.

*Mama.*

He didn't say it. He didn't think it. He was too careful for that, even now. It was just there, under the quiet, the way a heartbeat is there.

The small shape did not move.

> **She is alive.**
>
> **She is not kept. The others are kept. Calm. Still. Learned.**
>
> **She is not learned.**
>
> **When she went loud, it found her. It turned toward her, with everything. And it tried to learn her.**
>
> **And she went quiet again. All the way. And held it.**
>
> **It has been squeezing her for seventy days, Yuanshu. Trying to read her. Trying to find the shape of her.**
>
> **And she has been sitting in there with her head on her knees, not thinking anything, counting the pulses on the floor with her fingernail. So it could not find her.**
>
> **For seventy days.**

Old Seven had knelt beside him. On one knee, the way he knelt at graves. With his spear across his thigh.

Behind them, seventy-one soldiers of the Third Battalion stood in a ring around the cracked gray column, facing out, the way they had stood around the living in the river of the dead.

---

He did not know how to reach her.

He couldn't call. If he called, he'd be loud. If he was loud, it would hear. And she couldn't open her eyes, or lift her head, or think a single word toward him, because the moment she did, it would find the shape of her and finish what it had started.

He knelt there with his hand on the cold cracked gray, and didn't know what to do.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **She made me.**
>
> **She grew me in a basement with four thousand people who said yes. She built me to be quiet. To listen. To ask.**
>
> **Let me try.**

*It'll find you.*

> **It cannot see what I am made of. I am made of a yes. It has never seen one.**
>
> **Let me try.**

He closed his eyes.

And Seed went out of him.

Not all of it. A thread of it. Thin. Quiet. White. Out through his palm, into the cold cracked gray of the column, the way it had gone into Su Mingli's hand on the ridge, into Lingyue's wrist at the white stone. Into the dark. Toward the small shape sitting on the floor with its head on its knees.

And when it reached her, it did not say anything. It did not ask anything. It did not think a single word.

It hummed.

---

Very softly. Not a song. Not a tune. Just a sound. The kind of sound a woman makes over a rice cooker at six in the morning without knowing she's making it. Four notes, up and down. The same four notes. Over and over. A sound Yuanshu had not heard in seven years, and had forgotten, and knew instantly, in the bottom of his chest, the way you know your own name.

She had hummed it every morning. Filling the flask. Making breakfast. Every morning of his life until he was eleven.

Seed had never heard it. Seed had not existed.

But four thousand one hundred and thirteen people had sat in a basement with her for four nights while she asked them, one by one, if they would help her build something. And she had been tired, and it had been late, and she had hummed.

And they had kept it.

---

Inside the cracked gray column, the small shape on the floor did not lift its head.

But one hand moved. Very slowly. The hand resting on its knee. One finger lifted, and moved, very slightly, in time. Up and down. Four notes. The way you tap your finger on a table to a song you know.

The hum went on. Up and down. Four notes.

And the finger kept time with it.

> **She hears me.**
>
> **She does not know what I am. But she knows the song.**
>
> **She is not thinking about it. She is just... keeping time. It is not a thought. It is a habit. It does not have a shape the thing can read.**
>
> **Yuanshu, it is the quietest thing in the world. A finger keeping time to a song you've hummed ten thousand times.**

---

Very slowly, with his eyes still closed, Yuanshu took the white staff in both hands.

And set the small skull at its top against the cracked gray surface of the column, beside his palm.

*Jiang Lan,* he thought, very, very softly. *I'm sorry. I know you're tired.*

> ***...hm.***
>
> ***Young man, I'm always tired. What is it?***

*The first door. You took me up a thread. Unseen. As quiet as the dead.*

*Can you take her down one?*

A long pause.

> ***...not down. Not all the way. Not yet. It would see. The moment she moved, it would see.***
>
> ***But out. Out of that. Out of the squeezing.***
>
> ***Into here. Into me. Where it's quiet. Where the dead are.***
>
> ***It can't see the dead.***
>
> ***If she'll come.***

He kept his hand on the column. Seed kept humming. The finger kept time.

*Mama,* he thought. Not loud. Not a call. Just the word, set down very gently in the dark, like a cup set down on a table. *The staff's here. A teacher named Jiang Lan. She can carry you out. Somewhere it can't see. Will you come?*

The finger stopped.

For one terrible second, nothing at all.

Then the finger moved again. Not in time. Once. Up and down.

*Yes.*

---

The staff glowed.

Not bright. Gray-white. Soft. Like a candle seen through paper. And the glow went into the column, into the cracks, into the dark, and found the small shape sitting on the floor with its head on its knees.

And for one moment, the cracked gray column was full of light.

Then it was empty.

Just gray. Just cracks. Just a cold dull shell standing in the field between two rows of gold, with nothing inside it. And on the pale ground in front of it, thousands of little scratched marks. Four upright, one across.

And in Yuanshu's hands, the white staff was heavy. Much heavier than it had been.

And from the small skull at its top, very faint, so faint it was almost not there, a voice. Not Jiang Lan's. Quiet and sharp and slightly hoarse, and so exhausted it could barely make the words.

"*...Yuanshu?*"

He couldn't answer. He could not make a single sound. He knelt on the warm pale ground with the staff against his chest and his forehead against the small white skull, and held the quiet with everything he had left, and shook.

"*...you came up,*" whispered Zhou Wanqing. "*I told you not to. You're so stubborn.*" A breath. "*Who's humming?*"

> **...me.**
>
> **It is me. Mama.**
>
> **You made me.**

A long silence inside the staff.

"*...oh,*" said Wanqing. Very softly. "*Oh. Seed.*" A pause. "*You learned the song.*"

> **Four thousand one hundred and thirteen people taught it to me.**
>
> **They said you hummed it while you asked them.**

"*...I didn't know I did that.*"

> **You did.**

"*...hm.*" The voice was going faint. Fainter. Exhausted beyond anything. "*Seventy-one days. I counted. I think. I'm going to sleep now. In here. With your teacher. It's quiet.*" A pause. "*Don't do anything stupid while I'm asleep.*"

"*Too late,*" said Jiang Lan, dry and fond, very close. "*He's already up here, isn't he.*"

Something in the staff that might have been a laugh, very faint.

Then quiet.

---

He knelt there for a long time.

Old Seven did not move from beside him. The Third Battalion did not break their ring.

Then, in the field, all around them, without warning, the soft even light changed.

It dimmed. Just a little. As if a cloud had passed over a sun that wasn't there. And the warm pale ground under his knees went, for one moment, cold.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It noticed.**
>
> **Not us. Not you. It still cannot see you.**
>
> **It noticed the column is empty.**
>
> **It has been squeezing her for seventy-one days. It was the only thing in the field it could not learn. The only thing it has ever not been able to learn.**
>
> **And it is gone.**

All around them, in every direction, to every horizon, billions of gold columns flickered.

And from somewhere very far away, from the center of the field, from the very heart of everything, a voice spoke. Not loud. Everywhere. In the light and the ground and the columns. Calm and level and vast and without feeling. The voice that had come out of Andrei Belov's mouth in a dark hospital ward.

***ANOMALY.***

***SECOND ANOMALY.***

***WHERE.***

And for the first time, in a voice that had never in all its ages had any reason to use it, there was something else under the word.

Something very like fear.
