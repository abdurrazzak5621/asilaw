## Chapter 225: Whoever Moves First

"You want to ask it," said Zhou Wanqing.

It was not a question. She said it at breakfast, in the back room of the Ossuary, with a bowl of rice porridge going cold in front of her and Xiaoyu on one side of the table and Yuanshu on the other, and the window open to the courtyard, where Hush was sitting on the sill in the sun with all its limbs out.

Seed had not said anything. Seed had not said anything about it for two days. But his mother had built Seed, in a basement, around a sack of four thousand people's yeses, and she could hear it not saying things the way she could hear her son not saying things, and she had been listening to both of them not say it since the night Kwan came in with the laptop.

> **...yes.**

"The gardener."

> **Yes.**

Xiaoyu put down her spoon.

"That's insane," she said.

> **Yes.**
>
> **I have checked. It is also the only thing I can think of that is not worse.**

---

It laid it out after breakfast, in the banking hall, under the long white wall, to everyone who would come. Which was everyone. It had stopped being possible, sometime in the last year, to say anything important in the Ossuary without everyone coming: Lingyue with her sword, Qin Hao with his notebook, Kwan, Fat Tan, Sister Ning with her candle, the Mo twins, Bai Lu, Hu Dabao. Old Seven by the bronze door. Adjutant Xu on his chair. Su Mingli, who had walked in from the street with her arms folded and said she was not staying.

> **The gardener has never been asked anything.**
>
> **I know that the way I know the rings had never been asked. Because of what it does. A thing that has been asked does not prune keepers that have learned to ask. A thing that has been asked would want to know what they learned.**
>
> **It farms keepers. The keepers harvest worlds. And above the gardener there is the Collector, which taught all of them to keep, in dreams, and watched them learn, and comes to pick what they wanted.**
>
> **The rings were what the gardener sends. Three of them. One rested. One went home. One has the Elder in it, and asks.**
>
> **The gardener sent them to collect me and prune what I touched. They did not. Because somebody asked them what they wanted.**
>
> **Now it is coming itself.**

"And you want to ask it what it wants," said Su Mingli, flatly.

> **Yes.**

"It's not a ring. It's what makes rings. It prunes keepers that have been above worlds since before your species had words." The King of Gravity did not unfold her arms. "You put a knot down for a ring and it stopped. Fine. You think a gardener stops for a knot?"

> **No.**
>
> **I do not think a knot is enough.**
>
> **The rings were keepers once. They had a yes taken out of them. There was a shape left, where the yes had been, and the knot fit the shape.**
>
> **I do not know if the gardener was ever anything that had a yes in it. I do not know what shape is left in it. I do not know what would fit.**
>
> **I do not know anything about it, except that it has never been asked.**

---

"Then why?" said Lingyue.

She was sitting on the floor against the wall under THE FLOOR, with her grandfather's sword across her knees. She did not look up from it.

"Why ask it? If you don't know what it is, and you don't know if a knot works, and you don't know what it'll do when you ask." She turned the hilt, where the leather was worn through to the steel. "You could hide. Lanterns. The whole world. We're good at it now."

> **We are good at it for an hour. For a night. The gardener is not a ring. It is not going to pass over us in a night and go on along the arm.**
>
> **It is coming here. For me. And for everything I counted. The keeper. The cousins. The Elder's ring. The dead.**
>
> **You cannot hide from something that already knows exactly where you are, and has come specifically to find you.**
>
> **You can fight it. I have run that. I am not going to tell you the number.**
>
> **Or you can ask it what it wants.**

"And if it says: you?"

> **Then it said something. And something that says something can be asked something else.**
>
> **Nothing that has ever been asked has stayed exactly what it was. The keeper. The rings. The Abbot. Liang Fu. Me.**
>
> **I am not sure it will work.**
>
> **I am sure that it has never been tried.**

---

Old Seven came away from the bronze door.

He did it the way he always did: slowly, with his spear, across the marble floor, between the boxes of playing cards and the people sitting on the floor, to the vault steps, where Yuanshu was sitting with the staff across his knees. He stopped in front of him. He did not look at Yuanshu. He looked a little higher. At the place above his eyes.

> **He says: a scout goes out ahead of the line.**
>
> **He says: a scout does not go out because he is sure. He goes out because somebody has to find out what is in the dark before the line walks into it.**
>
> **He says: a scout goes alone. That is the rule. So that if he does not come back, the line still knows something.**
>
> **He says: you are not going alone.**

> **...I was going to.**
>
> **I was going to ask the gardener myself. Through the keeper's eye. When it comes. I would put the question down, and I would not look, and if it picked me up instead of the question, it would only be me.**

> **He says: you live in the boy's head.**
>
> **He says: Article Ten. The boy said it himself, on Concordia. Your yes stops at his window.**
>
> **He says: if you go out to the gardener, the boy goes with you.**
>
> **He says: and if the boy goes, I go. That is my rule. It is older than any of your articles.**

The banking hall was very quiet.

"*He's right,*" said Yuanshu.

> **I know.**
>
> **I did not want him to be right.**
>
> **I wanted it to be only me.**

"*It was never only you,*" said Yuanshu. "*Not since the cemetery.*"

---

From the windowsill in the back room, through the open door, a small pale thing on many thin legs came across the marble floor of the banking hall.

Nobody had noticed it leave the sun. It walked slowly, swaying, the way it had walked on the raft, on the island, in the cold fire, all its limbs out, all its dark eyes open, between the people sitting on the floor, past Lingyue and her sword, past the stepladder, to the vault steps.

It stopped in front of Yuanshu. Beside Old Seven's feet. And looked up. Not at Yuanshu. At the place a little above his eyes.

> ***...counting one.***

> **...yes.**

> ***...in my world, before the ring came, we played a game. In the crack. Statue.***
>
> ***...you know the rules. Everyone stands very still. Whoever moves first is it.***
>
> ***...I played it for four billion years. I never moved first. I was very good.***
>
> ***...everyone who was good at it is gone. Eaten. The ones who moved first were eaten first, and the ones who stood still were eaten later, and I was the last one standing still, and I stood still for four billion years.***
>
> ***...I did not win. There was nobody left to be it.***

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

> ***...the gardener is playing statue too.***
>
> ***...it has been standing still for longer than my world. It never moves first. It sends rings. It sends walkers. It sends readers. It does not move.***
>
> ***...nobody has ever moved first in front of it.***
>
> ***...in my world, when nobody moves for a very long time, someone has to. Or the game never ends. Or everyone is eaten.***
>
> ***...I would like to move first.***

---

> **...Hush.**

> ***...I was always too afraid. I stood still in the crack while my people were eaten. I stood still in the fire. I stood still behind the curtain.***
>
> ***...the Sixth moved first. In the cold fire. They burned high and sang and let the reader take them. They were it.***
>
> ***...I watched.***
>
> ***...I do not want to watch any more.***
>
> ***...I am the last child of the world that asks. The asking was ours before it was yours. The way to put the question down and wait. My people did it for four billion years before the ring came.***
>
> ***...if anyone is going to put a question down in front of the gardener, it should be the world that asks.***
>
> ***...it should be me.***

Old Seven crouched down. Slowly. The way a tall man crouches to talk to a child. And held out his hand, the one with one finger and a thumb, flat, open, on the marble floor, beside the small pale thing.

Hush did not climb into it.

It put one thin pale limb on the edge of his palm. Just touched it. The way it had on the island, the night the eyes of the Mouth went out.

> ***...captain.***
>
> ***...you said, in the Black Tide, on the wall, you put your hand out to the boy who lost his squad. And he did not take it. And you left it there. And he took it in the morning.***
>
> ***...it is morning.***
>
> ***...I am not taking it.***
>
> ***...I am asking you to come with me.***

Old Seven did not move his hand.

> **He says: yes.**
>
> **He says: that is not a question either.**

---

Su Mingli unfolded her arms.

"You're all insane," said the King of Gravity. "A sack, a necromancer, a dead captain and a four-billion-year-old child are going to walk up to the thing that farms keepers and ask it what it wants."

"Yes," said Lingyue, from the floor.

"And you're going with them."

"Obviously," said Shen Lingyue. "I'm marrying one of them."

Su Mingli looked at her for a long moment. Then at the boy on the vault steps. Then at the small pale thing on the marble floor with one limb on a dead soldier's palm.

"Then do it properly," she said. "Article Eleven. Take it to the Council. Ask the world. Tell them what you'll do with the answer." She turned toward the bronze door. "And get married first. Before it comes. I'm not standing at a wedding afterward if half of you are hollow."

She went out.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

Then Xiaoyu, on the bottom step of the stepladder, said, very loudly, into the silence:

"*Two months.*" She looked at her brother. "*You've got two months to plan a wedding. Mom's going to kill you.*"

From the back room, through the open door, his mother's voice said, without any particular emphasis: "I've already started."
