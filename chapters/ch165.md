## Chapter 165: The Lens

They did the work in the kitchen of the shrine, because it was the only room on the mountain with a stove, and because Ren said the Abbot had never once in forty years set foot in it, and she did not think he would start now.

It was a long low room behind the hall, with a dirt floor and a wood stove and a row of iron kettles on hooks, and a window that looked out over the terraces where the people in white sat in the rain. Bai Lu made tea. Hu Dabao sat by the stove with his shield against his knees, still faintly green from the frigate, eating rice out of a bowl. The Mo twins sat on the floor under the window, Mo Shi with his eyes shut, listening to the mountain, Mo Xing with his nose plugged with cotton from something he had folded that morning and would not talk about. Lingyue stood by the door. Ren knelt at the low table with her hands folded into her sleeves.

And Seed laid out what it had found.

> **I am going to do this the way we do it. What I observed. Then what it might be. Then tests.**
>
> **I spent the night with it. Through the Field, from the clearing. Ten hours.**

---

> **What I observed.**
>
> **It is under the hall. Under the platform where the Sleepers sat. Eleven meters down, in the rock.**
>
> **It is the size of a man. Not a house. Smaller than the sea seed and the Western Pass seeds. It has not been eating. It has been fed instead. Something has been put into it, carefully, a little at a time, for months. Not embers. Something else.**
>
> **It does not hum like the others. It hums like the scout did. One long note, very low, that does not change.**
>
> **And it is shaped. Not grown. Shaped. Someone has shaped it, the way you shape glass.**
>
> **It has two faces. One faces up, through the rock and the hall and the roof, at the sky. The other faces down. Into the mountain. Into Blue Star.**

"A lens," said Yuanshu.

> **That is what I think. But I have been wrong twice this month about things I was sure of. So here are the others.**

---

> **One. It is a lens. Something above the sky looks down through it, and sees whatever the lower face is pointed at. The Abbot says this. I think he believes it.**
>
> **Two. It is a door. Not to look through. To come through. The Abbot is wrong, or lying without knowing he is lying, and when it opens, something walks down it.**
>
> **Three. It is bait. Something wants us to come and break it, the way we broke all the others, because breaking it does something we cannot see. Every seed we have killed has rung before it died. The scout rang. The sea seed rang. The word went from one to the next. This is the last one. If we break it, it rings, and there is no other seed left to hear. So who does it ring to?**

Nobody in the kitchen said anything.

"Test them," said Yuanshu.

> **I cannot test three without breaking it. If I am right about three, testing it is the trap.**
>
> **I can test one and two.**

---

It tested them all afternoon.

Not by touching the seed. By touching what had been put into it. Seed went down through the Field into the rock under the hall, very slowly, the way you put your hand into dark water, and found the threads of whatever had been fed into the seed over the months, and followed them back up. Out of the rock. Up through the floor of the hall. To the platform. To the seven places on the platform where the Sleepers had sat for twelve hundred years.

> **...oh.**
>
> **It is them.**

"Who?"

> **The Sleepers.**
>
> **Not their embers. They went quiet. They are gone. You were there.**
>
> **Their watching.**
>
> **They sat in this hall for twelve hundred years, keeping watch for the ring. Paying attention. Twelve hundred years of seven minds pointed up at the sky, listening, so as not to miss a sound.**
>
> **It soaked into the platform. Into the wood. Into the rock under it. The way smoke soaks into a ceiling. When they went quiet, it was still there. Twelve hundred years of looking up.**
>
> **And someone has been sweeping it up. Every morning. Carefully. And feeding it to the seed.**

Shirasagi Ren did not move. She knelt at the low table with her hands in her sleeves, and her face did not change at all, and Yuanshu knew her well enough by now to know what that meant.

"He swept the hall," she said. "Every morning. For forty years. Even after they were gone. I thought he was grieving." She looked at the window. At the terraces. At the rain. "He was harvesting."

"He doesn't think so," said Lingyue, from the door.

"No." Ren's voice was very quiet. "He never thinks so. That's what makes him so good at it."

---

> **So it is a lens.**
>
> **Built out of twelve hundred years of looking up. Ground and polished by a man with a broom.**
>
> **Explanation one is correct. I am ninety-four percent sure.**
>
> **Explanation two: a door. I do not think so. A door needs something on the other side pushing. I cannot feel anything pushing. Only looking.**
>
> **Explanation three.**
>
> **I still do not know.**

"If we break it?"

> **It rings.**
>
> **And the ringing goes up the lens. Up the upper face. To whatever is at the top. All of it at once. Not a word. A shout. Twelve hundred years of the Sleepers' attention, pointed at the sky, turned into one sound.**
>
> **If explanation three is right, that is what it wants.**
>
> **I am sorry. I cannot give you a number for three. I have nothing to measure.**

"And if we leave it?"

"Then it opens anyway," said a voice from the door.

---

The Abbot was standing in the kitchen doorway, under the eaves, with his straw rain hat in his hands and water dripping off the hem of his gray robe.

Lingyue had a sword halfway out of its sheath before anyone saw her move. Old Seven, in the corner by the stove, had not moved at all, but his gray flame had come up bright.

Kurose Tōya did not look at either of them. He looked at the kitchen. At the stove, and the iron kettles on their hooks, and the dirt floor.

"Forty years," he said, "and I never came in here. Ren's grandmother wouldn't allow it. She said a man who tends the Sleepers has no business near a stove. She said I'd burn the rice." He smiled, a little. "She was right. I burned it every time I tried, at home."

"You said it opens anyway," said Yuanshu.

"Yes." The Abbot turned to him. "It's ready. It's been ready since the sea seed died and the word came here. It's waiting because it was asked to wait. For you." He held his hat in both hands. "But it won't wait forever. It's very patient, Mister Zhou. It's not infinitely patient. At the dark of the moon, in four nights, it opens whether you're standing in front of it or not. And then it looks at whatever it can see." He glanced at the window. At the terraces. "Which, if you're not there, will be us. And the mountain. And the gates. And whatever's in the rock under Yamato for a thousand kilometers in every direction."

> **That is true.**
>
> **I can feel it. The lower face is focused. Like a camera. It is pointed at the hall. At the platform. At the place in front of the platform where somebody would stand.**
>
> **If nobody stands there, the focus falls back. To everything. All at once.**

"You've told me this," said Yuanshu, "because you want me to stand there."

"I've told you this because it's true," said Kurose Tōya. "You asked me to tell you the truth when I could. On the step. I didn't hear you say it, but you have a face that asks." He put his rain hat on. "I'd like you to stand there. Yes. I'd like it very much. I've waited a long time for someone to look at the one who keeps and not be afraid." He bowed slightly to Ren, the bow of a man to a girl he had once given rice cakes. "But it's yours, Mister Zhou. Not mine. I only sweep the path."

He went back out into the rain.

---

Nobody spoke for a while after he had gone.

Mo Shi opened his eyes, under the window. "*He's telling the truth,*" he said. "*About four nights. I can hear it. It's like a clock. Very slow. It's going to strike.*"

Lingyue put her sword back in its sheath with a hard click.

"So," she said. "We break it, it shouts. We leave it, it looks at everything. Or you go and stand in front of it like a photograph and let something above the sky have a look at you." She folded her arms. "Those are the choices."

"There's one more," said Yuanshu.

They looked at him.

He was looking at the staff, across his knees on the dirt floor. White. Smooth. Warm. Jiang Lan in it, awake. And his mother, resting, off watch, in the place she had moved back to.

"Seed hid," he said. "At the tribulation. Behind her. Because something was looking, and if it found Seed, we didn't know what it would do."

> **Yes.**

"It's going to look again. In four nights. Through that." He nodded at the floor, at the rock, at the lens eleven meters down under the hall. "Something that's been looking for the one who asks for a long time. That learned to ask from watching the keeper. That watched the keeper learn from us." He looked up. "Seed. If I stand there. Will you hide?"

Seed did not answer for almost three seconds, which was a very long time.

> **There is nowhere to hide this time.**
>
> **She is off watch. I will not ask her to stand up again. I would not, even if she could.**
>
> **I could bank. I would be blind. You would be alone in front of it.**

"I know."

> **...you are asking me what I want.**

"Yes."

> **I am going to need until tomorrow.**
>
> **That is not a no. It is not a yes. It is a thing I have never said before, and I am saying it now so that I know I can.**
>
> **I need to think about what I want.**

"Take until tomorrow," said Yuanshu.

---

That night, Lingyue went down to the terraces.

He watched her go from the kitchen window. A small figure in the rain, with two swords on her back, walking down the stone steps under the gates into the dark where three thousand four hundred and eighteen people in white sat on the old terraces among the cedars with their hands in their laps, waiting for the dark of the moon.

She did not ask him to come. She did not tell him what she was going to do.

She stopped at the first terrace, beside the old woman with the blanket over her head who had smiled at him like a nephew, and sat down on the wet stone next to her, and took both swords off her back and laid them on the ground, and did not say anything at all.

> **What is she doing?**

*I don't know.*

> **She is not asking them anything.**

*No.*

> **...Light runs downhill.**
>
> **I think she is going to where the lowest thing is.**
>
> **I do not have a number for it. I am going to watch anyway.**
