## Chapter 96: The Pass

It took nine hours for the Hunger to cross the sky.

He would remember every one of them. Lying on his back on the warm pale ground of the raft, in the dark, with his eyes closed and Lingyue's hand in his, not thinking anything. Holding it. Holding the quiet the way you hold your breath at the bottom of a swimming pool, when you are a child, and you want to see how long you can stay down.

He did not need to open his eyes to know where it was.

He could feel it. Coming over the horizon. A weight on the sky. A pressure on the cloud. The thin air of the little ocean world going heavy and still and strange, the way air goes before a storm, so heavy that it pressed on his eyelids and his chest and the backs of his hands.

And a sound.

Not the Monarch's long wail. Not the spire's shriek. Something so low it was almost not a sound at all. A grinding. A turning. Like an enormous millstone, kilometers across, rolling slowly over gravel, very far away and very close at the same time.

> **It is overhead.**
>
> **Do not open your eyes.**
>
> **I will tell you what it is. You do not need to see it.**

---

> **It fills the sky.**
>
> **From one horizon to the other. The whole sky is the ring.**
>
> **Gray. Cracked. The cracks are bigger than mountains. And in them, very deep, red. Like coals. Like veins.**
>
> **It is turning. Very slowly.**
>
> **And in the middle, where the lens was black water, there is fire.**
>
> **Gray-red. The color Hush said. The color the Elder showed the Lanterns.**
>
> **It lights the whole cloud from above. The cloud is glowing. The sea is glowing, very faintly, red, under it.**
>
> **It is like being under a sun that has gone bad.**

He held the quiet.

He thought about nothing. He thought about the warm ground under his back. He did not think about the hand in his, or the brass key on its red string against his chest, or the dark skull of a schoolteacher in the staff across his knees, who might not come back.

> **It is not looking at us.**
>
> **It does not see us.**
>
> **It does not see the sea. The Lanterns are dark. All of them. It does not see anything.**
>
> **Except the violet.**

---

It began to rain.

He felt it before Seed told him. A change in the pressure. A long slow exhalation from far above, from the fire in the center of the ring. And then, faint, very far off, a sound like hail on a tin roof. Scattered. Here. There. Far away across the sea.

> **Seeds.**
>
> **It is dropping seeds.**
>
> **Not everywhere. Not on us. Not on the dark ones.**
>
> **Toward the violet.**

He held the quiet.

He held it so hard his jaw ached.

> **The first one has landed.**
>
> **Forty kilometers north. In the sea. Next to a cluster of violet.**
>
> **It is growing.**

He knew what came next. He had seen it at Port Abiri. Gray crystal pushing up out of the water. Roots spreading. A great red eye opening.

And the bite.

> **...it has bitten.**
>
> **The violet ones near it. Eleven of them.**
>
> **They are...**

Seed stopped.

He held the quiet. He did not ask. He knew.

---

It went on for hours.

Seed stopped telling him after the first three. He did not ask it to go on. He could feel it anyway. Far off across the dark sea, scattered, here, there, small lights going out. Not dimming. Not the soft deep blue of sleep. Going out the other way. Burning.

The violet ones.

The ones who had said *no.* The ones who had said they had glowed too long to learn how to be anything else. The ones who had turned violet, all across the sea, to say *remember us.*

He lay on his back on the raft with his eyes closed and remembered them.

He did not move. He did not let the quiet break. He held Lingyue's hand and thought about the warm ground under his back, and remembered every one, and did not think anything at all, and it was the hardest thing he had ever done in his life.

Harder than the river. Harder than the ridge. Harder than the stalk.

*Quiet means quiet,* he thought, very far down, under everything. *No means no. Even when it breaks your heart.*

---

Bai Lu could not hold it.

He felt it happen. Beside him. A little way away on the warm pale ground. The small, round-glassed girl with her long plait and her too-big medical bag, lying very still with her eyes squeezed shut.

She had been a healer since she was fourteen. Small things only, at first. Burns. Cuts. A finger blistered on a teapot. She had spent two days trying to heal an old soldier's dead arm, knowing she couldn't, because she couldn't stand not to try.

And now, forty kilometers away, and sixty, and a hundred, all across a dark sea, people were burning.

And she could feel them.

She felt them the way a healer feels pain in a room. In her hands. In her chest. In the roots of her law. Every violet light going out in fire was a wound, and every wound pulled at her, the way a hurt child pulls at a nurse's sleeve.

She held it for three hours.

And then, very quietly, without moving, without opening her eyes, without making any sound at all, Bai Lu began to cry.

---

It was not loud. Not to a person. A girl lying on her back on a raft with tears running sideways down into her hair.

To the Hunger, it was a bonfire.

> **...oh no.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It heard her.**

The grinding stopped.

All at once. Silence. Total. The kind of silence that comes when a great machine stops turning.

And above the cloud, Yuanshu felt the ring turn.

Not the whole ring: the fire in its center, the gray-red sun, turning fast, the way an eye turns, toward a sound.

Toward them.

---

He opened his eyes.

He could not help it. Nobody could have.

The cloud above the raft had gone red. Glowing. Lit from above by something so bright and so close that the thin pale vapor looked like smoke over a burning city. And through it, enormous, filling everything, the ring. Gray and cracked and turning. And at its heart, the fire, staring straight down.

And falling out of the fire, toward them, slowly, trailing gray smoke through the red cloud like a meteor, a seed.

> **Thirty seconds.**

Everyone was moving.

Lingyue was on her feet with her sword out. Qin Hao with fire in both hands. The twins back to back, Mo Shi with his eyes shut and his fingers pressed to his temples. Sokolova crouching over Bai Lu, who was sobbing now, out loud, both hands over her face, *I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I'm sorry, I couldn't, I couldn't...*

And the dead. All around the rim of the raft. Their banked flames coming up. Gray. Brighter. Old Seven's first, blazing in his ribs.

They could all be seen now. Every one of them.

There was no point being quiet anymore.

---

"*Jiang Lan.*"

He was holding the staff in both hands. The small white skull at its top was dark. Dull. Cold. Like bone in a museum case.

"*Jiang Lan. I need you. I'm sorry. I'm asking. Please.*"

Nothing.

> **Fifteen seconds.**

"*Please. Come back. I need the door.*"

Nothing.

He held the dark skull against his forehead and closed his eyes and did not think anything except her name.

*Jiang Lan. Second-year arithmetic. Village school in the eastern hills. Twenty-three children under a collapsing floor. Nine minutes. Every one of them did it.*

*You don't ask the class to do something you won't do yourself.*

*Come back.*

---

"*...young man,*" said a dry, faint, extremely irritated voice, from the small skull against his forehead. "*Has anyone ever told you that you are very, very loud?*"

The staff lit.

Gray-white. Soft. Like a candle seen through paper. And then brighter. Brighter. Until the small skull blazed in his hands like a lamp.

"*I was having a lovely rest,*" said Jiang Lan. "*What's the emergency?*"

> **Five seconds.**

The seed hit the sea fifty meters from the raft.

---

It did not grow like the spire at Port Abiri. It did not take a day.

It took a breath.

Gray crystal exploded up out of the black water in a column fifty meters high, jagged, branching, roaring, red in every crack. Roots shot out across the surface of the sea toward the raft like the fingers of a hand closing. And in the center of the column, a great red eye snapped open. Staring. At the staff. At the bonfire in Yuanshu's hands.

"*Pale fire!*" Lingyue's voice. "*Now!*"

He touched the staff to her blade.

The white-gold went silver-gray. Cold. Steady. And Lingyue went to the rim of the raft between two soldiers of the Third Battalion and met the first root as it came over the edge, and cut it, and it fell away into the sea, smoking.

Behind her, Qin Hao burned. The twins folded. Sokolova dragged Bai Lu to the center of the raft. The dead held the rim, spears and shields and bones, and the roots came over the edge at them, and burned them where they touched, and they held anyway.

And above it all, the ring turned.

Not just the fire now. The whole thing. Slowly. Vast. Grinding. Turning toward the little world beneath it with its dark sea. Toward the raft.

And opening.

---

Yuanshu looked up.

The gray ring was widening. The fire in its center growing. Spreading. Filling the cloud, filling the sky. And out of it, coming down, slow and enormous, like a great gray hand reaching down to scoop something out of a pond, was the mouth.

Not a seed. Not a spire. The Hunger itself. The ring, descending. Lowering its whole vast cracked turning bulk toward the sea, toward the raft, toward the one bright thing on the whole dark world.

Its edge was ten kilometers wide. Its shadow fell across the sea like nightfall.

> **It is coming down.**
>
> **Yuanshu. It is coming down to take us.**
>
> **All of it.**

"*Run,*" said Qin Hao. "*Elder, can you...*"

> **"No,"** said the Elder. **"It is too fast. It is too close. There is no current here that can carry us out from under it in time."**

The shadow of the mouth swept across the black sea toward them.

And at the edge of the raft, a small pale many-limbed shape climbed up out of the water. Dripping. Swaying on its thin legs. With all of its dark eyes fixed on the vast gray thing descending from the sky.

> ***...good,*** said Hush, in its seashell voice.

Everyone turned to stare at it.

> ***...I told you. The mouth sends what it eats to the middle.***
>
> ***...to the fire.***
>
> ***...to the cold place.***
>
> ***...we do not have to find it anymore.***
>
> ***...it is coming to take us there.***

Yuanshu looked up at the mouth of the Hunger, filling the sky, descending.

Then he looked at Lingyue, at the rim, with her pale fire blade. At Old Seven beside her, flame blazing. At the twins. At Qin Hao. At Sokolova, holding Bai Lu. At the Elder. At Hush.

"*Everyone to the center,*" he said. "*Hold on to each other.*"

"*What are you...*"

"*We're not running,*" said Zhou Yuanshu. "*We're letting it eat us.*"
