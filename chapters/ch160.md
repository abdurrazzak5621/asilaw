## Chapter 160: The White Boat

He asked her that night, in the fish market at the shore end of the jetty at Sandu, where her household had laid her on a long wooden table under a sheet, with candles at her head and her feet, because there was nowhere else.

He did not want to. He sat on an upturned crate beside the table for an hour with the staff across his knees, and Lingyue sitting on the floor against his legs, and the two household men standing at the door, before he could make himself do it. But he had asked everyone. Every one of them, since a cemetery at three in the morning. He was not going to stop at the one he most wanted to leave alone.

He put the staff against the edge of the table.

"*Ifeoma,*" he said. "*I'm sorry. I'm going to ask you. You don't have to answer.*"

For a long moment there was nothing.

Then, faint, dry, very far away, like a voice from the other end of a long wooden jetty:

> ***...I know how you ask, child.***

---

She was very tired. Seed said so, afterward. Two hundred and thirty-one years, and a King's ember, the largest Yuanshu had ever held through the staff, larger than Jiang Lan's, larger than anything in the round chamber, burned so low that it was barely there at all. She had spent all of it on the sea. Everything she had, from the bottom of a jetty to the bottom of the ocean, in one column of water. There was very little left to answer with.

She answered anyway.

> ***...only to speak.***
>
> ***...don't raise me. I don't want to stand up. I've stood up enough.***
>
> ***...put me in the boat. The white one. At Port Abiri. Untie it and let it go. Don't steer it. It knows the way.***
>
> ***...tell Akosua two hours. I'll know if they cheat.***
>
> ***...tell my household to stop fussing. Tell them the palace is theirs. They'll argue about it for a year. Good. It will keep them busy.***
>
> ***...tell the Compact they'll need another King. Tell them not to be in a hurry. The sea will be all right for a while without one. It's lazy.***

A long pause. The candles at the head of the table guttered in the sea wind coming in through the open sides of the market.

> ***...and child.***

"*Yes.*"

> ***...you asked me what I wanted. On the harbor wall. Do you remember what I said?***

"*To hear the Architects say sorry. To go home and watch the fishing boats. To see what I'd do when I was a Lord.*" He held the staff. "*And for eleven thousand people on the river road to get to the hills. That most of all.*"

> ***...how many got to the hills?***

> **All of them.**
>
> **Eleven thousand and forty. The last truck went over the pass at 17:12. Nobody on the river road died today.**

> ***...good.***
>
> ***...then I had the one I wanted most. That's more than most people get, child. Don't look like that.***
>
> ***...I'm going to stop speaking now. Don't ask me again. I'd like to be quiet.***
>
> ***...tell Emeka I'm coming down, and he can tell me it's cold.***

And then she was quiet.

---

They took her north at dawn, by road, in the back of the army truck, with her household beside her, and the twins, and Bai Lu holding one of her cold hands the whole way, because nobody had asked Bai Lu to and nobody was going to tell her not to.

The coast road was full again. Going the other way now. The buses were coming back from the hills, slowly, nose to tail, with their windows open, and when they saw the army truck coming north with the sheet in the back and the old woman's household walking beside it on foot in white, they stopped. All of them. The whole road. People got off the buses and stood at the side of the coast road in the early sun, in a long line from Sandu to Kesse to Port Abiri, and did not say anything, and watched it go past.

Somebody on the radio had told them. Nobody ever found out who.

At Kesse, an old man on the side of the road took off his hat. At Port Abiri, a woman with a baby on her hip held the baby up to see.

> **Sixty thousand people.**
>
> **All of them were on this road yesterday, going the other way. All of them are alive.**
>
> **I am counting them. I do not need to. I would like to.**

---

The white boat was where she had left it, tied up at the end of the harbor wall at Port Abiri, rocking on the green water with its blue stripe and no engine.

Her household laid her in the bottom of it in the afternoon, on a bed of blankets, in her green headwrap, with her thin hands folded on her chest. One of them put a fishing knife beside her. The other put her husband's old cap, which nobody had known she kept, folded, under her hands.

Half the town was on the harbor wall. More on the beach. And on the hill above the bay, among the white stones, a woman in a black dress, standing, with her basket on her arm.

Yuanshu untied the boat.

He did not steer it. She had asked him not to. He untied the rope from the iron ring in the harbor wall and let the end of it fall into the water, and stood back, and the white boat sat there for a moment on the still green water at the end of the wall, rocking very slightly, as if it were thinking about something.

Then the current took it.

Not a push. Nobody pushed it. There was no King left on the coast to ask the water anything. It was just the ordinary afternoon current of the bay at Port Abiri, the one the fishermen knew, going out along the harbor wall toward the mouth of the bay the way it had gone out every afternoon for ten thousand years. The white boat went with it. Slowly. Out past the end of the wall. Out past the rocks at the mouth of the harbor where Lingyue had stood in the swell yesterday morning with two swords. Out into the open bay.

Toward the point.

> **It is going toward the point.**
>
> **Where he went over the side. A hundred and forty years ago.**
>
> **Yuanshu, the current does not go that way. I modeled it last night. It goes north, along the coast.**
>
> **It is going to the point.**

"It knows the way," said Yuanshu.

---

It took the boat an hour to reach the point.

Nobody on the harbor wall left. They stood in the afternoon sun and watched a small white shape go out across the blue bay, smaller and smaller, until it was only a white fleck at the edge of the dark water past the point, where the shelf fell away and the sea went black.

And there, it stopped.

> **Emeka.**
>
> **He is under it. At the bottom. Three kilometers down. He walked back from Sandu in the night. He has been standing on the seabed under the point since dawn, looking up.**
>
> **He is asking if this is her.**

*Tell him yes. Tell him she said he can tell her it's cold.*

> **I cannot tell him anything. There is no pipe.**

Adjutant Xu stepped up to the edge of the harbor wall beside Yuanshu, with his cane, and hummed one note into the sea.

It went down three kilometers and arrived as a blur, the way a voice arrives through a closed door. But Emeka had been asked three nights ago, at the end of a pipe of still water, by the woman who was now lying in the bottom of a white boat over his head. He knew what a blur from the surface meant. He had been waiting for it his whole death.

> **He says: Ify.**
>
> **He says: I told you not to come down. It's cold.**
>
> **He says: you never listened.**

The white boat filled.

Slowly. Gently. The way a boat fills that has been at sea for a hundred and forty years and finally decided to stop. The green water came in over the gunwales and the boat settled lower, and lower, and then, without any fuss at all, it went down.

The sea closed over it.

On the harbor wall, sixty thousand people, or as many of them as could fit, stood in the sun and did not say anything. Up on the hill, the woman in the black dress put her basket down on the grass and stood with her hands at her sides.

> **He says she is here.**
>
> **He says he is going to lie down now. Next to her. Under the point. He says he does not want to fight anything else. He says he would like to be quiet.**
>
> **He says: thank you for asking.**

---

He went up the hill afterward, when the crowd on the harbor wall had gone home.

Akosua Mensah was sitting beside her husband's stone with her basket beside her, the way she had been sitting every evening for a year. She looked up when he came, and did not get up.

"She told me two hours," she said.

"She told me to tell you again."

"I know. I heard her say it. Up here, the night before she went." The widow looked out at the bay. At the place past the point where the white boat had gone down. "She was the only one who ever told me what to do. Everyone else asked." She was quiet. "I liked it."

"She'd have liked that you said so."

"No, she wouldn't. She'd have said, *don't be soft, girl.*" Akosua Mensah's hard cracked hands were folded in her lap. "She called me girl. I'm fifty-three."

He sat down on the grass a little way from her, facing the sea.

"In five months," said the widow, after a while. "They'll come back. The seven. They'll kneel down in the grass, there, where they knelt last time. And they'll say it." She looked at the place in the grass. "And I'll make them say it for two hours. One for me, and one for her." She looked down at her hands. "And I'll count."

---

> **Yuanshu.**

He was walking back down the hill in the dusk, between the white stones.

> **I have to tell you something. You have not asked. I am telling you anyway, because you asked me to tell you the numbers even when you do not want them.**

*Go on.*

> **[Level 58 → 61.]**
>
> **Three trunks. A seed. Your dead, under your command. The drowned. The twelve.**
>
> **You can break through.**
>
> **Lord.**

He stopped on the path.

Below him, the lights of Port Abiri were coming on, one window at a time, as people came home and found their houses where they had left them. Out in the bay, the water past the point was dark and smooth and still.

> **It is a realm tribulation. An ordinary one. Not a Forbidden one. Every Lord on Blue Star has passed it. Qin Hao passed it on a spire.**
>
> **But it is still a tribulation, and you are still you, and the heavens have noticed you twice before.**
>
> **It will come when you open the door. You can hold the door closed for some days. Not many.**
>
> **I would like you to open it somewhere that is not a town.**

"After the funeral," said Yuanshu.

> **The funeral was today.**

"Her household's funeral. In the palace. In three days." He started walking again, down the hill, toward the lights. "And Lingyue's going to Yamato in four. Ren called. The seed's at the shrine. She's going to need help."

> **...you want to go with her.**

"I want to go with her," said Yuanshu. "And I want to be a Lord when I get there." He looked up at the first stars over the bay. "So I'll open it on the way."

> **On the way.**
>
> **Between the Southern Compact and Yamato there are thousands of kilometers of open sea.**
>
> **...oh.**
>
> **Yes. That is not a town.**
