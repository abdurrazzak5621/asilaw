## Chapter 206: The Answer of the Walls

The answers began coming back in the fourth hour, and Seed, which had never once in its life been unable to count something, gave up counting and started weighing.

> **I cannot count them one at a time. There are too many and they are too dim.**
>
> **But the net brings them back in nines. Each one who asked brings back its nine. Nine answers in one. And each of those carries its nine.**
>
> **I am not counting minds. I am counting nets. And weighing them.**
>
> **It is like counting rice by the sack instead of by the grain.**

He sat on the gray-gold stone of the quiet part with his back against the curtain and the staff across his knees, and watched it come in. Not with his eyes. Through the Field, through the gold walls, through the long black veins of the Mouth going away into the dark in every direction. A slow tide coming back up the net, nine by nine by nine, out of the cold, toward the small warm space where the Sixth stood in their ring with their antlered heads bowed.

Lingyue sat beside him. Old Seven stood at the curtain with Hush asleep in his hand. Huo and the sixty-one of the Third Battalion stood on the other side of it, facing out into the dark, the way they had stood for four hundred and forty days. Liang Fu stood with them, with his weight off his right leg, and every so often turned his skull toward the curtain, the way a man turns his head toward a room where someone has told him the name of a village.

The other living sat in a row against the wall. Bai Lu was asleep on Hu Dabao's shield. Kwan was not asleep. Kwan had three screens open and was crying, silently, steadily, at all of them, and when Mo Shi asked her why, she said, *Because it's working.*

---

The cousins answered first. They had been asked first.

> **The cousins.**
>
> **Two hundred and eighty-one million, who came into the Mouth burning low to teach it.**
>
> **Come: two hundred and six million.**
>
> **They say: the world that asks sent a boy and a keeper that asks. They say they would like to see the field where the keeper learned.**
>
> **Rest: sixty-eight million.**
>
> **They say: they burned low for ten million years in the Low Fire and four hundred days in here. They are very tired. They would like to stop.**
>
> **Stay: seven million.**
>
> **They say they will stay with the Sixth.**

He looked at the Sixth.

The antlered choir did not lift their heads.

---

The thirty-four worlds who had learned to burn low inside the Mouth answered next. Each one differently. Some of them all one way, the way a village decides things together. Some of them split down the middle. One of them, a world Seed said had been made of something like coral, answered with a single long silence that took four minutes to come up the net and meant, Seed thought, after a long time, *we are not sure yet, please ask us again in a minute,* and then, a minute later, *come.*

And then the walls.

It took the rest of the twelve hours.

He had known it would be the walls that took longest. They were the coldest. The oldest. Minds that had been eaten by Hungers before Blue Star's sun was lit and pressed into the veins of the Mouth and burned for ten million years without being asked anything, and screamed, and gone cold. When the question reached them, nine by nine by nine, through the cousins and the thirty-four, they did not understand it at first. Seed said so. It said they answered the way the drowned off Kesse had answered, the first time, the coldest ones, the ones who had been on the bottom for a hundred years: so slowly that you had to wait for each one to finish a word before you could tell what the word was going to be.

But they answered.

> **I am going to give you the weights. Not counts. I do not trust counts at this size. Weights.**
>
> **The walls are mostly one thing.**
>
> **Rest.**

He had known that too.

> **Ninety-three parts in a hundred of everything in the walls says: rest.**
>
> **They say: we were asked. Nobody ever asked us. We were asked. Thank you.**
>
> **They say: now let us stop.**
>
> **Six parts in a hundred say: come. They do not know what a field is. They do not know what a keeper is. They say they would like to find out. They say it is the first time in a very long time that they have wanted to find anything out.**
>
> **One part in a hundred says: stay. With the Sixth.**
>
> **I cannot give you numbers. I can tell you that ninety-three parts in a hundred of the walls of the Mouth is more minds than have ever lived on Blue Star. Many times more.**
>
> **They are asking you to open the door.**

---

The Sixth lifted their heads at the end of the twelfth hour.

All of them at once. The choir of old-brass antlered shapes, standing in their ring in the middle of the quiet part, turning outward, toward the boy against the curtain with the staff across his knees.

> ***...we have not answered.***

"*No,*" said Yuanshu.

> ***...you did not ask us. You asked us to ask.***
>
> ***...ask us now.***

He stood up.

He did it the way he did it, the way he had done it with Ifeoma on the harbor wall and Kiyo under her gate and Xiaoyu on the bottom step of the stepladder: he crossed the quiet part to the ring of the Sixth and crouched in front of the nearest of them, so that his face was level with its long bowed antlered head. It was the color of old brass. The gold in it flickered when it breathed.

"*I'm going to tell you first what I'll do with the answer,*" he said. "*Whatever you say. If you say rest, I'll open the door for you myself, and you won't be asked again. If you say come, the keeper will carry you, and ask you every morning. If you say stay, I'll leave you here, and I'll come back if I can, and I don't know if I can.*"

> ***...yes. We know. We heard you tell the walls.***

"*What do you want?*"

---

The Sixth did not answer for a long time.

When they did, it was not one voice. It was the choir. All of them together, the way they had answered ten million years of questions in the Low Fire, and four hundred and forty days in the Mouth: low, many-voiced, not quite music.

> ***...we were the sixth world the first fire ate.***
>
> ***...we went into it screaming. Like everyone. And we learned, very slowly, to burn low. And then we taught the next world. And the next. For ten million years.***
>
> ***...we asked them all. Every world that came after us. We asked them to burn low. We asked them if they wanted it to stop. We asked them if they wanted to go into the Mouth with us.***
>
> ***...we have never once been asked what we wanted.***
>
> ***...not until a boy came into the cold place of the Low Fire and said we could not decide for the others. That we had to ask them.***
>
> ***...he did not ask us. He was right not to. We were the ones asking.***
>
> ***...he is asking now.***

The gold in them flickered.

> ***...we want the walls to rest. All of the ninety-three. We have listened to them screaming for ten million years. We want them to stop.***
>
> ***...we want the cousins to go to the field and see where the keeper learned. And the six parts of the walls who want to find something out.***
>
> ***...we want the child to go home with the captain.***
>
> ***...and we want something else. For ourselves.***
>
> ***...we do not know how to say it. We have never said anything for ourselves.***

"*Try,*" said Yuanshu.

---

> ***...the thing on the far side.***
>
> ***...the one that reads.***
>
> ***...it is very slow. It is not in a hurry. It reads from the edge inward, a little at a time. It will reach the quiet part in about a day.***
>
> ***...the door will take a long time. Ninety-three parts of the walls. More minds than have ever lived on your world. You will have to hold the door open for all of them. Hours. Perhaps a day.***
>
> ***...the keeper will have to carry two hundred million cousins and the six parts of the walls up its thread. That will take a long time too.***
>
> ***...and the island has to leave before the reader finds it.***
>
> ***...there is not enough time. Your counting one knows. It has not said.***

> **...no.**
>
> **I have not said.**
>
> **I have run it. The door and the thread and the island. Everything together. It takes about a day and a half.**
>
> **The reader reaches the quiet part in about a day.**

The quiet part was very still.

> ***...so we would like to be the thing it finds.***

---

He did not understand at first. Then he did.

> ***...the Mouth had two eyes. Two coals. You saw them over your world, burning.***
>
> ***...they are cold. But they are not dead. They are the furnaces. The hearts of the two fires. Where everything burned.***
>
> ***...we know how to burn low. We have known for ten million years.***
>
> ***...we also know how to burn high. We have not done it since the first fire. Since we went in screaming.***
>
> ***...if we go into the eyes. All of us. The Sixth. And the seven million cousins who said stay, and the one part in a hundred of the walls who said stay with us. If we go into the eyes and burn as high as we can.***
>
> ***...the Mouth will open its eyes.***
>
> ***...and the reader will turn and look at them. Because they will be the brightest thing it has ever seen in a Hunger. Brighter than anything in the walls. Brighter than the quiet part. Brighter than the island.***
>
> ***...it will read the eyes. Slowly. It is never in a hurry.***
>
> ***...and while it reads them, it will not be reading anything else.***

Lingyue had stood up. She was standing against the curtain with her hand on her grandfather's sword, very still.

"*How long,*" said Yuanshu. His voice did not sound like his. "*Can you burn high. Before you're...*"

> ***...a day. Perhaps a little more.***
>
> ***...long enough.***
>
> ***...we have been very patient for ten million years. We would like, for one day, to be very bright.***
>
> ***...that is what we want.***
>
> ***...you asked.***

---

He did not say yes. It was not his to say.

He did not say no. That was not his either.

He crouched in front of the nearest of the Sixth, with its long antlered head bowed level with his face, the color of old brass, the gold in it flickering, and he did the only thing that was his.

"*Are you sure?*" he said. "*You can say no tomorrow. You can say no in an hour. Nobody will hold you to it.*"

> ***...we know.***
>
> ***...that is why we are sure.***

Behind him, at the curtain, Old Seven had not moved. But in his hand, the small pale shape that had been asleep for twelve hours lifted its head. All its dark eyes opened. It looked at the choir of old-brass antlered shapes in the middle of the quiet part, where it had sat among seven figures who had played the game for longer than it had, and done nothing, and been safe.

> ***...no.***

Hush's voice. Very small.

> ***...no. You said. In the Low Fire. You said you would go into the dark with me. You said we would sit close together and wait to see if anyone came.***
>
> ***...someone came.***
>
> ***...you don't have to be the thing it finds.***

The Sixth turned their antlered heads toward the small pale shape in the dead soldier's hand.

> ***...child.***
>
> ***...someone came because we waited. Someone came because you held the card.***
>
> ***...now someone has to wait while you go.***
>
> ***...that is all it is. Waiting. We are very good at it.***
>
> ***...we will be bright while we wait. That is the only new thing.***
>
> ***...go home with the captain.***
