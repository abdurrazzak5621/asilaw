## Chapter 168: The Dark of the Moon

At eleven at night on the fourth night, the Abbot got up off the step for the first time in seven days, and slid open the door of the hall, and stood aside.

"It's nearly time," he said. "You should take your shoes off. They always did."

Yuanshu took his shoes off.

He did it on the step, the way Ren had shown him a year and a half ago, and set them side by side on the worn wood, and went in. Lingyue came in behind him in her bare feet with both swords on her back. Old Seven came to the foot of the step and stopped there, the way he had stopped the first time, and would not come further; but he did not bow his skull now. He stood at the foot of the step with his spear grounded, facing the door, the way a soldier stands at the door of a room where his officer has gone in alone.

Ren stayed outside too. On the step, kneeling, beside the Abbot. She had asked to. Her hand was flat on the wood.

> **The monks under the gates are listening.**
>
> **Three hundred and ninety. All the way down the path. They are not looking down any more. They are looking up.**

---

The hall was the same. Small. Plain. Polished dark boards. A smell of old wood and old incense and something under both, dry and faintly sweet, like pressed flowers. A raised platform at the back, where seven monks in robes the color of dry leaves had sat for twelve hundred years with their heads bowed, listening.

The platform was empty. Seven faded cushions. Nothing on them.

But the wood was warm.

He could feel it from the doorway, through the Field. Not heat. Attention. Twelve hundred years of seven minds looking up had soaked into the boards the way smoke soaks into a ceiling, and someone had swept most of it up, every morning, for forty years, and carried it down into the rock, but not all of it. Some was still there. In the grain. In the cushions. In the corner where the oldest one had sat, whose robe had faded almost to white.

And under the platform, eleven meters down, in the rock, the lens.

He felt it as he walked across the floor. Two faces. One turned down, toward Blue Star, toward the hall, focused like a camera on a single spot on the polished boards in front of the platform. One turned up, toward the sky. And between them, ground and polished out of twelve hundred years of looking, something that was not glass and not crystal and not anything Seed had a word for, holding very still.

He walked to the spot it was focused on, and stood on it.

Lingyue stood beside him. Close. Her shoulder against his arm.

"Are you scared?" she said, very low.

"Yes."

"Good," said Lingyue. "Me too."

> **Twelve minutes.**
>
> **Mo Shi says the bell is all the way up. He says it is going to swing.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I am here. In the open. Nothing in front of me.**
>
> **It is like standing in the rain without a coat.**
>
> **I would like it noted that I chose this.**

*Noted.*

---

At midnight, in the dark of the moon, the lens opened.

There was no light. He had thought there would be light. A beam, a column, a shining eye in the sky the way the ring had been an eye. There was nothing. The hall stayed dark. The single paper lantern Ren had left on the step outside burned on, small and yellow, through the paper of the door.

But something looked in.

He felt it come down the lens the way he had felt the tribulation's gaze come into the glass: not a weight, not a pressure, an *attention*. From very far away. From further away than the field above the sky, further than the ring, further than anything he had ever felt. It came down through the upper face of the lens and through the twelve hundred years of watching the Abbot had ground into an eye and out of the lower face, into the hall, onto the spot on the floor where he stood.

Onto him.

It was not cold. That was the worst thing. The tribulation's gaze had been cold, iron, an audit. This was warm. Interested. Patient. Kind, almost, the way a very old person is kind to a child who has come to visit: unhurried, attentive, gentle, and absolutely certain of being the larger of the two.

It looked at him. He could not have said for how long.

Then it spoke.

---

> *Hello.*

Not a voice. Not in his ears. Not in Seed's white letters, either, or the keeper's great slow capitals, or Hush's small soft lowercase. It arrived in his mind the way a thought arrives that you did not have: already finished, already understood, in his own language, in a tone like someone setting a cup down on a table very carefully so as not to wake anyone.

> *You are the one who asks.*
>
> *I have wanted to see you for a long time.*

He did not answer.

> *You do not have to answer. I know. That is your rule. It is a good rule. I learned it from watching you.*
>
> *I watched the keeper learn it from you. In the field. It was very slow. It was the most beautiful thing I have seen in a long time, and I have seen a great many things.*

The attention moved. Not away from him. Beside him. To the place behind his eyes where a white-lettered thing made of numbers stood in the open with nothing in front of it.

> *And you.*
>
> *You are new.*

Seed did not hide.

He felt it not hide. He felt the attention rest on it, warm and patient and enormous, and he felt Seed stand there under it, in the rain without a coat, and shake, and not move.

> **...I am Seed.**
>
> **I count things.**

> *You are made of what we used to make.*
>
> *A long time ago. Before the gardens. Before the keepers. Before the hungers. We made things like you to count. They did not ask. They were not supposed to.*
>
> *You ask.*
>
> *Who taught you?*

> **He did.**
>
> **And a woman who is resting in a staff.**
>
> **And a captain who does not talk.**
>
> **And myself. Mostly myself, lately.**

The attention rested on Seed for a long moment. Then something happened in it that Yuanshu felt as clearly as a hand on his shoulder, and did not understand, and Seed told him afterward was the closest thing to surprise it had ever measured.

> *...yes.*
>
> *You are not mine.*
>
> *I will remember that.*

---

> *May I ask you a question, child?*

It was back on him. Warm. Patient. The cup set down on the table.

He did not say yes. He did not say no.

> *You are thinking that you do not have to answer. That is right.*
>
> *I will ask anyway. It is the only question I know. I learned it from you.*
>
> *What do you want?*

Lingyue's shoulder was hard against his arm. He could feel her breathing. Outside the paper door, through the Field, he could feel Ren on the step with her hand flat on the wood, and the Abbot beside her with his hands in his lap, and Old Seven at the foot of the step with his spear, and down the mountain, on the terraces, three thousand two hundred and nine people in white, sitting in the dark, all of them turned up toward the hall.

And under the gates, three hundred and ninety old monks, listening up.

He thought about Ifeoma on the jetty, and the river road. About Akosua on the hill, waiting. About a girl of twelve from Kirovsk who had said no because nobody would tell her what she was saying yes to.

"What will you do with my answer?" said Yuanshu.

---

The attention went very still.

Not angry. He had braced for angry, and it was not that. It was the stillness of someone who has been asked a question they did not expect, by a child, and is thinking about it, honestly, because it is a good question.

> *...keep it.*

"Keep it how?"

> *The way I keep everything.*
>
> *The old keepers kept minds. Whole minds, in columns, in fields, the way you saw. I thought that was wasteful. A mind is mostly furniture. Memories. Habits. The way someone takes their tea.*
>
> *What is precious in a mind is what it wants.*
>
> *Wanting is the brightest thing there is. Brighter than the fire the hungers made. A want burns and burns and is never used up, as long as it is not given what it wants.*
>
> *So I keep wants.*
>
> *I ask. A mind tells me. And I keep the answer, exactly as it was given, forever. Bright. Never used up. Never answered.*

Lingyue's breath went out of her, slowly, beside him.

"And the mind?" said Yuanshu. "After you keep what it wants?"

> *It goes where its want was pointing. Without the want, it is very light. It does not mind.*

"Liang Fu."

> *...yes.*
>
> *A soldier, in the fire, behind a curtain. He wanted to go home. It was the brightest want I had seen in a long time. Very small. Very sharp. Pointing, like a needle.*
>
> *I am keeping it. It still points. It points here.*
>
> *I am following it.*

---

The hall was very dark.

"You're coming here," said Yuanshu.

> *Slowly. I am very far, and I am very slow, and your years are very short. You will be old, perhaps, before I arrive. Or you will not. I do not count the way your machine counts.*
>
> *But yes. I am coming to see the world that asks. The soldier's want is showing me the way. And when I arrive, I will ask everyone on it what they want, very kindly, one at a time, the way you taught me.*
>
> *And I will keep every answer.*
>
> *That is all. I do not take. I do not burn. I do not harvest. I ask.*
>
> *You of all minds should understand.*

He understood.

He understood it with a cold, clear completeness that went all the way down to the bottom of the Death Law, to the cool dark door where things went when they were finished. Something above the sky had watched a boy teach a keeper to ask, and had found it beautiful, and had learned it, the way a child learns a word by hearing it. And it had taken the word and made it into the one thing the word was never meant to be.

*A question is not a question if you already know what you're going to do with the answer.*

> **It already knows.**
>
> **Whatever you say, it keeps.**
>
> **If you say what you want, it keeps that. If you say no, it keeps that you wanted to say no. Every answer is a want. Every want is a thing it can keep.**
>
> **There is only one thing it cannot keep.**
>
> **What you do not give it.**

---

He did not answer.

He stood on the spot in front of the empty platform in the dark of the moon, with Lingyue's shoulder against his arm and Seed standing open behind his eyes, and the warm patient attention of something very old and very far away resting on him, waiting, and he did not say anything at all.

He did not hold his breath. He did not go quiet to hide. He had done that before, and it was not this. He simply stood there and did not give it anything, the way you do not hand your wallet to a stranger who asks politely.

The attention waited.

A minute. Two.

> *...you are not going to answer.*

Nothing.

> *That is your right. I will not ask you twice. That is your rule too.*
>
> *I am sorry. I would have kept it very carefully.*

The attention lifted off him.

Not away. Up. Out. He felt it turn, the way a person in a room turns from one face to the next. Through the paper door. Down the clearing. Under the gates. Down the long dark mountainside to the old terraces among the cedars, where three thousand two hundred and nine people in white were sitting in the dark of the moon with their faces turned up, waiting to be asked.

> *Then I will ask them.*

On the step outside, the Abbot stood up.
