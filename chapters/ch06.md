## Chapter 6: The Spear That Teaches

The training hall on the twelfth floor of Tianshu was a white box fifty meters on each side.

No windows. No decoration. The walls were made of a pale, faintly soft material that swallowed sound, so that footsteps landed dead and voices went flat, like talking inside a snowdrift. They had been built to absorb a King's full strike without cracking. Along one wall stood a rack of training weapons: plain steel spears, blunt swords, round wooden shields.

At five in the morning, the hall held two living people and one dead one.

"He can't talk," said Su Mingli, leaning against the wall with a paper cup of tea steaming in her hand. "So how exactly is he going to teach you?"

"The same way he talked to Grandpa Xu," said Yuanshu, and took a spear down from the rack.

---

Old Seven stood in the middle of the white floor with his own spear grounded beside him.

In the daylight of the hall's ceiling panels he looked less like a corpse and more like a statue someone had carved out of dark wood and then left unfinished. The soil was gone from his bones. They had darkened, over thirty-one years in the ground, to the color of old tea. The small gray flame in the cage of his ribs burned without flickering.

> **I can translate his intent into language. Roughly.**
>
> **His echo thinks in movement, not in words.**
>
> **Right now it means: "Show me how you stand."**

Yuanshu took a stance.

Feet apart. Left foot forward. Spear held low in both hands, point at the height of an enemy's throat. He had trained basic spear for three years in high school, like every student on Blue Star, and he had been top of his class at that as well. It was a clean, correct stance. It would have scored full marks on any exam.

Old Seven looked at it for three seconds.

Then he walked across the floor, unhurried, and tapped the inside of Yuanshu's left knee with the butt of his spear.

Hard.

The knee buckled half an inch. Yuanshu corrected it.

*Tap.* The right hip.

*Tap.* The back of the left wrist.

*Tap. Tap.* The top of each shoulder, one after the other, like someone knocking on a door.

> **Translation: "Your body is pretty. Your body is wrong. You stand like someone who has fought exams. Not beasts."**

Against the wall, Su Mingli made a small sound into her tea that might have been a laugh.

"Fair," said Yuanshu.

---

Old Seven stepped back four paces. He lifted his own spear.

And then he moved.

Not fast. Slowly. One single thrust, from guard to full extension, so slow that Yuanshu could see each joint lock into place, one after another, in a chain.

Ankle.

Knee.

Hip.

Spine.

Shoulder.

Elbow.

Wrist.

Each joint turned a fraction and then locked, and the moment it locked, the next one began, so that the force traveled up through the skeleton's body like water through a series of canal locks, each gate opening into the next, adding a little more each time, until at the very end it all arrived at the spearhead at once.

The air in front of the spearhead tore.

It made a sound like a bedsheet ripped in half. A pale ripple shot off the point and crossed twenty meters of empty hall in an instant and hit the far wall, which rang, *bong,* like an enormous bell that someone had struck with a mallet. The ring went on for three full seconds before the soft wall swallowed it.

From a slow thrust.

Su Mingli's tea had stopped halfway to her mouth.

> **Spear Intent observed: 21.3%.**
>
> **Modeling.**
>
> **This is different from watching him in the cemetery. There, he was killing. Here, he is showing.**
>
> **A lesson has more structure than a fight. It is built to be understood.**
>
> **He is making it easy for me.**

*Run it.*

The white hall went gray.

Yuanshu was standing inside his own head, and Old Seven's thrust was replaying in front of him. Slow. Slower. From the front, from above, from behind, from inside the bones. Silver lines lit up along the chain of joints, showing the path of the force like a current in a wire. Then a second figure appeared beside the skeleton, a gray copy of Yuanshu himself, holding the same spear, doing the same thrust.

His own silver line broke in three places.

The inside of the left knee. The right hip. The back of the left wrist.

Exactly where Old Seven had tapped.

The dead soldier had found every flaw in three seconds, with his eyes.

Seed needed nine thousand simulations to understand why.

> **Spear Intent: 0% → 4.1%.**

He opened his eyes. Took the stance again. This time he could feel it, the places where the chain leaked, like a drafty window you suddenly notice in winter. He closed them as well as he could.

He thrust.

It wasn't the skeleton's thrust. The force still leaked at the hip. But at the end of the strike, the air in front of the training spear went *snap,* sharp and small, like a whip crack, and the steel shaft shivered in his hands as if it had come alive for a moment.

Old Seven tilted his skull to one side.

Then he raised one hand and flicked two fingers sideways. A tiny, impatient gesture.

> **Translation: "Again."**

---

They went again.

And again.

By seven o'clock the blisters on Yuanshu's palms had come up, and by half past seven they had broken, and he wrapped his hands in strips torn from a towel and kept going. By eight, Su Mingli had finished three cups of tea and had given up pretending she was there for any reason except to watch. By nine, Xiaoyu had wandered in in her pajamas with her phone, been told very firmly by Su Mingli that she could not livestream from a classified research institute, and sat down cross-legged against the wall to watch anyway.

Old Seven never said a word. He corrected with taps. With gestures. With the slow demonstration, over and over, ankle-knee-hip-spine-shoulder-elbow-wrist, as patient as a tide.

Once, around ten, Yuanshu got careless. He was tired, and his guard dropped by a hand's width at the end of a thrust.

The skeleton's spear shaft came around in a low flat sweep, almost lazily, and took both his feet out from under him.

The white floor came up and hit him in the back.

He lay there staring at the ceiling panels, breathing hard, sweat stinging his eyes.

Old Seven walked over and stood above him. Looked down. Then, very deliberately, he reached down and tapped Yuanshu once on the forehead with one bony finger.

*Tap.*

> **Translation: "Dead."**
>
> **Additional note: I believe he is enjoying this.**

"I can tell," said Yuanshu, and started to laugh, lying on the floor, for the first time in two days.

Over by the wall, Xiaoyu laughed so hard she fell over sideways.

> **Spear Intent: 4.1% → 9.8%.**

---

At noon they stopped.

Yuanshu sat against the wall with a towel around his neck and his wrapped hands resting on his knees. Every muscle in his body hummed like an overworked wire. His soul-power was down to thirty percent. Feeding Seed's simulations for seven hours straight had felt like holding his breath underwater.

> **9.8% in one morning.**
>
> **For a gifted student with a living master, ten percent Spear Intent usually takes about three years.**
>
> **But I want to be accurate. It was not only me.**
>
> **I could not have modeled it this fast from a battle. He taught in a way that was easy to model. He is a very good teacher.**

Across the hall, Old Seven had walked over to the far wall, where a narrow observation window looked out over the city. He stood in front of it with his spear on his shoulder, very still, the way the living stand at windows when they are thinking about something.

*What was he like?* Yuanshu asked. *Before?*

> **I cannot read memories yet. Only intent.**
>
> **But when he looks at the city, his intent is very simple.**
>
> **It is: "Still standing. Good."**

The door opened.

Grandpa Xu came in slowly, leaning on his cane, with something wrapped in oilcloth under his other arm. He didn't speak to anyone. He crossed the floor at his own slow pace and stopped beside Old Seven at the window, and for a while the old man and the dead one stood there side by side, looking out at Lingzhou.

"He used to do this," Grandpa Xu said, at last, to no one in particular. "Stand on the wall in the morning. Count the rooftops. He said if he could still count them, we were winning."

In the skeleton's chest, the gray flame brightened, just for a moment.

Then the old man turned away from the window and came over to where Yuanshu was sitting, and held out the oilcloth bundle in both hands.

"I've had this for thirty-one years," he said. "I kept it because nobody else would believe what's inside it." His hands were not quite steady. "I think it's time it went to the right person."

Yuanshu took it. It was heavier than it looked. Through the stiff old cloth, his fingers found the corners of a book.

> **Faint residue.**
>
> **Death. Old. Very strong.**
>
> **And something else underneath it. A signature I do not recognize.**

"What is it?"

Grandpa Xu lowered himself onto the bench beside him with a long, tired breath.

"Lu Xuanming's notebook," he said. "The Black Tide Necromancer. He wrote in it every night of the war. Right up to the last one." The old man folded both hands on top of his cane. "The last page isn't addressed to me. It never was."

He looked at the bundle in Yuanshu's hands.

"It's addressed to *the next one.*"

Across the hall, Old Seven turned away from the window. And for the first time all morning, he did not move at all.
