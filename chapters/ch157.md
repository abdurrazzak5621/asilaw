## Chapter 157: Light Runs Downhill

At 05:52, eight minutes before the land breeze, the trunk off Port Abiri stopped growing and began to listen.

> **It knows.**
>
> **It cannot see the drowned. They are banked. But it can feel the sand moving around its root. Nearly three thousand pairs of feet on the bottom of the sea. It is like a man in a dark room who hears breathing.**
>
> **It is pulling its roots in. Toward the heap. Tight.**

Yuanshu stood at the end of the harbor wall with the staff planted on the stone and the Banked Field folded over the whole bay, five kilometers of slope running downhill toward him through the water, and felt the trunk out there in the dark like a cold column in his chest. Three kilometers tall. Gray crystal, cracked, glowing the color of a coal at every seam, rising out of a heap of sand on the seabed the size of a village, through the cold dark layers of the sea, almost to the surface. Its top was forty meters down. The water above it had been steaming since four.

At the bottom, around the heap, in the cold, two thousand eight hundred and forty-six of the drowned of the Southern Compact coast stood in the dark in a ring a kilometer wide, banked, waiting.

And they could not hear him.

---

He had known that since midnight. Seed had told him. Without Ifeoma's pipe, a question sent down from the harbor wall scattered in the layers of the sea and arrived at the bottom as a blur, the way a voice arrives through a closed door: you knew someone had spoken, but not what they said. The Field reached the bottom. The Field could feel every ember down there. But the Field could not speak.

So he had done the only thing he could think of. At one in the morning, before Ifeoma left, while she still held the pipe for a last hour, he had sent down one order.

Not many. One. Simple enough to remember in the cold and the dark for five hours without hearing anything else.

*When the sea starts to rise, go in. All of you. To the heap. Find the cracks. Pale fire, if it comes. If it doesn't come, go in anyway, with your hands.*

> **They said yes.**
>
> **Captain Efua Asante said: that's a good order. You can remember it drunk.**
>
> **Emeka said: it's the kind of order Ify would give.**

---

At 05:55, at the top of the beach, Mo Xing began to fold the people in white.

A hundred and fifty-one of them were still there, sitting in their rows on the cold sand, facing the bay. The rest had gone up the hill road in the night, the way Hendrik Maas had said they would. Yuanshu had gone down to them at five and told them again, in person, that it was time, and why. Then Mo Xing folded them, two at a time, from the sand to the backs of the three trucks Ifeoma's household had left at the top of the beach, and Bai Lu sat with each of them for a moment with her hands on their shoulders, and Hu Dabao shut the tailgates. Some of them wept. One woman spat at him. Most of them just looked at the sea as the trucks pulled away, the way you look at a door someone has closed in your face.

He did not watch them go. He had not earned that.

---

At 06:00, the land breeze came down off the hills behind Port Abiri, warm and smelling of grass, and pushed the warm water at the top of the bay away from the shore. And three kilometers down, at the foot of the shelf, the cold deep water began to rise.

The drowned went in.

He felt them go. Through the Field, all at once, two thousand eight hundred and forty-six small banked embers moving inward across the seabed toward the heap, through the cold and the dark, the way the ferry's wedding party had probably moved toward the rail when the storm hit. Not running. Walking. Bent against the rising water. Fishermen and sailors and a girl who had been a very good swimmer, actually, everyone said so.

The trunk felt them too.

It did not see them. It could not. But it felt the sand shift as they reached the edge of the heap, and every root it had pulled in tight went out again at once, blind, sweeping, the way a man in a dark room swings his arms.

> **It found eleven.**
>
> **It does not need to see them. It only needs to touch them.**
>
> **They are burning.**

He felt them go out. Eleven small cold embers at the edge of the heap, touched by a blind root in the dark, flaring once, the way the graves had flared on the Western Pass, and gone.

*Pale fire*, he thought. *They need pale fire.*

And pale fire was a nineteen-year-old with two swords, and he did not know where she was.

---

She came along the beach at 06:04, running.

He saw her from the end of the wall. A small figure in the gray light, sprinting along the hard wet sand at the edge of the bay from the direction of the point, with her grandfather's sword in her left hand and her own in her right, both bare, and her hair coming loose. She did not come to the wall. She ran past the end of it and straight out onto the rocks at the mouth of the harbor, where the land ended and the sea began, and stopped there, at the very edge, with the swell breaking white around her ankles.

"*Don't count me yet,*" she said, through Seed, through the staff, through the Field. Out of breath. "*I've only got it half.*"

> **Got what?**

"*I'll show you.*"

She raised her own sword over her head, point down, the way you would hold a sword to plant it in the ground. And she lit it.

---

Yuanshu had seen Lingyue's Light a thousand times. Silver-white, bright, sharp, the color of a knife in the sun. The Light of the Shen family, which had been shining for three hundred years, and was proud of it, and should have been. It went out from her blade the way Light always went out. Away. Outward. Up. A thing that pushed the dark back.

Inside the Field, it went pale. Silver-gray. Pale fire. The Death Law wearing her Light like a coat.

And she did not shine it.

She stood on the rocks at the mouth of the harbor with the pale fire burning on her lifted blade, and he saw her shut her eyes, and he felt something change in the Field that he had never felt before.

The pale fire got heavy.

> **...what is she doing.**
>
> **Yuanshu. What is she doing.**

He did not know. He felt it through the Field the way you feel a stone dropped into your open hand. The silver-gray light on her sword was not going out from the blade any more. It was going down. Pooling. Collecting at the point of the blade like water at the tip of an icicle. Heavier. And heavier.

And then she let go of it.

Not the sword. The light.

It fell off the point of her blade into the sea, a single drop of silver-gray fire the size of a fist, and went into the water, and did not stop. It did not spread. It did not scatter in the layers. It went straight down through three kilometers of cold dark sea the way a stone goes down a well, faster and faster, because the Field was a slope, and everything in the Field ran downhill, and she had made her Light into something that had weight.

He felt it hit the bottom.

At the edge of the heap. Among the drowned. A single drop of pale fire, on the seabed, in the cold and the dark, three kilometers down, landing on the rusted gaff hook of a fisherman from Kesse named Yaw who had gone over the side in a storm forty-one years ago.

The hook lit.

---

She did it again.

And again. And again. Standing on the rocks at the mouth of the harbor in the gray dawn, with the swell breaking around her ankles, lighting her blade and letting the light fall off the point of it into the sea, one drop after another, and every drop went straight down the slope of the Field to the bottom, to the drowned, to whatever edge they were holding: a gaff, a knife, a ship's spanner, the iron rim of a ferry's wheel that a captain had been holding when she went down.

Then she lit her grandfather's sword too.

A King's blade. A hundred kilos of it. She lit it with her left hand and let the light fall off that one too, two streams of silver-gray drops going down into the bay from two sword points, like rain falling from the eaves of a house.

"*Light runs downhill,*" said Shen Lingyue, through the staff, with her eyes shut, breathing hard. "*Grandfather said that. When he gave me his sword. He said he was going home to tell three hundred years of Shens that Light runs downhill.*" Another drop. Another. "*I thought he meant it like a saying. Like water. Like... Light goes to whoever needs it. Like be humble. I've been thinking about it since the train.*" Another. "*He didn't mean it like a saying.*"

> **...she is right. Inside the Field, it has weight. Because the Field gives Death weight. And pale fire is Death wearing Light.**
>
> **I have known that since the Western Pass. I never thought of dropping it.**
>
> **I did not count this. I did not know it was there to count.**

"*You never asked,*" said Lingyue.

> **[New technique observed: not mine.]**
>
> **[Shen Lingyue. Light Law. "Light Runs Downhill."]**
>
> **I am writing it under her name.**

---

At the bottom of the sea, the drowned of the Southern Compact went into the heap with fire in their hands.

He felt every one of them through the Field. Two thousand eight hundred and thirty-five banked embers, pressed in against a mound of waking sand the size of a village, with pale fire running down out of the dark above them like rain, onto their hooks and knives and spanners and bare finger bones. They did not need orders now. They had the one he had given them at one in the morning. *Find the cracks.* They were fishermen. They had spent their lives finding the weak place in a net, the rotten plank in a hull, the place in a reef where the current ran through.

They found the cracks.

The heap began to come apart.

> **The trunk is pulling everything in. It is not growing any more. It is trying to hold itself together.**
>
> **Six minutes of the morning tide left. If it holds for six minutes, the tide lifts it to the surface, and it bites.**
>
> **It is going to hold, Yuanshu. The drowned are not enough. There is a seam at the center of the heap, where the seed is, and they cannot reach it. The sand is too thick. They are digging with their hands.**

He felt it. The seed, in the middle of the heap. The small hot coal it had all grown out of. Three hundred meters of sand between it and the nearest drowned.

*Where is the Spear Corps?*

> **Where you told them. On the slope below the harbor. Eight hundred meters down. Walking. They will not reach the bottom for an hour.**

*Mo Xing.*

> **On the wall behind you. He cannot fold anything three kilometers. He has never folded anything more than forty meters.**

He stood at the end of the harbor wall with the staff in both hands.

The trunk. Three kilometers of gray crystal standing up through the sea, from the heap to forty meters below the surface, riding the rising tide. And at the bottom of it, at the very root, where it came up out of the sand, the seed.

The trunk was not a tree. It did not grow from the top. It grew from the bottom, the way the trunks at the Western Pass had grown, up out of the seed, through the rock. Everything it had flowed up the inside of it from the root.

And everything that flowed up could flow down.

---

"*Lingyue,*" he said. "*Not the drowned. The top of the trunk.*"

She opened her eyes.

"*It's forty meters down. You can see it. There's steam. Drop it on the top. The trunk's hollow, the way the spire on the Western Pass was hollow. Everything it eats goes up the inside. If you put pale fire in at the top, it'll run down. All the way. Inside. To the root.*" He held the staff tight. "*To the seed.*"

> **...it will go downhill inside the trunk the same way it goes downhill through the water. Faster. The inside of the trunk is the steepest part of the Field. It leads to the seed.**
>
> **Yuanshu. That is right.**
>
> **Ninety-one percent.**

Lingyue looked at the steaming water off the mouth of the harbor, forty meters above the top of a three-kilometer trunk of crystal she could not see.

Then she put both swords together, point to point, her own and her grandfather's, the way two hands come together, and lit them both at once, and let the light run off the joined points, not in drops now, but in a single unbroken stream of silver-gray fire that fell into the steam and went down.

---

He felt it go in at the top of the trunk like water into a drain.

Down the inside. Three kilometers. Faster than anything he had ever felt move through the Field, a thin bright thread of pale fire running down the hollow core of the trunk, through every ember it had eaten in a year, the old cold drowned it had sipped one at a time in the dark, past them, not touching them, down, down, down, to the root, to the heap, to the small hot coal in the middle of three hundred meters of sand.

It hit the seed.

The seed cracked.

He felt it the way he had felt the seeds crack at the Western Pass. A small sharp sound, three kilometers down, that he heard through his feet and his teeth and the stone of the harbor wall. And then the trunk above it, with nothing left at its root to hold it, began to come apart. From the bottom. Sand. Gray, fine, pouring off the seabed in long soft clouds, through the cold dark water, while the drowned of the Southern Compact stood in their ring around it, holding their lit hooks and knives and spanners, and watched it go.

At the mouth of the harbor, the steam stopped.

> **[The Port Abiri trunk is dead.]**
>
> **06:19. Two minutes before the tide would have lifted it.**
>
> **Of the drowned: one hundred and four burned. I have their names. I asked all of them two nights ago. I will read them out.**
>
> **Captain Efua Asante is one of them. She was at the front, at the heap. She said she should not have sailed.**
>
> **Emeka is not one of them. He is standing at the edge of the sand, holding a fishing knife with pale fire on it. He is asking which way Sandu is.**

On the rocks at the mouth of the harbor, Shen Lingyue sat down very suddenly in the breaking swell, with two swords across her knees, soaking wet to the waist, and put her head down on her arms.

He went out along the rocks to her. She did not look up.

"I said give me until dawn," she said into her arms.

"It was dawn."

"It was 06:04. That's after dawn." She lifted her head. Her face was gray with tiredness and there was salt in her hair. "Don't you ever count me as a girl on a wall again."

"No."

"Promise."

"I promise," said Yuanshu.

> **I promise too.**
>
> **Kesse surfaces in nine hours.**
>
> **And Ifeoma has started.**
