## Chapter 105: The Low Fire

It took nine days to ask them all.

Not because they were loud. They were not loud. They were the quietest fire in the Star Sea. Billions of minds burning low in the dull orange rivers of the second ring, calm, patient, closed-eyed, waiting out a winter that had lasted ten million years.

It took nine days because there were so many.

The Sixth led. The gold antlered shapes went out from the cold place along the rivers, a few hundred of them, spreading through the vast dim interior of the ring the way a choir spreads through a cathedral before a service. And at every world, in every river, they stopped. And bowed their long necks. And called softly into the dull orange, the way you'd call into a sleeping house.

And Yuanshu followed.

On the raft. With the staff. With Seed and the Elder and Jiang Lan and the keeper at the far end of the gold thread. Following the Sixth from world to world through the cold dim fire, and asking.

*Do you want it to stop?*

---

He would remember some of them.

Not all. There were too many. Even Jiang Lan, who had counted ninety million names, gave up counting by the third day and started keeping tallies instead, very fast, in her dry brisk voice, like a teacher marking a stack of exams.

But some.

A world of beings like great slow turtles, whose minds moved so slowly that each answer took an hour, and every answer was the same word, said with enormous patience: *Please.*

A world of creatures that had been, Seed thought, something like birds. Their faces flickered in the orange like sparks. They answered all at once, a thousand of them, a single bright chorus, *YES,* so loud and so sudden that the Sixth winced, and then apologized for them, and said they had always been like that.

A world whose minds were so strange that Seed could not translate them at all, only the shape of the answer, which felt like a long breath let out after holding it for a very, very long time.

And a world, very old, very deep in the coal, near the cold place, whose minds were not like anything. Small. Quiet. Curled. Like Hush. Many-limbed and many-eyed and pale. And when the Sixth called softly into their river, the answer came back in a voice like the inside of a seashell.

> ***...what do you want?***

Hush, in Old Seven's hand, went perfectly still.

> ***...not the Asking World,*** said the Sixth softly. ***...a cousin world. Near to yours. The same people, once, a long time ago. Before the Hunger.***
>
> ***...they kept the greeting too.***

Hush climbed down out of Old Seven's hand and walked to the edge of the raft and looked down into the dull orange river.

Then it answered them.

The way you answer a greeting. *What do you want?*

The voices in the river were silent. Then, softly, all together:

> ***...cousin.***
>
> ***...you are not burning.***
>
> ***...how?***

Hush told them. It took an hour. Nobody interrupted.

---

They answered in three ways.

Seed kept the count. Jiang Lan checked it. The Sixth checked it against their ledger in the fire. And the keeper, at the far end of the thread, listened.

*Do you want it to stop?*

Every one said yes.

Every single one. Billions. Not one, in all the dim rivers of the second ring, said no.

But that was not the only question. Yuanshu asked the other one too, every time, because it was the question that came after. The one he had asked three thousand people at Port Abiri, and nineteen thousand in a valley of bones, and seven monks in a hall on a mountain.

*And then what do you want?*

---

> **QUIET: most of them.**
>
> **They have been burning low for so long. They are so tired. They want to rest.**
>
> **THE KEEPER: some. They want to be held. Somewhere cool. Somewhere they can remember who they were, before they choose.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **Some of them want to stay.**

*Stay where?*

> **Here.**
>
> **In the ring.**

---

The Sixth explained.

They gathered in the cold place on the ninth day, when the asking was done. The seven Architects in their seats. The living on the raft. The dead at its rim. And the choir of the Sixth, standing in the air around them, gold and antlered and soft.

> ***...this ring is the only home we have,*** they said. ***...our world is gone. It was eaten a very long time ago. There is nothing to go back to.***
>
> ***...but we know this ring. Every river. Every throat. Every mouth. We have lived in it for longer than your sun has been lit.***
>
> ***...when the fire stops, the ring will be dark. Cold. Empty. A stone. Like the first one.***
>
> ***...unless someone stays.***

"And keeps it lit?" said Yuanshu.

> ***...not lit. Not burning.***
>
> ***...we have been burning low for ten million years. We know how to make a very little light go a very long way.***
>
> ***...we would like to hold it. Ourselves. Not as fuel. As... a hearth. A lamp in a window. Something that does not eat.***
>
> ***...we would like to keep the ring. And make it into something else.***
>
> ***...if you will let us.***

He looked at the choir.

"It's not mine to let you," he said. "It's yours. You've been in it for ten million years. You asked everyone. You kept the ledger." He looked around the cold place. At the seven Architects. "Ask them," he said, nodding at the builders. "They made it. And then ask the others who want to stay. And then decide."

The Sixth turned to the Architects.

The oldest one looked at them for a long, long time with the soft gray light in the seam of its face.

> ***...we built it to burn,*** it said slowly. ***...we never thought of anything else it could be.***
>
> ***...yes.***
>
> ***...make it into something else.***
>
> ***...and when you do, I hope you will let us come aboard sometimes. To see what it became.***

---

The oldest Architect laid its hands on the seal.

It looked around the circle. At the Sixth. At the dust of its cousins in the seats beneath it. At the boy with the staff.

> ***...every mind it is burning has agreed,*** it said.

And it pressed down.

---

It did not go out the way the first one had.

The first furnace had gone out like a candle pinched between two fingers. Instantly. Totally. A sun going dark.

This one went slowly.

Through the translucent walls of the cold place, they watched it. The dull orange of the vast coal dimming. Dimming. The dim orange rivers in the walls of the throat going dark one by one, the way a city's lights go out late at night, window after window. And down every river, along the gold thread, out through the mouth and across the Star Sea toward the keeper's field, billions of small gold lights drifting away. Going to be held. Going to rest.

And then the coal was dark.

And for one long moment, the whole second ring was dark. Silent. Cold. Stone.

And then, very slowly, somewhere deep in the walls, a light came on.

---

Gold.

Low. Soft. Steady. Like a candle in a window. Not in the furnace. Not in the rivers. In the stone itself. Faint lines of gold spreading through the cracked gray walls of the ring, through every throat and passage and chamber, like veins, like the patterns of frost on a window catching the morning sun.

Not burning.

Glowing.

Held. By a few hundred million minds who had chosen to stay. Who had spent ten million years learning how to make a very little light go a very long way. Spreading their own low gold through the stone of the thing that had eaten them, the way you'd hang lanterns through a house after a long winter.

They watched it from the cold place, through the walls. The gold spreading. Outward. Through the whole vast ring. Until the second Hunger, the thing that had eaten worlds since before Blue Star had a moon, was glowing from inside like a lantern.

Like a Lantern.

> ***...we have a name for it,*** said the Sixth softly. ***...we decided. While you were asking.***
>
> ***...the Low Fire.***

---

On the outer face of the ring, all its mouths were closed. Tens of thousands of them. Frosted shut.

As they watched, the frost on every one of them began to melt.

And the mouths did not open.

They softened. Changed. The cracked gray lips going smooth. Rounding. Filling in. Until, all across the vast face of the ring, where there had been mouths, there were only small round windows. Glowing gold.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is not a Hunger anymore.**

---

They told him the rest that night, in the cold place, with the gold glowing in the walls.

About the other two.

> ***...the third and fourth rings are different,*** said the Sixth. ***...younger. Built later. After the builders learned to fear what they had made.***
>
> ***...after the builders in the first ring built their lock.***
>
> ***...the builders of the third and fourth saw the lock. And they were afraid of it. They were afraid that one day someone would come and ask the fire to stop. And the fire would say yes.***

The seven Architects had gone very still.

> ***...so they built them without a cold place.***
>
> ***...without a seal.***
>
> ***...without a lock.***
>
> ***...there is no question you can ask the third and fourth rings that will make them stop.***
>
> ***...no matter how many say yes.***

Nobody in the cold place said anything.

> ***...and they are not burning low,*** said the Sixth. ***...we have tried, for ten million years, to reach the minds in their fire. To ask them to burn low. Through the echoes.***
>
> ***...they cannot hear us. They are screaming too loudly.***
>
> ***...the third and fourth are the hungriest things in the Sea.***
>
> ***...and they have turned toward your world.***

---

"How long," said Yuanshu, very quietly, "until they get there?"

> **The keeper says: sixty-eight days.**

"And how long for us to get home? On the currents?"

> **"Ninety,"** said the Elder softly. **"Perhaps more."**

Nobody moved.

Then the choir of the Sixth stepped forward. All of them. Gold and antlered and soft.

> ***...we can carry you.***
>
> ***...the Low Fire is weak. Starved. Ten million years.***
>
> ***...but it is still a Hunger's body. And a Hunger does not ride currents.***
>
> ***...it falls.***
>
> ***...if you ask us, we will fall toward your world. As fast as we can.***

Yuanshu looked around the cold place. At Lingyue, with her hand on her sword. At the twins. At Bai Lu. At Old Seven, with a small pale shape curled in his three-fingered hand. At the seven Architects. At the Elder. At sixty-two soldiers of the Third Battalion and a dead captain in a doorway.

He did not have to ask them. He could see it in their faces.

He asked anyway.

And then he turned to the Sixth, and bowed his head, the way they had bowed theirs.

"Yes," he said. "Please."
