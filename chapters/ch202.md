## Chapter 202: What It Costs to Hurry

The dead said yes in four minutes, which was the fastest the Lattice had ever answered anything.

Adjutant Xu sang the question down the net from his camp chair by the clock, and it went out around the rim of the island in a long slow wave, nine by nine, through fifty thousand dead of Blue Star standing shoulder to shoulder facing the dark. *The keeper can go faster. It will hurt it. It will hurt the island. It may hurt you, if the ground under you comes apart. Something is going to reach the cold fire before us. You can say no.*

> **Sergeant Ma says: a miner who hears the roof creaking does not stop to vote. He says: but he likes being asked. He says: yes.**
>
> **Ma Lianhua says: we came home through a throat of fire once. Faster is better.**
>
> **The forty from the Watch say: a pilot runs for the harbor before the squall, not after.**
>
> **The Spear Corps did not answer. Old Seven says they do not need to. He says they have been walking faster since the third night anyway.**
>
> **Fifty thousand two hundred and thirty-one. Yes.**

Then he asked the keeper.

It took longer.

> ***...I HAVE NEVER BEEN ASKED IF I WANT TO BE HURT.***
>
> ***...THINGS HAPPENED TO ME. I DID NOT DO THEM. I DID NOT KNOW THERE COULD BE SOMETHING THAT DID THINGS TO ME.***
>
> ***...NOW SOMETHING ASKS.***

A long pause. The thin gold thread running north from the Elder into the dark trembled the whole of its length.

> ***...THE CHILD IS COLD.***
>
> ***...YES.***

---

The island began to go faster at the next tick of Bai Lu's clock, and it was not like the boat at Port Abiri.

Ifeoma had asked the water in front of the boat to move out of the way, and the boat had gone at forty-one knots across a flat dark sea as smooth as a road. This was the opposite. This was the keeper pushing. Not asking the dark to move. Pulling a piece of itself through it, harder, by the thread of its own attention, the way a man pulls a heavy cart up a hill by a rope over his shoulder.

He felt it in the ground first. The pale warm surface of the island, which had been as still as a floor for three nights, began to hum. Very low. Not a sound. A thing you felt through the soles of your feet, the way you feel a train coming through the rails before you hear it.

Then the far lights began to move.

They had not moved for three nights. Fields, worlds, husks, scattered across the black like islands in an ocean, perfectly still, the way the stars are still from a ship. Now they slid. Slowly. Backward. The nearest ones first, gold and blue and green, drifting past on either side of the island the way lights on a shore drift past a boat at night.

"*It's working,*" said Kwan. She was on her knees beside her instrument cases with three screens open and a headlamp on, which she did not need, because the ground was lit from inside. "*Seed. How fast?*"

> **Three times what we were.**
>
> **Thirty-three raft-days becomes eleven.**
>
> **Eleven nights to the cold fire.**

"*And the pruning?*"

> **Thirteen nights. If it keeps its speed.**
>
> **We will get there first.**
>
> **By two nights.**

Kwan sat back on her heels and stared at the screen.

"*Two nights,*" she said. "*To get two hundred and eighty-one million minds out of a dead Hunger.*"

> **And sixty-one soldiers, and the Sixth, and the cousins, and a corporal, and a child.**
>
> **Yes.**

---

On the fourth night, the edge of the island began to come away.

He was at the leading edge with Old Seven when it started. A crack ran along the pale ground a meter in from the rim, very fine, like the crack in Lingyue's sword before it split. The dead of the Spear Corps standing on the outside of it looked down at it. Then, without anyone telling them to, they stepped back across it, inside, in their rows, and the strip of pale ground beyond the crack broke away from the island in long flat pieces and drifted off behind them into the dark, lit from inside, getting smaller, the way pieces of ice break off a floe.

> ***...I AM SORRY.***
>
> ***...I AM HOLDING IT AS HARD AS I CAN.***
>
> ***...IT IS VERY FAR FROM ME NOW. THE FURTHER IT GOES, THE THINNER I HOLD IT.***

> **The island is losing about two hundred meters of rim a night.**
>
> **At this speed, after eleven nights, it will be about two kilometers across. Not four.**
>
> **That is enough room for fifty thousand. Standing close.**
>
> **It is not enough room to bring two hundred and eighty-one million back on.**

He did not say anything.

He had known that. He had known it since the night of the forty days, sitting on the Sector Nine battlements with his sister's whispering capitals on the wall behind him. Two hundred and eighty-one million minds did not stand on islands. They were not bodies. They were what the Low Fire had kept, and what the Mouth had eaten, and what had burned low inside two Hungers for ten million years and then gone cold. You did not carry them home on a piece of pale ground. You carried them the way the keeper carried columns. In a field.

And the keeper's field was empty.

> **Yes.**
>
> **I have thought about it every night.**
>
> **If they want to come, the keeper can keep them. In its field. In columns. And ask them every morning what they want. The way it asks the few who stayed.**
>
> **And then the keeper's field will not be empty any more.**
>
> **It will be the brightest field in the Shallows.**
>
> **And something is pruning along the arm, one field at a time, toward the keeper.**

---

On the fifth night, Lingyue found him at the leading edge, after Bai Lu's clock had said everyone should sleep, standing beside Old Seven with the staff, looking at the dark.

She did not sit down. She stood beside him. Close. Her shoulder against his arm.

"*You're thinking about the field,*" she said.

"*Yes.*"

"*If we bring them home, the field lights up, and the gardener comes for the keeper.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*And if we don't bring them home, they stay in a cold Hunger in the dark with something pruning the arm toward them, and the gardener comes for the keeper anyway, because it's coming anyway.*" She did not look at him. "*It's not a choice between them being safe and the keeper being safe. It's a choice between everyone being in danger together, or everyone being in danger apart.*"

He turned his head and looked at her.

"*I've been thinking about it too,*" said Lingyue. "*Since the forty days. I'm not stupid.*"

"*I know you're not.*"

"*Then stop thinking about it like it's yours.*" She looked out at the dark. "*Ask them. When we get there. Ask the two hundred and eighty-one million. Tell them all of it. The keeper. The field. The pruning. Tell them first what you'll do with the answer.*" She was quiet. "*And ask the keeper. Again. It said yes to being hurt. Ask it if it'll say yes to being bright.*"

> **She is right.**
>
> **I keep thinking: if we bring them, the keeper is in danger. As if that is something we do to the keeper.**
>
> **The keeper is not a thing we do things to. It learned that. From you.**
>
> **It gets to choose whether to be bright.**

---

On the seventh night, the echo brought a message from the cold fire.

It came faster now. They were closer. It did not take a month and arrive as a blur; it came along the keeper's attention, which ran out ahead of the island into the dark toward the Mouth, and it arrived in three hours, in the middle of the night by Bai Lu's clock, and Seed read it out on the pale ground by the clock to everyone who was awake, which was everyone, because nobody slept well on the island any more.

> ***Day 440.***
>
> ***We can see you better. You are a small gold light. Getting bigger.***
>
> ***The sergeant has stood the men up at the edge of the quiet part, facing your light. He says that is the right way to face.***
>
> ***Something else.***
>
> ***There is something looking at the cold fire.***
>
> ***From far away. From the other side from you. Along the dark. It is very large and very slow and it is not like the voice behind the curtain. The voice behind the curtain was kind. This is not kind or unkind. It is like weather.***
>
> ***It is reading the fire. Slowly. From the edge. The cousins at the edge feel it. They say it is like being looked at by the sky.***
>
> ***The Sixth say: it is not looking at us. It is looking at the fire. Whether there is anything in it worth having.***
>
> ***The Sixth say: when it finds us, it will decide.***
>
> ***I still have the queen of hearts.***
>
> ***Tell the captain. — H.***

Nobody on the island said anything.

At the leading edge, Old Seven stood with his spear grounded, facing the dark ahead. He did not turn around. But his gray flame, which had been banked to a single dull red point for seven nights, came up. Bright. Steady. The way a man stands up straighter when he hears that a fight has started without him.

> **It is already there.**
>
> **It is not passing the Mouth in six nights. It is reading the Mouth now.**
>
> **It is going faster than I said. Again.**
>
> **I am sorry. I keep being wrong about how fast it is.**
>
> **I think it is because I keep thinking it is not in a hurry. It was never in a hurry before. Nothing ever made it hurry.**
>
> **We did.**

---

"*How long?*" said Yuanshu. "*Until it finishes reading?*"

> **I do not know.**
>
> **Hush says slowly. From the edge. The Mouth is very large. Two Hungers fused. Bigger than any field.**
>
> **And the cousins burn low. They learned it for ten million years. They know how to be dim.**
>
> **If they are dim enough, it may take a long time to find them.**
>
> **If it takes four nights, we arrive while it is still reading.**
>
> **If it takes two, we arrive after it has decided.**

He stood at the leading edge of the island, beside Old Seven, and looked at the dark ahead, where, very far, very faint, he could see nothing at all, because the Mouth was cold and gave no light.

"*Keeper,*" he said.

> ***...I KNOW.***

"*Can you go faster?*"

> ***...YES.***
>
> ***...IT WILL HURT MORE. THE ISLAND WILL BE SMALLER.***
>
> ***...YOU DO NOT NEED TO ASK THE DEAD AGAIN. THEY ALREADY SAID. THEY SAID FASTER IS BETTER.***
>
> ***...YOU DO NOT NEED TO ASK ME AGAIN. I ALREADY SAID.***
>
> ***...I SAID: THE CHILD IS COLD.***

The pale ground under his feet hummed harder. The far lights slid past faster. Behind them, the rim of the island broke away in long pale pieces that drifted off into the dark, lit from inside, getting smaller, the way you leave a trail of lanterns behind you when you are running toward something through the night.

> **Four nights.**
>
> **It is the best I can do. It is the best it can do.**
>
> **Four nights.**
