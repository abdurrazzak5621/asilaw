## Chapter 120: The Lamp

Walter Ashby spoke to his great-granddaughter at six in the morning, in the children's ward of the Port Halvard hospital, by candlelight.

The power was still out. All of it. The diesel had run dry in the night, when the sphere pulled everything back into itself, and the backup generator had coughed twice and died. So the nurses had lit candles. Dozens of them. On every windowsill and every bedside table, and in jars on the floor down the middle of the ward, the way the people of Lingzhou set candles on the steps of a gate on a hill.

Ellie was sitting up in bed with her inhaler on its string around her neck and her mother asleep in the chair beside her.

She was awake. She had not slept.

Yuanshu came in quietly with the white staff in his hand, and Old Seven ducking under the door frame behind him, and Lingyue, and he sat down on the edge of the bed.

He did not have to explain. She looked at the staff. At the small gold light the size of a candle flame, sitting on the white skull at its top.

"Is that Grandpa?" she said.

"Yes."

"Is he burning?"

"No," said Yuanshu. "Not anymore. I got him out."

Ellie looked at the little gold light for a long time.

"He wanted to be the lighthouse," she said.

"He was. For eleven minutes. I was there. He could see your window."

---

The old man's voice came out of the air beside the bed. Not a wheeze. Clear. Young. The way it had sounded in the fire, when he could still see the light going round.

It was very short.

Yuanshu did not ask Seed to translate it. It was in her language anyway. And some things are not yours to hear.

But at the end, Ellie laughed. A small cracked surprised laugh, through tears. And then she put her hand flat on top of the little gold light on the staff. The way you'd put your hand on someone's head in a crowd to make sure they were still there.

And the light went quiet.

Gently. The way a candle goes out at the end of a very long evening. A little gold flame, dimming, and dimming, under a seven-year-old's hand, until there was nothing on the white skull but bone and silver.

---

Cassia Lowell suspended Project Hearth at nine o'clock that morning.

Not from a podium. Not in a press conference. From the small plain office at the back of what was left of Hearth One, with rain coming in through a broken window, on a phone call to the Concordia Council, in a voice that did not change at all.

"Suspended," she said. "Indefinitely. Every registration. Every yes. Frozen." A pause. "I'm resigning as Secretary. Effective when my replacement is sworn in." Another pause. "No. I'm not going to argue about it. I asked about the people. I never asked about the thing. That's not good enough. Not for this."

She put the phone down.

Then she sat for a long time, looking out of the broken window at the dark lighthouse on the point.

"Four hundred thousand," she said. Not to anyone. "Four hundred thousand people said yes. They wanted to be useful. They wanted their dying to light something." She closed her eyes. "And I almost put every one of them into a fire with no door."

Yuanshu stood in the doorway. He did not say anything.

"What do I tell them?" said Cassia Lowell.

He thought about it.

"Tell them the truth," he said. "Tell them about Walter. All of it. The light, and the eleven minutes, and the yes burning away, and the *stop.*" He leaned on the staff. "And then ask them again."

---

At dusk, Cassia Lowell climbed the lighthouse.

A hundred and twelve stairs. Iron. Spiral. Rusted. She did it slowly, holding the rail, in her plain dark suit, with a can of lamp oil in one hand and a box of matches in her pocket. Behind her, Ellie, in a raincoat over her pajamas, carrying her great-grandfather's old brass lighter, which her grandmother had found in a drawer at his flat.

Nobody had told them to.

Yuanshu watched from the headland, in the rain, with Lingyue beside him and Old Seven behind.

It took them twenty minutes. They found the old lamp at the top, behind its great dusty glass lens. The one the Federation had shut off nine years ago and replaced with nothing. A wick. A reservoir. Clockwork for the turning, wound by hand.

They did not know how it worked. They worked it out.

And at twenty-past six, in the last gray light, the Port Halvard lighthouse lit.

Not gold. Not blazing. Not powered by anything except oil and a wick and a seven-year-old with a brass lighter. A small yellow flame, behind a great glass lens that made it bigger. And then, slowly, as someone wound the clockwork, it began to turn.

A beam. Thin. Yellow. Sweeping out across the black harbor mouth and over the rocks under the point.

And around. And around.

"That's it," said Lingyue softly. "That's the whole thing."

"Yes," said Yuanshu.

---

The calls came that night. In the hotel, at midnight, one after another.

Marshal Kirill Orlov, from the Northern Union, in his careful old-fashioned Huaxian. The mining consortium that had bought a seed. Sergeant Sokolova had found it. In a sealed vault under a coal company's headquarters in the north. Asleep. Orlov had stood in front of it for an hour with frost spreading across the floor around his feet, and then he had frozen it solid, from the inside out, and broken it with his own hands. *It did not ask,* said Orlov. *So I did not ask either.*

Shirasagi Ren, from Yamato. The shell company. A warehouse on the docks of the eastern coast. The seed was gone. The crate was there. Empty. Its lid pried open. A receipt inside. Paid in gold.

Lu, from Lingzhou.

His voice was very quiet.

"Garrett Hale came to see me," he said. "This afternoon. At the Bureau. With Harlan Voss." A pause. "He's well. Mostly. He still turns his head sometimes. Voss sits with him." Another pause. "He brought his firm's records. All of them. The ones that were edited. He'd had someone restore them. He said, and I'm quoting: *It's my name on the door. Whoever did this did it in my name while I couldn't say anything. I'd like to say something now.*"

"What did they say?"

"Six private buyers. We've found two. Orlov's and Ren's." Paper rustling. "Three more, I can't trace. Offshore. Gold. The trail ends at the sea."

"And the sixth?"

Lu did not answer for a long time.

"The sixth was delivered," he said. "Eight months ago. To a loading dock in Lingzhou." A pause. "Dock B. One Riverside North."

The Institute.

"Who signed for it?"

"Nobody," said Lu. "The record says *accepted by: night watch.* The night watch that week was a Bureau contractor. Agency staff. Rotating. We're checking every name." His voice was very tired. "Gu was in the Listening Pavilion with a candle. I checked that first. Every night. Thirty witnesses."

"Where did it go? After the dock?"

"That's the thing," said Lu. "It didn't go anywhere. There's no record of it leaving the building." A pause. "It's still in there, Yuanshu. Somewhere. In the Institute."

---

He did not sleep.

He sat by the window of the hotel room in Port Halvard with the white staff across his knees, and watched the thin yellow beam of the lighthouse sweep across the harbor mouth. Around. And around.

In the staff, very faintly, two old women were asleep. One dry and brisk. One quiet and sharp. Snoring, both of them.

> **Yuanshu.**

*I know. The Institute.*

> **No.**
>
> **I mean: I can feel it.**
>
> **From here. From the other side of the world.**
>
> **It is not loud. It is very, very quiet. It has been quiet for eight months.**
>
> **But tonight, for one second, when the sphere died, every Hunger seed on Blue Star felt it. They echo. Like their mothers.**
>
> **And one of them, far away, in Lingzhou, flinched.**

*Where?*

A long pause.

> **Three hundred meters down.**
>
> **Under the Institute.**
>
> **The sealed lab. The round chamber. The cylinder of glass.**
>
> **Where your mother went in. Where the faceless one has been standing for thirty-one years.**

He went cold.

> **Specimen Zero.**
>
> **Someone did not hide the seed in the Institute, Yuanshu.**
>
> **Someone fed it to the faceless one.**
>
> **It has no eyes. It has no face. It never did.**
>
> **But tonight, for the first time since it turned toward you, the faceless one moved. Not three degrees.**
>
> **It turned its head.**
>
> **And it is looking up.**
