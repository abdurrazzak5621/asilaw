## Chapter 117: The Light

Walter Ashby died at 3:40 in the morning, in a white bed in the round room under the glass dome, with the rain running down it and the dark sphere on its pedestal three meters away.

He was not alone.

His granddaughter was there, a tired woman of forty in a cardigan, holding one of his enormous knotted hands. His great-granddaughter was there too, though she was not supposed to be. Ellie. Seven. Small and pale, with an inhaler on a string around her neck, sitting on the end of the bed in her pajamas because she had refused, absolutely, to stay in the children's ward, and the nurse had looked at the old man and looked at the child and let her come.

Cassia Lowell stood by the door. Roman Sterling stood outside it.

Yuanshu sat on a chair on the other side of the bed, with the white staff across his knees.

Lingyue stood behind him. Old Seven stood by the pedestal, with his spear in his three-fingered hand and his skull turned toward the sphere, and he had not moved in six hours.

> **It is waking.**
>
> **Very slowly.**
>
> **It can feel him. Dying. The way a dog smells food cooking in another room.**

---

At the end, the old man opened his eyes.

He looked at Ellie, on the end of the bed. Then at his granddaughter. Then, last, at Yuanshu.

"Properly," he said. It was barely a breath.

"Properly," said Yuanshu.

Walter Ashby closed his eyes.

His breathing slowed. And slowed. And stopped.

And in the center of the round room, on its pedestal, inside its ring of steel, the sphere opened its eye.

---

It did not look like an eye. Not at first. A crack in the cloudy gray surface, widening. And behind it, deep inside, a light. Dull. Red. Like a coal.

And then something came out of it.

Not a thread. Not a root. A pull. Yuanshu felt it the way you feel a door open in a cold house, a sudden draft across the floor. Reaching out from the sphere across the three meters of white stone to the bed. To the still figure in it.

And Walter Ashby's mind went.

Yuanshu saw it go. Through Seed. A faint warm golden light rising out of the old man's chest, the way it had risen out of the girl on the straw mat in the monk's vision, the way it had risen out of a whole country twelve hundred years ago. Rising. Drawn across the room. Into the crack in the sphere.

Gone.

And the sphere lit up.

---

It was beautiful.

That was the worst thing. It was the most beautiful light Yuanshu had ever seen. Not the gray-red of a furnace. Gold. Warm. Clean. Pouring out of the sphere and through the steel ring and up through the glass dome into the rain, so bright that the whole round room went white, so bright that the rain on the glass turned to falling sparks.

And outside, on the headland, the lighthouse lit.

For the first time in nine years. The great lamp at the top of the red and white tower blazing up, gold, and beginning, slowly, to turn. A long bright beam sweeping out across the black sea, across the harbor mouth, across the rocks under the point where three men had died in a storm the spring before.

And down the coast road, the hospital lit. All of it. Every window. The children's ward and the wards around it and the corridors and the car park and the sign over the door.

And then the street beyond. And the street beyond that. Port Halvard, lighting up in a long wave down the hill, block by block, window by window, the way it had gone dark every night for two years.

From the end of the bed, Ellie made a small sound.

She was not looking at the sphere. She was looking out through the glass wall of the round room, at the lighthouse on the point, its beam sweeping around, and around, across the dark sea.

"Grandpa's light," she said.

---

Yuanshu did not let himself look.

He put the white staff across his knees and both hands flat on the small white skull and closed his eyes.

"*Jiang Lan.*"

"*I'm here,*" said the dry voice. "*I know. The first door. Into that.*" A pause. "*I don't like it. It's very hungry.*"

"*I promised.*"

"*I know you promised. I was there.*" Another pause. "*Hold on to me. Don't let go. Whatever it does. If you let go, I can't get you back.*"

He held on.

And Jiang Lan opened the door.

---

It was not like the field. It was not like the furnace of the first ring.

It was small.

A room. Round. Close. Hot. The walls were gold and glowing and moving, like the inside of a flame seen very close. And in the middle of it, very small, very bright, burning, a shape.

An old man. Thin. With enormous knotted hands.

He was not screaming.

He was standing very still in the middle of the gold fire, with his face turned up, and his eyes wide open. Looking at something Yuanshu could not see. And on his face, an expression of such pure astonished joy that for a moment Yuanshu could not move.

> ***...I can see it.***

The voice was Walter Ashby's. But it was not a wheeze anymore. It was clear and young and wondering.

> ***...the light. From up here. From inside it. I can see it going round. Over the water. Over the rocks.***
>
> ***...I can see Ellie's window.***
>
> ***...son, I'm the lighthouse.***

---

Yuanshu stood in the gold fire beside him and did not say anything.

He held the staff. He held the quiet. He let the old man look.

And then he asked.

*Mister Ashby. I promised I'd ask you properly. After.*

*Do you still want to burn?*

The old man did not look away from whatever he was seeing.

> ***...yes.***
>
> ***...yes, son. Look at it. Look at it going round.***

*All right,* thought Yuanshu. *All right.*

He stayed.

He did not leave. He did not let go of the staff. He stood beside the old man in the small gold room in the middle of the fire, and watched him watch the light.

And waited.

---

It took eleven minutes.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is starting.**

He felt it before he saw it. A change in the gold. Not dimming. Pulling. The walls of the small round room drawing inward, very slightly, the way lungs draw in. And with them, from the old man in the center, something thin and bright, a thread of gold, being drawn out of him. Into the walls. Into the light.

Walter Ashby's face changed.

Not much. A flicker. The wide astonished joy faltering, like a candle in a draft.

> ***...son.***
>
> ***...son, what's that.***

*It's the burning,* thought Yuanshu. *Seed told you. It takes a little at a time. And turns it into light.*

> ***...it's taking.***
>
> ***...I can feel it taking.***

Another thread. Gold. Drawn out of his chest into the walls.

And the old man's face went blank.

Not empty. Puzzled. The way a face goes when you walk into a room and can't remember why you came.

> ***...why.***
>
> ***...why did I...***
>
> ***...there was a reason. There was a good reason. I said yes. I know I said yes. Twice. In front of you.***
>
> ***...why did I say yes?***

---

> **The first thing that burns away is the yes.**
>
> **You remember that you chose. But you cannot remember why.**

*You wanted to be the lighthouse,* Yuanshu told him. Very gently. *For Ellie. For the boats. For the three men under the point. You kept the light forty-three years. You wanted to keep one more.*

The old man looked at him with puzzled pale blue eyes.

> ***...did I?***
>
> ***...that sounds like me.***
>
> ***...I can't feel it. I know it's true. I can't feel it.***

Another thread. Drawn out. Gold.

And Walter Ashby began, very quietly, to cry.

> ***...it hurts, son. It hurts. The voice in your head was right. It hurts like being a fire.***
>
> ***...I don't know why I wanted this.***

*Ask me again,* the old man had said. *If I say no, you get me out.*

Yuanshu held the staff so hard his knuckles cracked.

*Mister Ashby,* he said. *Do you want to stop?*

---

The old lighthouse keeper looked at him.

And for one moment, under the puzzled pain, something came back into his face. Not the yes. Older than the yes. The thing underneath it. Forty-three years of climbing a hundred and twelve stairs every night in every weather to light a lamp for strangers.

> ***...let me see it once more.***
>
> ***...the light going round. Over Ellie's window. One more time.***

He turned his face up. To whatever he was seeing.

And Yuanshu waited, and the gold fire pulled, and somewhere outside, very far away, a beam swept across a dark sea and over a hospital window where a small girl with an inhaler on a string was pressing her face against the glass.

Walter Ashby closed his eyes.

> ***...yes.***
>
> ***...yes. Stop.***
>
> ***...get me out, son. Please. Like you promised.***

---

"*Now,*" said Yuanshu.

He reached out through the staff and took hold of the old man. Not his hand. Him. The small bright burning shape at the center of the gold room. The way the keeper had reached into the spire at Port Abiri. The way you reach into a burning house for someone you can hear.

And pulled.

And the sphere woke up.

All the way.

The gold room went red. Instantly. The walls stopped glowing and started burning, gray-red, like the furnace of a Hunger. And the pull that had been drawing threads out of Walter Ashby turned, all at once, with its whole hungry weight.

Toward the new thing in the room. The bright thing. The living thing with a staff full of ninety million names.

Toward Yuanshu.

"*Don't let go!*" Jiang Lan's voice, very far away, very sharp. "*Whatever it does! Don't let go of me!*"

He did not let go.

He held the staff with one hand and the old man with the other, in the middle of a fire that had just realized there was something much bigger and brighter in it than an eighty-one-year-old lighthouse keeper.

And the fire pulled.
