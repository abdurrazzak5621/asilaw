## Chapter 252: The Mark

It was Gao Min who saw it, and she saw it because she had spent forty-two years writing marks down.

They were at the long table in the banking hall at three in the afternoon, the day after the Collector told them what the one who picks was. All of them. Kwan with the laptop. Qin Hao with his notebook. Su Mingli with her arms folded. Lingyue on the stairs with the sword. Xiaoyu at the end of the table with Shi Lei, who came every afternoon now with his steel spear and sat without being asked. Old Seven at the bronze door. And Gao Min, in her gray Bureau coat, with the old taped graph from the lid of the box unfolded in front of her, looking at it the way she had looked at it for twenty years.

They had been at it since nine. How do you tell something with nobody in it that the house is empty.

Nobody had an answer.

"*It reads marks,*" said Gao Min.

She did not say it loudly. She said it to the graph, in her flat careful voice, with her finger on the pencil line, the way she said everything that mattered.

"*Say that again,*" said Su Mingli.

"*The knot in the stone,*" said the acting director. "*READ WHAT RIPENS. MARK HOW BRIGHT. SEND THE MARK UP THE THREAD. WHEN THE ORCHARD IS BRIGHT, THE PICKER COMES.*" She looked up. "*It doesn't see the orchard. It's never seen anything but burning. It knows the orchard is bright because the stones send marks up the thread and it reads them.*" She tapped the graph. "*That's what this is. Forty-two years of what it read. Every mark. That's how it knew where to come.*"

The long table was very quiet.

"*It can't be asked,*" said Gao Min. "*There's nobody in it. Fine. But it reads. It's read marks off those threads since before the gardens. It's the one thing it's ever done that isn't picking.*" She took off her glasses. "*You don't need to ask it. You need to send it a mark.*"

---

> **...yes.**
>
> **I did not see that. I have been trying to find a way to ask it. For two days. Since the hall. Every model I have is a model of asking.**
>
> **It does not need to be asked. It needs to be told. In the only language it has.**
>
> **A mark.**

"*Saying what?*" said Qin Hao.

Nobody answered for a moment. Then everybody looked at the end of the table.

Xiaoyu had gone very still.

"*The house is empty,*" she said. "*You can stop.*"

"*And who writes it?*" said Lingyue, from the stairs. Not as a question. She knew. She said it so that it was said.

His sister looked at the marker in her hand.

"*It's true when I write it,*" she said slowly. "*Seed said. When he reads what I write, it's like being told by someone who was there.*" She turned the marker over in her fingers. "*If I write it, and it reads it, it's not just words. It's true. It's... it knows it.*"

> **I do not know if it works on something with nobody in it.**
>
> **I do not know what your Law is. Nobody does. It is not on the tree in the stone. Wen Jia opened what you said, and you said: so that nobody can say afterwards it wasn't there.**
>
> **That is not reading. That is not knowing. That is something else.**
>
> **I have run it. I cannot run it. There is nothing to run it against.**

"*So we try,*" said Xiaoyu.

---

They asked Wen Jia that evening.

Through the staff, at the foot of the platform steps in the Awakening Hall, with the warm stone in front of them and the short line of holders up the aisle who had not wanted to stop.

*Can you send a mark up the thread?*

> ***...I can.***
>
> ***...I haven't. Not once. Not since the night the captain put his hand on me. We said we wouldn't send anything and we haven't.***
>
> ***...but the knot knows how. It's what it was made for. MARK HOW BRIGHT. SEND THE MARK UP. It's like breathing for it. I've been holding my breath for three weeks.***

*One mark. Not a brightness. Something Xiaoyu writes.*

The stone was quiet for a while.

> ***...it'll go up all eleven. The threads join. Whatever goes up one goes up all of them.***
>
> ***...and it'll read it the way it reads everything. Fast. All at once. Like a glance.***
>
> ***...I don't think a glance is enough. It's been reading marks since before the gardens. Millions. Billions. It doesn't stop and look at a mark. It reads it and moves on. That's what it's for.***
>
> ***...if you want it to really see one, it has to be looking. Not glancing. Looking.***
>
> ***...and it's only ever looked at one thing.***

Nobody said it. Everybody knew.

On the platform, under her own letters, Xiaoyu said it anyway.

"*Callum,*" she said. "*It's looking at Callum.*"

---

> **It is holding him up to its face.**
>
> **It stopped walking to look at him. The first thing in all the long time it has ever looked at.**
>
> **If the mark is on him. If he is holding it. When it looks.**

"*You want to write on a man,*" said Su Mingli, flatly. "*A man who's burning. In the hand of the oldest thing in the dark. Forty-eight days away.*"

> **Not on him. Give it to him. To hold.**
>
> **Wen Jia sends it up the thread. It goes past, along all eleven. The one who picks glances at it and moves on.**
>
> **But someone carries it the rest of the way. In their hand. Up the thread, to the lantern, and puts it in his.**
>
> **And he holds it up.**

At the end of the table, Shi Lei made a sound.

"*Told me what I was,*" he said. Everyone looked at him. He went red. "*That's what he said. Callum Ward. You said, when you came back from the thread the first time. He kept saying it. It told me what I was. F. So I told it what it was.*" He held on to his spear. "*He hit it with a hammer because it told him what he was. And now he'd... he'd be the one who tells it.*"

The banking hall was silent.

> **...yes.**
>
> **The one nobody picked, telling the one who picks.**
>
> **I did not see that either. I would like to stop not seeing things.**

---

He went up at midnight and asked Callum.

The short way. Not near. To the edge of the light, where a man was burning in a vast hand in the deep water, held up close to the face of something that had stopped walking to look at him.

*Callum. It's me. It's tomorrow.*

*...yeah.*

He told him. All of it. The mark, the stone, the girl who wrote things that were true. The house, which was empty. The thing holding him, which had been carrying fruit to it since before the gardens, and did not know. That someone would come up the thread and put the mark in his hand. That when it looked at him, he could hold it up. Or not. That if he did, nobody knew what would happen. That afterward, whatever happened, they would cut the hand open and bring him home.

*I'm telling you first,* he said. *You don't have to. You can just come home.*

The fire roared. White and gold and every color.

At the bottom of the well, a long silence.

*...it told me what I was,* said Callum Ward.

*Yes.*

*...seven years. F. It told me. And I went and hit it. Because I wanted to tell it what it was.*

*Yes.*

*...and I couldn't. I just burned. I hit it and it opened me and I just burned.*

The fire roared.

*...so that's the deal. Is it. I get to tell it what it is. Properly. The thing that took me. With something true. Before I come home.*

*If you want.*

*...yeah,* said the voice at the bottom of the well. *...yeah, I want. Tell your sister to write it big. It's got a big face.*

---

They asked the dead on the fourth night after Port Halvard.

Down the net, nine by nine, slow, the way everything went now. Seed wrote it with Xiaoyu, the way Article Eleven said. What it was. Where. Forty-eight days of the deep water, which on the thread would be minutes, and then nine hours there, because Seed had run the body and the holders and said nine hours was what a Lord's body could hold empty with fifteen hundred people anchoring it, and not one minute more. A hand to cut open, in the dark, beside a thing that had seen him bright. A man to bring home. A mark to carry.

*It is not your border,* the question said. *It is not defense. It is a man who asked to come home. You can say no.*

And it came back the way the net answered things now: not a list. A pile of index cards on the long table, and on top of it, the one that had gone round six hundred times.

Kwan read it.

"*The Spear Corps,*" she said. "*Stone No. 7. They said: he was an F. Nobody picked him. We'll go and pick him.*" She turned the card. "*Everybody's added to it. Yes. Yes. Grid Nine says they'd like to see the deep water too. The Watch says it's not the sea but it's close enough.*" She stopped. "*And there's one at the bottom. It came in an hour ago. It's not from the net.*"

"*Where's it from?*"

"*The keeper,*" said Kwan. "*It came down the gold thread. It's not a yes. It's a message. From the cold fire.*"

> **Shiomi rang it to the keeper. The keeper wrote it down. It does not usually write things down. It says it is learning from Xiaoyu.**

Kwan read it.

"*The sergeant says,*" she said. "*The Sixty are at the cold fire. Facing out. The thread to the deep water goes right past them. Right over their heads. The sergeant says he's been watching it for three weeks.*" She swallowed. "*He says: when the boy comes up it, we're coming with him.*"

"*He says: tell the captain that arm had better still have some fingers on it.*"

At the bronze door, Old Seven turned round.

---

Lingyue did not say anything until everyone had gone to bed.

She sat on the stairs with her grandfather's sword across her knees and the pale flame in her open palm, the size of a lamp, and did not look at it, and he sat on the step below her with the staff, and for a long time neither of them said anything at all.

"*I can't come,*" she said at last.

"*No.*"

"*It's a thread. You go up it without your body. I can't do that. I'm not a necromancer. I don't have a thing in my head.*" Her voice was very level. "*I've come with you everywhere. The rift. The north wall. The island. The cut. The pier.*" She looked at the flame. "*Every time something came down out of the sky, I was there. With the sword. Uphill if I had to. I said it on the slope. At the wedding. In front of everyone.*"

"*I know.*"

"*And this time I have to sit in a hall with my hand on your chest and hold your body down while you go and do it without me.*"

He did not say anything.

She closed her hand on the flame, and opened it again. It was still there.

"*Your fire goes,*" he said. "*The pale fire. It's yours and mine, wound together. It goes up through the staff. Every time I cut anything up there, it's your light.*"

"*I know.*"

"*So you're there.*"

"*It's not the same.*"

"*No,*" he said. "*It's not.*"

She sat on the stairs with her hand open and the light in it, and the house was quiet round them.

"*Nine hours,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*Seed said nine hours. Not one minute more.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*If you're not back in nine hours,*" said his wife, "*I'm going to pull. I don't care if you're in the middle of something. I don't care if the house is empty or full or on fire. I'm going to put my hand on your chest and pull, the way I did at the pier, and you're going to come down that thread whether you've finished or not.*" She looked down at him. "*That's what I'll do with your answer. I'm telling you first.*"

"*Yes,*" said Yuanshu. "*Please.*"

She hit him on the arm.

Then she did not take her hand away. She left it there, on his arm, on the stairs, in the light from her other palm, until the lamp over the board went out.

---

At the top of the long white wall, in the space above HUNDRED-ARROW LIN that the stepladder did not quite reach, Xiaoyu had written the Collector's want and the one who picks, and under them, now, on her toes on the top step with Lingyue holding her ankles, she wrote one more line.

Not the mark. The mark she would write tomorrow, on the floor of the Awakening Hall in front of the stone, for Wen Jia to send. This was just so it was written.

*THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU CAN STOP. — TO BE CARRIED BY CALLUM WARD, F, WHO WAS NOT PICKED, AND WHO ASKED TO BE THE ONE WHO TELLS IT.*
