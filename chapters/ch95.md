## Chapter 95: The Dimming

Hush went into the water on the fifth morning.

It did not ask anyone's permission. It simply walked to the edge of the raft on its many thin pale legs, between two soldiers of the Third Battalion, and looked down at the dark-green sea for a long moment, and stepped off.

Old Seven lunged. His three-fingered hand closed on nothing.

> ***...it is all right,*** came the seashell voice, from below. ***...I do not need air. I have not needed air for four billion years.***
>
> ***...I need to be close to them. Light is very loud from far away. Close, it is quieter.***

They watched it sink. A small pale shape, limbs folded, drifting down through the green water, down into the glowing crowd of Lanterns, until it was a speck among the lights, and then nothing.

Old Seven did not move from the edge for the rest of the day.

---

It took the Lanterns a day and a night to decide.

Yuanshu watched it happen from the edge of the raft. He could not have looked away if he had tried.

It began beneath the raft. One Lantern. The oldest one, the one that had glowed every color at once. It turned a color he had not seen before, a pale searching blue, and held it. And then the Lanterns around it turned the same blue. And the ones around them. And the blue spread out across the sea, in a great slow ring, the way the silver had spread, the way the gray-white had spread, Lantern by Lantern, until it reached the horizon.

> **That is not a question.**
>
> **That is the question being passed on.**
>
> **Every one of them is asking every other one. The same thing. "Will you go dark?"**
>
> **A million of them. Asking each other. One at a time.**

And then the answers came back.

Not all at once. In patches. Here a cluster of green. There a long drifting ribbon of green. There a single Lantern, alone, turning green and holding it. The green spreading, slowly, through the blue. Yes. Yes. Together. Yes.

And here and there, scattered through the green, Lanterns that did not change.

That stayed blue. Or gold. Or rose. Whatever color they had been. Not turning. Not answering.

Which was how the Lanterns said no.

---

> **Most of them say yes.**
>
> **Not all.**
>
> **Perhaps one in fifty does not change.**
>
> **I asked Hush why.**

Down in the green water, very faintly, the seashell voice.

> ***...some of them are very old.***
>
> ***...they say they have glowed for a very long time. Longer than anyone. They say they do not know how to be anything else.***
>
> ***...they say if they go dark, they are afraid they will forget how to come back.***
>
> ***...they say they would rather glow.***
>
> ***...even if it sees them.***

Yuanshu knelt at the edge of the raft and looked at the scattered lights that had not changed. Gold and rose and blue, drifting among the green. One in fifty. Thousands of them.

*They'll be seen,* he thought. *If it comes close. They'll be the only lights on the whole world. It'll see them from a long way off.*

> ***...yes. They know.***
>
> ***...they say: that is what they want.***

He closed his eyes.

*Quiet means quiet,* Sister Ning had said. In Article Three of the Charter. In a crater on Concordia. On a pier with ten thousand candles. *Yes means yes. And no means no. Even when no is the wrong answer. Even when no breaks your heart.*

He opened his eyes.

*Then tell them we heard,* he thought, down into the water, to a small pale shape among the lights. *Tell them we'll remember them. Whatever happens.*

> ***...I told them.***
>
> ***...they say: violet.***

The Lanterns that had not changed, every one of them, scattered all across the green sea, turned, slowly, a soft and steady violet.

*Remember.*

---

The dimming began that night.

Hush taught them. From below, in the green water, among the lights. Not in words. In color. In stillness.

It began with deep blue. The color for sleep. The Lanterns nearest Hush went deep blue, and held it. And then deeper. Darker. Not blue anymore. Something like the color of the sea at midnight. And then darker still.

And then, one by one, they went out.

Not dead. Not gone. Still there. Drifting, just under the surface, soft round shapes in the water. But dark. Not glowing at all. Not a flicker. Like lamps with the wicks turned all the way down.

It spread.

From below the raft, outward. A ring of darkness, spreading across the glowing green sea, Lantern by Lantern, the way the light had spread when they first said hello. Slowly. Carefully. Each one going deep blue, then midnight, then dark, then nothing. The whole vast shining ocean dimming, from the center out, like a city turning off its lights one window at a time, late at night, as people go to sleep.

It took eleven hours.

When it was done, the sea was black.

---

Not all of it.

Scattered through the black, like stars in a night sky, the violet ones. Thousands. Small soft lights, drifting, alone in the dark water. Glowing. Remembering.

And beneath the raft, the oldest Lantern. Still glowing every color at once. Holding it. Slow. Steady.

> ***...it is waiting for me,*** said Hush, from the water. ***...it says it will go dark last. After it is sure everyone else is safe.***
>
> ***...it says that is what the oldest one does.***

"*Of course it does,*" said Jiang Lan, very softly, from the staff. "*That's what teachers do. Last one out of the building. Lights off.*"

Yuanshu looked down at the staff in his hands.

At the small white skull with its silver reading glasses. Glowing gray-white. Soft. Like a candle seen through paper.

*Ninety million embers,* the report had said. *Highest value.*

The brightest thing on the raft. A bonfire, Hush had said.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **You know what you are going to have to ask her.**

He knew.

---

"Jiang Lan," he said aloud. Very quietly. "When it comes."

"*I know,*" said the dry voice. "*I've been listening.*" A pause. "*You want me to go dark.*"

"I want to ask you if you'll go dark. There's a difference."

"*I know there's a difference. I'm the one who taught you there's a difference.*" A longer pause. "*The Lanterns are afraid they won't come back. Are you asking me whether I'm afraid of that?*"

"Yes."

The small skull was silent for a long time.

"*I was in the ground for sixty-eight years,*" said Jiang Lan. "*Under a white stone. Quiet. Waiting. Xuanming asleep beside me. I knew how to be dark then.*" Another pause. "*It's been a long time since then. I've gotten used to the light. To talking. To you writing names down wrong.*" Something in the dry voice softened. "*Yes. I'm afraid.*"

"Then you can say no."

"*I know I can say no.*" A short, dry breath, almost a laugh. "*Young man, I made a whole class of children sit perfectly still under a collapsing school for nine minutes while the floor came down, so that they wouldn't panic and run the wrong way. Twenty-three of them. Six years old. Every one of them was terrified. Every one of them did it.*" A pause. "*You don't ask the class to do something you won't do yourself.*"

"Is that a yes?"

"*It's a yes,*" said Jiang Lan. "*Write it down properly. And if I don't come back, you write the ninety million down yourself. Every one. Correctly. No spelling mistakes.*"

"I promise."

"*Hm,*" said the schoolteacher. "*I've seen your handwriting.*"

---

The dead learned too.

Hush taught them. From the water. Old Seven first, because Old Seven was always first. Standing at the edge of the raft with his spear grounded, and his skull bowed toward the black sea, and his gray flame burning steady.

> ***...you glow like the Lanterns,*** said Hush. ***...not as bright. But you glow.***
>
> ***...you have to bank it. Like a coal under ash. Not out. You must not go out. Just... under.***

Old Seven stood very still.

And his gray flame, slowly, sank. Down. Smaller. Smaller. Not out. Down. Into the cage of his ribs, until it was a single tiny dull red point, like the last coal in a fireplace at the end of a long night, covered in ash, barely there at all.

Behind him, one by one, seventy-two soldiers of the Third Battalion did the same. Iron Wall Zhao. Hundred-Arrow Lin, whose iron arrowhead went dark in her chest for the first time since Yuanshu had known her.

And the ring of the dead around the rim of the raft went dim.

---

The living lay down in the center of the raft, close together, the way they had lain on the ice at Grid Nine. Hands touching. Eyes closed.

The Mo twins back to back, elbows touching.

Bai Lu with her brass clock stopped. She had wound it down herself. She said the ticking was too loud.

Lingyue with her sword unlit across her chest and her hand in Yuanshu's.

Above them, the gold thread that ran back across the Chaos Sea to the keeper glowed faintly in the dark.

And then, as they watched, it dimmed too.

Not cut. Not gone. Still there. Just... down. From gold to dull amber to a faint dark line that you could only see if you knew where to look.

> **"The keeper asks me to tell you,"** said the Elder, very softly, from where she knelt at the center of the raft, her own white light turned so low she was almost invisible. **"It has never been quiet before. In six hundred and forty million years. It was always listening. Always reaching."**
>
> **"It says it is learning."**
>
> **"It says it is very bad at it."**

---

Far above the thin pale cloud, in the black, a gray ring hung in the sky.

It was not small anymore.

It filled a quarter of the heavens. Gray. Cracked. Clouded. Turning slowly. And in its center, where the lens of the keeper had been black water, there was a light.

Gray-red. Dull. Hungry.

Like a coal.

Yuanshu lay on his back on the warm pale ground of the raft, with Lingyue's hand in his, and looked up at it through the thin cloud, and held the quiet.

Below the raft, in the black water, a small pale shape and a very old Lantern glowing every color at once looked at each other for a long moment.

Then the old Lantern went deep blue. And then midnight. And then dark.

And the last light on the whole green world, except for a few thousand scattered points of violet, went out.

> **One day.**
>
> **It will pass over us in one day.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **Go quiet now.**

He closed his eyes.

And in his hands, very softly, the small white skull at the top of the staff dimmed. From gray-white to gray. From gray to a faint dull glow. From a faint dull glow to nothing.

"*...see you,*" whispered Jiang Lan, so faintly that it was barely a sound at all. "*...on the other side. Mind your spelling.*"

And then she was dark.
