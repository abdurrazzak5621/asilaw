## Chapter 94: The Lanterns

The world was an ocean, and the ocean was full of light.

They came down toward it on the twelfth day. A small world, smaller than Blue Star, wrapped in thin pale cloud. And under the cloud, from pole to pole, water. No land. No islands. Not a single rock breaking the surface. Just one vast dark-green sea, calm and deep and endless, under a soft gray sky lit by a small cool sun.

And in the sea, everywhere, glowing, the Lanterns.

That was what Bai Lu called them, the first time she saw them, standing at the rim of the raft as the Elder brought it down through the cloud. She said it without thinking, the way you'd name something you'd seen in a dream. And after that, nobody called them anything else.

They were drifting. Just under the surface. Millions of them. Each one perhaps the size of a person, round and soft and translucent, like a jellyfish, like a paper lantern on a summer night. And each one glowing. Not one color. Every color. Green and gold and pale rose and deep sea-blue and a violet so soft it was almost gray. The colors moved inside them. Shifting. Swirling. Flowing from one Lantern to the next where they drifted close, like a conversation passing from mouth to mouth across a crowded room.

The whole sea was lit from beneath. Glowing. Shimmering. Pulsing very slightly, all over, like the surface of a great slow-breathing animal.

It was the most beautiful thing Yuanshu had ever seen.

> **They are talking.**
>
> **The colors. That is what they are.**
>
> **They are talking to each other, all the time, all of them, everywhere, in light.**
>
> **And Yuanshu. They have seen us.**

---

Below the raft, the sea had changed.

The Lanterns nearest them had stopped drifting. They had turned, the way flowers turn. Upward. Toward the pale disc hanging in the gray sky above them, with its ring of the dead at the rim and its small cluster of living at the center and its tall pale shape of white light.

And their colors had changed.

All of them. Hundreds. Then thousands. Every Lantern within sight, going the same color at once. Not green. Not gold. A soft, bright, shimmering silver-white, rippling outward across the sea from beneath the raft in a great slow ring, the way rain spreads across a pond.

> **"They are asking,"** said the Elder. **"What we are."**
>
> **"I think silver is a question."**

"How do I answer?"

> **"I do not know. I have never spoken to them. The keeper never spoke to anyone. It only listened. And then it took."**
>
> **"No one has ever answered them."**

---

He knelt at the edge of the raft.

Inside the ring of the dead, with Old Seven a meter to his left and Hush curled at the old soldier's feet. He looked down at the sea. At the thousands of silver lights turned up toward him, rippling, waiting.

He did not know their language. Seed did not know it. Nobody alive knew it.

So he did what he always did.

He went quiet. All the way down. And he held the white staff out over the edge of the raft, over the water, and asked Jiang Lan, very gently, to glow.

Not bright. Not like a bonfire. Soft. Gray-white. Like a candle seen through paper.

And he held it there.

Just a light. Over the water. Answering their light.

---

The sea went still.

Then, beneath the raft, one Lantern changed color.

Just one. Near the surface. Directly below the staff. Its silver flickered. Faded. And slowly, very slowly, it turned a soft gray-white.

The same as the staff.

Then the one beside it. And the one beside that. Gray-white. Spreading outward. Lantern by Lantern. Copying the color of the candle-through-paper light at the end of the staff, very carefully, the way a child copies a word you have said to it.

Until the whole sea beneath the raft, as far as he could see, was glowing the same soft gray-white as a schoolteacher's bones.

"*Oh,*" said Jiang Lan, very quietly, from the small skull. Her dry voice had gone strange. "*Oh, look at them.*"

> **They are saying it back.**
>
> **Not a word. Not yet. Just... your light. They are holding up your light. To show you they saw it.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I think that is hello.**

---

It took four days to learn enough.

Seed and the Elder worked together, the way they had on the beach at Port Abiri, pulling a language apart one piece at a time. Not words. Colors. Gold was something like *warm,* or *safe,* or *here.* Green was *yes,* or *together.* Rose was a thing Seed could only describe as *the feeling of someone you love being nearby.* Deep blue was *sleep.* Violet was *remember.* And silver, always, everywhere, rippling out across the sea whenever anything new happened, was a question.

They had no word for *no.*

They did not seem to need one. Seed said that when a Lantern did not want something, it simply did not change color. It stayed what it was. And the others understood.

> **They do not argue.**
>
> **They do not shout. They do not have anything to shout with.**
>
> **They drift. They talk in light. They have been doing it for a very long time. Hundreds of millions of years.**
>
> **They have never seen anything like us.**
>
> **They have never seen anything at all, except each other and the sky.**

---

On the fourth day, he told them.

He did not know how to say *Hunger* in light. Neither did Seed. So the Elder did something none of them expected.

She stepped to the very edge of the raft, a tall pale shape of soft white, and she knelt, her long body folding, her silver eyes on the sea. And she let her light change.

She dimmed her white until it was almost gone. And then, very slowly, in the center of her chest, where a heart would be, she let a single small spot of light come.

Gray-red.

Like a coal.

And it grew.

Slowly. Spreading outward from her chest. Through her long pale body. Gray-red, then darker red, then a dull hungry orange, like the eye of the spire in the bay at Port Abiri. Until the whole tall shape of her was glowing like a fire seen through smoke.

The sea beneath the raft went dark.

Every Lantern. All at once. Thousands. Their gray-white went out like candles in a wind, and for one terrible second, the whole sea was black.

Then, slowly, they came back.

Silver.

Everywhere. Rippling. Over and over. Wave after wave of it, spreading out from beneath the raft across the whole dark-green sea, faster and faster, brighter and brighter, until the silver reached the horizon in every direction.

A question.

A million Lanterns, all asking the same thing.

---

The Elder let the red go out of her. She knelt at the edge of the raft, white again, very dim, her long hands pressed together. When she spoke, her glass-note voice was very soft.

> **"They have seen it before."**
>
> **"Not here. Not them. But in the sky. A long time ago. A gray ring, passing far away, very far. Their oldest memories. In violet. They remember."**
>
> **"They thought it had gone."**
>
> **"They are asking if it is coming back."**

Yuanshu looked down at the silver sea.

He did not know how to say *yes* in light. Green was yes. But green was also *together.* He did not want to say *together* about this.

So he held out the staff. And asked Jiang Lan to glow. And she glowed gray-white. And he held it over the water.

And then, very slowly, he lowered it. Down. Until the small skull at its top touched the surface of the sea.

And held it there. Under the water. Glowing.

So they could see it was still there.

---

The sea was silent.

Then, below the raft, one Lantern drifted up. Slowly. Out of the silver crowd. Up toward the surface. Toward the small glowing skull at the end of the staff, held just under the water.

It was larger than the others. Older, Seed thought. Its colors moved more slowly. And it stopped, just beneath the staff, close enough that Yuanshu could have reached down and touched it.

It glowed.

Not silver. Not green. Not gold.

A color Seed had not seen yet. A deep, soft, shimmering color like the inside of a seashell, like oil on water, every color at once, moving slowly.

> **I do not know that one.**
>
> **Elder?**

> **"...I do not know it either."**

At Old Seven's feet, Hush had uncurled. All its limbs. It was standing at the very edge of the raft, swaying, with every one of its dark eyes fixed on the old Lantern beneath the water.

> ***...I know it,*** said Hush, in its seashell voice.
>
> ***...we had it too. On my world. Before.***
>
> ***...it is the color for 'what do you want.'***

---

Yuanshu knelt at the edge of the raft with the staff in the water and the oldest Lantern glowing beneath it in every color at once.

*What do you want?*

He did not know how to answer in light.

So he answered the way he answered everyone.

*I want you to live,* he thought, into the staff, into the water, into the glowing shape beneath it. *I want it to pass you by. I want it to not even know you were here.*

*I don't know how to do that. But I know someone who does.*

He turned his head. And looked at Hush. Standing at the edge of the raft on its many thin legs, swaying, with all its eyes on the sea.

The small pale shape looked back at him.

> ***...oh,*** it said. ***...you want me to teach them the game.***

"Will you?"

Hush looked down at the sea. At a million lights, glowing silver, asking.

> ***...they are made of light.***
>
> ***...to be still, they would have to go dark.***
>
> ***...all of them. All at once. For a long time.***
>
> ***...I do not know if light that goes dark comes back.***

It looked up at him with all its eyes.

> ***...I will ask them,*** said Hush. ***...what they want.***
>
> ***...that is how it works. Isn't it.***
