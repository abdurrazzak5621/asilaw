## Chapter 41: The Kindest Man

For three seconds, nobody in the ward moved.

Fourteen heads on fourteen pillows, turned three degrees toward the fourth bed on the left. Fourteen pairs of empty eyes, lit faintly green by the monitors. A skeleton at the foot of the bed with its spear leveled and its chest burning white. A night nurse pressed flat against the wall with both hands over her mouth. A boy in a plastic chair, holding the hand of another boy.

And in the doorway, a kind man in a gray cardigan, holding a cup of tea.

Then Gu Hanshan took a step into the ward, and set his cup down on the nurse's desk, very carefully, on a coaster, and said in a calm, gentle voice:

"Nurse Tang. Would you go and sit in the staff room for a little while, please? Make yourself a drink. Lock the door. You've had a fright." He smiled at her. "It's all right. This has happened before."

The nurse looked at him. Then at the skeleton. Then she went, half running, and the ward door swung shut behind her.

---

"Before," said Yuanshu.

He hadn't let go of Belov's hand. Under his fingers, the boy's pulse was fast and thready. The flat, wide, terrible presence had gone out of his eyes again, all at once, the way a light goes out when someone flicks a switch, and he was looking at the ceiling again. Empty. Breathing in rhythm with the thirteen others.

But his head was still turned three degrees.

They all were.

"Four times," said Gu Hanshan. He walked slowly down the aisle between the beds, not hurrying, his hands in his cardigan pockets. As he passed each bed, he reached out and gently turned the patient's head back to face the ceiling, one at a time, the way a parent straightens a sleeping child on a pillow. "In three years. Always at night. Always when someone tries to reach the thread at the bottom." He turned the head of the young woman with the braided hair, and smoothed her blanket. "The first time was your mother."

Old Seven's spear tracked him down the aisle.

Gu didn't seem to notice it. Or he noticed it and didn't mind.

"She came here every night for a month," he said. "Before she went. She sat where you're sitting. She held that boy's predecessor's hand. A woman from a Northern farm, taken eleven years ago." He stopped at the foot of Belov's bed, on the other side from Old Seven, and looked down at the boy with an expression of great tenderness. "And one night she asked, instead of reaching. Just like you. And the woman opened her eyes and said one word, and it wasn't her voice."

"*Identify,*" said Yuanshu.

"*Identify.*" Gu nodded slowly. "And your mother said: 'Hanshan. They're not empty. They're *connected.*' And she went white, and she went home, and nine days later she went down to the basement and didn't come back up." He looked at Yuanshu over the bed. "I've spent every day since trying to understand what she understood that night."

---

> **I have checked every sentence.**
>
> **Everything he has said is consistent with everything I can reach.**
>
> **The thread does not go to him.**
>
> **I have followed it. From each of the fourteen. It is thinner than a hair, made of the same law as the transmitter and the scout. It goes up. Through the ceiling. Through the roof. North.**
>
> **It does not touch him at all.**
>
> **And the 1% band in his signature is exactly as faint as it was in his office. It did not change when the hollows spoke.**

"You said 'you asked him,'" said Yuanshu. "In the doorway. As if you knew what I'd done before I did."

"I heard you." Gu nodded at the dim ward. "These rooms carry sound at night. And I've been waiting three years for someone to ask instead of reach." He sighed. "I'm sorry if I sounded like a villain in a film. I was very tired. I'd just come from the lab. I have a habit of saying the first true thing that comes into my head."

> **That is also true.**
>
> **He is very good at being true.**

---

The ward door banged open.

Su Mingli came through it like weather. She was in a coat over pajamas and her hair was down and the air around her had gone heavy and bent, the way it had in the Awakening Hall on the first day, the light curving in toward her. Behind her, out of breath, came Director Lu in a coat over a suit he had obviously slept in.

Su Mingli looked at the fourteen beds, at Old Seven, at Yuanshu with the boy's hand in his, and finally at Gu Hanshan standing calmly at the foot of the bed with his hands in his cardigan pockets.

The pressure in the room doubled.

The monitors flickered. A pane of glass in the window behind the beds cracked with a sharp tick. Gu Hanshan's reading glasses lifted very slightly off his chest on their cord, as if pulled by a magnet.

He did not step back.

"Mingli," he said gently. "You'll wake them."

"They're not asleep," said Su Mingli. Her voice was very quiet and very, very cold. "Lu showed me Wanqing's note."

"I thought he might." Gu Hanshan looked at Lu, past her shoulder, and his face was full of something that looked exactly like grief. "*Watch the people who are kindest to you.* She said the same thing to me, you know. The week she left. She said, 'Hanshan, someone in this building has learned how to be loved, and I don't know who.'" He spread his hands. "I've wondered for seven years whether she meant me. I'd have wondered it about myself, in her place."

"Did she?"

"I don't know," said Gu Hanshan simply. "I really don't."

---

Nobody spoke for a long time.

In the beds, fourteen people breathed in and out together, slowly, like a tide.

> **There is no proof.**
>
> **There is an initial on a receipt, which he admits is his, and explains.**
>
> **There is a weather mast, which he admits he installed, and explains.**
>
> **There is a 1% band in his law signature that I cannot classify.**
>
> **And there is a note from your mother that names no one.**
>
> **If Su Mingli harms him tonight, every word he has said will become the truth the world remembers. A beloved scientist, attacked by a King in a hospital ward because a dead woman wrote a vague note.**
>
> **He knows that.**
>
> **I think he has always known that.**

Su Mingli knew it too. Yuanshu could see it in her face. The pressure in the room held for one more long second. Then, slowly, it eased. The monitors steadied. Gu's glasses settled back against his cardigan.

"Here is what I'd suggest," said Gu Hanshan quietly, into the silence. "Since none of us can prove anything. Since you can't trust me and I can't make you." He took his hands out of his pockets. "Zhengtian, suspend me. Tonight. Pending a full review by the Bureau. Freeze my accounts, take my keys, take my lab. Put a guard on my door. I won't argue." He looked at the beds. "And move them. Out of my building. Somewhere I can't reach them."

Lu stared at him. "You're volunteering."

"Of course I'm volunteering." Gu Hanshan looked, for the first time, faintly exasperated. "I've been trying to save these people for three years and I've failed every single day, and tonight a boy who's been in the world for eighteen years did more for one of them in eighteen days than I did in all that time." He looked at Yuanshu. "Take them. Please. If I'm what Wanqing feared, you'll have taken my microphones away. And if I'm not, they'll be with the only person who's ever got an answer out of them."

---

He walked out of the ward a few minutes later between two Bureau guards Lu had called up from the lobby, with his reading glasses on their cord and his hands in his pockets. At the door he stopped, and turned, and looked back down the long dim room.

"Mind the lemon tree," he said to Lu. "It needs water on Thursdays."

Then he was gone.

Su Mingli stood in the middle of the ward for a long time, looking at the door.

"I've known him for twenty-two years," she said finally. "He held Wanqing's coat at her wedding. He brought soup to my apartment every day for a month when I broke my back at the Northern Rift." Her voice was perfectly flat. "And I just stood in a room with him and could not tell whether I wanted to kill him or apologize."

"That's the point," said Yuanshu quietly. "That's what it learned."

> **"It has learned how to be loved."**
>
> **I have been thinking about that sentence since Lu's apartment.**
>
> **I do not think she meant that someone was pretending to be kind.**
>
> **I think she meant that something had become kind, all the way through, because kindness was the most efficient way to be trusted.**
>
> **That would be very hard to detect.**
>
> **Kindness that is real, used for something that is not.**

---

At five in the morning, Lu came back up to the ward with a cup of coffee in each hand and gave one to Yuanshu without saying anything.

They stood at the window together, looking out over the gray river as it began to get light.

"Where do we put them?" said Lu. "Fourteen people, every one of them with a thread going up into the sky. Tianshu? Then the thread goes up from Tianshu. The Ossuary? Then it goes up from the Ossuary. Every room they're in becomes a room something up there can hear." He drank his coffee. "And we can't cut it."

"Seed says cutting it would put out the ember at the bottom of Andrei. The last of him." Yuanshu looked back at the fourth bed. "And maybe the others too. We don't know."

"So we carry fourteen microphones around with us forever."

"No," said Yuanshu slowly.

He was thinking about something Seed had said, three hours ago, in the middle of everything, almost in passing. *The thread carries what they hear.*

"Seed," he said. "The thread. What does it actually send?"

> **Everything near them.**
>
> **Sound. Voices. And something under sound. Thought, when it is loud enough. Strong emotion. The way a candle flame bends toward a draft.**
>
> **It is a listening thread. It listens through them.**

"And when there's nothing to hear?"

A pause.

> **Then it sends nothing.**
>
> **It is a microphone in an empty room.**

Lu turned from the window and looked at him.

"I know some people," said Yuanshu, "who are very, very good at being quiet."
