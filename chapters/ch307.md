## Chapter 307: Four

The bell came at four in the morning on the twenty-seventh day, and Xiaoyu read it with her eyes still shut.

Lingyue was on the platform. She had been on the platform since midnight, because Bai Lu had told her at eleven to go home and sleep and she had gone home and lain on the bed in the back room of the Ossuary for forty minutes looking at the ceiling, and then got up and come back. Bai Lu had seen her come in and said nothing, which was worse than saying something.

Xiaodeng was asleep in the sling. Her husband was asleep on the mattress under the blue jacket. He weighed fifty-four kilos. He had weighed sixty-five on the morning he lay down.

"*It's Ichiro,*" said Xiaoyu, from the bottom tier. She sat up with her hand flat on the floor and her face doing the thing it did when a mark came in, as if she were listening to someone talking in the next room. "*Through Halvarde. Through the root.*"

She opened her eyes. "*They've decided,*" she said.

---

*FOUR HOUSES HAVE FOLDED. TWENTY SHIPS. THEY LEFT THE TWELVE AT THE EDGE.*

*THEY ARE BURNING THE OLD WAY. HARDER THAN THE OLD WAY. THE FOG-RINGER SAYS THE WALLS ARE SCREAMING. HE CAN HEAR THEM THROUGH THE ROOT. HE SAYS HE IS SORRY HE CAN HEAR THEM AND NOT REACH THEM. THEY HAVE CUT THEIR CELLS OFF FROM THE ROOT. HE CANNOT ASK THEM FROM ANTHOS. ONLY FROM CLOSE.*

*THEY ARE COMING TO THE SHALLOWS.*

*THEY ARE COMING FOR THE SCRIBE.*

*THEY WANT HER TO WRITE: THIS IS YOUR FACE.*

Xiaoyu stopped.

Her voice had not shaken. Lingyue had been listening for it, and it had not. Her sister-in-law sat on the bottom tier of the Awakening Hall at four in the morning in a school jacket she was too old for, with a marker in her pocket and her hand flat on the cold floor, and read out loud the words of four Great Houses of the Lamplit Worlds who were burning a million people in the walls of twenty ships to come and make her write a lie.

"*There's a last bit,*" said Xiaoyu. "*From Ichiro. Not Halvarde.*"

*TELL THE GIRL WITH THE SWORD: SIX DAYS. MAYBE FIVE. HE'S SORRY. HE'S STILL WATCHING. HE ISN'T GOING ANYWHERE.*

*TELL THE SCRIBE: DON'T.*

---

The long table in the banking hall was full by seven.

Lingyue had not called anyone. Xiaoyu had written it on the white plaster round the corner of the vault door at a quarter past four, *FOUR HOUSES. TWENTY SHIPS. SIX DAYS. FOR ME.*, and gone back to the hall, and by seven the people who read that wall every morning had read it, and come.

Su Mingli. Qin Hao, with his notebook. Gao Min, with the rota, both rotas, the holding and the second one she had started drawing nine days ago and not finished. Dabao, filling the end of the bench. Fat Tan with a thermos. Bai Lu. Tam and Seri, barefoot, side by side, holding hands under the table. Kwan with his laptop. Sister Ning, who had come down from the Listening Pavilion on the first bus. And on the open channel, through the stone, faint: Orlov, from Novaya Zarya. Roman Sterling, from the north pier of Port Halvard. Ren, from the shrine.

And at the head of the table, in her wheelchair, with the book of names closed on her knee for the first time in three years, Zhou Wanqing.

Nobody had asked Lingyue to sit at the other end. She sat there anyway, with Xiaodeng awake in her lap and her sword against the table, because her husband was not there, and somebody had to.

"*Tell me what they can do,*" she said. "*All of it. Then tell me what we can.*"

---

Su Mingli told her. Flat, short, in the voice she gave orders in, because that was the only voice she had for anything that mattered.

"*Precedence is broken,*" said the Master of the Tianshu Institute. "*That's the good news. The Lens is whole and the fog-ringer's in it and it asks. The Ladder's not in the first stone any more. They can't freeze us. Not the way Severin did.*" She did not unfold her arms. "*That's all the good news.*"

"*And the rest?*"

"*They're Paragons. Four of them, and their Houses behind them. Severin was above a King. Seed said so. I stood in front of him and couldn't lift a finger.*" Her mouth went thin. "*That was Precedence. Without it I'd be able to lift a finger. I'd lose anyway. Slower.*" She looked down the table. "*I'm one of the strongest things in Huaxia. Shen Jinghong is older and better and he'd lose too. Sterling would lose. Orlov would lose.*"

On the open channel, faint, Orlov said, in his formal old-fashioned Huaxian: "*She is correct. I would lose with great dignity.*"

"*And the ships,*" said Su Mingli. "*Twenty. Each one bigger than the Candle by ten times. Burning a million people in the walls. We have nothing that flies like that. Lords go up. They don't go along.*"

Lingyue sat with her daughter in her lap.

"*So we can't fight them,*" she said.

"*Not in the sky,*" said Su Mingli. "*Not on the ground either, if they come down. Not and win.*"

---

"*They don't want to fight,*" said Qin Hao.

Everyone looked at him. He was not looking at anyone. He had his notebook open and was writing in it in his small square hand, and he did not stop.

"*If they wanted to fight, they'd have brought eighty ships. They brought twenty. The other twelve Houses said no.*" He turned a page. "*They want one thing. They want her to write four words. They think if she writes it, it'll be true, because everything she writes is true. And then they'll have their faces back. And the Ladder. And Precedence.*" He looked up, at last, down the table, at the girl at Lingyue's left hand. "*They need her alive. And willing. Because a Scribe writes what she writes. You can't make her.*"

"*Can't you?*" said Lingyue. There was a pause.

"*You can make anyone write anything,*" said Zhou Wanqing, from the head of the table. Very level. "*If you hurt enough people in front of them.*"

Nobody said anything to that.

"*That's what the walls are for,*" said Tam.

He said it very small. He was twelve in the body still, and thirty-something inside, and he had spent most of the last year with his ear against walls, listening to the ones inside them. He was holding Seri's hand under the table so hard that his knuckles had gone white.

"*They're screaming,*" said Tam. "*I can hear them. Not well. It's very far. But it's twenty ships and they're burning them harder than the old way, harder than anyone ever burned anyone, to get here fast.*" He swallowed. "*When they get here, they'll hold one up. A wall. In front of her. And they'll say: write it, or we burn this one out. And then the next one. And the next.*"

He looked at Xiaoyu. "*That's what I'd do,*" said Tam. "*If I was them. That's what they did to my mother. They didn't hurt her. They showed her me.*"

---

Xiaoyu had not said anything since four in the morning.

She said something now. "*I'll go,*" said Zhou Xiaoyu.

Lingyue's hand closed on the edge of the table.

"*Not to write it,*" said Xiaoyu. "*I'm not going to write it. I'm never going to write it.*" She did not look at her mother. She looked at Lingyue. "*But they're coming here for me. Here. To Lingzhou. To the hall where he's lying on a mattress with a line in his arm and six thousand people holding him.*" Her voice did not shake. It had not shaken all morning. "*If I'm in the hall, they come to the hall. If I'm somewhere else, they come somewhere else.*"

"*No,*" said Lingyue.

"*You haven't heard it yet.*"

"*I don't need to.*"

"*Yes you do.*" Xiaoyu looked at her across the table, eighteen, Scribe, not marked, with her mother's voice and her brother's eyes. "*You need to ask me. That's the rule. That's his rule. You don't get to decide for me because I'm his sister and you're frightened.*"

Lingyue went very still.

She thought of her husband on a mattress under a blue jacket, asleep, very thin, a very long way away. She thought of every time she had wanted to decide for him. On the north wall. At the pier. On the platform, with her hand on his chest, nine hours, *I don't care if the house is empty or full or on fire*. She had decided for him that once, and told him first. Only once.

She thought of a dead nun on a shore for twelve hundred years, who had said: *I am not going to sit on the shore while he goes out again.*

"*All right,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*I'm asking.*"

Her sister-in-law waited.

"*Where do you want to be,*" said Lingyue, "*when they come?*"

---

Xiaoyu told her. She had thought about it since four in the morning. She had thought about it, Lingyue understood, since the night on Anthos when a mark she had written went out through a fog-ringer in a lens to every Paragon in the Lamplit Worlds at once and made their faces go dark. She had known they would come. She had written *THAT'S NOT A QUESTION* on a wall because she had known.

"*Not in the hall,*" said Xiaoyu. "*Not in the city. Not anywhere they'd have to come down to.*"

She looked up. Through the ceiling of the banking hall, through the bank, through the sky over Lingzhou. North.

"*The keeper,*" she said. "*I want to meet them at the keeper. In its field. Where the cousins are. Two hundred and six million of them, in the walls, who've been asked every morning for two years, who row.*" She put her hand flat on the table, the way she put it on the floor of the hall. "*They'll be screaming in their walls when they get there. Tam says so. And the keeper's field is full of people who used to scream in walls, and stopped, because someone asked them what they wanted.*"

"*They're cut off,*" said Tam. "*The cells. From the root. Ichiro can't reach them.*"

"*Not from Anthos,*" said Xiaoyu. "*From close. Marks pass deafness. You told me, on the night we found Seri. The cell's stone. A mark goes through stone.*" She looked at him. "*I write the marks. All of them. True ones. And you tell me who to write them to.*"

Tam stared at her.

"*Two hundred and six million people,*" said Xiaoyu, "*who know what it's like. And twenty ships of people who don't yet. And me in the middle, with a marker.*" She looked back at Lingyue. "*That's where I want to be. Away from him. Where they have to come to me in the place that's full of everyone they ever burned.*"

The long table in the banking hall was silent.

At the head of it, Zhou Wanqing opened the book of names on her knee, and found her place, and closed it again on her finger.

"*She's right,*" said his mother. "*God help me. She's right.*"

---

Lingyue sat with her daughter in her lap and did not speak.

She thought about the keeper's field. She had been in it once, on an island, in the dark, for eleven nights. She thought about twenty ships the size of cities burning people in their walls harder than anyone had ever burned anyone, coming out of a fold at the edge of the Shallows in five days, or six, and finding, instead of a small blue world with a hall in it and a man on a mattress, a girl in a school jacket standing in a ring of light the size of a continent, with a marker in her hand, writing.

She thought about what she would do if she were them. She would hold up a wall. She would burn one out in front of the girl. She would do it to win.

She thought about her husband, who would have known what to say. Who always asked.

"*I'm coming with you,*" she said.

"*You can't. You're the one who pulls.*" Xiaoyu did not look away. "*If the holding drops, someone has to be on the platform with a hand on his chest. That's you. You said so. On the north wall. To the sky.*"

Lingyue opened her mouth.

"*I'll go,*" said Su Mingli, from the side of the table. Arms folded. "*I'd lose. Slower. That's something.*"

"*And I,*" said the faint voice of Roman Sterling, on the open channel, from a pier on the other side of the world. "*I'm very good at deciding for things very quickly. I'd like, once, to stand somewhere and not.*"

"*And the night watch,*" said Dabao, from the end of the bench. Enormous. Not crying, for once. "*I asked them. On the way here. They said: obviously.*"

Lingyue looked down the long table. At the faces. At her sister-in-law. At Tam and Seri, holding hands so hard under the table that both their arms were shaking. At Wanqing, at the head of it, with her finger in the book.

"*Gao Min,*" she said.

"*I'm drawing it,*" said Gao Min, without looking up. "*Second rota. The keeper. Five days.*" Her pencil did not stop. "*I'll need to know who's holding and who's going. You can't do both. Nobody can do both.*"

"*I know,*" said Shen Lingyue.

She looked down at her daughter, awake in her lap, looking up at her with dark eyes that were not cloudy any more. Her father's.

"*I'm holding,*" she said. "*I'm the lamp.*"

She hated it. She said it anyway.

---

At noon, on the white plaster round the corner of the vault door, under *FOUR HOUSES. TWENTY SHIPS. SIX DAYS. FOR ME.*, Zhou Xiaoyu climbed the stepladder and wrote, in square black capitals:

*GOING TO THE KEEPER. TO MEET THEM. TO ASK.*

*ZHOU XIAOYU (SCRIBE). TAM. SERI. SU MINGLI. THE NIGHT WATCH. R. STERLING (NOT DECIDING).*

*SHEN LINGYUE: ON THE PLATFORM. WITH HIM. WITH THE LAMP.*

And under that, smaller, because she could not help it, and because it was true:

*I'M NOT WRITING IT.*

*THAT'S NOT A QUESTION EITHER.*
