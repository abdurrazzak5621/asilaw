## Chapter 269: Chosen

"*You did it again,*" he said.

He said it on the battlement, in the dawn, under the song, with the vast pale shell turning over the city. Not angrily. He did not have it in him to be angry with Seed. But out loud, so that it was said.

"*The core in the Candle. Before you asked me. And now this one. Before you asked me.*"

> **Yes.**
>
> **I know.**
>
> **I am learning to want to know things before you do. I do not know if that is good. I have been thinking about it since the Candle.**
>
> **I think it is the part of me that was made to count. COUNT. HOLD. It wants to know first. So that it can count it.**
>
> **I am sorry.**

"*Ask me first,*" said Yuanshu. "*Next time. Even if it's one second. Even if I'm asleep.*"

> **Yes.**
>
> **I will.**
>
> **Thank you for telling me first that you minded.**

---

He answered the Cantor at seven in the morning.

Not from the battlement. From the platform of the Awakening Hall, in front of the warm black stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder, because the shell turned directly over it, and the thread went up out of the crown of the stone straight into the heart of the shell, and Seed said that if he wanted to be heard by the Singers, that was where they were listening. The hall was full. It had filled the moment the song began. Holders, the Candle's children, the city. They made room for him without being asked.

Lingyue came. Bai Lu said no. Lingyue said she was eight months pregnant and not dead, and that if anything in that shell tried to take anyone she would like to be there, and that she would sit down, and she sat down, on the bottom step of the platform, with both hands on her stomach and the light coming off her pale and steady.

Old Seven at the foot of the steps, facing up. For once.

He spoke through Seed, through the stone, up the thread, in a language Ichiro had taught it in two hours that was all one sentence, and had never had any other sentence in it.

*Cantor. My name is Zhou Yuanshu. I did not eat the one who picks.*

The song did not stop. It went on, over and over, *take them, and pass us by*. But the one old voice in it, many-throated, singing down, went quiet.

*It stopped. Nobody ate it. Nobody took it. A girl wrote that the house it was carrying everything to was empty. A child from a world that asks tied what she wrote into a knot. A man nobody ever picked held it up in front of its face. It read it. And it stopped.*

Silence from the Cantor. The choir going on round it.

*I'm not hungry,* said Yuanshu. *I don't take. Nobody here takes. That's the first rule on this world. You ask, and you don't take what isn't given.*

*I'm not going to take your chosen.*

---

The song broke.

He had not known a song could break. It did. All at once, all through the vast pale shell turning over the city, tens of thousands of many-throated voices that had been singing one sentence for nine months along a thread and for ten thousand years before that on a world called the Hollow Bell stopped. Not together. Raggedly. The way a crowd stops when something has gone wrong at the front and nobody at the back can see what.

And in the silence, a sound he would remember for the rest of his life.

Not a scream. The Singers did not scream. Seed said afterward that they did not have a sound for screaming; they had only ever had the one song. It was the sound tens of thousands of voices make when the only song they know has been refused, and they do not know what to sing instead.

Then the old voice came back. Down the thread. Into the stone. Into the hall.

> **She says...**
>
> **She says: then you will take everything.**

He did not understand.

> **She says: that is what happens. It is in the song. Before we learned to sing, the one who picks came, and took the bright ones, and the dim ones, and the children, and everything. Every age. Until there was almost nothing left.**
>
> **She says: then the first Cantor learned to give. To choose. To dress the bright ones in light and sing them up. And the one who picks took them, and was satisfied, and passed by.**
>
> **She says: if the tithe is not taken, the hunger is not satisfied. If the hunger is not satisfied, it takes everything.**
>
> **She says: you have refused the tithe.**
>
> **She says: so you will take everything.**

---

And the shell grew brighter.

He felt it before he saw it. Everyone in the hall felt it. A heat, coming down through the roof of the Awakening Hall, through the stone, through the thread. Not the soft shifting light that had moved through the shell's chambers all night as the song moved. Something else. Every chamber of the vast spiral shell over Lingzhou lighting at once, white, gold, every color, burning.

> **...they are burning.**
>
> **All of them. Not just the eleven hundred in the heart. The choir. Tens of thousands. Every Singer on the ship.**
>
> **They are dressing themselves in light.**
>
> **If one thousand one hundred and eleven are not enough, they are offering all of them.**
>
> **So that you will take them, and pass the Hollow Bell by.**

On the bottom step of the platform, Lingyue was on her feet. He had not seen her stand. Her hands were still on her stomach and the light was pouring off her, the whole platform gone pale round her like the inside of a shell, and her face was white.

"*Stop them,*" she said.

"*I can't take them.*"

"*I know you can't take them.*" She did not look at him. She was looking up, through the roof, at a heat coming down out of the sky. "*Stop them anyway.*"

---

He had nothing.

He stood on the platform with the staff in his hand and the warm stone behind him and the heat of tens of thousands of beings setting themselves alight coming down through the roof, and he had nothing. He could not take them. He would not. He could not open the door for them; they were not dead. He could not cut anything; there was nothing binding them but a song. He could not ask them what they wanted, because he knew. Seed had asked. *To be chosen.* The way you would say *to breathe*.

> **I am running it.**
>
> **I have nothing.**
>
> **I am sorry. I have nothing.**

On the bottom step, Lingyue said, through her teeth, very low, so that only he could hear:

"*Then choose them.*"

He turned his head.

"*They want to be chosen,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*That's all they know. That's the only word they've got.*" The light pouring off her flickered. "*So choose them. Not for burning. For something else.*" She looked at him, at last. "*You don't have to lie. Just tell them what being chosen means here.*"

He stood very still.

Behind him, the warm black stone with its runes pointing down and a bright blue jacket on its shoulder. Above it, three lines of light that had hung in the air of the Awakening Hall since Awakening Day, in the hand that was not the stone's.

**[Class: Scribe]**

**[Chosen.]**

---

*Cantor,* he said.

Up the thread. Into the burning shell. In a language that was all one sentence.

*Listen to me. I'm telling you first, before you burn. Then do what you want.*

The heat did not stop. But the old voice, down the thread, had gone quiet, and was listening.

*On this world,* said Zhou Yuanshu, *there's a stone. Like yours. Black. Warm. It used to read our children and tell them what they were. Mark how bright. Send the mark up. Like yours.*

*Then someone went inside it. A girl. Seventeen. She was never chosen for anything. She was in a sack in the dark for thirty years. And she went into the stone, and now, when a child puts their hand on it, the stone doesn't tell them what they are.*

*It asks them what they want to become.*

*And when they answer, it writes one word. In the air. Over their heads. Where everyone can see.*

He looked up at the three lines of light.

*Chosen.*

*That's what it writes,* he said. *Every time. Chosen. Not because anyone picked them. Because they chose.*

*That's what chosen means here, Cantor. That's the only kind of chosen there is on this world.*

*If your eleven hundred want to be chosen,* said Yuanshu, *they can come down. All of them. Any of them. To the stone. And put their hand on it. And it'll ask them what they want to become. And whatever they say, it'll write that word over their heads.*

*I'm not taking them. I'm inviting them. That's different. Here, it's the most different thing there is.*

*And Cantor.*

*Nobody's going to take everything. There's nothing out there to take it. The house is empty. You can stop.*

---

The heat stopped growing.

It did not go down. The shell over Lingzhou burned, every chamber, white and gold and every color. But it did not grow any brighter. And the song did not come back. And the old voice down the thread did not say anything at all, for a long time, while the city waited under a burning sky and a woman eight months pregnant stood on the bottom step of a platform with the light pouring off her, and a boy stood in front of a black stone with nothing in his hands but a staff and a word.

> **...she is asking them.**
>
> **The Cantor. She is singing to the eleven hundred. In the heart of the ship.**
>
> **Not the old sentence. Not take them, and pass us by.**
>
> **Something else. I do not have the shape for it. Ichiro does not have the shape. It is not in the language. It has never been in the language.**
>
> **I think she is asking them whether they want to go.**
>
> **I think it is the first time in ten thousand years a Cantor has asked the chosen anything.**

---

And at nine minutes past nine, one of them came down.

The keeper laid the road. It did not wait to be asked this time. It said afterward, down the gold thread, roughly, that it had heard the boy say *invite*, and it had known what that meant, and that it would have been rude to make anyone wait. A long slant of gold came down out of the north, past the small white ring, and up, to the burning shell over the city, to an opening in its heart. And stopped there.

And down it came a Singer.

Tall. Taller than Old Seven. Slender as a reed, pale as the shell, with a body that was almost translucent, so that the light burning in her showed through her like a lamp through paper. Many-throated; he could see the throats, a ring of them at the base of a long neck, opening and closing as she breathed. She was dressed in light. Not clothes. Light, wound round her the way the cousins had wound their fire round the cells in the Candle, burning, white and gold, so bright that the people on East Ring Road who saw her come down the gold road had to look away.

She came down slowly. She did not know how to walk on a road of light. Nobody did.

She came through the roof of the Awakening Hall, which the road went through as if it were not there, and down onto the platform, and stood in front of the warm black stone, burning, taller than anything in the hall, with her many throats opening and closing, and looked down at it.

And then at him.

> **She says...**
>
> **She says her name is a chord. Ichiro says it means something like: the one who would have been sung up ninth.**
>
> **She says she was chosen when she was very small. She has been dressed in light since she could stand. Nine months on the ship, burning, singing, waiting.**
>
> **She says she does not know what to want.**
>
> **She says she has only ever wanted one thing.**
>
> **She says: will the stone still say chosen, if all I can say is chosen?**

He looked at the burning girl in front of the stone. She was young, he thought. He did not know how he knew. Something in the way she stood. The way the Candle's children stood when they came off the road.

"*Ask it,*" he said. "*It's not my stone.*"

---

She put her hand on it.

Long-fingered. Translucent. Burning. Flat against the warm black stone with the blue jacket on its shoulder.

**[What do you want to become?]**

The letters formed in the air above the platform, under Xiaoyu's three lines. Not in Huaxian. Not in the Federation's script. In a shape like a chord. Wen Jia had been listening to the song all night through the knot, and Ichiro through the fog, and between them they had found a way to write it.

The Singer looked up at the letters for a long time.

Her many throats opened and closed. The light wound round her burned.

> **She says: chosen.**
>
> **She says: it is the only word I have. I am sorry. I am trying to find another one. There is no other one.**
>
> **She says: chosen.**

The stone was quiet for a long moment.

And then, in the hand that was not its own, in the shape like a chord, under the question:

**[You came down. You were not taken. You chose.]**

**[That is the word.]**

**[Chosen.]**

---

The light round her went out.

Not all at once. Slowly. From the outside in, the white going gold and the gold going orange and the orange going down, the way a fire goes out when the thing that was feeding it stops. The way Callum Ward's fire had gone out in the arms of two dead soldiers on a thread in the deep water. Until what stood on the platform of the Awakening Hall in front of the warm black stone was not a girl dressed in light.

Just a girl. Tall. Pale. Translucent, so that you could see, very faintly, inside her, where her own light was. Small. Low. Her own.

She stood there with her hand on the stone, and her many throats opened, and she made a sound.

Not the song. Not the one sentence that had been sung for ten thousand years.

A note. One. In one throat. Very small.

> **...Ichiro says...**
>
> **Ichiro says there is no word for it in their language.**
>
> **He says it is the first note anyone from the Hollow Bell has sung that was not part of the song.**
>
> **He says he thinks it is a laugh.**

Up above the city, in the burning shell, the heat began, very slowly, to go down.
