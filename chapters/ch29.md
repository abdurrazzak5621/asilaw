## Chapter 29: The Ossuary

The building had been a bank once.

It stood on the corner of East Market Road and Tannery Lane, three stories of gray stone with tall arched windows and a heavy bronze door, built a hundred years ago when banks wanted to look like temples. For the last ten years it had been empty. Pigeons in the upper windows. A faded *FOR LEASE* sign. Next door, on the corner, Dabao's mother parked her red dumpling cart every morning in its shadow.

Tianshu bought it on a Tuesday.

On Friday, a new sign went up over the bronze door. Plain gray letters on black stone, no decoration, except a small four-pointed star in the corner.

*THE OSSUARY*

*Lingzhou Registry of the Dead's Consent*

Underneath it, smaller:

*Yes. Ask my family. Only to speak. Quiet.*

*Your choice. Your name. Nobody else's.*

---

On Saturday at six in the morning, when Yuanshu came down East Market Road, the queue already went around the block.

It went down East Market Road past the noodle shop, round the corner into Tannery Lane, past the closed shoe factory, round again, and back up the far side, and its tail was out of sight. People in work clothes. People in pajamas with coats over them. Old women on folding stools, the way the Quiet sat. Young men holding coffee. A mother with a baby asleep in a sling. A whole school class in uniform, with a teacher, who were too young to sign anything and had come to watch.

The noise of it was extraordinary. Not shouting. Talking. Thousands of people talking to the strangers next to them, the way people talk in a queue when they have all come for the same strange reason.

*My grandfather was on the Eastern Line.*

*My husband says I'm mad. I said, it's my body, I'll decide.*

*Do you know which one you'll pick? I keep changing my mind.*

Dabao's mother had opened her cart an hour early. Steam was pouring off it. She was selling dumplings to the queue as fast as she could fill the paper boats, and shouting at Dabao to bring more flour, and Dabao, enormous in a black T-shirt that said *OSSUARY STAFF* across the chest, was running between the cart and the bronze door carrying sacks on both shoulders.

At the door itself, on folding stools on either side of it, sat two people.

On the left: Sister Ning, with her candle and her notebook.

On the right: Iron Wall Zhao, in the place of a doorman. Seated, his empty black shield leaning against his knee, his skull turning slowly to look at each person who came up the steps. Children in the queue kept trying to touch the shield. He let them.

---

Inside, the old banking hall had been cleared down to its marble floor.

Twelve plain wooden desks stood in two rows under the high arched ceiling where the tellers' windows used to be. At each desk, a volunteer: Tianshu staff, Defense Corps soldiers in their off-duty clothes, a few of the Quiet. At each desk, a small black screen with the four choices on it in plain gray letters, and a place for a thumb.

And at the back of the hall, where the bank vault had been, its round steel door rolled open, there was a single quiet room with a chair and a lamp and a box of tissues.

That was for people who wanted to sit down after.

Xiaoyu had designed that room. Nobody had asked her to. On the first morning, a man came out of it after forty minutes with red eyes and found her and shook her hand without saying anything, and she went into the stairwell and cried for a while and then came back out and kept working.

> **Two hundred and eleven names in the first hour.**
>
> **YES: 41%. ASK MY FAMILY: 23%. ONLY TO SPEAK: 19%. QUIET: 17%.**
>
> **I want you to know something. Every time someone chooses Quiet, I feel it.**
>
> **It is not sadness. It is closer to respect.**

---

By Wednesday there were twenty thousand names.

By the next Saturday, a hundred thousand.

On the long marble wall of the old banking hall, Xiaoyu put up a single plain board, the kind schools use for exam results, and every evening she wrote the totals on it by hand in marker. People came in off East Market Road just to look at it. Not their own names. Just the numbers. Four columns, rising.

And on the pavement across the road, the other crowd began to gather.

---

They came on the second Sunday.

A few hundred at first, then more. They stood across East Market Road from the bronze door holding printed signs, all the same font, all the same size, as if they'd come out of the same machine.

*LET THEM LIE.*

*THE DEAD ARE NOT YOURS.*

*ASI = ABOMINATION.*

Some of them were angry, ordinary people. Some of them were sincerely, deeply frightened, and Yuanshu understood that, and didn't blame them. Some of them had lost people in the Black Tide and could not bear the idea of those people being anything but gone.

But some of them had matching jackets. And a sound truck. And a man with a megaphone who spoke Huaxian with a faint accent nobody could place, and who had very good teeth.

> **The signs were printed at a commercial shop in the port district four days ago.**
>
> **The order was paid for by a charity registered in the Atlantic Federation. "The Eternal Rest Foundation." It was founded eleven days ago.**
>
> **Eleven days ago is the day after the Ossuary was announced.**

"Of course it was," said Yuanshu.

He did not cross the road. He did not use the megaphone Xiaoyu kept offering him. He sent a tray of dumplings over with Dabao, and a thermos of tea, and a note in Xiaoyu's handwriting that said *If you're cold, the vault has chairs.*

About forty of the protestors came over that evening and sat in the vault. Six of them signed the Ledger. Three of them chose Quiet. One of them chose Yes.

The man with the very good teeth did not come over.

---

On the eleventh night, at 2:20 in the morning, somebody threw a bottle of petrol at the bronze door.

The street was empty. The queue had gone home. The Quiet's candles along the curb had burned down to wax. A gray van with no plates came down East Market Road slow, its lights off, and stopped across from the Ossuary, and the side door slid open, and a man in a balaclava stood up in it with a bottle in his hand and a rag burning in the neck of the bottle.

He threw.

It turned over and over in the air, end over end, trailing fire, a bright orange spark arcing across the dark street toward the bronze door.

The bronze door was not unguarded.

Iron Wall Zhao rose off his folding stool.

He did not hurry. He stood up, and took one step, and lifted his empty black shield, and the bottle hit the iron face of it dead center and broke.

Fire splashed across the shield in a bright sheet. It ran down the dented iron, dripping, and puddled at the skeleton's feet. It lit him up from below, every bone, every scratch on the old shield, the gray flame in his chest, so that for a moment he stood there on the steps of the Ossuary in the dark like a figure in a painting of the end of the world.

He did not step back.

Behind him, the bronze door did not even get warm.

In the van, the man in the balaclava stared. Then he screamed something at the driver and the van lurched and took off down East Market Road with its door still open and its tires shrieking.

It got forty meters.

Then a single gray arrow came out of the dark from the roof of the old shoe factory across Tannery Lane, and went through its front left tire, and the van spun twice on the wet street and came to rest against the red dumpling cart with a crunch.

On the roof, a figure made of ash and smoke lowered her bow.

---

Dabao arrived ninety seconds later in pajamas and one shoe, with a fire extinguisher.

He found Iron Wall Zhao standing on the steps of the Ossuary with burning petrol dripping off his shield, perfectly calm, and two men in balaclavas sitting on the wet curb with their hands on their heads, with Old Seven standing over them, spear grounded, looking down.

Dabao put out the shield. Then he went and looked at his mother's dumpling cart, which had a van-shaped dent in its side.

He came back up the steps very slowly.

"Captain Zhao," he said. "That's my mom's cart."

The skeleton looked at the cart. Then at Dabao.

> **Captain Zhao's intent: "The cart is a cart. The door is the door."**
>
> **"You will learn the difference."**

Dabao looked at the bronze door, untouched, with the thousands of names behind it.

Then he looked at the cart.

"Yeah," he said finally. "Yeah. Okay." He sat down on the steps next to the dead captain. "She's going to kill them, though."

> **He says: "Good."**

---

In the morning, Gao Yinping came to the Ossuary.

She came alone, in her pale gray suit, at seven, and stood in the queue like everyone else, for two hours, and did not let anyone recognize her, though everyone did. When she reached the front, she did not sit at a desk. She asked for Yuanshu.

He came down the marble stairs and met her in the middle of the banking hall, under the board with the four columns of numbers on it.

"You said you'd help me keep men out of the deep face," she said, without preamble. "Machines. Rescue teams. Better roof supports." She held out a folder. "I'd like to take you up on it. Heishui Number Three reopens next month without a core. I'd rather it didn't start killing people again."

He took the folder.

Then she turned, and walked to the nearest desk, and sat down in front of the small black screen, and put her thumb on it.

He didn't look at which choice she made. Nobody did. That was the point.

But on her way out, at the bronze door, she stopped by Sister Ning's stool, and bent down, and said something very quietly into the old woman's ear.

Sister Ning looked up at her for a long moment. Then she opened her notebook and wrote down a name.

> **Two hundred and ninety-one thousand names.**
>
> **And one van, impounded.**
>
> **And a foundation in the Atlantic Federation that is going to be very unhappy.**
