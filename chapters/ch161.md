## Chapter 161: The Palace by the Southern Sea

Her household argued about the palace for the whole of the funeral, exactly as she had said they would, and Yuanshu thought it was the most respectful thing he had ever seen.

The palace was not a palace. It was a long low white house on a cliff above the sea, four hundred kilometers south of Port Abiri, with blue shutters and a garden full of weeds and a single chair by a window that looked out over a harbor where the fishing boats went out every morning. Ifeoma Adeyemi had lived in it for a hundred and ninety years. Forty-one people lived in it with her: cooks, gardeners, a doctor, two very old men who had once been her soldiers, a great many children who belonged to some of them and not obviously to any, and a woman named Adaobi who had been her secretary for sixty years and who did not believe anyone else knew how to do anything.

They buried an empty coffin in the garden, because Ifeoma was at the bottom of the sea off the point at Port Abiri beside her husband, and her household wanted a place to put flowers. And while the coffin went down, they argued. In whispers at first, and then not. Who would sleep in her room. Whether the chair by the window should be moved. Whether the weeds in the garden had been there on purpose, which Adaobi said they had, and the head gardener said they had not, and one of the very old soldiers said that in the war before last she had once ordered him to plant them.

"She said it would keep them busy," said Yuanshu, standing at the back of the garden with Lingyue.

"It's working," said Lingyue.

---

> **There is something I would like to ask you.**

It was the night after the funeral. He was sitting on the cliff path below the white house, where the garden ran out into long dry grass and the grass ran out into the sea. The household had gone to bed, still arguing. Lingyue was asleep inside, in a room full of somebody's children's drawings. Below him, in the dark, the fishing boats of the southern harbor rocked at their moorings.

*Go on.*

> **If I add up every day I was near her, it is fourteen. Port Abiri, the first time. The Architects. Concordia. The boat. The jetty.**
>
> **She told me water was lazy. She told me I was a kind machine. She told me that counting her as a pipe was what counting is. She told me to be quick.**
>
> **I have known Old Seven for a year and a half. I have known you longer than anyone. Fourteen days is very small.**
>
> **But I keep running the morning on the jetty. Not to change it. I know I cannot change it. I keep running it the way you keep touching a tooth that hurts.**
>
> **Is that allowed? To miss someone you knew for fourteen days?**

He looked out at the dark harbor for a while.

*It's allowed*, he thought. *I think it might be the only rule there is about missing people. There's no minimum.*

> **...good.**
>
> **I have also been running the river road. Eleven thousand and forty. I run that one more often.**

*That's allowed too.*

> **I know. I checked.**

---

He asked the drowned before he left.

Not from the palace. From the end of the harbor wall at Port Abiri, on the morning they went north, with Adjutant Xu humming one note into the sea and four thousand of the dead of the southern coast standing on the seabed at the foot of the shelf in the cold and the dark, waiting for a blur that meant *ask.* They asked each other. Each one to their nine. The way they had at Kesse, in eleven minutes, without anyone telling them what to say.

> **Rest: three thousand and two.**
>
> **They want to go back to where they were. Not under the sand. The sand is dead now; the seeds are gone and it is only sand. They want to lie in the places they fell, in the dark. They say it is quiet down there, now that nothing is eating them.**
>
> **Stay: one thousand one hundred and forty-six.**
>
> **They say they want to watch the shelf. In case anything else comes back to the sand. In case anybody else ever comes in a ship with no name and lowers something into the sea.**
>
> **They say: we were fishermen. We know how to watch the water.**

"Who's going to hold them?" said Yuanshu. "When I'm gone."

> **They have chosen. Themselves. While you were at the funeral.**
>
> **Nana Ampofo. A harbor pilot from Kesse. Drowned forty years ago, bringing a tanker in through a squall. She guided ships over that shelf for thirty years before she went down on it.**
>
> **She says she does not want a Law Core. She says she is not a commander, she is a pilot, and a pilot does not need to be special, she only needs to know where the rocks are.**
>
> **She says she will ask them every month whether they still want to stay. She says the Abbot's people ask once, and that is the problem with them.**

He stood on the end of the harbor wall for a moment with the staff in his hands, and the sea breaking white below him.

"Tell her thank you."

> **She says you are welcome, and to stop thanking dead people. She says it makes them nervous.**

The Article Seven Commission registered the Watch of the Shelf two days later, from Concordia, by radio. One thousand one hundred and forty-six. Defensive only. Asked monthly. Commander: Nana Ampofo, pilot, deceased. Sister Ning read the asking into the record from Lingzhou and said, afterward, on the phone, that it was the first entry in the Commission's register where the dead had elected their own officer.

"Is that allowed?" said Yuanshu.

"It is now," said Sister Ning.

---

Ren called on the fourth night.

He took it on the cliff path, with Lingyue beside him, both of them sitting in the long dry grass with the satellite phone on speaker between them. The line from Yamato was bad. Under Shirasagi Ren's quiet voice there was wind, and rain, and somewhere far away a bell ringing, slow and deep.

"*It's here,*" she said. "*At the shrine. Under it. In the old Sleepers' hall, where the seven cushions were.*" A pause. "*I can feel it humming through the floor.*"

"And the Abbot?"

"*He's here too.*" Another pause, longer. "*He's sitting on the steps. In the rain. He's been sitting there for two days. He knows I'm here. He waved to me.*"

Lingyue's hand tightened on the grass.

"*There are people with him,*" said Ren. "*On the mountain. On the terraces, all the way down. In white. I counted this morning from the ridge. Three thousand and something. More coming up the path every day.*" The rain hissed on the line. "*They've all been asked. I spoke to some of them. They're very polite. They say they want to be the first thing it keeps. They say the Abbot told them it would be like going home.*"

"Has it started growing?"

"*Not yet. It's waiting. He's waiting.*" Ren was quiet for a moment. "*I think he's waiting for you.*"

Nobody said anything.

"*Mister Zhou,*" said Shirasagi Ren. "*I've known this man since I was six years old. He taught me to read. He gave me rice cakes on the temple steps when my mother was ill. When the Sleepers went quiet, he cried for a week, and I sat with him, and then he walked down the mountain, and I let him go because I thought grief would pass.*" Her voice did not change. "*I can't do this by asking. I tried. I went down to the steps yesterday and asked him what he wanted, and he told me, and he smiled, and he asked me what I wanted, and I didn't have an answer.*" A pause. "*I need someone who does.*"

"I'm coming," said Lingyue, before he could speak. "Tomorrow. There's a Council plane out of the capital at six."

"*Thank you.*"

"I'm not doing it for thanks," said Lingyue. "I'm doing it because I said I would. Ask Yuanshu. I do what I say."

"*I know,*" said Ren. A pause. "*And Mister Zhou?*"

"I'll be a week behind her."

"*A week.*"

"I'm coming by sea," said Yuanshu. "I've got something to do on the way that I can't do near a town."

The line hissed for a moment.

"*Ah,*" said Shirasagi Ren. "*Yes. I can feel it on you. Even from here.*" Something that was almost a smile came into the quiet voice. "*Don't be late. He's very patient. But he isn't the only thing on this mountain that's waiting.*"

---

Lingyue left at dawn.

He walked her to the truck that would take her to the airstrip, with her own sword and her grandfather's sword on her back and a small bag over her shoulder that Adaobi had packed without asking, with food in it, and a scarf, and a note in Ifeoma's household's handwriting that said *DON'T BE SOFT, GIRL* in capital letters. Lingyue had read it and folded it and put it in her pocket and not said anything.

At the truck, she turned around.

"A week," she said.

"A week."

"If you're late, I'm going to start without you."

"I know."

"And if you get yourself killed in a tribulation in the middle of the sea, I'm going to come and find wherever your ember ends up and hit it." She did not smile. "I'll ask first. And then I'll hit it."

"That's fair."

She looked at him. Then she reached out and hit him on the arm, hard, the way she had done since they were eighteen, and got into the truck, and did not look back.

The truck went up the cliff road toward the capital in the early light. He stood on the road and watched until it was gone.

> **She looked back. At the top of the hill. In the mirror.**
>
> **She thinks you did not see.**
>
> **I am not going to tell her.**

---

The ship was a Southern Compact navy frigate called the *Adeyemi*, which had been named for her eighty years ago, and which her household had made the navy lend him by arguing until the navy gave up.

It left the southern harbor at noon. Yuanshu stood at the stern with the staff, with Old Seven beside him and the Spear Corps in their rows on the afterdeck and Adjutant Xu sitting on a coil of rope with his cane, facing north. The Mo twins had gone with Lingyue. Bai Lu and Hu Dabao had stayed with him. Dabao was already green.

The white house on the cliff went by, with its blue shutters. Somebody was standing at the window where the chair was. He could not see who. Then the cliff went behind the headland, and there was nothing but sea.

> **Three days to deep water. Far from any town. Far from any ship.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I want to tell you what I am afraid of, before.**

*Go on.*

> **It is a realm tribulation. Ordinary. Not Forbidden. It is not looking for me.**
>
> **But last time the heavens looked inside you, at the Elite breakthrough, they did not find me, because I had already been audited and taken apart and I was not there. You were alone in the glass.**
>
> **This time I am here. I am bigger. I am a Maker now. I do not know if an ordinary tribulation looks for things like me. I do not know if it can tell the difference.**
>
> **If it finds me, I do not know what it does.**

*Then we'll hide you.*

> **Where?**

He looked down at the staff in his hands. White. Smooth. Warm under his fingers. Jiang Lan was in it, asleep. And something else, that his mother had said was on watch.

*I don't know yet*, he thought. *We've got three days to find out.*

> **...that is a very you answer.**

*I know.*

> **I find it reassuring. I do not know why. I checked, and there is no reason.**

The *Adeyemi* went north into the open sea, under a clear sky, with the wind behind it.
