## Chapter 181: The Few Who Stayed

Afterward, in the field, with the silk of two dead walkers lying across the pale ground in long glittering drifts, Major Zhuo Wenli opened his eyes.

Yuanshu was standing in front of his column when he did it. He had walked the fifty meters from the place where the first walker had come apart, slowly, with Bai Lu holding his elbow because his legs were not entirely his yet, and stopped in front of a gold column on the near edge of the cluster, beside three empty rings in the pale ground. Inside it, a thin tired man in the winter whites of the Ninth Mountain Battalion stood with his hands at his sides.

His eyes opened.

He looked at Yuanshu through the gold for a long moment. Then down. At the three empty rings beside his column, where three other columns had stood once, a woman and two girls, before a boy asked them what they wanted and they said quiet.

> ***...Mister Zhou.***

The voice was very faint. Not the keeper's great capitals. A man's voice, through the gold, the way voices came from the columns when they chose to speak.

> ***...it was holding me. For a day and a half. I could feel it. Like a hand around the back of my neck, lifting. Very slowly.***
>
> ***...it didn't ask.***

"No."

> ***...the keeper asks me every morning. If I still want to be kept. I've said yes every morning since the mountain.***
>
> ***...that thing didn't ask. It just started lifting.***

He did not say anything for a while. Behind him, across the pale ground, the dead of the Spear Corps were going through the silk of the first walker, carefully, row by row, looking for the twenty-three who had been unwoven, so that Old Seven could say their names over the place where they had stood.

> ***...I thought being kept was being safe. That's what the Abbot said. That's what I wanted. For Lili.***
>
> ***...it isn't, is it. It's just being somewhere something can reach you.***

---

"I'm going to ask you," said Yuanshu. "You don't have to answer."

> ***...I know how you ask.***

"What do you want?"

Major Zhuo Wenli stood in his gold column beside three empty rings in an empty field above the sky, and looked at them for a long time.

> ***...when I said yes on the mountain, I wanted to be kept because I wanted someone to keep that Lili said it. Quiet. Tell Papa I'm not tired any more. I wanted it kept forever. Not in your Ledger. Where you could let it go.***
>
> ***...but you've got it in your Ledger. Your sister wrote it on a wall. I heard the keeper say. Under my name.***
>
> ***...it's kept. It's on a wall in Lingzhou. People read it.***
>
> ***...I don't need to stand here to keep it.***

He was quiet.

> ***...I'd like to go quiet now. Please. Where they went.***
>
> ***...I'm very tired. I wasn't, on the mountain. I am now.***

"All right," said Yuanshu.

> ***...will you do it? Not the keeper. You. The keeper's kind. But you're the one who asked them.***

---

He did it the way he had done it for the four thousand on the Western Pass, and the fifty-three of the Third Battalion in the valley a long time ago, and for the people in WARDEN's core: through the staff, gently, opening the Death Law under the column like a cool dark door. Not *Return*. Only rest.

The gold column dimmed. From gold to pale gold to the soft color of the light in the field. And then it was not there, and there was only a faint ring on the pale ground beside three other faint rings, the way a glass leaves a mark on a table.

Four rings. Side by side.

> ***...THANK YOU FOR ASKING HIM.***

The keeper's voice. Slow. Enormous. Very tired.

> ***...HE SAID YES TO ME EVERY MORNING. I DID NOT KNOW HOW TO TELL HIM HE COULD STOP.***
>
> ***...YOU KNEW.***

---

They asked the rest before they went down.

All three hundred and eighty-one. One at a time, the way he always did, through the staff and the Lattice and the keeper, with Seed counting. He told them what had happened. What the walkers were. That one had walked away into the gold haze at the edge of the field to tell someone what it had seen. That the keeper could not promise that nothing would reach them here again.

> **Quiet: two hundred and six. They saw the major's column rise. They do not want to be somewhere something can reach them.**
>
> **Stay: one hundred and seventy-five.**
>
> **They say: the keeper asks us every morning. It came between us and the walkers when it could not fight them. It sent for help. It asked for help. They say a thing that does that is worth staying with.**
>
> **They say: if the gardener comes, we would like to be here, so the keeper is not alone.**

The keeper did not say anything for a long time after that.

> ***...I DID NOT KNOW THEY WOULD SAY THAT.***
>
> ***...NOBODY HAS EVER STAYED FOR ME BEFORE.***

---

> **[Level 62 → 65.]**
>
> **Two walkers. Killed under your command, by your living and your dead, in a place nobody from Blue Star has ever fought.**
>
> **Death Law: 36.4% → 37.6%. Not from killing. From the Lattice running in a field above the sky, with the dead banked and the living in a pocket and a note going down from a dead signaler that no living ear could hear. You learned that the Field works anywhere the dead can stand.**
>
> **Lingyue: Level 61 → 63. She did a thing with light that nobody has ever done.**
>
> **Qin Hao: Level 64 → 66. He is going to tell everyone.**

"And the cost," said Yuanshu.

> **Twenty-three of the Spear Corps. Unwoven. Gone. Not quiet. Not resting. Gone. Like the eleven in row eighty. There is nothing left to ask.**
>
> **They were the first three hundred. From the eastern field. The ones who came out of the ground for you on the first night. Before the Ledger. Before Rule One had a number.**
>
> **Old Seven knew all of their names. He is saying them now.**
>
> **And Mo Xing.**

He looked across the pale ground.

Mo Xing was sitting on the edge of the gold road where it came down into the field, with his head in his hands and his brother sitting beside him with an arm around his shoulders. Bai Lu was kneeling in front of him with both hands on his face. The front of his shirt was dark with blood. It had stopped. But his hands, when he took them away from his face, were shaking.

> **Two hundred and twelve folds. In fifty minutes.**
>
> **He has burned something. Bai Lu says it is not permanent. She says she thinks it is not permanent. She says she has never seen this, and she is guessing.**
>
> **He cannot fold. He tried, just now, a pebble, from one hand to the other. It did not move.**
>
> **She says: a month. Perhaps two. Perhaps more. She does not know.**

---

He went and sat down on the gold road beside the twins.

Mo Xing did not look at him. He was looking at his own hands, turning them over, the way his brother turned over a cup when he was thinking.

"I couldn't stop it," he said. "The one that walked away. I tried. I couldn't."

"Nobody could."

"I was the only one who could have." Mo Xing's voice was very flat. "If I could have folded one more time. Put Qin Hao on its back. He'd have had one more go at the knot." He closed his hands. "One more fold."

"You did two hundred and twelve."

"I know how many I did," said Mo Xing. "Shi counted."

His brother, beside him, did not say anything. He just tightened his arm around his shoulders, and leaned his head against his brother's head, the way they had sat on the floor of the banking hall a hundred times, elbows touching.

"It's going to tell someone," said Mo Xing. "Isn't it."

> **Yes.**

"Who?"

---

They asked the keeper that, on the road going down. Seed asked, the way it had learned: telling the keeper first what it would do with the answer. *We will write it down. We will tell the Council. We will get ready, as much as anyone can get ready. That is all.*

The keeper was quiet for most of the road. The gold went down under their feet through the dark, out of the ring at the end of the world, across the sky over the Wastes, toward a long gray wall with eighteen thousand banked gray points along it and a King of Gravity standing at the top of the stairs.

> ***...THE WALKERS DO NOT DECIDE.***
>
> ***...THEY WALK. THEY LOOK. THEY TAKE THE OLDEST, THE BRIGHTEST. THEY TAKE THEM BACK.***
>
> ***...TO WHAT SENT THEM.***
>
> ***...IT WILL TELL WHAT SENT IT THAT MY FIELD IS EMPTY. THAT I TURNED. THAT THERE ARE DEAD WHO STAND UP IN A SLOPE, AND A GIRL WHO THROWS LIGHT UPHILL, AND A BOY WITH A WHITE STAFF, AND A THING MADE OF THE OLD STUFF THAT STANDS IN THE OPEN AND SAYS: LOOK AT ME.***
>
> ***...AND THEN WHAT SENT IT WILL DECIDE.***

"*The gardener,*" said Yuanshu.

> ***...YES.***

"*How long?*"

> ***...I DO NOT KNOW. WALKERS ARE SLOW. THE GARDENER IS SLOWER. IT HAS NEVER HAD TO BE FAST. NOTHING HAS EVER MADE IT HURRY.***
>
> ***...PERHAPS A LONG TIME.***
>
> ***...PERHAPS IT WILL HURRY NOW.***

At the bottom of the road, Su Mingli was standing at the top of the stairs at Sector Nine with her arms folded, exactly where they had left her, as if she had not moved all night. She probably had not.

She looked at them coming down the gold road out of the dark. At the twenty-three gaps in the rows of the Spear Corps. At Mo Xing, walking with his brother's arm around him. At Lingyue, with her grandfather's sword on her back and the borrowed Academy sword broken in half and stuck through her belt, because she would not leave it in the field.

"Well?" she said.

"Two dead," said Yuanshu. "One got away."

Su Mingli was silent for a moment.

"Then something knows," she said.

"Yes."

"Good," said the King of Gravity, flatly, and turned and walked down the stairs into the city. Over her shoulder: "I'm tired of being the only one who's frightened."
