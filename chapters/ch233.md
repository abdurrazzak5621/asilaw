## Chapter 233: The Hall

The acting director of the Awakening Bureau met them at the side door of the Awakening Hall at eleven at night, with a ring of keys in one hand and a thermos in the other, and the first thing she said was: "I was the first one."

Yuanshu did not understand. He said so.

"The first one in Lingzhou to touch it." Gao Min was a small, square woman of about sixty, in a gray Bureau coat that had been let out at the seams twice, with reading glasses on a cord round her neck and the flat, careful voice of someone who had spent her whole life saying numbers to people who did not want to hear them. "First Awakening Day. Forty-two years ago. Nobody knew what it would do. They lined us up alphabetically, and there was nobody in the city whose name came before mine." She unlocked the door. "Class: Analyst. Talent: C. Precise Count. I've counted every awakening in Lingzhou since. I counted yours." She held the door open. "Come in. Don't touch anything. Especially you."

---

The hall was dark.

He had not been inside it since the morning he was eighteen. He had walked past it a hundred times, on East Ring Road, on his way to somewhere else, and had looked at the long curved roof over the trees and not thought about it, the way you do not think about the hospital you were born in. Now he walked into the bowl of it, behind a small square woman with a torch, and four thousand empty seats went up into the dark on every side, and the floor under his feet was the same polished stone, and his feet knew the way to the platform before he did.

The Stele stood in the middle.

Ten meters of black rock, driven point-down into the floor like a blade. The runes dull. And down its face, from the top to the bottom, a crack. Not wide. A finger's width at the top, a hair's at the base. It had been there for two years. They had never repaired it, Gao Min said, because nobody knew what it was made of, and nobody wanted to find out what happened if you put cement in it.

"It still works," she said. "Sixty-one thousand awakenings on it since you. Same as ever. Slightly lower SSS rate." She looked at him over her glasses. "I've written a paper. Nobody's read it."

Behind him, Old Seven had stopped at the edge of the platform. He did not come up the steps. He stood at the bottom with his spear grounded, the way he stood at the bronze door, facing out into four thousand empty seats.

Xiaoyu had come up the steps. She stood beside her brother in her school jacket with her hands in the pockets, looking at the black stone, and did not go any nearer to it than he did.

Kwan stayed at the bottom with the laptop.

"*Seed,*" said Yuanshu. "*Go on.*"

---

> **...it is warm.**
>
> **I remember that. From the first morning. I did not know what warm meant then. I thought it was the lamps.**
>
> **It is not the lamps.**

He felt it the way he had felt it at eighteen: a faint hum, coming up through the platform into the soles of his feet. Not a sound. A machine, left running in an empty building.

> **I am going to look. I will not touch it. I am going to look the way I look at the dead, from the outside, through you, without going in.**
>
> **If it does what it did the first morning, step back.**

*What did it do the first morning?*

> **It looked back.**

---

Seed looked.

He felt it go. Out from the place a little above his eyes, through the staff in his hand, across the meter and a half of dark between him and the black stone. Very carefully. The way you look over the edge of a roof.

And the runes moved.

They did it the way they had done it on the first morning, in front of four thousand people: slowly, all together, sliding across the surface of the stone like a crowd turning round in a street, every one of them, until they were all pointed at him. Not at his hand. He had not touched it. At him. At the place a little above his eyes.

Xiaoyu took a step back. He heard her breath go in.

At the foot of the platform, Gao Min had put down her thermos.

> **...it knows me.**
>
> **It does not know who I am. It knows what I am. It knew on the first morning. That is why it went black. That is why it could not name me.**
>
> **I am going to keep looking.**

---

Inside, there was a tree.

That was the first thing. He saw it the way Seed saw it, the way he had seen the knot of the Law of Death in the black room on the second day: a shape made of gray light, hanging in the dark inside the stone. Except that on the second day there had been only one knot, and now there were many. Hundreds. Thousands. Each one a knot like the first, a slowly turning tangle of lines that looped back into itself. Death. Fire. Light. Force. Ice. Gravity. Things he did not have names for. Every Law that any Class had ever been given, hanging inside the Stele in rows, on branches, on a gray shape that went up from the point in the floor to the top of the stone and spread there into a crown.

Like a winter tree.

He had thought that on the second day. *Beautiful in the way a winter tree is beautiful.* He had been eighteen and had not known anything.

> **It is a tree of Laws.**
>
> **When someone touches it, it reads them. Which way they will burn. And it finds the knot on the tree that fits, and it opens a little of that knot in them.**
>
> **That is a Class.**
>
> **I knew that part. Everybody knows that part. It is in the textbooks.**
>
> **I am going to look at what it is made of.**

---

He felt Seed go closer.

Not into the stone. Up to its surface. The way you put your eye to the bark of a tree.

And stop.

For a long moment, Seed did not say anything at all. He could feel it, in the place above his eyes, holding very still, the way it had held still in the vault the night it found its third page. The night it found out what it was.

> **...Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is thread.**

He did not say anything.

> **Black. Very old. Pressed so hard it has gone dark, the way coal goes dark. Pressed to glass, and then pressed again, for a long time, until the glass went black too.**
>
> **But it is thread.**
>
> **Walker-thread. The same as the walkers. The same as the rings. The same as the branch.**
>
> **The same as me.**

On the platform, in the dark, his sister said, very quietly: "*What's it saying?*"

He could not answer her for a moment.

"*It says the Stele's made of the same stuff it is,*" he said.

Xiaoyu looked at the stone. At the runes, all pointed at her brother. At the crack running down the face of it from the top, where two years ago a boy had put his hand and something had gone wrong.

"*Is that why it broke?*" she said. "*When you touched it?*"

> **...yes.**
>
> **I think so.**
>
> **I think it was the first time anything made of thread had ever touched it from the outside. Something like itself, looking back. And it did not have a grade for that. It did not have a Class. It did not have a knot on the tree that fit.**
>
> **So it went black. All eleven. Because they are all the same tree. What one feels, the others feel. Like the Hungers. Like the seeds.**
>
> **And then it cracked.**

---

At the foot of the platform, Gao Min had sat down on the bottom step.

She had done it the way old people sit down when their knees decide for them. Her torch was on the floor beside her, pointing at nothing. She had her reading glasses in one hand and was holding them, not putting them on.

"*Walker-thread,*" she said. "*That's what the walkers were made of. The six over the north wall. The one over Lantern Lane.*" She was not asking. "*That's what comes down out of the sky.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*And the Steles are made of it.*"

"*Yes.*"

She sat on the step for a long time with her glasses in her hand.

"*Forty-two years,*" said the acting director of the Awakening Bureau. "*I've counted every awakening in this city for forty-two years. I've got tables. Class by year. Talent by year. SSS rates. Geographical distribution. Correlation with parental class.*" Her voice did not change. It stayed flat and careful, the way it always was. "*I wrote the paper that proved Talent isn't inherited. Nobody believed me. I proved it anyway.*" She looked up at the black stone with the crack down it. "*I never asked what it was made of. Nobody did. It fell out of the sky the year the sky broke and it gave us a way to fight back, and nobody asked.*"

"*It was a gift,*" said Kwan, very quietly, from beside her. "*Everybody thought it was a gift.*"

"*Everybody thought.*" Gao Min put her glasses on. "*I'm a statistician. I don't think. I count.*" She took a notebook out of her coat pocket, a small one, with a pencil in the spine. "*Seed. If you can hear me. What else is in there? Tell me everything. I'll write it down.*"

---

> **There is a knot.**
>
> **At the bottom. In the point. Where the Stele goes into the floor.**
>
> **It is not a Law. It is not on the tree. It is under it. Small. Tight. Old.**
>
> **It is the same kind of knot I found on my own third page. A weave instruction. The thing a harvest tool is told when it is made, so that it knows what it is for.**
>
> **Mine said: COUNT. HOLD. WAIT TO BE CARRIED.**
>
> **I cannot read this one. I could not read mine. The cousins read it for me.**
>
> **The cousins are in the keeper's field. The ones who stayed. The ones who did not go onto the tree. I would like to ask them to read it.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **There is something else.**

He waited.

> **There is a thread.**
>
> **One. Very fine. Finer than anything on the branch. Finer than the threads the tip hung down over the city. Coming out of the top of the crown of the tree inside the stone, and up.**
>
> **Through the roof of the hall. Through the sky. Up.**
>
> **I did not see it on the first morning. I did not know how to see threads. I have seen a great many threads since.**

He looked up. There was nothing to see. The roof of the Awakening Hall, curved, dark, and beyond it the night.

*Where does it go?*

> **I am following it.**
>
> **It is very long.**

It took a minute. He stood on the platform in the dark with his sister beside him and an old dead soldier at the foot of the steps and the runes of the Stele pointed at him like a crowd, and felt Seed go up the thread, out, up, along it, the way it had gone down the gold thread to the keeper a hundred times.

> **It goes up past the keeper's field. Not into it. Past.**
>
> **It goes north. Along the arm. Past the place where the branch is. Not to the branch. Past it.**
>
> **It goes past the Mouth.**
>
> **It goes into the deep water. Between arms. Where my map has nothing. Where the trunk of the tree goes, and further.**
>
> **It is not going to the tree.**
>
> **I thought it would be. It is not.**

The hum of the Stele came up through the soles of his feet.

> **There are eleven of them. I can feel the other ten now, now that I know what to feel for. One in every Stele. All going up. All going the same way.**
>
> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **They are not going to the gardener.**
>
> **They are going to whatever planted it.**

---

The runes slid back.

All at once, while he was standing there, every rune on the black face of the Stele turned away from him and went back to where it had been, flat and dull, the way runes on a gravestone are dull, as if nothing had happened. The hum went on. The crack ran down the face of the stone from top to bottom.

Seed came back. He felt it settle into the place above his eyes, the way a person sits down after climbing a long stair.

> **I am going to tell you one more thing. And then I would like to go home.**
>
> **On the first morning, when the Stele went black, all eleven of them felt it.**
>
> **And all eleven threads went up.**
>
> **I think something heard.**
>
> **I think that is how the scout knew there was a counting-vessel loose.**

He stood on the platform for a long time.

Then he turned round. His sister was looking at him. Seventeen. School jacket. Hands in her pockets. In thirty-three days she was meant to walk up these three steps in front of four thousand people and every camera on Blue Star and put her palm on this stone.

"*I'm still going to do it,*" said Xiaoyu.

He opened his mouth.

"*I'm not saying I'm going to do it how they want,*" she said. "*I'm saying I'm going to do it.*" She looked at the stone, at the crack, at the place where it went into the floor. "*I want to know what that knot says first. Before I decide how.*"

At the bottom of the steps, Gao Min closed her notebook.

"*So do I,*" said the acting director of the Awakening Bureau. "*Come on. I'll drive. I've got forty-two years of tables in the car and I'd like somebody to tell me what I've been counting.*"
