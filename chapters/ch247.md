## Chapter 247: Bright

In the morning, on the ferry landing at Shuanghe, Liang Fu asked about the queen.

He asked it the way he asked everything now, Seed said: all at once, as if a door had opened in a house where he had lived alone for thirty years and every room was full of things he had forgotten he owned, and he did not know which to pick up first. He had been talking since dawn. To Zeng Yan, who had not slept and sat on the top step of the landing in a dry coat with her hands round a cup of tea. To the river. To Old Seven, who stood at the edge of the stones facing out and let him.

He came up the steps with his weight off his right leg and stopped in front of Yuanshu, and looked up at him, and asked.

> **He says: the little one. In the fire. The one with all the legs.**
>
> **He says: I taught it cards. A game with no queens, so it couldn't cheat. It cheated anyway. It kept one back. The queen of hearts. It thought I didn't see.**
>
> **He says: does it still have it?**

"*It gave it to my sister,*" said Yuanshu. "*It's on the wall. On a pin. At the end of the INSIDE column. Under your name.*"

The short dead man in the rags of a Third Battalion uniform stood on the landing for a moment, with the two rivers going by behind him.

> **He says: good.**
>
> **He says: it was never mine anyway. I took all the queens out of the deck so it couldn't lose, and it took one back so it could. That's how you know someone's getting better. They want to be able to lose.**

He turned round, and went back down the steps to the ferry, where a man from the village was waiting at the post with his hat in his hands, and untied the rope.

> **He says: there's a sad one. Excuse me.**

---

They drove back north at nine. Kwan, the clutch, both hands. Xiaoyu asleep against the window. Lingyue in the back with her hand open on her knee and the pale flame in it, the size of a lamp, not looking at it.

He did not sleep. He sat in the front with the staff and the radio, and listened to Sterling.

"*Holders: fourteen thousand,*" said the King of Force from Port Halvard, at ten. "*The stone's stopped. Second day. Net zero.*" A pause. "*Seed's told you what the Collector said.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*Then you know what I'm going to ask.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*Do we tell them?*" said Roman Sterling. "*Fourteen thousand people holding a stone down so the thing can't pull it up. And the thing's coming down the thread for them. Because they're holding.*" His voice did not change. "*If we tell them, half of them go home. The stone comes out of the ground in nine days. The docker in it goes up the thread with it. If we don't tell them...*"

He did not finish.

"*Article Eleven,*" said Yuanshu.

"*I know what Article Eleven says.*"

"*Then you know the answer.*"

A long pause on the line. Static. Wind off the Atlantic.

"*Yes,*" said Sterling. "*I just wanted somebody else to say it first.*"

---

They told them at noon. All eleven halls, on the open channel. Gao Min read it in Lingzhou, standing at the foot of the platform in front of four thousand people with their hands on the stone and the queue going out of the side door into the sun. Sterling read it in Port Halvard, standing on a crate at the harbor end of a line of fourteen thousand that went round the dock and up past the fish market and onto the coast road.

The same words. Seed had written them. Tell first.

*The thing coming down the thread sees only what burns. The stones open a little holding in each of you. A little holding is a little bright. To it, you are the brightest thing on Blue Star. It is coming to the stone at Port Halvard first, in four days. It is coming for you. You can let go. Nobody will think less of you. The stone will rise, and we will try to find another way. Or you can stay. We are telling you before you decide.*

He was in the banking hall of the Ossuary when it went out, on the vault steps, with the radio. He listened to the silence after it on the open channel. Eleven halls. Tens of thousands of people with their hands on black stones, hearing that they were bait.

Nobody spoke on the channel for a long time.

Then, from Port Halvard, through Sterling's handset, a woman's voice. Not young. A coastal accent, flat and hard, like the voices of the dockers on the radio from that city, who sounded as if they had been shouting over engines all their lives.

"*It took my son,*" said Callum Ward's mother. "*It's got him out there. Burning. He's the lantern. The boy with the thing in his head went up and talked to him.*" A pause. "*If it's coming down here to look for bright, it's coming to where my son came from. I'm not letting go. I'd like it to see my face.*"

On the channel, in eleven halls, nobody let go.

---

> **Some did.**
>
> **I am counting. Gao Min is counting. The one in the eleventh stone who counts the no's is counting.**
>
> **In Lingzhou, two hundred and nine let go in the first hour. In Port Halvard, eight hundred and forty.**
>
> **And in the second hour, in Port Halvard, two thousand and six more came.**

He looked at the radio.

> **They heard. On the channel. The woman. They came to stand where she is.**
>
> **Port Halvard: fifteen thousand one hundred and seventy holders.**
>
> **I do not know what that does to how bright it looks. I think it makes it very bright.**
>
> **I think that is what they want.**

---

He went to the bronze door at four in the afternoon.

Old Seven was in it, back from Shuanghe, facing out into East Market Road. The street was full again, the way it had been full since Awakening Day: the holders coming and going from their shifts at the hall on East Ring Road, the dumpling cart, the Number Nine going by every forty minutes with *TO THE HALL. FREE.* in the window and stopping outside the Ossuary on the way, because Wen Hao had added it to his route, and counting to ten.

He stood beside the tall dead captain in the doorway and looked out.

"*I'm going to Port Halvard,*" he said.

Old Seven did not turn his skull.

> **He says: yes.**
>
> **He says: that is not a question.**

"*I'm going to fly. I can be there in two days if I don't stop. I'll need the dead.*" He was quiet. "*Article Seven says the dead may defend. It doesn't say where. The nineteen thousand on the voyage said crossing into the Sea was crossing a border, and they wouldn't. This is crossing an ocean. Into someone else's country.*"

> **He says: ask them.**
>
> **He says: and ask the man whose country it is.**

---

Sterling said yes in four seconds.

"*Bring everyone,*" he said, on the radio. "*Everyone who'll come. I'll get the Federation Council to sign it tonight. If they won't, I'll sign it, and they can fine me.*" A pause. "*That's twice I've decided for something this week. Put it on the wall. I'd like your sister to write it in red.*"

The dead took a day.

The question went down the net, nine by nine, round and round, slow, the way everything went now. Seed wrote it the way Article Eleven said: *Something is coming down a thread to a stone on the Atlantic coast, in another country, to take the living who are holding the stone down. They asked to hold it. They were told what is coming and they stayed. The King of that country asks you to come and stand with them. You can say no. It is across an ocean. It is not your border. It is defense, but it is someone else's.*

And it came back the way the Lattice question had come back, the one about the top: not a list of names. A pile of index cards on the long table in Kwan's careful hand, and on top, the one that had gone round the net six hundred times and had a word added by every knot it went through.

Kwan read it out. Slowly.

"*It started with the Spear Corps,*" she said. "*Stone No. 7. They said: the woman's son was an F. We were spears. Nobody asked us either. Yes.*" She turned the card. "*And then everyone added to it. The First Legion: yes, a wall is a wall. The cemetery companies: yes. Grid Nine: yes, tell the boy we've never seen the sea. The Western Legion: Sergeant Ma says the pass will hold without us for a week, the pass held for thirty years without anybody. Yes. The Watch of the Shelf.*" She stopped.

"*What did the Watch say?*"

"*Nana Ampofo says: we live at the bottom of the sea. It's the same sea. It goes all the way round. We'll walk.*"

---

Nineteen thousand said yes. Mostly from the First Legion, the Spear Corps, the cemetery companies, Grid Nine. The Western Legion sent four hundred, because Sergeant Ma said that was what the pass could spare and he would not send a man the pass needed. The Watch of the Shelf did not send anyone across the land. They went down into the sea off Kesse, eleven hundred and forty-six of them, Nana Ampofo at their head with water streaming from her coat, and began to walk along the bottom of the ocean toward the Atlantic coast in the dark.

The keeper laid the road.

It came down the gold thread at three in the morning in its rough voice, a third smaller, and said that it had laid a road to Kirovsk without asking, once, because there was no time, and it had been sorry, and it would like to lay this one properly. It asked the dead whether they wanted a road. It asked Sterling whether a road could come down in his country. It asked Yuanshu whether he would walk on it or fly.

"*Fly,*" said Yuanshu. "*Beside it.*"

> ***...THEN I WILL LAY IT SO THAT YOU CAN SEE IT. ALL THE WAY.***
>
> ***...SO YOU ARE NOT FLYING ALONE IN THE DARK.***

---

They left at dawn on the second day after Shuanghe.

From the north wall, a gold road went up out of the War Memorial Cemetery into the sky in a long slant toward the east, toward the sunrise and the sea and a coast eleven thousand kilometers away. And up it, in rows of nine, rusted weapons on their shoulders, nineteen thousand dead of the First Legion and the Spear Corps and the cemetery companies and forty of Grid Nine who had never seen the sea.

He flew beside it.

He had learned to fly sideways on the sixty-third day of the long count, because Lingyue had told him that a Lord decides where down is and falls there. He decided down was east. He fell. Beside him, deciding the same thing, his wife, with her grandfather's sword on her back and the pale flame in her hand the size of a lamp, her hair loose in the wind because the red ribbon was gone. Behind them, Qin Hao, falling east with his notebook buttoned into his coat and his hands open and the white fire banked in them.

And at the front of the gold road, walking, where he always walked, two meters ten, with his spear grounded in one finger and a thumb: Old Seven.

Xiaoyu did not come. She said somebody had to ask the Collector, and somebody had to write it down. She stood on the north wall in her school jacket with her marker and watched them go, and when they were small, she uncapped the marker and wrote on the parapet, in square black capitals, where the wind and the rain would not take them, because nothing she wrote came off now:

*PORT HALVARD. THE DEAD WHO SAID YES, AND THREE LIVING. THEY WERE ASKED.*

> **Three days.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **It is morning. I said I would tell you every morning.**
>
> **And you said you would ask Callum Ward every day.**

*I know.*

He flew east over the sea with the gold road beside him and the sun coming up, and closed his eyes for a moment, and went up, the short way, not near, through the docker's knot in the Port Halvard stone and along the thread to the edge of the light, where a man was burning in a vast hand in the deep water, and called his name. Not near enough to be seen. Just near enough to be heard.

*Callum. It's me. It's tomorrow.*

*...yeah,* said the voice at the bottom of the well, very small, under the roar. *...still don't know.*

*That's all right.*

*...is Mum still holding?*

*Yes. She's at the front. Fifteen thousand people came to stand where she is.*

The fire roared.

*...that's Mum,* said Callum Ward. *...ask me again tomorrow.*
