## Chapter 222: Prune What It Has Touched

The third ring did not look for Seed. It had read the knot too.

Not the knot Seed had tied. The other one. The scout's, folded at the end of a dying shout in a round chamber under a city a year ago, and carried sideways to four seeds, and from the seeds to the sea, and from the sea to the gardener. REQUEST. A VESSEL IS LOOSE. COLLECT THE VESSEL. AND PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED.

The first ring had gone for the vessel and been asked a question and stopped. The second had been cutting, and been burned clean, and asked, and asked to rest.

The third went for what the vessel had touched.

> ***...YUANSHU.***

The keeper's voice. Not through the Elder. Straight down the gold road, which was still lying across four thousand kilometers of night from Kirovsk to Lingzhou, into the place behind his eyes, while he sat in the gray sand on the battlements of Kirovsk with Qin Hao's notebook in his hands.

> ***...IT IS IN MY FIELD.***
>
> ***...IT IS REACHING DOWN INTO MY FIELD. WITH THREADS. THE WAY I USED TO REACH DOWN INTO YOUR WORLD.***
>
> ***...IT IS CUTTING THE COUSINS.***

---

He was on the gold road before he had decided to be.

He did not remember standing up. He remembered Lingyue's hand on his arm, very hard, pulling him up out of the sand, and her voice saying *go, go, I'm coming,* and Old Seven already on the road ahead of him, and the Spear Corps behind, and Qin Hao on his knees in the gray sand with his burned hands in his lap, saying *I can't, I can't, not again, not tonight,* and Orlov putting a frosted bare hand on his shoulder and saying in his formal old-fashioned Huaxian, *No. You have done enough. Stay with me.*

The road carried them. Not back along the ground, the way it had come, four thousand kilometers of snowfields and frozen rivers. Up. The keeper bent it, the way you bend a rope, out of the night sky over the Northern Union and up through the dark toward the end of the world, toward a small white ring low over the north, and beyond it, a glow like the light over a city.

The field.

And above the field, gray and vast and cracked, with a dark center like the pupil of an eye, the third ring.

And between them, rain.

---

He had been in the field more times now than he could easily count. He had seen it full: endless rows of gold columns going away into a haze. Empty: faint rings on pale ground where the columns had stood. Full again: clusters of cousins and walls and the hundred and seventy-five who stayed, standing close together like people who have just arrived somewhere and have not decided yet where they want to be.

He had never seen it raining.

The threads came down out of the third ring's dark center onto the field the way they had come down onto Blue Star twelve hundred years ago, and thirty-one years ago, and an hour ago over Kirovsk. Silver. Fine. Millions. Falling slowly through the soft gold light, onto the columns. And where a thread touched a column, the column dimmed. Not all at once. From the top. The way a candle dims when something is drawing on the air around it.

And up the thread, out of the column, light.

> **It is taking the cousins.**
>
> **The way the second ring took Kirovsk. The way the keeper used to take Blue Star.**
>
> **They have been in the field for six weeks. They were asked every morning. Most of them said: ask again tomorrow.**
>
> **It is not asking.**

---

The keeper was trying.

He felt it as soon as his feet touched the pale ground at the top of the road. The whole field was moving. Not the ground. The keeper. Its attention, which was the field, which was all around them, warm and vast and frightened, pulling. The way a man pulls a blanket over a sleeping child when the window blows open.

It was pulling the columns together. Toward the center. Toward the far door. Away from the rain. Clusters of gold drawing close, and closer, the cousins and the walls and the hundred and seventy-five, into the middle of the field where the threads had not reached yet.

It could not move them fast enough.

> ***...I CANNOT FIGHT IT.***
>
> ***...I DO NOT KNOW HOW TO FIGHT. I ONLY KNOW HOW TO KEEP. AND REACH. AND TAKE. AND NOW ASK.***
>
> ***...IT IS WHAT I WAS. I KNOW EXACTLY WHAT IT IS DOING. I DID IT FOR LONGER THAN YOUR SPECIES HAS HAD WORDS.***
>
> ***...I DO NOT KNOW HOW TO STOP IT.***

---

Lingyue was already cutting.

She had come up the road behind him with her grandfather's sword lit and gone straight past him into the rain, the way she had gone down the wall at Kirovsk, and she was running between the columns at the edge of the field with the King's blade in both hands, cutting every thread she reached. Silver-gray. A sound like a string snapping. A column brightening as the thread over it recoiled, smoking, back up into the dark.

But the field was not a wall. It was a field. Millions of columns, spread across a plain of pale ground that went away into the haze, and the threads were falling on all of it.

> **She can reach a few hundred a minute.**
>
> **The ring is reaching tens of thousands.**

The Spear Corps went out across the field in their rows. They could not see the threads. They did what they had done at Kirovsk: wherever a column was dimming, a dead soldier of the Eastern Line put his rusted spear up over it, lit pale by a drop of Lingyue's light, so the thread touched the spear before it touched the column, and snapped.

Two hundred and seventy-two spears. Millions of columns.

> **It is not enough.**
>
> **It is not enough, Yuanshu.**
>
> **Qin Hao is in Kirovsk. He cannot do it again tonight. The white fire went all the way up the second ring. It took everything he had.**
>
> **I have one knot left.**

---

He stood at the top of the gold road where it came down into the field and looked up at the third ring.

Gray. Vast. Cracked. A dark center like the pupil of an eye. Raining onto the field. Not looking for him. Not looking for the vessel. It had read the scout's knot, and it was doing the other half. PRUNE WHAT IT HAS TOUCHED. The keeper. The cousins. Everything Seed had counted.

It was not going to send a thread down to him. It did not want him.

> **It does not want me.**
>
> **It wants what I touched.**
>
> **If I cannot get a thread to touch me, I cannot put the knot on it.**

"*Then I'll touch one,*" said Yuanshu.

> **...what?**

He was already walking.

Out across the pale ground of the field, between the clusters of columns drawing in toward the center, into the rain. Not running. Walking. With the staff in his hand. The way Old Seven walked into things.

The threads came down all around him. Silver. Fine. Millions. Falling past him onto the columns and not touching him, because he was not what they were for. He walked between them the way you walk through a bead curtain. He felt them brush past his shoulders and his arms and his face, cold and very fine, and slide off, and go on down to the gold.

"*It doesn't have to want me,*" he said. "*I just have to be touching it when you put the knot down.*"

> **...Yuanshu. If you take hold of a thread that is cutting, it does not care that it was not for you. It will cut whatever is on it.**
>
> **It will take you up with the column.**

"*I know.*"

> **Then do not do this.**
>
> **That is not a question. I am telling you. In person. With the reason.**

"*You can tell me,*" said Yuanshu. "*It's still mine.*"

He stopped beside a cluster of cousins at the edge of the field, small and pale, a little like Hush, standing close together in their columns with their eyes shut. A thread was coming down onto the nearest of them. Silver. Very fine. He watched it come.

And he reached up, and took hold of it.

---

It was not like being found.

The first ring, the six lamps, the walker in the field: they had found him. Held him straight up. Read him. This was not that. This was like putting your hand on a rope that is already being pulled. He felt the thread take him the way a current takes a swimmer. Not looking at him. Not knowing he was there. Just pulling, up, toward the dark center of the ring, and he was on it, and he was going.

His head went straight up.

His feet left the pale ground. A few centimeters. He was a Lord; he could fly. He could not fly against this.

> **I am putting it down.**
>
> **I am putting it down. I am putting it down. I am not looking.**

He felt the knot go. The last fifty-six names nobody remembered except a girl with a marker and a woman in a wheelchair. Set down on the thread, very gently, the way you set down an empty bowl between you and someone you have never met. And the thread took it, the way it was taking him, up.

> **It is gone.**
>
> **It is in the thread. It is going up. With you.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Let go.**

He could not let go. His hand would not open. The thread had him the way a current has a swimmer, and he was rising, a meter above the field, two, with his face turned straight up and his mind going up a thread into the dark, and the knot going up ahead of him, very small, very fast, toward the dark center of the third ring.

Below him, very far, somebody was shouting his name. Not the way the Quiet say a name. The other way.

> **Let go. Let go. Let go.**

---

The Elder caught him.

He did not see her come. Nobody did. She had been at the far door of the field, where the keeper was pulling the columns, where she always stood when the gold thread ran through her. And then she was not at the far door. She was beside him, in the rain, tall and pale and very still, with her silver eyes wide open and her long hands not pressed together.

She put one long hand around his wrist. The one that was holding the thread.

And she said, in her glass-note voice, not to him, not to Seed, not to the keeper, but up, along the thread, into the dark, toward the third ring:

"*Not him.*"

The thread stopped.

Not slack. Stopped. As if something at the other end had heard a voice it knew.

"*He is not what you came for,*" said the Elder. "*He is not a column. He is not what the vessel touched. He is the one who touches.*" Her hand did not move on his wrist. "*Let go of him.*"

The thread did not move.

"*I made a keeper once,*" said the Elder. "*With fifty-one others. In a valley. Under a red sun. We said yes together, and the yes became something that keeps.*" She looked up into the dark. "*I know what you were. I know what was taken out of you. I know who taught us to make you.*"

And then, very gently, she opened his hand.

Finger by finger. With her long pale fingers. The way you open the hand of a child who is holding on to something they should not.

He fell.

Two meters. Onto the pale ground of the field. Lingyue caught him. He did not know how she got there. She had her grandfather's sword in one hand and him in the other arm and her face was white and she was saying his name, the other way.

He looked up.

The Elder was still standing in the rain where he had been. With one long hand around the thread. Holding it.

And the thread was not pulling her.

> **She is holding the thread.**
>
> **It is not taking her. It does not know how. She is not a column. She is not a mind it was sent for.**
>
> **She is one of the fifty-two.**
>
> **She is a yes.**
