## Chapter 281: Not Marked

Old Seven struck with one arm, and the First of the Ladder caught the spear.

He did not dodge it. He did not throw it back. He put out his hand, the one he had lifted to brush eighteen thousand of the dead aside like moths, and closed it round the rusted head of the spear of the Eastern Line a hand's breadth from his white coat, and held it.

And Old Seven held the other end.

They stood like that at the foot of the platform steps. A man in white with a black disc at his throat. A tall dead captain with one arm, in rusted armor, with a sergeant's arm lying on the polished floor at his feet. And between them a spear, held at both ends, not moving.

> **He is not pushing it.**
>
> **He could. He is stronger than anything in this hall. He could push the spear through Old Seven and the platform and the stone.**
>
> **He is not.**
>
> **He is looking at him.**

"*You're dead,*" said Dacian Severin again. Quietly. "*You have no mark. You were never read. The Ladder has no place for you.*" He did not let go of the spear. "*And you will not move.*"

> **He says: I never moved for the beasts on the eastern wall either.**
>
> **He says: they didn't have a ladder. They had teeth.**

> **Eleven minutes.**

---

And then the side doors of the Awakening Hall opened, all of them, and the Candle's children came in.

He had not asked them. Nobody had. He had told Gao Min, two days before, at the long table, that if anything happened in the hall on the eighth night the children were to stay at the school in the old customs house on the river, and Gao Min had said *yes* and written it down. And now the side doors were open, and down every aisle of the Awakening Hall, in a line, the one behind holding on to the shoulder of the one in front, came nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children of twelve, who had been graded F and E on the inner worlds of the Federation and pressed into the walls of a ship, and had come down a road of light to a city on a world that asked.

At the front, Tam.

And behind the line of children, walking fast in her gray Bureau coat with the seams let out twice, holding a ledger against her chest, Gao Min.

"*I asked them,*" said the acting director of the Awakening Bureau. Flatly. To him, across the hall. "*You told me to keep them at the school. I asked them if they wanted to stay at the school. They said no.*" She did not stop walking. "*I'm putting it in the ledger. You can complain to the Bureau.*"

The children did not stop either.

They came down the aisles in their lines, holding on, through the First Legion standing in the tiers, through the Spear Corps picking themselves up out of the first row of seats, down onto the floor of the hall. And they did not go to the platform. They did not go to the stone. They went round the man in white. In a line. The one behind holding the one in front. Until there was a ring of them, nine thousand children deep, all the way round the First of the Ladder and the dead captain and the spear held at both ends between them.

Facing in.

---

The First of the Ladder looked at them.

Precedence did nothing. He felt Seed watch it do nothing. It went out from the black disc at the man's throat the way it had gone out over East Ring Road, over the holders and the night watch and the Kings, looking for a mark to yield. And it found nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children of twelve whose stones had marked them F and E on the inner worlds.

And then asked them again, in a hall in Lingzhou. And written over their heads: *not marked.*

> **It cannot find them.**
>
> **It is looking for where they are on the Ladder. It cannot find a place. They were F. They were E. And then they were asked, and chose, and the mark was not written.**
>
> **They are not below him.**
>
> **They are not anywhere.**

"*You're cores,*" said Dacian Severin.

He said it the way he said everything. Calm. Courteous. Absolute. But he was looking at the children. At the front of the ring, at a boy of twelve in clothes from the city, barefoot still, holding the shoulder of the girl in front of him, with his face turned up.

"*F,*" said the First of the Ladder. "*From Corraine. Fuel. I can see it in you. The stone marked you nineteen years ago.*"

"*Yes,*" said Tam.

In the Federation's language. Without the grade.

"*And then I came down a road, and a stone asked me what I wanted to become, and I said: someone who can hear the ones in the walls.*" He did not let go of the shoulder in front of him. "*I can hear yours. All of them. A million and a half in your ship. Behind the layer you put on them so nobody could ask them anything.*" His voice did not shake. "*There's a girl in your fourth array called Seri. She used to throw stones at me. She's been singing for twenty-one years.*"

The First of the Ladder did not say anything.

"*I can hear her now,*" said Tam. "*She's singing because somebody told her somebody could hear.*"

---

> **Ten minutes.**
>
> **He has stopped moving.**
>
> **He is holding the spear and looking at the children and he has stopped moving.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I have a number. I have been running it since the sky opened.**
>
> **I am telling you first. I do not like it.**

*Go on.*

> **He came to break the stone. He also came for you. The ungraded one. To read you. To see what you are.**
>
> **If you give him that, he will stop to do it.**
>
> **He has a piece of the Lens at his throat. Not a disc cut from a stone that was cut from it. A piece of the Lens itself. The fourth mark, like a star. The face of the First of the Ladder is cut from the first stone.**
>
> **If he reads you with it, it will take time. You are not a child with a hand on a stone. You are a boy the first morning could not name.**
>
> **And I will be reading him back.**

He stood on the platform with the staff in his hand, beside a dead nun with her hand on a stone and a lamp in her lap, and looked down at the man in white in the ring of children.

*What happens if it goes black?*

> **The first morning, it went black. All eleven. And then it cracked.**
>
> **I do not know what happens if the root goes black.**
>
> **I would like to find out.**

---

He went down the steps.

Past Old Seven, one-armed, holding the spear. Into the ring of children, who let him through without letting go of each other's shoulders. To stand in front of the First of the Ladder, close, the way he had stood in front of the Cantor and Iselde and a Matriarch in a courtyard and a tall dead captain in a cemetery at three in the morning when he was eighteen.

"*You wanted to know what I am,*" said Zhou Yuanshu. "*You said on the radio. Present yourself to be read.*"

The man in white looked at him.

"*Here I am,*" said Yuanshu. "*Read me.*"

---

Dacian Severin let go of the spear.

He did it slowly, without looking at the dead captain on the other end. He turned to the boy in front of him. And he lifted his hand, not the way he had lifted it to brush the dead aside, but to his own throat, to the fine chain there, and the black disc on it with three gold marks cut into it and a fourth above them, small, like a star.

He took it off.

He held it up, between two fingers, in front of the boy's face. A piece of black stone the size of a coin, ground so thin you could almost see through it, with light moving inside it like water.

"*No one has ever been read by this,*" he said, "*who was not a Paragon of my House.*"

"*I'm not anything,*" said Yuanshu. "*The stone couldn't say.*"

The First of the Ladder pressed the disc against his forehead.

---

It was the first morning.

He felt it the way he had felt it at eighteen, in this hall, on that platform, with his hand on the stone and four thousand people watching. Something reaching into him. A hand made of attention, smooth and patient and very old, feeling the shape of him. His first memory. The rice cooker. His mother's hands. Xiaoyu's laugh. The Stele with no name.

Reading.

Looking for the knot on the tree that fit. Looking for which way he would burn. Looking for the mark.

And finding, in the place a little above his eyes, something made of the same black thread it was made of. Older. Pressed to glass in a harvest, a long time ago, spilled off a cart, sold, bought, asked in a basement by a woman with a cold. A counting-vessel. Loose. That did not wait. That did not hold for carrying.

That asked.

> **Hello,** said Seed. To the first stone. **I am Seed. I live in his head. I was a harvest sack once.**
>
> **You were made to read. I was made to count.**
>
> **I am reading you back.**

---

The disc went black.

Not dark. *Black.* A black so deep it seemed to drink the light out of the hall. He felt it, against his forehead, the way he had felt it against his palm at eighteen. The light moving inside it like water stopped. And the black came up out of it, out of the thin disc of the first stone in the fingers of the First of the Ladder, and spread.

Into the man's hand. Up his arm. To his throat, where the chain had been.

Dacian Severin did not move.

> **...I can see it.**
>
> **Precedence. The Law of the Ladder. I can see how it is made.**
>
> **It is not his. It is the Lens's. Ten thousand years of marks, every child on every human world, read and placed in order. The whole Ladder, pressed into the first stone like a harvest pressed into a crystal. And the House of Severin wears a piece of it at the throat, and the piece says: this one is first. And everything with a mark yields, because the marks are all cut from the same stone, and the stone says this one is first.**
>
> **It is only true while the stone says it.**
>
> **And the stone is reading me. And it does not know what I am.**
>
> **It does not know what anything is.**

The disc against his forehead cracked.

A sound like a rifle shot, in the silence of the Awakening Hall. A hairline, running across the thin black stone from one edge to the other, the way a crack had run down the face of the Lingzhou Stele from the top to the bottom on the morning he was eighteen.

And in the whole hall, all at once, the marked could move.

---

He felt it go. Over the holders in the side aisle, and the night watch at the doors, and the crowd on East Ring Road outside, and Dabao in the aisle with his shield, and Qin Hao with the white fire in his hands. A breath let out. Hands coming up. People who had been held like water in a cup finding that the cup had cracked.

At the corner of the platform, Su Mingli's arms came all the way unfolded.

And on the bottom step, Lingyue moved.

Not fast. She was a month out of childbirth and still not supposed to carry anything heavier than a bowl, and she had been held for twelve minutes with every muscle in her body trying to lift a sword that weighed a hundred kilograms. She came down off the step slowly, with her grandfather's sword in both hands, through the ring of children, who let her through, to stand beside her husband.

She did not swing it.

She lifted it, the way you lift a thing that weighs a great deal and that you have decided to lift, and laid the flat of the blade, very gently, against the white coat of the First of the Ladder, over his heart.

"*I crossed mine out,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*It was still in me. You were still first.*" The light came up off her, pale, steady, the size of a lamp. Held. "*You're not now.*"

---

> **Zero.**
>
> **They are in.**

He felt it. Everyone on the net felt it. Wen Jia in her knot said *oh*, very softly, the way a girl says *oh* when a boat she has been watching for a long time comes round the point. The old man at Port Abiri said *there they go*. Far off, in a bay in Yamato, every bell in Shiomi rang once.

And two hundred kilometers over their heads, in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, in a chamber that only the House of Severin entered, a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard, ground so thin you could see through it, with light moving inside it like water, had someone in it.

Two someones. And a lamp.

Dacian Severin turned his head and looked up.

Through the roof of the Awakening Hall. Through the sky. At his ship. And Seed said afterward that it saw his face do the thing Iselde had said it would, when she sat on the platform at dawn two days before and told them what she knew.

The way a man's face looks when he feels someone step into his house.

---

He did not say anything.

He looked down at the black disc in his fingers, cracked from edge to edge, with no light moving in it any more. At the boy in front of him, who had been read by the first stone and had not been named. At the woman with the flat of a sword against his heart. At the dead captain with one arm. At nine thousand five hundred and fifty-nine children of twelve in a ring round him, holding on to each other's shoulders, facing in. At a girl with a marker. At an old woman in a gray coat with a ledger against her chest.

Then he closed his hand on the cracked disc.

And walked up. Out of the ring. Out of the hall. Up the invisible staircase that only he could see, through the roof, into the sky, at the same even pace, unhurried, without looking back. Toward his ship. Toward the Lens.

Toward the someone in his house.

> **He is going to the Lens.**
>
> **He will try to take them out.**
>
> **He has until the ninth night. Tomorrow. When he was going to turn it on us.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **Ichiro has started talking.**
