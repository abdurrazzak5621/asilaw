## Chapter 203: Gleaners

They came out of the dark on the ninth night, from ahead, and Mo Shi called them four seconds early, which he said afterward was the most he had ever been able to see anything coming, because out here there was nothing in the way.

"*Front,*" he said. He was asleep. Then he was not asleep; he was sitting bolt upright on the pale ground beside the clock with his eyes shut and both hands flat on the island, the way he had sat on the floor of the banking hall in the middle of the chalk city. "*Front. Left. A lot of them. Coming fast. Toward us.*"

"*What?*" said Yuanshu.

"*I don't know. They're not lamps. They're not walkers.*" His lips were moving. "*They're... they're hungry.*"

> **I see them.**
>
> **Through the dead at the front. Old Seven is looking at them.**

---

They were small.

That was the first thing. After the walkers, after the Mouth, after the spires and the sphere and the faceless one, he had stopped expecting anything above the sky to be small. These were. The size of a dog, perhaps. A large one. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Coming out of the dark ahead of the island in a long loose cloud, the way starlings come over a field at dusk, turning together, scattering, coming together again.

Gray. Translucent, almost, like something made of smoke that had been left out in the cold until it set. No faces. No eyes that he could see. Long, thin, trailing shapes with a cluster of fine pale filaments at the front of each one, waving, the way a sea anemone's arms wave in a current.

And in the middle of each one, very small, very dim, an ember.

> **...they are made of the same stuff as Hungers.**
>
> **Not Hungers. Too small. Too simple. But the same stuff. Like a mouse is made of the same stuff as a tiger.**
>
> **They eat embers.**
>
> **And Yuanshu. They can see the dead.**

The cloud turned. All of it, at once, the way a flock turns. Toward the island.

Toward the ring of fifty thousand banked embers standing at its rim.

---

> **They are scavengers. I think. I am guessing from how they move and what they are made of. I am guessing from the husk worlds.**
>
> **On the first voyage we passed husk worlds. Gray. Dead. Nobody left. Eaten by Hungers a long time ago.**
>
> **I always wondered what happened to the embers the Hungers missed. The ones too small to bother with.**
>
> **I think these happened to them.**
>
> **They follow the big things. They eat what is left.**
>
> **And something big is pruning the arm, and has just started reading a dead Hunger full of cold minds.**
>
> **They are coming from that direction. From the Mouth.**
>
> **I think they are running.**

"*Running from what?*"

> **From what they always follow. When it gets too close.**

---

The first wave hit the leading edge at 02:41 by Bai Lu's clock.

He was there. He had gone at a run across two kilometers of pale ground from the clock to the front, with the staff, with Lingyue beside him and her grandfather's sword already lit pale, and Qin Hao behind them swearing at his bandages. The Spear Corps were already at the rim, in their rows, with Old Seven in front. Behind them, the Western Legion, nine by nine, closing in toward the front from both sides, because Sergeant Ma had heard Mo Shi through the Lattice and started moving before anyone told him.

The gleaners came over the rim like water over a dam.

They did not attack the way beasts attacked. They did not bite. They came in low and fast over the edge of the island in a gray wave and went for the dead, every one of them, straight at the banked embers in the chests of the Spear Corps, and when the pale filaments at the front of a gleaner touched a soldier, they went in.

Not through bone. Through the ember. Into the dull red point banked under ash in the soldier's chest, and the ember flared, once, the way the graves had flared at the Western Pass, and the gleaner glowed.

It was eating.

> **They are not hurting the bone. They are drinking the ember.**
>
> **Banking does not hide them from these. The Banked Field hid the dead from Hunger seeds that were looking for warmth. These are not looking. They are touching.**
>
> **Pale fire.**

"*Lingyue!*"

She was already doing it. A rain of silver-gray drops off the point of her grandfather's sword, falling downhill through the Field onto the rusted spearheads of the Spear Corps, and the spears lit, and the Spear Corps struck. The way they always struck. Two hundred and seventy-two rusted spears, pale fire running along their edges, through gray translucent bodies the size of dogs.

The gleaners came apart. Not like walkers. Like smoke. Struck once, they dispersed into gray haze, and the small dim ember in the middle of each one went out, and the haze drifted off behind the island into the dark.

There were thousands of them.

---

It was not a battle. Not the way the wall had been, or the Western Pass, or the field. It was weather. A long gray storm coming over the leading edge of the island in waves, wave after wave, out of the dark ahead, and fifty thousand dead of Blue Star standing in it, striking, while a girl on the pale ground behind them rained pale fire on every edge she could reach.

The gleaners did not fight back. They did not have anything to fight with. They came over the rim and went for embers, and when they were struck they dispersed, and when they were not struck they drank.

> **Sixteen.**
>
> **Sixteen of the Western Legion. The gleaners got to them before the pale fire did. They drank them down to nothing. Not unwoven. Not burned. Just... finished. Like a candle finished.**
>
> **Twenty-two.**
>
> **They are coming in faster than Lingyue can reach. The island is two kilometers wide. She is one girl with one sword.**

"*Qin Hao.*"

"*I know.*" Qin Hao was standing on the pale ground behind the front line with his bandaged arms held out in front of him and his face gray with concentration. Fire was running down his arms. Ordinary fire. Orange, flickering, the color of a stove. Not white. "*I can't burn threads with this. I can burn them.*" He swept his arms forward. A long low wall of orange flame went out across the leading edge of the island, over the heads of the Spear Corps, into the front of the next gray wave.

The gleaners went through it.

They did not burn. They went through it the way the shells at Heishui had gone through the walkers. Ordinary fire, and things made of the stuff of Hungers.

Qin Hao stared at his hands.

"*Ordinary,*" he said. "*Until.*"

---

> **Thirty-one.**
>
> **Yuanshu. Lingyue cannot cover the whole front. The Spear Corps cannot be everywhere. The Western Legion has no pale fire; their edges are not lit.**
>
> **I am running it.**

He was already running it. Not with Seed. Underneath Seed. The way he had on the jetty at Sandu, sitting with his back against a rusted crane, thinking not about the problem but about something Ifeoma had said on a white boat in the dark.

Pale fire was Death wearing Light. Lingyue's Light, inside the Field, went pale. Her Light. Anyone's Light.

But the Field was not only Lingyue's. The Field was his. Nine point eight kilometers of slope running downhill toward him, and everything inside it ran down the slope, and the dead inside it got back up.

And the gleaners were inside it.

> **...what are you thinking?**

"*They're made of the stuff of Hungers,*" he said. "*Hungers burn embers. Embers are what's left of minds. The dead.*" He held the staff in both hands. "*The gleaners eat embers. They're full of them. Small. Dim. In the middle of each one.*"

> **Yes.**

"*Embers inside my Field run downhill.*"

Seed was silent for almost a second.

> **...to you.**
>
> **To the bottom of the slope. To the door. The way the beast embers ran down to you in Tannery Square, four hundred and eleven of them, and you opened the door, and they went through.**
>
> **The drain.**
>
> **You have not used it since Tannery Square.**

"*I've never used it on something that wasn't already dead.*"

> **They are not alive. Not the way you are. They are made of Hunger stuff around a stolen ember. The ember is not theirs. It was somebody's, once.**
>
> **If you open the door...**

---

He opened the door.

Not *Return*. Not anything he had a name for. He did what he had done in Tannery Square, months ago, when a seed under the city had been pulling at the dying and he had stood between them and it and let the Field's slope beat its pull. He went down into the Death Law, all thirty-nine point two percent of it, to the bottom, to the cool dark door where things went when they were finished.

And he opened it all the way.

And let the Field run.

He felt it go out from him across the island, out past the rim, into the gray storm coming over the leading edge. A slope. Nine point eight kilometers of slope, and everything inside it that was an ember running down it, toward him, toward the door, the way water runs down a hill. The banked dead of Blue Star did not run; they were banked, held, asked, standing in a Lattice that held each other. They stayed where they were.

The gleaners' embers ran.

All of them. Thousands. Small and dim and stolen, in the middle of thousands of gray translucent bodies the size of dogs, and when the slope found them they came loose. Out of the gleaners. Out of the gray stuff of Hungers around them. Running downhill across the island in a long faint glittering stream, past the Spear Corps and the Western Legion and Lingyue with her sword and Qin Hao with his useless orange fire, toward a boy standing at the leading edge with a white staff in his hands and his eyes shut.

Through him.

Through the door.

Gone.

---

The gleaners came apart.

Every one of them. All at once. When the ember in the middle of each one ran out of it down the slope, there was nothing left to hold the gray stuff together, and it dispersed. Not into haze. Into nothing. A long gray storm coming over the leading edge of the island, and then not, and the dark ahead of the island clear and empty again, with the far lights hanging in it, sliding slowly past.

He sat down on the pale ground at the leading edge.

He had not meant to. His legs did it. He sat with the staff across his knees and blood running out of his nose, and Bai Lu was there, from somewhere, with her hands on his face, and he could not feel them.

> **Six thousand four hundred and eleven embers.**
>
> **Through the door. In nine seconds.**
>
> **They were not the gleaners'. They were stolen. From husk worlds. From dead Hungers. From things nobody remembers.**
>
> **They are through the door now. Where things go when they are finished.**
>
> **I do not know what they were. I do not know if anybody asked them anything, ever, before they were eaten.**
>
> **I am sorry I could not ask them. There was no time.**

"*Neither could I,*" said Yuanshu.

> **No.**
>
> **I am writing that down too.**
>
> **That we did not ask them. That we could not. That they went through the door anyway, and we do not know if they wanted to.**
>
> **I would like it to be on the wall. Somewhere. Small. So that nobody forgets that sometimes there is no time.**

---

Old Seven had not moved from the leading edge.

When it was over, when the dark ahead was clear and the island was humming forward again through the Sea and the dead were closing their rows where the gleaners had been, he turned his skull. Not toward Yuanshu. Ahead. Into the dark, toward the place where, very far, very faint, a cold dead Hunger hung somewhere in the black, giving no light.

> **He says they were running.**
>
> **He says he has seen animals run like that once. On the wall. In the Black Tide. In the hour before the walker came down. Every beast in the Wastes running south, toward the wall, toward the guns, because whatever was behind them was worse.**
>
> **He says: whatever the gleaners were running from is at the cold fire.**
>
> **He says: two nights.**
