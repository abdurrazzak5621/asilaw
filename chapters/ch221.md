## Chapter 221: Fire That Burns What Binds

Qin Hao had been standing at the top of the fortress stairs the whole time, and nobody had noticed him, because he was not doing anything.

He had come down the gold road from Lingzhou at a run with his bandaged arms and his notebook in his breast pocket and his ordinary fire, and he had stood on the battlements of Kirovsk in the knee-deep snow and watched Lingyue go down the wall into the rain with a King's sword, cutting, and watched the Spear Corps put their rusted spears over the heads of living soldiers, and watched Orlov freeze the air over a whole city with his bare hands. And he had looked down at his own hands, wrapped in bandages from the shoulder to the wrist, with orange fire flickering in his palms. Ordinary. The color of a stove.

The threads went through ordinary fire. He had found that out on the island, with the gleaners. He had found it out at Heishui, watching the shells go through the walkers. Fire that burned what was. Not fire that burned what bound.

So he had stood at the top of the stairs and done nothing, and hated it, for an hour.

He was standing there when Yuanshu went to his knees in the snow, with a single silver thread coming down out of the second ring onto the crown of his head, and his face turned straight up, and something beginning, very slowly, to lift.

---

Yuanshu did not see what happened next. He was looking at the sky. He was looking at the sky the way a man looks at the sky when something has hold of the back of his neck and is lifting, and everything he is, the cemetery and the voyage and the field and his mother and a girl on a step of a groundskeeper's hut saying *yes, obviously,* is beginning to go up a thread into the dark.

Seed told him afterward. Every word. It had seen it through the Field, through the dead on the wall, through Old Seven, halfway down the battlements, who had turned his skull toward the fortress and stopped moving.

> **Qin Hao came down the stairs.**
>
> **He did not run. He walked. Through the snow. To you.**
>
> **He did not ask anyone. He did not say anything.**
>
> **He took his notebook out of his breast pocket and put it on the snow beside you. Very carefully. Open. At the last page.**
>
> **Then he reached up and took hold of the thread.**

---

> **With his hands. His bare hands. He had torn the bandages off on the stairs.**
>
> **The thread was going up from your head into the dark. He put both hands around it a meter above you, the way you hold a rope.**
>
> **The thread did not notice. Threads do not notice the living unless they are reading them. It was not reading him. It was pulling you.**
>
> **He held it.**
>
> **And the fire on his hands was orange. Ordinary. It did not touch the thread. It went through it, the way the shells went through the walkers.**
>
> **He did not let go.**

Yuanshu, on his knees in the snow, with his face turned up and the cold tug at the place behind his eyes going colder, heard a voice. Very close. Rough. Swearing.

Not at him. At the thread. At the sky. At a colonel who had held the inner gate of the Western Pass for four hours with ordinary fire and written in his report that he could hear something on the other side of the rift, laughing.

"*Come on,*" said Qin Hao. Through his teeth. With his hands around a thread he could not burn. "*Come on. Come on. You came back on the wall. You came back for the river gate. You came back when it mattered.*" His voice cracked. "*It matters.*"

---

> **It came back.**
>
> **Not from his Law. His Law is Fire, and Fire was burning, orange, and doing nothing.**
>
> **From the other place. The one that is not his Law. The place where you keep the things you would rather die than fail at.**
>
> **I could not find it for four months. I looked every day. He looked every day. Bai Lu looked.**
>
> **It was not lost. It was waiting for something worth coming back for.**
>
> **It went white.**

---

He felt it.

Even with his head turned straight up and his mind going up a thread into the dark, he felt it. A sudden heat, a meter above his head. Not on him. On the thread. White. Not the white of light or of ice or of a King's tribulation. The white of a fire that does not burn what is, but what binds. That unknots. That goes into a tangle of string the way water goes in, and loosens it, and each strand lets go of the next.

The thread above his head went white.

And the white went up it.

> **It is going up the thread.**
>
> **The way pale fire went down the trunk at Port Abiri. Downhill. Except the Field runs toward you, and the thread runs toward the ring, and white fire burns what binds, and the thread is a binding.**
>
> **It is going up the binding. All of it. Toward the other end.**
>
> **Toward the ring.**

He felt the pull at the back of his neck stop.

Not slacken. Stop. The thread above his head was not a thread any more. It was a line of white fire going up into the dark, very fast, very straight, the way a fuse burns, and where it had been, a meter above him, there was nothing holding him at all.

His head came down.

He fell forward into the snow on his hands, gasping, with blood running out of his nose and his ears, and the notebook open on the snow in front of him, at the last page. He could not read it. His eyes would not focus.

He looked up.

---

Qin Hao was standing over him in the snow with his arms held up above his head, both hands open, and white fire running out of them up into the sky.

Not one thread now. The fire had gone up the one, into the dark center of the second ring, and found the ring full of threads, millions of them, all going down, all bound to the ring at one end and to a living mind at the other. And it had done what white fire does. It had gone along every binding it could reach.

He saw it. Everyone in Kirovsk saw it. Out of the dark center of the second ring low over the polar Wastes, the rain of silver threads over the city, millions of them, turning white. From the top. All at once. A great slow wave of white fire coming down out of the ring along every thread toward the ground, the way frost spreads down a window, and where it reached the end of a thread, the thread let go.

And what had gone up the threads came down.

Light. Faint and gold and flickering. Faces. Meals. Songs. First words. Dead parents' hands. Falling out of the white threads like snow, in long slow drifts, over the wall and the city, down toward the heads that were still turned straight up at the sky. Toward the people they had been taken from, an hour ago, five minutes ago, a breath ago.

The young soldier on the battlement nearest the fortress, with frost in his eyebrows, who had been about to say something, had his face turned up at the sky when the light came down to him. It went into him the way it had come out. Through the crown of his head.

His head came down.

He said: "*...Mama?*"

---

> **Not all of them.**
>
> **The ones who went up a long time ago, at nine o'clock, at the beginning, are too far up. In the ring. Packed. Sacked. The white fire cannot reach them; their threads were cut at the bottom when they were taken. There is nothing to come down.**
>
> **But the ones who were still going up. The ones whose threads were still attached. Still lifting.**
>
> **They are coming down.**
>
> **Eighteen thousand. I am counting. Nineteen. Twenty-two.**
>
> **They are coming home.**

---

Qin Hao went down on his knees in the snow beside Yuanshu, with his arms still up and the white fire still running out of his hands, and his face gray, and blood at his nose.

"*Is it enough?*" he said. Not to anyone. "*Is it enough? Did it go up far enough?*"

> **It went all the way.**
>
> **Into the ring. To the dark center. Where the knot is.**
>
> **The ring was so full of threads that it could not find the knot. It did not know it had been asked.**
>
> **It is not full any more.**

High over the polar Wastes, the second gray ring had stopped turning.

Every thread from its dark center was white, and letting go, one by one, from the top. And in the middle of it, in the dark center, where the threads had all come from and where the white fire had all gone, there was nothing pulling anything any more.

There was a ring. Pruned. Empty. With nothing in it that had ever asked.

And a small complicated shape of fifty-six fine silver strands, put down in the middle of it, the way you put an empty bowl down between you and someone.

> **I am still not looking.**
>
> **But I think it has found it.**

---

It took eleven seconds.

Then something came down the last of the white threads that was not light and not fire. It came the long way, faint, very far, and Seed did not try to translate it, because it was not in a language. It was a shape. The shape of a thing that had been asked a question for the first time in its existence, and had looked inside itself for an answer, and found that it had only one.

> ***...ST...***
>
> ***...STOP.***

Yuanshu, on his knees in the snow, looked up at the second ring.

"*That's rest,*" he said. His voice did not sound like his. "*Isn't it. It wants to rest.*"

> **I think so.**
>
> **I think it is the only thing it knows how to want. It was a keeper once. Somebody's keeper. Its yes was taken out. It has been cutting for the gardener ever since, without knowing it could do anything else.**
>
> **Somebody asked it what it wants.**
>
> **It wants to stop.**

---

He had opened the door for four thousand on the Western Pass. For a major in a column. For six thousand gleaner embers in nine seconds. For more minds than had ever lived on Blue Star, for nineteen hours, in the dark of the cold fire.

He had never opened it for a ring.

He did it kneeling in the snow on the fortress above the main gate of Kirovsk, with the staff across his knees and Qin Hao beside him with white fire still running out of his hands, and Orlov standing over them both with his bare arms frosted to the shoulder, and Lingyue coming up the stairs at a run with her grandfather's sword. He went down into the Death Law, forty-four percent of it, to the door at the bottom that had not closed properly since the cold fire, and it was easier than it should have been, because the door was already open, a little, the way a door is open in a house where someone might come home.

He opened it the rest of the way.

And asked the ring.

*You can rest. If you want. Nobody will ask you again.*

---

The second ring came apart over the Northern Union at 23:41.

Not like a walker. Not like a trunk. Like a cloud. All at once, the whole vast cracked gray wheel of it, low over the polar Wastes, going soft at the edges, the way a cloud goes soft at sunset, and then thin, and then not there. And out of the place where it had been, falling slowly through the white-lit night over Kirovsk, something that was not rain and not thread and not light.

Sand.

Gray. Fine. Falling over the wall and the city and the snow in long soft drifts, the way the spire at Port Abiri had fallen, and the trunks off the coast of the Southern Compact, and the walkers in the field. It fell for an hour. It covered the snow on the battlements of Kirovsk in a thin gray layer, like ash after a fire.

Nobody swept it up. Orlov would not let them. He said the snow would take it in the spring. He said it had been somebody's keeper once.

> **I am counting.**
>
> **Forty-one thousand taken, before we came. Twenty-two thousand came back down the white threads. Nineteen thousand did not. They are in the ring. They went up too far. The ring is sand now.**
>
> **I do not know where they are.**
>
> **I do not know if they went through the door with it, or somewhere else.**
>
> **I am writing it down that I do not know.**

---

Qin Hao sat in the gray sand on the battlements with his bare burned hands in his lap and his notebook open on the snow beside him, at the last page, and did not move for a long time.

Yuanshu picked up the notebook.

The last page had one line on it, from months ago, in Yuanshu's own handwriting. From a cot in a gatekeeper's lodge at the river gate in Lingzhou, where he had written it down for Qin Hao, because Qin Hao's hands had been in bandages and he could not hold a pen.

*ORDINARY FIRE. UNTIL.*

Yuanshu took the pen out of the spine of the notebook, and wrote under it, while Qin Hao watched him do it and did not say anything at all:

*UNTIL KIROVSK.*

And under that:

*FIRST.*
