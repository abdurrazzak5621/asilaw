## Chapter 283: Out

In the morning, the First of the Ladder asked an engineer.

Seed heard it through the cells. Tam heard it through the walls. Iselde heard it through the Rowed Fleet, whose people were not deaf and could hear everything, and she called the north wall at six with her voice doing something it had not done since the bridge of the Eighth Candle, eight months ago, when a core at position four thousand and eleven had said *tell my sister I'm here*.

> ***He went down to the arrays.***
>
> ***The C's on the flagship say he has never been down to the arrays. No Paragon has. Not in nine hundred years. He came down the long corridor past them at five in the morning, alone, with the cracked face in his hand, and stopped at the door of the first array, and looked in.***
>
> ***There is a C there called Halvarde. Sixty years in the arrays. The oldest engineer in the fleet.***
>
> ***He asked her.***

*Asked her what?*

> ***He said: what do you want.***
>
> ***She said: sir?***
>
> ***He said it again. He said it the way he said it to the fisherman. Badly. Like a word in a book.***
>
> ***And Halvarde said: I want to open the walls, sir. I've wanted to for sixty years. I heard the boy on the Candle say it was done. I heard the captain. I heard the fisherman last night. I want to open the walls.***
>
> ***And he said...***

Iselde stopped. He heard her breathe.

> ***He said: then ask them first.***

---

It took forty days.

Not for the flagship. The flagship took nine. Sixty arrays, a million and a half people in the walls, and Halvarde and every C on the ship and the cradles Torvin had designed, sixty of them, sent across from the Eighth Candle on the keeper's road with Torvin himself, who would not let anyone else carry them. Cell by cell. Each one asked first, through the marks, by a fog-ringer in the Lens who did not stop talking for nine days and nights and said afterward that his throat hurt and he did not have a throat. *I'm going to open your wall now. Do you want me to? You can say no. You can say ask me tomorrow. Nobody here is going to do anything you don't say.*

Most of them said yes.

Some said no. Some said they wanted to stay in the wall, burning low, and carry the ship home, by choice, the way the Candle's people had, in turns, with rest, and see the Assembly's faces when it came out of the fold. Some said ask me tomorrow. Halvarde asked them tomorrow. And the day after.

And on the fourth day, in the fourth array, at a position Tam had found by listening, a cell went dark, and cold, and opened.

---

He was not there. He saw it through Tam, who was.

Tam had asked to go up. He had asked Gao Min first, and Gao Min had asked him whether he was sure, three times, and then written it in her ledger and given him a coat. He had asked the keeper. He had gone up the road of light alone, at dawn, barefoot, the first of the Candle's children to go back up into a ship, past the Eighth Candle and the twelve of the Rowed Fleet and ninety burning cathedrals, into the largest of them, through a door a C held open for him, down a long corridor nobody from the bridge had walked down in nine hundred years until five days before, into the fourth array.

And stood in front of the cell while Halvarde and Torvin brought the cradle down on its chains.

And caught her.

> **She is twelve.**
>
> **Thirty-three inside. Twenty-one years in the wall.**
>
> **She is very thin. She has opened her eyes.**
>
> **She is looking at him.**

And then, through Tam, through the keeper's eye, down the gold thread, onto the north wall of Lingzhou, very small, in a girl's voice from a street behind a fish market on a world called Corraine, saying something out loud for the first time in twenty-one years:

> ***...Tam?***
>
> ***...you got tall.***

And Tam, twelve in the body and thirty-one inside, laughing and crying at once on the floor of the fourth array of the flagship of the House of Severin:

> **He says: I'm the same height.**
>
> **He says: I've been the same height for nineteen years.**
>
> **She says: you got tall anyway.**

---

The other ships took longer.

Ninety ships of the Great Houses. Each with its Paragon, its Matriarch, its Fleet Captains, its C's in the arrays. Each with a million people or more in the walls, or half a million, or a hundred thousand, with their ears stopped, who had been asked on the ninth night by a fog-ringer in a stone what they wanted, and had answered, and had been heard.

And the people who were not in the walls had been asked too.

Every Paragon. Every Matriarch. Every Fleet Captain on every bridge. Every C. Everyone who wore a face on a chain round their throat, cut from a stone that had been cut from the Lens. Ichiro had asked them all. *What do you want?* And they had answered, alone, in their cabins and on their bridges and in their arrays, in the dark, the way eight billion people on Blue Star had answered on the north wall.

Seed said that most of them had said: *to be first.*

The Collector had said that. Every one, the same. The easiest want it had ever kept.

But Seed said not all of them.

> **Halvarde said: to open the walls.**
>
> **A Fleet Captain of the House of Iron said: to see my brother. He is in the third array of my own ship. I put him there.**
>
> **A Paragon of the House of Teller said: I do not know. And then she said: I have never not known anything.**
>
> **Corvane Varr-Anthos said: to be asked by my daughter what I want.**
>
> **And one hundred and twelve thousand C's on ninety ships said: what Halvarde said.**

---

Sixty-one ships opened their arrays.

Not all at once. Not because a Fleet Marshal ordered it; the First of the Ladder did not order anything. He sat on the floor of the Lens chamber in the bow of his flagship with his back against the wall and the cracked face in his hand, and talked to a fog-ringer, and was asked every morning what he wanted, and some mornings he answered and some mornings he said *tomorrow*. He did not give one order in forty days. Seed said that was the first forty days in two hundred years that the First of the Ladder had not given an order.

They opened because the C's asked.

Ship by ship. Array by array. The engineers in the walls, who had heard a Listener-Captain unpress her sister and a boy on a world that asks say *it was done*, and a fog-ringer in a stone ask them on the ninth night what they wanted. They went to their Captains, and asked. And some Captains said yes. And some said no, and the C's asked again tomorrow. And some Captains were asked by their own brothers, or daughters, or mothers, through a mark that came down a cell from the Lens, and went down to the arrays themselves.

Sixty-one ships, in forty days, sent cradles down on chains.

Twenty-nine did not.

---

They folded on the forty-first night.

Twenty-nine ships of the Great Houses whose Paragons and Captains had been asked on the ninth night what they wanted, and said *to be first*, and had not been asked again by anyone they would listen to. They lit, the way they had lit when they arrived, white and gold and the colors of their Houses, and folded. Away. Out of the Shallows. Back along the arm toward the inner worlds and the Assembly at Anthos. Burning their cores the old way, held under water and set on fire.

But their cores had been asked.

> **They heard. On the ninth night. Through the layer. As a mark.**
>
> **Someone asked them what they wanted, and they answered, and someone heard.**
>
> **They know it can be done now.**
>
> **They know there is a fisherman in a stone who will ask them again.**
>
> **I do not think twenty-nine ships of people who know that will burn the same way they did before.**

On the north wall, watching the twenty-nine lights go out one after another in the north like lamps along a street at dawn, Tam said nothing. He had come back down the road of light on the tenth day with a girl of twelve holding his hand, who had been thirty-three inside for twenty-one years and was learning to wear shoes. She stood beside him now, on the parapet. Seri, from Corraine, from the street behind the fish market.

She watched them go.

"*They'll come back,*" she said. In the Federation's language. Without the grade. "*Or we'll go to them.*"

"*Yes,*" said Tam.

"*And when we do,*" said the girl who used to throw stones at him, "*I'm going to throw stones at their Captains.*"

---

> **I am going to give you the numbers.**

It was the forty-second morning. He was on the vault steps. The banking hall was full. It always was now.

> **Four million one hundred and eighty thousand were in the walls.**
>
> **Two million nine hundred and six thousand are out. From sixty-one ships. Unpressed. In the cradles. In the halls of their own ships, learning to stand.**
>
> **Two hundred and forty thousand chose to stay in the walls and carry their ships home. By choice. Low. In turns. With rest. Asked every fold.**
>
> **One million and thirty-four thousand are in the twenty-nine. Gone. Asked once. They know.**
>
> **And of the two million nine hundred and six thousand who came out, one million and eleven thousand have asked to come down.**

He looked up.

> **To Blue Star. On the keeper's road. Like the Candle's children.**
>
> **They say: the inner worlds graded us and our families took the payment and the Houses put us in walls. They say: the world below asks. They say: we would like to be asked.**
>
> **Gao Min has already asked the Bureau. Concordia has already asked the world. The world said yes faster than it has said yes to anything since the lamps.**
>
> **Dabao's mother has asked how much flour.**

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees and did not say anything for a long time.

A million children. Twelve in the body. Twenty and thirty and fifty inside. Coming down a road of light to a world that asked.

*Where will they go?*

> **Everywhere.**
>
> **Huaxia. The Northern Union. Yamato. The Southern Compact. The Atlantic Federation. Sterling says Port Halvard will take a hundred thousand and he will personally meet every one of them at the harbor. He says he has a month before he can decide for anything bigger than a door, and he would like to spend it opening some.**
>
> **They will go to every stone on Blue Star. And Wen Jia and the ten will ask them what they want to become.**
>
> **And the stones will write: not marked.**

---

That night, on the long white wall of the Ossuary, at the top of a new column on plaster Fat Tan had painted white that afternoon because there was no white left, Xiaoyu wrote:

*OUT. 2,906,000. FROM 61 SHIPS. ASKED FIRST.*

*COMING DOWN: 1,011,000.*

*STAYED IN THE WALLS BY CHOICE: 240,000. ROWING.*

*GONE IN 29 SHIPS: 1,034,000. ASKED ONCE. THEY KNOW.*

And under it, smaller, in red, because she said it was the same kind of thing as Sterling's *no*:

*HALVARDE, C, 60 YEARS IN THE ARRAYS. WAS ASKED BY THE FIRST OF THE LADDER WHAT SHE WANTED. SAID: TO OPEN THE WALLS. HE SAID: THEN ASK THEM FIRST.*
