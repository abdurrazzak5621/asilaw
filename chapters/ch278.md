## Chapter 278: The Lamp in the Lens

> **The Lens is a stone.**
>
> **The oldest one. The first that fell, on the first human world, ten thousand years ago. Every other stone in every human orchard was cut from it, or from stones cut from it, or from stones cut from those. The Federation does not know this. I think the House of Severin does.**
>
> **It is not a stone like ours. It does not read one child at a time with a hand on it. It was ground into a lens so that it could read a whole world at once. Everyone. Everything that breathes. In one night.**
>
> **It reads everything at once because it is the root. Every stone cut from it is, a little, still part of it. Every cell in every wall of every ship. Every face on every chain round every throat in the Federation.**
>
> **When it reads, it reads through all of them.**

He sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees, and the banking hall was full, and nobody said anything.

> **On the ninth night, the Fleet Marshal will turn it on Blue Star and it will read eight billion people and mark them.**
>
> **Unless there is someone in it.**
>
> **Wen Jia went into our stone and it stopped reading and started asking. The Old Chord will go into the stones of the Hall of Throats.**
>
> **If someone goes into the Lens, then on the ninth night, when the Fleet Marshal turns it on Blue Star, it will not read.**
>
> **It will ask.**
>
> **Eight billion people. At once. What do you want to become.**

Gao Min had put her thermos down.

> **And because it is the root. Because every cell in every wall of every ship is cut from it. Whoever is in the Lens could ask them too.**
>
> **All of them. At once. Through the layer that stops their ears. Not as a sound. As a mark. The way Seri read Tam's.**
>
> **Four million one hundred and eighty thousand. In one night.**
>
> **That is the idea.**

---

"*And whoever goes in,*" said Su Mingli. Flatly. "*Doesn't come back.*"

> **No.**
>
> **The Lens is in the bow of the Severin flagship. Whoever goes into it will be in the Fleet Marshal's ship, in his House's oldest treasure, in the heart of the Ladder. When he understands what has happened, he will try to take them out. Or break the Lens. I do not know if he can. Nobody has ever tried to break the first stone.**
>
> **Wen Jia can leave her stone. The door is always a little open. She has only to ask.**
>
> **Whoever goes into the Lens may not be able to reach the door. They will be very far away, in a ship that folds, and I do not know where the ship will go.**
>
> **They may be in the Lens for a very long time.**
>
> **I do not want it to be anyone.**

"*It has to be someone,*" said Lingyue, from the stairs. "*Or two billion people get collected.*"

> **I know.**
>
> **I still do not want it to be anyone.**

---

"*Who?*" said Yuanshu.

> **Someone on the net. Someone who can go along the threads, sideways, from our stone into the Lens. The way the eleven went into the stones.**
>
> **Someone who is not alive. Or not alive the way you are. The living cannot go into a stone. The base layer can. The dead can, I think. Minds that are kept, or were.**
>
> **And someone who can ask. Not just sit in a knot and hold. Ask. Eight billion people at once. And four million in the walls. All of them in one night.**
>
> **Someone who is very good at talking.**

He went very still.

> **I did not want to say it.**
>
> **I have not asked him. I am not going to ask him. I am going to tell you who I thought of, and then you decide whether anyone asks him, and who.**
>
> **He has been quiet for nine hundred years. He came home six months ago. His sister lit a lamp for him on the shore.**
>
> **He talks to fish.**

---

He flew to Yamato at dawn.

Alone. He told Lingyue he wanted to ask it himself, and she looked at him for a long moment from the stairs with Xiaodeng asleep against her chest, and then said *yes, obviously, go*, and then *come back by tonight or I'm coming after you with the baby and the sword and you'll have to explain that to Bai Lu*. Old Seven did not ask. He was on the north wall when Yuanshu got there, at the top of the Sector Nine stairs, facing east, and he did not get on any road. He said, through Seed, that he would wait. He said this was not a thing a captain could go out ahead of the line for. He said: *go and ask the fog-ringer. Tell him I said he was always too fast in the middle.*

He came down at Shiomi bay at noon.

The fog was on the water. It always was now. White, still, standing off the end of the old stone breakwater from the point to the shore, the way a fog does not stand in the noon sun. The fishermen of the town had stopped minding it. They went out round it. They said it was good luck; they said the fish had been better since it came.

And on the end of the breakwater, sitting cross-legged on the stones with a lamp in her lap, as she had sat every day and every night for six months, Sister Kiyo of Shiomi.

She did not look up when he landed.

> **She says: you think very loudly. You thought loudly all the way across the sea.**
>
> **She says: he heard you coming. He has been talking since dawn. He says he knows what you want.**
>
> **She says: sit down. You're making the fog nervous.**

---

He sat down on the stones beside her.

The lamp in her lap burned pale and steady, the color of whale oil in a fog. Lingyue's light, held in the dry cupped hands of a nun of twelve hundred years. The fog stood round them, white, close. And in it, the small dim lights that were a village, three hundred and eleven, home.

*Ichiro,* he thought. *I came to tell you something. And then I'm going to ask you. Or not. I don't know yet.*

> **He says: you came to ask me to go into the Lens.**
>
> **He says: Tam told the Candle's children and the Candle's children told the city and the city told Ren and Ren told the monks and the monks told Kiyo and Kiyo told me. He says news goes faster than the net. He says it always did, in Shiomi.**
>
> **He says: yes.**

*I haven't asked.*

> **He says: I know. I'm saying it anyway. So you don't have to.**
>
> **He says: you asked the walker. You asked the rings. You asked the tree. You asked a thousand children who only knew one word. You asked a lady from the Ladder whether she'd asked her daughter. You asked a stone.**
>
> **He says: you don't have to ask me. I'm telling you first. That's your rule. I learned it. I'm good at rules when they're about talking.**

He sat on the stones and did not say anything for a long time.

*It's in his ship,* he thought. *The Fleet Marshal's. In the bow. You'd be in the heart of the Ladder. He'd try to get you out. Or break it. And the ship folds. And I don't know where it would go. You might never reach the door. You might be in the Lens for a thousand years.*

> **He says: I was in a column for three hundred. And the deep water for nine hundred. Quiet. He says you have no idea how quiet. Him. Quiet. For nine hundred years.**
>
> **He says: a thousand years in a Lens, talking to everyone at once? He says that sounds like the best job anyone ever had.**

---

Sister Kiyo had not said anything.

She sat on the stones with the lamp in her lap and her paper face turned to the fog, and listened to her brother, and did not say anything, and he understood that she had known since dawn, since Tam told the Candle's children, since before. That she had been sitting on the end of the breakwater for six months with a lamp in her lap, beside the brother she had waited twelve hundred years for, knowing that he would never in his life be able to stay quiet when there was something to say.

*Sister,* he said.

> **She says: he talks too much.**
>
> **She says: he always did. When he was small, he talked to the fish. When he was taken, he talked to himself in the column for three hundred years so he wouldn't forget how. When everyone in the deep water said be quiet, he rang the bell anyway, because he saw a keeper learn to ask.**
>
> **She says: he is the only person in Shiomi who cannot ring a boat call properly.**
>
> **She says: of course it's him.**

Her dry hands moved on the lamp.

> **She says: one condition.**

*Anything.*

> **She says: I am coming too.**

---

He did not understand at first. Then he did.

> **She says: a Lens is a thing for seeing. He is going to sit in it and talk to eight billion people and four million in the walls. He will talk all night. He will not stop. He never stops.**
>
> **She says: someone should hold a lamp.**
>
> **She says: so that when they are asked, in the dark, in the walls, with their ears stopped, they can see where the shore is.**
>
> **She says: that was always my job. I sat on the shore and held the lamp and he rowed in. Every night. For twenty-two years. Until the night he didn't.**
>
> **She says: I am not sitting on the shore again while he goes out.**

She lifted the lamp in her dry cupped hands. Lingyue's light. Pale. Steady. Held.

> **She says: I have been dead for twelve hundred years. I am not afraid of a ship.**
>
> **She says: ask Lingyue if I may take her light. It is hers. I am only holding it.**

---

He called Lingyue from the breakwater. She answered on the first ring; she had been holding the phone.

He told her. All of it. Ichiro. The Lens. Kiyo. The lamp.

She did not say anything for a long time. On the other end, very small, Xiaodeng made a sound, and he heard Lingyue's hand settle on her back.

"*Tell her yes,*" said Shen Lingyue. "*Tell her it was always hers. I just lit it.*" A pause. "*Tell her: make it bright enough that they can see it. But not like the wall. Not everything at once. Just enough.*"

"*She knows.*"

"*Tell her anyway,*" said his wife. "*So it's said.*"

> **She says: thank you.**
>
> **She says: tell Lingyue she held it up for nine hours for a thousand children of the Hollow Bell and then she couldn't put it down and then the baby came. Tell her the whole of Yamato felt it, under the gates. Tell her the monks woke up.**
>
> **She says: tell her I will not hold it for nine hours. I will hold it for as long as it takes.**

---

The headman's bell rang at dusk.

Once. The slow deep one. The bell that had told three hundred and eleven people to be quiet nine hundred years ago in the deep water, when gleaners came looking for what had fallen.

And then every bell in Shiomi rang. Not the warning. Not the name. Not the boat calls.

Something else. Ichiro said, through Seed, that it had not been rung since before the cutting. That it had no name. That it was the bells the shore rang when a boat went out that was not coming back. Not grief. Not quite. The sound a village makes on a shore when it knows exactly where one of its boats is going, and why, and is proud.

> **He says: they're ringing me out.**
>
> **He says: he never thought they would. He always thought he'd be the one ringing someone else out. He says he was going to be very good at it.**
>
> **He says: tell the Fleet Marshal I'm coming. Tell him I'm going to talk to everyone on his ship. Tell him I'm going to talk to his engines and his crew and his Lens and his House, and I'm not going to stop, and he'd better get comfortable.**
>
> **He says: tell Old Seven I know I was always too fast in the middle.**
>
> **He says: tell him this time I'm going to take it slow.**

---

> **Four days.**
>
> **They will go along the threads on the eighth night. From our stone. Through Wen Jia. Into the net. Across. Into the Lens.**
>
> **Ichiro. And Kiyo. And a lamp.**
>
> **I have run it. I do not know if it will work. I have never run anything with a nun and a fog-ringer and a lamp in it.**
>
> **I would like to stop being the one who does not know.**
>
> **I would like it to work.**
