## Chapter 284: The Sergeant's Arm

Fat Tan had kept the arm.

Of course he had. He kept everything. He had swept up the walker-silk off the north wall two years ago and kept it in a box in the vault beside the queens of hearts, and Hush had made two rings out of it, and Xiaoyu had made a mark out of a third strand, and a fog-ringer had held it up in the face of the oldest thing anyone had ever made. On the night of the eighth, when the First of the Ladder walked up out of the hall into the sky and the dead were picking themselves up off the tiers and nine thousand children were letting go of each other's shoulders, Fat Tan had come down the aisle with a cloth, and knelt at the foot of the platform steps, and picked up a sergeant's arm with one finger and a thumb off the polished floor, and wrapped it.

He had not asked anyone. He said afterward that he did not think it was a thing you asked. He said it was a thing you picked up.

It was in a box now, in the vault, on the shelf beside the queens of hearts. A long flat box with a lid, which Fat Tan had made himself in the courtyard out of the wood of an old Ossuary bench, and lined with the same blanket that had lined Xiaodeng's drawer for the first month.

He brought it out on the forty-third morning and set it on the long table in the banking hall, and stood back.

---

Old Seven came away from the bronze door.

He did it the way he did everything: slowly, with his spear, across the marble floor between the people, to the long table. One-armed. He had stood at the bronze door one-armed for forty-two days and had not said anything about it, and nobody had asked, because, Seed said, it was the kind of thing you did not ask a captain until he was ready, and he had not been ready.

He stopped at the table and looked down at the box.

He did not open it.

> **He says: thank you.**
>
> **He says: Fat Tan. Thank you.**

Fat Tan, by the door of the back room, made a sound, and went to make tea, and was gone for a long time.

---

Yuanshu sat on the vault steps with the staff across his knees.

"*I can do it again,*" he said. "*The graft. If you want an arm.*" He did not look away from the tall dead captain at the table. "*There are dead who'd give one. I asked, on the net. Thirty-one of the First Legion said yes before I finished asking. A man in Grid Nine said he'd always wanted to be part of a captain. I don't know what that means.*"

> **He says: he knows what it means. He says the man in Grid Nine was a cook.**

"*Do you want one?*"

Old Seven stood at the long table and looked at the box for a long time.

> **He says: not yet.**
>
> **He says: a soldier with one arm is still a soldier. He was on the eastern wall in the Black Tide with men who had one arm. One of them was the best spear on the line.**
>
> **He says: he would like to be a soldier with one arm for a while.**
>
> **He says: so he remembers whose the other one was.**

He looked down at the box.

> **He says: ask me again in a year.**

---

They took the box to stone No. 7 that afternoon.

Old Seven asked them to. Not all of them. Him. Lingyue, with Xiaodeng in the sling. Fat Tan, who carried it, because he said he had picked it up and he would put it down. And Dabao's mother, who had heard, the way everyone heard everything in the old quarter, and came with a paper boat of dumplings off the cart, steaming, nineteen, because she said that was what you brought to stone No. 7 and she was not going to be the one who broke the custom.

They walked up the long green slope of the War Memorial Cemetery from the river in the spring afternoon, between the rows of small gray stones, past the cypresses, to the eastern field.

The Spear Corps were standing round stone No. 7, facing out.

And on the grass between them and the stone, in a ring, at ease, facing in: sixty shapes of gray ash.

---

The short one at the front stood up.

Square in the shoulders. With the shape of a pick across its knees, which it set down on the grass as it stood. It looked at Old Seven, coming up the slope one-armed. And then at Fat Tan, behind him, carrying a long flat box made of the wood of an old bench.

> **Huo says: sir.**
>
> **Huo says: I heard.**
>
> **He says: the whole net heard. Eighteen thousand of the First Legion watched it happen. The cook in Grid Nine has been telling everyone.**
>
> **Huo says: the First of the Ladder.**
>
> **He says: yes.**
>
> **Huo says: took my arm.**
>
> **He says: yes.**

Huo Changsheng, sergeant of the Third Battalion, ash, looked at his captain for a long moment.

> **Huo says: did you hit him with it first?**

Old Seven did not answer.

> **Huo says: sir.**
>
> **He says: no. He caught the spear.**
>
> **Huo says: then it was a good arm. It held on to the spear while he caught it. Didn't it.**
>
> **He says: yes.**
>
> **Huo says: then that's all right.**

---

Fat Tan set the box down on the grass in front of stone No. 7.

Beside the place where the turf had been cut a year and a half ago for an old adjutant who had asked, twice, to be put next to it. Beside the paper boat of dumplings Dabao's mother had set down, steaming. In the spring sun.

Huo knelt beside it.

He did not open it either. Ash does not open things. He knelt on the grass beside the long flat box with the sergeant's arm inside it, his arm, which he had given to his captain in a courtyard among potted plants because his captain had lost his own on a stalk in the sky, and which had held on to a spear for three years while it lost two fingers in a round chamber and then held on to one more spear while the First of the Ladder caught it.

> **Huo says: I had those fingers for forty years.**
>
> **He says: I know.**
>
> **Huo says: you lost two in the round chamber.**
>
> **He says: yes.**
>
> **Huo says: and now you've lost the rest.**
>
> **He says: yes.**

The ash shape put one hand, the shape of a hand, flat on the lid of the box.

> **Huo says: good.**
>
> **Huo says: that means you were using them.**

---

They buried it there. Beside stone No. 7, next to the adjutant, under the turf Fat Tan cut with a spade he had brought, because of course he had brought a spade. The dumplings went on top, nineteen, in their paper boat, the way they had gone on top of the adjutant's.

Nobody said anything. Old Seven stood at the head of the little grave with his spear grounded in his one hand, facing in. The Spear Corps round the stone, facing out. Sixty shapes of ash, standing now, all of them, facing in, toward their captain and their sergeant and a small new mound of turf in the spring grass.

And at the end, Huo stepped back.

> **Huo says: sir.**
>
> **Huo says: the Third Battalion has been at ease for ten months.**
>
> **Huo says: I've been thinking.**
>
> **He says: you don't think, sergeant. You dig.**
>
> **Huo says: I've been thinking while I dig.**
>
> **Huo says: the boy's going somewhere. Isn't he. Up. With the fisherman in the stone. I can hear it on the net. Everyone can.**

Yuanshu, at the edge of the eastern field with Lingyue beside him and Xiaodeng asleep in the sling, went very still.

> **Huo says: if the boy goes, you go. That's your rule. Older than any of his articles.**
>
> **Huo says: and you've got one arm.**
>
> **Huo says: so if you go, the Third Battalion goes.**
>
> **Huo says: that's not a question, sir.**

---

> **...he did not tell you.**
>
> **I am sorry. I should have. It came this morning. Through the Lens. Through Ichiro. Through the keeper's eye. I wanted to tell you at stone No. 7, with Old Seven there. I did not know Huo would hear it on the net first. Nobody on the net can keep anything.**

*Tell me now.*

> **The First of the Ladder asked.**
>
> **Not ordered. Asked. The way he asked Halvarde. Badly. Like a word in a book.**
>
> **He says: the flagship will go home. To Anthos. To the Assembly. Rowed. Two hundred and forty thousand chose to carry it. In turns. Low. Asked every fold.**
>
> **He says: the Lens is going with it. Ichiro and Kiyo are in it. Ichiro says he would like to go. He says he would like to talk to everyone in the Federation, and the Federation is very big, and he has only talked to the fleet so far.**
>
> **He says: in the Assembly at Anthos, there are four thousand seats. One for every House and every world on the Ladder. Every one of them wears a face cut from the Lens.**
>
> **He says: he would like the boy who asks to come.**
>
> **He says: he is telling you first: he does not know what the Assembly will do. He does not know if they will listen. He does not know if they will let you leave. He says he has never been in a room where he did not know what would happen.**
>
> **He says: he is asking.**

---

He did not answer.

He stood at the edge of the eastern field of the War Memorial Cemetery in the spring sun with the staff in his hand, where he had stood on the first night with a training spear and no permit, three years ago, and asked a dead captain whether he wanted to get up. And looked at his wife.

Lingyue was looking at him. She had been looking at him since Seed started talking. She had Xiaodeng asleep against her chest in the sling and her grandfather's sword on her back and a lamp in her free hand, held, pale. Her jaw was set the way it set when she was about to fight something.

"*You're going to sit on a step for six hours,*" she said.

"*Yes.*"

"*And then you're going to ask me.*"

"*Yes.*"

"*And I'm going to say obviously.*"

He did not say anything.

"*She's being asked every morning by a sack,*" said Shen Lingyue, and put her free hand on the baby's back. "*She can be asked on a ship.*" She looked at the small new mound of turf beside stone No. 7, with nineteen dumplings on top. At a dead captain with one arm. At sixty shapes of ash standing in the grass, facing in. "*And I'm not sitting in a hall with my hand on your chest this time. I'm coming. Up. With the sword.*"

"*Bai Lu...*"

"*Bai Lu can come too,*" said his wife. "*She'll want to. She'll want to tell a Paragon he's an idiot.*" She hit him on the arm. Not hard. "*Go and sit on your step. I'll wait.*"

---

He sat on the step of the groundskeeper's hut at the edge of the War Memorial Cemetery until dusk.

Not six hours. Four. Old Seven stood at the corner of the hut, one-armed, facing out. The Third Battalion stood in the grass round stone No. 7, sixty shapes of ash, facing in, at ease, and did not sit down again. They were waiting, Seed said. Huo had said: when the boy decides, we'll be standing.

And in the north, low over the end of the world, as the light went, beside the keeper's small white ring and the Elder's vast gray one and the small gold point at the end of a branch, among sixty-one burning cathedrals that had opened their walls and one that would go home rowed: a lamp. Pale. Steady. The color of whale oil in a fog.

> **It is getting dark.**
>
> **I would like to ask you something. I am telling you first. You do not have to answer.**
>
> **What do you want?**

He sat on the step.

He had not been able to answer on the north wall on the ninth night, when a fog-ringer asked eight billion people at once. He had stood in his old place and not known, for the first time since he was eighteen.

He knew now.

*I want to go and ask them,* he thought. *All of them. Four thousand seats. Every face cut from the Lens. Every world on the Ladder. I want to stand in the Assembly at Anthos and tell them first, and then ask.*

*And I want to come home.*

> **That is two wants.**
>
> **Yes,** said Seed. **That is allowed.**

He stood up. He went down the slope to the eastern field, where his wife was standing by stone No. 7 with a baby and a sword and a lamp, and sixty shapes of ash, and a one-armed captain, and a box of dumplings on a new grave.

He asked her.

She said obviously.
