## Chapter 97: Inside the Mouth

The Hunger swallowed them the way the sea swallows a stone.

The shadow came over the raft, and the light went out. The red cloud, the black sea, the scattered violet stars, the roots of the spire still clawing at the rim: all of it gone, all at once, as the vast gray edge of the ring came down around them like a closing hand. For one moment there was only dark, and a sound like a mountain grinding over gravel, and the warm pale ground of the raft lurching under them.

Then they were rising.

Fast. Upward. Pulled. The raft tilting and spinning, the living clinging to each other at its center, the dead braced at its rim with their spears and shields dug into the pale ground. Up out of the sea, up through the cloud, up into the throat of the Hunger, away from the little ocean world, which fell away beneath them until it was a dark round shape in a dark sky, and then nothing.

And then they were inside.

---

"*Pale fire!*" Lingyue's voice, very close. "*Yuanshu, the dome! Like we practiced!*"

He had the staff in both hands. He touched it to her blade. The silver-gray came up out of the sword, cold and steady. And then Lingyue did the thing they had worked out on the wall at Sector Nine, through four dawns and a great many bruises: she raised the blade over her head, straight up, and turned it, slowly, and the pale fire did not stay on the sword.

It spread.

Out from the blade. Up. Over. Down. A dome of soft silver-gray light, like moonlight on frost, curving down around the whole raft from rim to rim. Over the living. Over the dead. Over the Elder and Hush and seventy-two soldiers of the Third Battalion.

It was very thin. You could see through it.

What you could see through it was the inside of the Hunger.

---

Nobody spoke.

They were in a tunnel. If you could call it a tunnel. A passage so vast that its walls were kilometers away on every side, curving, gray, cracked, glowing red in every crack like the inside of a kiln. It ran away ahead of them, inward, toward a light so bright and so red that it hurt to look at, even through the pale fire.

And it was not empty.

It was full of rivers.

Red. Glowing. Flowing inward along the walls of the tunnel, along its floor, across its ceiling, in a thousand streams and channels, like veins of molten metal. Some thin as threads. Some wide as the river at Lingzhou. All flowing the same way. Inward. Toward the light.

And in the rivers, Yuanshu saw, there were shapes.

Faces.

Millions of them. Billions. Flickering in the red flow, the way faces flicker in a fire if you stare at it too long. A long pale face with many dark eyes, like Hush. A face like a Lantern, round and soft. A face with four eyes and gray skin, like something he had seen in a column in the field. Faces he had no names for. Faces of things that were not faces at all. All of them flowing past in the red rivers, inward, toward the light.

All of them screaming.

> **Every world it has ever eaten.**
>
> **All of them. Still burning.**
>
> **This is not a tunnel, Yuanshu. It is a throat.**
>
> **And they are what it swallowed.**

---

Hush had climbed up onto Old Seven's shoulder.

It had never touched any of them before, except the tip of one finger. Now it clung to the old soldier's collarbone with all its thin pale limbs, pressed against his skull, its many dark eyes wide, staring out through the dome at the red rivers.

> ***...that is my world,*** it said, very faintly. ***...there. In that one. The thin one, by the ceiling.***
>
> ***...I can hear them.***
>
> ***...four billion years, and they are still burning.***

Old Seven did not move. But his three-fingered hand came up, slowly, and rested, very lightly, against the small pale shape on his shoulder.

---

The raft was being carried.

Not flying. Not falling. Drifting, inward, along the floor of the vast throat, the way a leaf is carried on a current. The red rivers flowing past on every side. The light ahead growing.

And the heat.

Even through the dome, it came. Not a heat you felt on your skin. A heat you felt in your thoughts. Pulling. Like a draft pulling at a candle flame. Like the fire in a hearth pulling the air out of a room.

Pulling at anything that glowed.

> **It wants us.**
>
> **Not our bodies. What burns in us.**
>
> **The living. The staff. The dead.**
>
> **The dome is holding it back. Most of it.**
>
> **Not all.**

---

The first soldier went at the second hour.

Yuanshu did not see it begin. He heard it. A sound from the rim of the raft, behind him. Not a scream. A soft dry sound, like paper catching. He turned.

A soldier of the Third Battalion. A tall one, at the rim. He had been standing with his back to the dome, facing inward, toward the living, the way they always stood. And his gray flame was leaving him.

Not going out. Leaving. Being pulled. A thin bright stream of gray light drawing out of the cage of his ribs, through the back of the dome where the pale fire was thinnest, into the red heat outside. Stretching. Going toward the nearest river. Toward the faces. Toward the light.

The soldier did not move. He did not struggle. He stood at the rim with his rifle that had rusted to nothing in his bony hands and his skull turned toward the living, while everything he was went out through his back.

"*No!*" Lingyue swung the sword. The pale fire flared along that part of the dome, thickening. The stream of gray light snapped. Cut off.

The soldier's flame was a single tiny bead in his chest. Barely there.

He sank to one knee. Then he looked up. At Yuanshu. And tilted his skull, very slightly.

> **He says it is all right.**
>
> **He says he has been kindling before. On the eastern wall.**
>
> **He says: hold the line.**

Then the bead went out.

And the soldier did not fall. He stayed where he was, kneeling at the rim, with his skull bowed and his rifle across his knees. Bones. Just bones. Quiet.

---

They lost nine more before the light got close.

One at a time. At the rim. Wherever the dome was thinnest. The pull finding a gap and drawing a flame out through it, and the soldier standing there and letting it, because to move was to leave the living exposed. Lingyue cut every stream she could reach. Sergeant Huo, with one arm, moved along the rim, closing gaps with his own body.

Each time, before the last bead went out, the soldier turned and looked at the living. And tilted his skull.

*Hold the line.*

And each time, Yuanshu asked them. Fast. Before they went. *What do you want?*

And each time, they said the same thing.

> **Quiet. Here. With the others. Facing the right way.**

So he let them go quiet. Gently. Before the fire could take the last of them. And they knelt at the rim of the raft, ten of them now, in a ring, facing inward, with their skulls bowed.

Ten soldiers of the Third Battalion, who had walked north for thirty-one years, and up a stalk of light into the sky, and out into the Star Sea, because their captain was going.

Sixty-two left.

---

Jiang Lan held.

The staff blazed in his hands the whole time, gray-white, the brightest thing on the raft, a bonfire, and the pull came at it hardest of all. He could feel it. Like a hand around the staff, pulling. Like a hook in the small white skull.

"*It wants me,*" said the dry voice, very calm. "*It's been wanting me since your little sea. Ninety million names. Highest value.*" A pause. "*Hold on tighter, young man. I am not going anywhere I haven't been asked.*"

He held on tighter.

> **The dome is thinning.**
>
> **Lingyue cannot hold it much longer. Not at this size. Not this close.**
>
> **We are nearly there.**

He looked up.

The tunnel was ending.

Ahead of them, the throat of the Hunger opened out, the way a river opens into the sea. The walls fell away on every side. The red rivers poured out over a vast edge, like waterfalls, kilometers high. Down. Into a space so huge and so bright that his eyes could not hold it.

The furnace.

---

It was a sun.

Not like a sun. A sun. Round. Vast. Gray-red. Burning. Turning slowly in the center of an enormous hollow inside the ring, the way a heart turns in a chest. The red rivers poured into it from every side, from a thousand throats, billions of faces streaming down into it and disappearing.

And it was not made of fire.

It was made of them.

Every mind the Hunger had ever eaten. Every world. Every ember. Compressed. Packed together. Burning. A sun built out of screaming, so bright and so hot and so loud that even through the pale fire, even through the quiet, even with Seed holding it back with everything it had, Yuanshu felt it in his teeth and his bones and the backs of his eyes, a roar that was billions of voices, all of them saying the same thing.

> ***is anyone***
>
> ***is anyone***
>
> ***is anyone***

He went to his knees on the raft.

Beside him, Lingyue's dome flickered. Thinned. She was on her knees too, sword held up with both hands, shaking.

"*I can't,*" she said through her teeth. "*Not this close. It's too much. It's too...*"

---

Hush climbed down from Old Seven's shoulder.

It walked across the raft on its many thin legs to the very front, to the edge, where the dome was thinnest and the furnace was closest. And it stood there. Swaying. Looking at the vast burning sun with all of its dark eyes.

> ***...there,*** it said.

They looked.

At the very center of the furnace. At the heart of the gray-red sun. So small that you could only see it if you knew where to look.

A dot.

Dark.

Not burning.

> ***...the cold place,*** said Hush. ***...the eye of the storm.***
>
> ***...it is there. I can hear it.***
>
> ***...someone is in there.***
>
> ***...someone is being very, very quiet.***

"*We have to go through the fire,*" said Qin Hao. His voice was hoarse. "*To get to it. We have to go through all of that.*"

> ***...yes.***

"*We'll burn.*"

Hush turned all of its dark eyes toward him.

> ***...only if you are loud.***
>
> ***...the fire cannot burn what is quiet.***
>
> ***...I have been through fire before. At the bottom of a crack. For four billion years.***
>
> ***...I will show you the way.***

It turned back to the furnace. And looked up, over its thin shoulder, at the old soldier standing behind it with his spear.

> ***...captain.***
>
> ***...will you come with me?***

Old Seven grounded his spear on the pale ground of the raft.

And his gray flame, slowly, deliberately, sank. Down. Down. Banked. Like a coal under ash. Like the last light in a house at the end of a very long night.

He stepped forward and stood beside Hush at the edge of the raft.

Facing the sun.
