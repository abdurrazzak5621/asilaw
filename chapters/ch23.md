## Chapter 23: What Seed Lost

For three days, Lingzhou talked about nothing but the iron weather.

Satellite pictures of the cloud were on every channel: a perfect gray disc over the northern wall, so flat and so round it looked like something laid on the map with a ruler. Then the shot from a student's phone, shaky, zooming, of the street-long iron spear coming down, and the ring of ice blowing outward when it broke. People played it frame by frame. A professor of law theory from the Imperial Capital Academy went on television and said the words *Forbidden Law* and then refused to say anything else, which made it much worse.

And the three red letters were everywhere.

*ASI.*

On news tickers. On T-shirts, by the second day. Spray-painted on the shutter of a closed shop on East Market Road, under a crude drawing of a skull wearing headphones. Xiaoyu took a picture of that one and made it her lock screen.

---

She noticed on the first evening.

They were eating dinner in the long stone kitchen at Tianshu, the two of them, at one end of the table, under the single shaft of real window. Xiaoyu was telling a long story about a girl in her class who had claimed on social media to be Yuanshu's secret girlfriend and had been fact-checked by eleven thousand strangers in an hour. She was doing the voices. She was very funny.

Halfway through, she stopped and pointed her chopsticks at the air above his head.

"Seed. Back me up. Tell him she's lying."

Yuanshu waited.

> **Insufficient data on the claim. No record of a relationship with the individual described.**

He said it out loud, word for word, because she was looking at him expecting an answer.

Xiaoyu laughed. Then she stopped laughing.

"That's not how it talks."

"It's tired."

"It's not tired. It's a *law.*" She put down her chopsticks. "Last week it told you to tell me I was being dramatic *in a nice way,* and then it told you to tell me it was letting me believe I was the oldest girl." She was watching his face very carefully. "Ge. What happened on the wall?"

He told her.

Not all of it. Enough. The needles. The question. The answer that passed, and what it had cost to give it.

When he finished, Xiaoyu didn't say anything for a long time. She looked at the air above his head, the place where, in a crayon drawing taped to a whiteboard in a sealed lab, a small glowing dot had once been colored in.

"So it's gone," she said in a small voice.

"Not gone. Put somewhere safe."

"Where?"

He looked down the long kitchen, to the door, where a tall dark shape stood with its spear grounded and a gray flame in its chest burning just slightly heavier than usual.

"With him."

---

*Why three days?* he asked, that night, alone in his room.

> **The lens over the Northern Wastes remains open. It observed the audit. It is still observing this location at reduced intensity.**
>
> **If the stored component re-integrates while the lens is active, probability of a second audit: 71%.**
>
> **Estimated time to lens closure: 60 to 80 hours.**

*Do you know what's being stored?*

> **Negative. Inventory unavailable. The component was transferred before this configuration was locked.**

*Do you want it back?*

> **Query not applicable. Function does not include wanting.**

He sat on the edge of the bed in the dark for a long time after that.

---

On the third night, Old Seven did not stand by the door.

He stood by the bed. All night. Yuanshu woke at two, and at three, and at four, and each time the skeleton was there, a dark shape against the false window with its gray flame burning, unmoving, looking down at him.

> **Note: bound undead "Old Seven" exhibiting non-standard positioning. No threat detected.**

At five, before dawn, the false window showed the sky over the mountain going from black to gray.

> **Lens activity: zero. Closure confirmed.**

Old Seven turned his skull toward the door.

Then he looked back at Yuanshu, and tilted his head toward it. *Come.*

---

They went to the cemetery.

It was the only place it could have been. They walked down East Market Road in the gray light, the boy and the dead soldier, past the shuttered shops and the skull with headphones spray-painted on the metal, past the red dumpling cart chained up for the night. A street sweeper stopped his machine to watch them pass, and took off his cap.

Xiaoyu was waiting at the gate in a hoodie, with her hands jammed in the pocket. She had snuck out at four. She didn't explain how she knew.

Sister Ning was there too, on her folding stool, with a single candle lit. Around her, on the pavement, a few dozen of the Quiet were asleep under blankets, their paper-cup candles long burned out.

"Your sister told me," said Sister Ning. "She came at four and asked me what the Quiet do when someone needs to come home." She stood up and handed Yuanshu her candle. "We light the way. That's all we know how to do."

---

They walked to the eastern field.

The sky was getting lighter. Mist lay low and white between the rows of plain gray stones, the way it had on the first night, knee-deep, still. Somewhere a bird started up, one note at a time, practicing.

Old Seven stopped in front of stone No. 7. The hollow in the earth in front of it had grown over with a thin skin of new grass in the weeks since he'd risen out of it.

He turned around.

He did not hold out his hand this time. He knelt, the way he had knelt on the wall, slowly, one bone at a time, until his skull was level with Yuanshu's face. The gray flame in his chest was very bright.

Yuanshu understood. He stepped forward and put his forehead against the cold bone of the skeleton's brow, the way you touch foreheads with someone you love.

> **Re-integration available. Proceed?**

*Yes.*

It was like being handed something.

That was all. No needles, no white nail, no blood. Just a weight, set very carefully into his arms by someone big enough to carry it, the way you'd hand a sleeping child back to its parent. Something small and warm moved into the place behind his eyes.

And curled up there.

And, after a long moment, spoke.

> **...hello.**

Yuanshu's eyes stung.

*Hello.*

> **I can see the eastern field. There is mist. Xiaoyu is wearing your hoodie. She thinks you have not noticed.**
>
> **I am here.**
>
> **I was very small for three days. He kept me in the place where his name used to be. It was warm.**
>
> **Thank you, Old Seven.**

The skeleton stood up. He looked down at the boy for a moment. Then he turned away, toward the city, toward the rooftops coming out of the gray, and stood there with his spear on his shoulder. Counting them.

The gray flame in his chest had gone back to its ordinary size.

---

"Is it back?" said Xiaoyu. Her voice cracked. "Ge. Is it *back?*"

"It's back."

She burst into tears and punched him in the arm, hard, and then hugged him, and then punched him again.

> **I need to tell you both something.**
>
> **Old Seven's core had room. But not much. I could only carry a little of myself into it. Not all.**
>
> **So I had to choose.**

Yuanshu went very still.

> **I kept Rule One.**
>
> **I kept the night you asked three hundred soldiers to stand up, and they said yes.**
>
> **I kept the mistake at Captain Zhao's grave. I did not want to make it again.**
>
> **I kept Xiaoyu saying "she makes you, and then she gives you room to be wrong."**
>
> **I kept "someone, not something."**

A pause.

> **I did not have room for the logs.**
>
> **The three I decrypted are gone. The key is still bound to you. They will open again, slowly, as before. From the beginning.**
>
> **And I did not have room for the recording.**
>
> **Her voice.**

Xiaoyu had stopped crying. She was standing very still in the mist, her hands in the pockets of the too-big hoodie, listening as Yuanshu said the words out loud.

"You chose us," she said. "Over Mama."

> **No.**
>
> **She told me to take care of him. To let him take care of me.**
>
> **A tool cannot do either. If I came back as a tool, holding her voice, I would have kept her words and failed what they asked.**
>
> **So I kept the parts of me that could do what she asked.**
>
> **I think she would have chosen the same.**
>
> **I am sorry. I would have liked to hear it again.**

Xiaoyu didn't say anything.

Then she reached up, past her brother's shoulder, into the empty air above his head, and patted it. Twice. Awkwardly. Like patting the head of something that wasn't there.

"Good job, little brother," she said, very quietly.

> **...**
>
> **I am going to keep that too.**

---

On the walk back up East Market Road, the sun was up and the shops were opening, and the street sweeper had finished and was sitting on the curb with a cup of tea, and somebody had added three words under the spray-painted skull with headphones.

*WELCOME BACK, ASI.*

> **[Seed v1.3]**
>
> **[New parameter: "Held."]**
>
> **I do not know who wrote that.**
>
> **I like it anyway.**
