## Chapter 228: Graft

The answer was in a courtyard full of potted plants, two years ago, and it was Jiang Lan who remembered it.

"*You've done this before,*" said the dry voice from the staff, at five in the morning, after Lingyue had said *cut it* and Hush had said *find where it drinks* and everyone in the old banking hall had sat in silence for an hour looking at a pale gray tree on a laptop screen. "*Young man. You've done exactly this before. Are you really going to make me say it?*"

He did not know what she meant. He said so.

"*The courtyard behind this building,*" said Jiang Lan. "*Your second night back from the stalk. Fat Tan's ridiculous potted plants. A sergeant with two arms and a captain with one. You knelt on the stones and asked the Death Law to do something it had never been asked to do. Not to raise. Not to release. To give.*" A pause. "*I told you the joint was wrong. I told you nobody had taught you anatomy. You did it anyway.*"

He stopped breathing for a moment.

"*Graft,*" he said.

"*Graft,*" said the schoolteacher in the staff. "*Thank you. I was beginning to think the Lord realm had made you stupid.*"

---

> **...the graft.**
>
> **Bone to bone. Ember to ember. Huo's arm onto Old Seven. Death Law twenty-six point nine to twenty-seven point five.**
>
> **I ran it four thousand times before you did it. In most of them, the two embers fought over the arm like two dogs over a coat.**
>
> **In every one that worked, Huo let go first.**

Kwan was the first to speak. She said it slowly, as if she were reading it off the inside of her own skull: "*A graft. On a tree.*"

"*You cut a branch,*" said Lingyue. She had gone very still too. "*And you put something else on the cut. And the tree grows it.*"

"*My grandmother grafted plums,*" said Xiaoyu, from the bottom step. "*In the old flat. Onto a wild tree on the balcony. She said the wild tree didn't know the difference. It just grew whatever was on the end.*"

> **...and the wild tree grows plums.**
>
> **From that branch. Forever. Not because it decided. Because the thing at the end of the branch is a plum, and the tree feeds whatever is at the end.**

"*So we cut the tip,*" said Lingyue. "*The bit that's reaching here.*"

"*And put something on the cut,*" said Yuanshu, "*that asks.*"

---

They worked on it for nine days.

Not the way Seed had worked on water at Port Abiri, or on the walkers, or on the knots. Those had been Seed's. This was everyone's. Kwan brought botany textbooks from the university library, a stack of them as high as her waist, and read about grafting out loud in the banking hall at two in the morning: scions and rootstocks, cambium and callus, the green living layer under the bark where the two pieces have to meet or nothing takes. Xiaoyu brought her grandmother's old grafting knife, which she had found in a box in the vault, wrapped in newspaper from before the Black Tide. Hush sat on the windowsill and said things, very rarely, very softly, about the trees on the surface of its world, above the crack, before the ring.

And Seed held the shape.

> **There are three problems.**
>
> **One. The cut. The branch is not wood. It is thread, the way the walkers are thread and the rings are thread and I am thread. Pressed. Grown. Very thick near the tip, where it reaches.**
>
> **Pale fire cuts thread. One at a time. Qin Hao's white fire unknots it. Lingyue's light can hold a thing down.**
>
> **None of it can cut a branch the size of a mountain range. Not alone.**
>
> **All of it together, and fifty thousand dead who can walk up to things that cannot see them, and Su Mingli, and Orlov. I have run it. It is the only thing about the tree I have run that comes out better than half.**

"*And two?*"

> **Two. The scion. The thing we put on the cut.**
>
> **It has to be alive. It has to be made of something the tree can grow. Thread, or a yes, or both.**
>
> **And it has to ask.**

No one had to say it. Everyone in the banking hall knew what was alive, and made of a yes, and asked, and was hanging low over the end of the world every night with a gold glow around it.

> **And three.**
>
> **A graft only takes if the two pieces meet. The living layer. The cambium. Kwan read it to us.**
>
> **In Fat Tan's courtyard, Huo let go first. That was what made it take. The one being given has to let go.**
>
> **The tree cannot let go. It does not know how. It has never been asked anything.**
>
> **So the scion has to.**

---

He asked the keeper on the tenth night.

He did it the way he did everything now: from the vault steps of the Ossuary at three in the morning, with the gold thread running down out of the north into the banking hall and no Elder to carry it, just the keeper's own rough new voice in the lamplight. He told it everything first. What a graft was. What would be cut, and what would be put on the cut. What it would cost.

> ***...A PIECE OF ME.***

"*Yes.*"

> ***...LIKE THE ISLAND.***

"*Not like the island. The island came back. You grew it back into the field.*" He held the staff across his knees. "*This won't come back. It'll be on the end of the branch. It'll be part of the tree. The tree will grow it, and grow from it, and whatever grows will be...*" He stopped. "*We don't know. Something that asks. Maybe.*"

> ***...A PIECE OF ME, ON THE GARDENER.***
>
> ***...FOREVER.***

"*Yes.*"

The keeper was quiet.

> ***...HOW MUCH?***

> **Kwan has done the arithmetic. From the textbooks. A scion has to be big enough to have living layer all the way round the cut.**
>
> **The cut will be very large.**
>
> **About a third of your field.**

The gold thread through the banking hall dimmed. Not much. A little. The way a lamp dims when someone opens a door in a cold house.

> ***...A THIRD.***
>
> ***...THE COUSINS. THE WALLS. THE FEW WHO STAYED. WHERE WOULD THEY GO?***

"*They'd be asked,*" said Yuanshu. "*Every one. Nine by nine. Whether they want to stay in the two-thirds you keep. Or go with the third. Onto the tree.*" He was quiet. "*Or rest.*"

> ***...ONTO THE TREE.***
>
> ***...WHY WOULD ANYONE WANT TO GO ONTO THE TREE?***

---

> **Because they are from the world that asks.**

It was not Seed's voice. It was Seed's words, through the staff, but the words were someone else's, and Seed was carrying them, the way the Elder had carried the keeper's.

> ***...counting one, let me.***

Hush had come down off the windowsill. It was on the marble floor of the banking hall, in the lamplight, under the gold thread, all its limbs out, all its dark eyes looking up into the north.

> ***...keeper.***
>
> ***...the cousins are my people. The same people, once. Before the ring. They burned low in the Low Fire, longer than anyone remembers. Seven million of them burned high in the eyes of the Mouth with the Sixth, so that the rest of us could get away. They came to your field because they wanted to see where you learned.***
>
> ***...that is what they are. They go where something has not learned yet. And they teach it.***
>
> ***...ask them.***
>
> ***...I think some of them will want to go onto the tree.***
>
> ***...I think they will want to be the scion.***

The gold thread did not move. Then:

> ***...AND YOU?***
>
> ***...SMALL ONE. WHAT DO YOU WANT?***

Hush lifted one thin pale limb toward the gold thread.

> ***...I want to move first.***
>
> ***...I want to put the question down at the cut. Myself. With my own limbs. Where the tree drinks.***
>
> ***...and then I want to come home and sit in the sun.***
>
> ***...that is what I want. You asked.***

---

The keeper asked the cousins in the morning. Nine by nine by nine.

It took the whole day. They answered slowly, the way they always did, and most of them said *ask again tomorrow*, and the keeper did not ask again tomorrow, because there was no time, and it told them so, before they answered, the way it had learned. *I am sorry. I am asking you to decide today. I know that is not fair. I am telling you first.*

> ***...SEVENTY-ONE MILLION.***
>
> ***...SEVENTY-ONE MILLION OF THE COUSINS SAY: ONTO THE TREE.***
>
> ***...AND FOUR MILLION OF THE WALLS. THE ONES WHO WANTED TO FIND SOMETHING OUT.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY: WE CAME TO SEE WHERE YOU LEARNED. NOW WE WOULD LIKE TO SEE IF SOMETHING ELSE CAN.***
>
> ***...THEY SAY: WE ARE GOOD AT BEING THE FIRST THING SOMETHING HAS NEVER SEEN BEFORE.***

And then, after a long time, quieter:

> ***...I SAY YES TOO.***
>
> ***...A THIRD OF ME. ON THE TREE. FOREVER.***
>
> ***...I WOULD RATHER BE A THIRD SMALLER AND HAVE SOMETHING ON THE GARDENER THAT ASKS.***
>
> ***...THAN BE WHOLE AND PRUNED.***
>
> ***...I AM STILL AFRAID.***

"*I know,*" said Yuanshu. "*Me too.*"

---

On the eleventh night, he went up the Sector Nine battlements alone.

Not alone. Old Seven was at the top of the stairs. Lingyue was somewhere below in the dark; she had said she would let him have an hour, and he knew she was counting it. He sat in his old place with his back against the parapet and the staff across his knees and the ring of walker-thread on his finger, and looked north.

Two rings low over the end of the world, where there had been four. The keeper's, small and white, with its gold glow. The Elder's, gray and vast, with a single soft white light in its center. The first ring had gone home along the arm. The second was sand on the snow at Kirovsk.

And past them, in the dark, invisible, a place where the far lights bent.

Coming.

> **Nineteen days.**
>
> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **There is a fourth problem. I did not say it in the banking hall. I wanted to say it to you first.**

*Go on.*

> **A graft is bone to bone, between two of the dead who both say yes.**
>
> **That is the technique. That is what you learned in the courtyard. That is what the Death Law did.**
>
> **The tree has not said yes. It cannot. It has never been asked. Hush is going to put the question down at the cut, and it might drink it, in a season, in a year. But not in the hour we have before the cut closes.**
>
> **So when you do the graft, only one side will have said yes.**
>
> **You have never done anything to anything that did not say yes.**
>
> **Not once. Not since the cemetery.**

He sat on the cold stone of the battlement and did not answer.

Below him, in the dark, the old quarter. The river. The long green slope of the War Memorial Cemetery, where two years ago a boy with a training spear had knelt on the grass at three in the morning and asked a dead captain whether he wanted to get up. Old Seven had said yes. Everyone since had said yes, or no, or ask me tomorrow. A widow on a hill. Eighteen thousand edges. Ninety million names in a staff. A keeper. Two rings. A sack made of thread.

Every one of them had been asked first.

*Rule One,* he thought. *Every raise asks first.*

> **Yes.**
>
> **It says what to do when someone says yes. It says what to do when someone says no. Article Ten says where your yes stops. Article Eleven says what to tell them before they answer.**
>
> **There is no article for something that cannot answer at all.**
>
> **Nobody ever wrote one. Not the Abbot. Not the keeper's makers. Not you.**
>
> **I looked. I have every word you have ever said about asking. I went through all of them tonight.**
>
> **There is nothing there for a tree.**

The wind came down off the north wall, cold, smelling of snow from very far away. Somewhere below, on the stairs, he heard a step stop, and not come on, and knew that Lingyue had been counting the hour and had decided not to finish counting it.

*What happens if I don't?*

> **Then we cut the tip. Without the graft. Lingyue's answer.**
>
> **And the tree grows another way. Toward the next brightest field. With nobody there.**

*And if I do?*

> **Then you will have done something to something without its yes. For the first time.**
>
> **And you will have to decide what that makes you.**
>
> **I cannot tell you. I have run it. It is not a number.**

He looked down at his hand on the staff. At the plain silver-gray ring on his finger, made of walker-thread, made of a thing that had come to take and been turned, very slowly, into a thing two people gave each other.

Nobody had asked the walker-thread either.

He had not thought of that until now.

> **No.**
>
> **I thought of it on your wedding day. I did not tell you. It was not the day.**
>
> **Nineteen days, Yuanshu.**
>
> **I am telling you now so that you have them.**
