## Chapter 128: Downhill

"We can't kill them," said Su Mingli, "and we can't let them through."

It was six in the morning in the Tianshu war room on Mount Qingyun, forty floors under the old observatory, and nobody had slept. The big table in the middle of the room was covered in Wei Desheng's map of Lingzhou, redrawn overnight by a Bureau cartographer who had cried twice taking dictation from a dead postman. Red circles for rifts. Blue lines for the routes beasts would take. A black square on the north bank of the river, in the old industrial district, for the Institute.

Around it, the people who had to decide. Su Mingli. Lu Zhengtian. Captain Lin Wen. Principal Yue Changfeng, in his tracksuit, eating sunflower seeds. Doctor Kwan, with a laptop. Lingyue. Dabao, who had not been invited and had come anyway and was standing at the back with his dented shield. And Yuanshu, with the staff, and Old Seven at the door.

"Every beast that dies within a kilometer of the river feeds the seed," Su Mingli went on. "We know that now. If we fight them where we fought them last night, we close the wound faster. If we don't fight them, they walk through three million people to get there."

"Fight them farther out," said Principal Yue, spitting a shell into his palm. "At the rifts. Before they start walking."

"There were eleven rifts last night," said Kwan. "In eleven places. Seed thinks there'll be more tonight. And bigger." She turned the laptop around. A chart. "The pulse gets stronger every time the seed eats. Last night was grade ones. Tonight, grade twos. Maybe threes."

"So we spread out," said Lin Wen. "Eleven fights instead of one."

"With what?" said Su Mingli. "His Field covers five kilometers. Outside it, his dead fall down and stay down. Outside it, the students have plain Light, which burns his dead." She looked at Yuanshu. "And he can't be in eleven places."

---

He had been thinking about it since midnight. Since Kwan's call. Since the canal road.

Not about where to fight. About what the seed was eating.

> **It is not eating bodies.**
>
> **When a beast dies, something leaves it. A thread. An ember. The same thing that left Walter Ashby when he died, and crossed the room to the sphere.**
>
> **The seed pulls. Every death inside a kilometer, it pulls the ember across to itself.**

*And the Field?*

> **...the Field pulls too.**

He had felt it on the canal road. He had not understood what he felt. When the soldier with the hairnet lost her leg, the leg had slid back across the cobbles to her knee. Downhill. Toward the river. Toward him. Everything that died inside the Field ran downhill.

Everything.

"The seed pulls the embers in," he said. Everyone looked at him. "So does my Field. The Field is a slope. Everything that dies inside it runs downhill, toward the river. Toward me." He put his finger on the map, on the north bank of the river, on the black square. "The seed's pull is strongest close to it. A kilometer. The Field's pull is strongest close to me."

"So it's a tug of war," said Lingyue slowly.

"It's two drains in one sink." He moved his finger off the black square, south, across the river, to an open space in the old quarter, four hundred meters from the Institute bridge. "If I'm standing here, between the city and the seed, then everything that dies between me and the city runs to me first. Not to it." He looked up. "We don't have to fight them far away. We have to fight them on the right side of me."

---

> **I have run it.**
>
> **If you stand at Tannery Square, at the center of the Field, the Field's slope beats the seed's pull for every death more than six hundred meters from the Institute. Inside six hundred meters, the seed wins.**
>
> **So: nothing dies inside six hundred meters. Everything that dies, dies on your side.**

*And what do I do with them?*

> **The embers?**

*Six hundred beasts. Maybe more. Every one of them runs downhill into me.*

A pause.

> **You open the door.**
>
> **The way you did under the Old Ferry Bridge, for ten thousand minds in a crystal. You let them through. Down the river. Wherever beasts go.**
>
> **You become a drain, not a dam.**
>
> **Yuanshu. It will hurt. Every death will pass through you. Hundreds. I do not know how many you can carry before you cannot carry any more.**

He told them that too. All of it.

Principal Yue stopped eating sunflower seeds.

"You want to stand in the middle of the city," said Su Mingli, very flatly, "and let every beast we kill tonight go through you."

"I want to stand between them and the thing that eats them. That's all a wall is." He looked at Dabao at the back of the room, and his dented shield. "Captain Zhao would understand."

Dabao, for once, did not say anything at all.

---

They built it around Tannery Square.

All day. Wei Desheng walked them through it, street by street, through Seed: every route a beast could take from every rift on the map to the Institute bridge, and where each route crossed the six-hundred-meter line. There were nineteen crossings. Nineteen places that had to hold, or the dying would happen on the wrong side.

Captain Ma Lianhua took the nineteen. She split the First Legion into nineteen companies, each under one of her sergeants, each counting its nines. Old Seven took the three hundred of the Spear Corps to the center, to Tannery Square, to stand around the boy. Dabao took the Defense Corps shield line to the Institute bridge itself, the last place anything could get through.

Lingyue took her students and split them into pairs and put a pair at every crossing.

"You don't burn anything that's inside six hundred meters," she told them, on the roof of the Academy at noon, with a red line drawn on a map in front of her. "Not one beast. If it's past this line, you don't kill it. You push it back. Burn its legs. Blind it. Make it turn around. It dies out here." She tapped the far side of the line. "Out here, where he can catch it."

"What if we can't push it back?" said the student with the hammer.

"Then you hold it until someone who can push it back gets there."

"What if nobody gets there?"

Lingyue looked at him. "Then you hold it until they do."

Principal Yue took Sector Nine. The wall. The Wastes beyond it. Whatever came from outside.

Su Mingli did not take anything.

"I'll be wherever the largest one comes out," she said. "There'll be a largest one. There always is." She rolled up the sleeves of her long gray coat, the way she had on the black glass in the north. "Kwan, when the first grade three opens, tell me where."

---

He was standing in Tannery Square at 01:40.

It was an old square, the kind the old quarter was full of: cobbles worn smooth, a dry fountain in the middle with a bronze crane on top, plane trees bare for the winter, the tannery itself long gone and replaced by a pharmacy and a bank and a shop that sold phone cases. The windows were dark. The Defense Corps had cleared the streets for a kilometer in every direction at midnight, door to door, and people had gone quietly, with their children and their cats, because they had been doing drills for this kind of night for a year.

Three hundred skeletons of the Spear Corps stood around the fountain in a ring, facing out. Old Seven stood beside him. Jiang Lan, in the staff, was humming something under her breath that might have been a school song.

> **The pulse is at 01:55.**
>
> **Field radius: 5.1 kilometers. Every crossing is inside it.**
>
> **Load: seventy-one percent.**
>
> **Yuanshu. When it starts, I will count the embers for you. I will tell you how many have gone through. You will not want to know. I will tell you anyway, because you need to know when to stop.**

*Don't let me stop early.*

> **I will not let you stop early. I will not let you stop late either.**

At 01:55, the city tore open.

Not eleven rifts. Twenty-six. Kwan's voice came over the radio from the Institute, reading them off as fast as the cameras found them, in a voice that was trying very hard to stay level. Grade two. Grade two. Grade two. Grade three, by the Exhibition Hall. Grade three, at the harbor mouth. Grade three on Jinhua Road. And then she stopped reading, and there was a pause, and then:

"*Mingli. Under the old Grain Exchange. North end of the market streets.*" Her voice broke very slightly. "*It's not a grade three.*"

Somewhere north of Tannery Square, two kilometers away, a sound came up out of the ground like a mountain clearing its throat.

Su Mingli folded the world and was gone.

---

The first beast died at 01:58, at Crossing Four, on the far side of the line, under a student's pale fire and a dead sergeant's bayonet.

He felt it come.

It came downhill. Through the Field. Through the cobbles and the drains and the old bones of the city, a small hot flicker of something that had been alive and frightened a second ago and was not any more. It came straight down the slope of the Field toward the river, toward the center, toward him. And it hit him the way a stone hits still water.

It hurt.

Not a wound. A grief. A little animal grief, frightened, confused, wanting to go back through the slit it had come out of, into the dark where it had been born. He felt it the way he had felt the minds in the crystal under the Old Ferry Bridge, the way he had felt the fox go first, a year ago, with the candles leaning.

*I'm sorry,* he told it. *You can go. The door's here. Go on.*

He opened the Death Law under it, the cool dark door.

It went.

> **One.**

The second came three seconds later. Then the fifth. Then ten at once, from Crossing Eleven, where a pack of ashbacks had run straight into the sergeant's nine and Lingyue's two students. Ten small hot griefs running downhill into him. He opened the door. They went.

> **Fourteen.**
>
> **The arrays in the chamber did not spike. Kwan says the line is flat.**
>
> **It is working.**

Then the grade threes came out of the ground, and the beasts came down the streets in hundreds, and the counting began in earnest.
