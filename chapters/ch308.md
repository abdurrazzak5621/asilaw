## Chapter 308: The Weir

Old Seven was still holding on when he went back up the slope.

He had left the Collector at the wall. It had not wanted to come; it had not said so, but he had felt it, the warmth of it pressed close to the small uncrossed mark at the bottom of the long count, and he had understood that it was not going to move for a while, and that this was allowed. He had said *I'll be back*, and it had said *yes*, and he had gone.

Up the slope. Out of the empty house. Back along the end of the carry road into the dark, where a tall dead captain with one arm stood off the road on someone else's Field, facing uphill, with a lost spark held against his chest.

There were three now.

> **He says: two more slid into him while you were gone.**
>
> **He says: he didn't do anything. He just stood there. They bumped into him and held on.**
>
> **He says: he's a stone in a stream. That's all. It's not hard.**
>
> **He says: there are a lot more coming down.**

He could feel them. All round, in the dark, on either side of the carry road. The thin scatter of dim gray sparks, a few hundred, sliding. All one way. Very slowly. A year of falling and they were most of the way down, and in a while, Seed said, at this rate, a few days, a few weeks, they would reach the middle of the hall, where he did not look.

And go through. Unasked.

---

He sat down on the carry road beside Old Seven, the way he sat on the vault steps on East Market Road when he needed to think, and thought.

*I can't stop the slope.*

> **No. It is not yours.**

*But I've got one.*

Seed did not answer at once, and when it did, it was one word.

> **Yes.**

He had been thinking about it since the first spark went by. A long time ago, in a square in Lingzhou, on one of the worst nights of his life, a seed in the ground had been pulling the dying toward it, and he had stood between the dying and the seed and opened his Field, and the slope of his Field had beaten the seed's pull. Beyond six hundred meters, Seed had said. Inside that, the seed won. Outside it, everything that was dying rolled downhill toward him instead.

That was Tannery Square. He had not thought about it in a long time.

*If I open the Field here,* he thought. *Not big. Small. Up the slope from the door. Between them and it.*

*Then they'd roll toward me instead.*

*Like a weir.* He thought of the river at Lingzhou, the old weir below the ferry steps, where the water came down and piled up against the stones and stopped. *They'd come down the slope and hit my Field and stop. And I could ask them.*

> **Yes,** said Seed. **I thought of it too. Two days ago. I did not say.**

*Why not?*

> **Because of where the two Fields would touch.**

---

It explained. Slowly, carefully, the way it explained things it was afraid of.

> **Your Field is a slope. So is this one. If you open yours here, there will be a line where they meet. On one side, things run toward you. On the other side, things run toward the door.**
>
> **On that line, the two slopes will push against each other.**
>
> **You will feel the other one push. You will not be able to help it. And when you feel something push against your Law, you learn it. That is how a Law grows. That is how yours grew at Tannery Square, and at the Western Pass, and in the deep water. Every time something pushed, you learned the shape of it.**
>
> **If you learn the shape of this one, your Law will go up.**
>
> **And it is very large. And the wall is just past 59.9.**

*How fast?*

> **I do not know. I am not looking at it. That is the whole point of not looking.**
>
> **If you keep your Field small, the line where they meet is short. Less pushing. Less learning.**
>
> **If you keep it very small, I think you can hold it for a while before the number moves too much.**
>
> **I do not know what a while is. I am telling you first that I do not know.**

He sat on the carry road and looked at the sparks sliding by in the dark. Old Seven, beside him, one-armed, with three lost lights held against his chest, did not say anything.

*What's the smallest it can be,* he thought, *and still catch them?*

> **They are coming down on both sides of the road. Spread out. About three kilometers wide, the scatter.**
>
> **Three kilometers.**

*Three kilometers is small,* he thought. *I used to do five. Then ten.*

> **Not here.**
>
> **Here three kilometers is the biggest thing you have ever held.**

---

He did it anyway. Not quickly. He had learned that, too. He sat on the carry road and let Seed walk him through it, very slowly, step by step, like a child learning to carry a full bowl across a room. Not the whole Field. A strip. Long and narrow, three kilometers wide across the slope and only a few hundred meters deep, laid across the dark uphill of the place where the carry road ran down into the house. A weir. Low. Just enough of a slope toward him that anything coming down would stop against it.

He opened it a crack, and felt the other one push.

It was not like anything he had felt. The seed at Tannery Square had pulled like a drain. The gardener's reader had pressed like a hand. This was neither. This was like standing in a river that had been running in the same direction since before rivers, and putting your hand flat against the current. Not hostile. Not anything. Only very old, and very large, and going one way, and his hand in it.

> **59.4% → 59.5%.**
>
> **Small. Keep it there.**

He kept it there. The first spark came down the slope into the weir eleven minutes later, and stopped.

He felt it stop. A dim gray light, burning low, a year of falling, sliding down out of the dark, and then the slope under it changing, very slightly, so that instead of going on down toward the middle it came to rest. Against the edge of his Field. The way a leaf comes to rest against a stone.

It turned. Toward him. The way they all turned.

> **It says: where am I.**
>
> **They all say that.**

*Not in the house,* he thought, down to it. *Not yet. You're with me. I'm going to ask you something. Nobody's going to make you go anywhere.*

*What do you want?*

---

Most of them wanted to go home. That was the thing he had not expected, and should have. The cloud at the edge of the deep water had mostly wanted the door. They had been picked a long time ago, and fallen, and been cold in the dark, and when somebody asked them, most of them had said: *let me rest*. But these had been on the carry road. They had been in the hands, on the way to the house, at the very end, when the picker stopped. They had been asleep in the hands and heard, the whole way, the only thing the hands ever said. *Home.* And they had thought it meant their own.

> **It says: I want to go home. It says a name. A world. I do not know it.**
>
> **This one too. A different name.**
>
> **This one wants the door. It says it is tired. It says: thank you for asking.**
>
> **This one wants to go home.**
>
> **This one does not know. It says: ask me again.**

He could not send them home. He knew that before Seed said it. He did not have their threads. Seed could not find their threads without looking at the slope, and they were not looking at the slope. He was one boy in someone else's Field at the bottom of the oldest road anything had ever made, holding a weir open with Seed counting his Law up one tenth at a time, and a hundred lost lights from a hundred worlds he would never see were piling up against it, asking him if they could go home.

*Not yet,* he told them. *Later. I'm sorry. Wait here. You're safe here.*

He did not know if that was true.

> **59.5%.**
>
> **Still.**

---

The mark came on the twenty-ninth day, two days and one hour after it was sent.

He nearly did not feel it. The thread behind him was so long now and so thin that a mark from home came in under him the way a sound comes from the far end of a valley, faint and late and whole. Square. Black. True from anywhere.

*FOUR HOUSES. TWENTY SHIPS. SIX DAYS. FOR ME.*

*THEY WANT ME TO WRITE: THIS IS YOUR FACE. I WON'T.*

*THEY'LL BURN WALLS IN FRONT OF ME. TAM SAYS. I BELIEVE HIM.*

*I'M GOING TO MEET THEM AT THE KEEPER. IN THE FIELD. WITH THE COUSINS. TAM, SERI, SU MINGLI, THE NIGHT WATCH, STERLING.*

*LINGYUE ASKED ME. I SAID.*

*SHE'S HOLDING. SHE HATES IT. SHE SAYS TELL YOU SHE HATES IT.*

*DON'T TURN ROUND. YOU CAN'T GET HERE IN TIME ANYWAY. QIN HAO DID THE SUM.*

*— XIAOYU*

He sat on the carry road in the dark with the weir open and a hundred lost lights against it and Old Seven beside him, and read it, and read it again.

Six days from when she sent it. Two days ago.

Four days. Less, by now.

---

*Seed.*

> **I did the sum too.**
>
> **It is twenty-nine days back along the carry road, if we go as fast as the Collector can go. Faster, because it is uphill for us and downhill for nothing. Twenty-five, perhaps.**
>
> **The ships will be at the keeper in four.**
>
> **You cannot get there.**
>
> **Nothing you can do from here will reach there in time. A mark takes two days. By the time a mark from you arrives, it will be two days before they come. By the time one from her comes back, it will be over.**

*Over,* he thought.

> **Whatever it is.**

He did not move. He thought of his sister. Eighteen. In a school jacket she was too old for. Standing in the ring of the keeper's field with a marker in her hand, with twenty ships the size of cities coming out of the dark burning a million people in their walls, and a Paragon on the bridge of each one who had stood in an Assembly and said the dim should burn so that the bright could live, and who would hold up a wall in front of her, and say *write it*, and burn one.

He thought of her saying, at the parapet, the day he left for Anthos: *You already decided. You don't get to sit on a step about it twice.*

He thought of her saying, on the north wall: *No. You tell it. When you get home.*

She had decided. She always decided before him. She had decided at sixteen, in the back room of the Ossuary, with a marker, that she was going to keep the board. She had decided on Awakening Day that she was going to ask the oldest thing in the dark what it wanted. She had decided at a long table two days ago, while he was sitting in front of a wall of tallies at the end of the universe, that she was going to stand in front of four Great Houses with a marker and tell them no.

And Lingyue had asked her. Lingyue, who hated asking. Who wanted to decide for everyone she loved. Had asked, and listened, and let her go.

*I want to go home,* he thought. *Right now. More than anything.*

> **I know.**

*I'm not going to.*

> **I know that too.**

He looked at the weir. At the hundred lost lights piled against it, from a hundred worlds, waiting, asking. At Old Seven, one-armed, holding three. At the dark below, where the carry road ran down into an empty house, and a very old warm thing sat by a wall at the end of a count, and at the middle, where he did not look, something that had never once asked anyone anything stood open.

He could not get home. He could not help her. He could do this.

*Send her a mark,* he thought. *Please. Now. It'll be two days. Send it anyway.*

> **Tell me what to write.**

He told it, and Seed wrote it down the way she would have, square.

*XIAOYU. I CAN'T GET THERE. I'M SORRY. I KNOW YOU'VE DECIDED. YOU ALWAYS DECIDE BEFORE ME.*

*DON'T WRITE WHAT THEY WANT. WRITE WHAT'S TRUE.*

*AND ASK THEM WHAT THEY WANT. NOBODY EVER HAS. THAT'S HOW IT STARTS.*

*I'M AT THE END OF THE ROAD. THE HOUSE IS EMPTY. YOU WERE RIGHT. YOU'RE ALWAYS RIGHT. IT'S VERY ANNOYING.*

*— GE*

The mark went up the thread behind him, small and square and true, into the dark, toward a small blue world twenty-nine days away, where it would arrive in two days, two days before a girl with a marker stood in a field of light and waited for twenty ships.

He let it go. Then he turned back to the weir, and the lights against it, and the next one sliding down out of the dark toward him, and asked it what it wanted.
