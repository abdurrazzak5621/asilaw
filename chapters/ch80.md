## Chapter 80: The Star Sea

At the foot of the white column, before he went down, the keeper asked him one more thing.

He was standing in the clearing with the staff in his hand. Around him, the dead: Old Seven, Iron Wall Zhao, Hundred-Arrow Lin, Sergeant Huo and seventy-one soldiers of the Third Battalion. Behind him, the column. Dimmer now. Fifty-two tall slender beings standing hand in hand inside it with their silver eyes open, watching. And beyond the clearing, the field. Still enormous. Still endless. But not gold anymore, not all of it. Patched. Dark here, where the quiet ones had gone. Gold there, where some had chosen to stay. And everywhere, empty columns, standing like doorways with no one in them.

***YOU.***

The keeper's voice was very close.

***YOU HAVE NOT SAID WHAT YOU WANT.***

He thought about it. Honestly. Standing there.

"Stop cutting," he said. "Stop taking people who didn't say yes. Not just on Blue Star. Anywhere. Ever." He held the staff a little tighter. "If you go to a field, ask. Like you asked them. And if they say no, let them go."

***MOST WILL SAY NO.***

"Probably."

***...THEN I WILL BE ALONE.***

"No," said Yuanshu. He looked at the fifty-two in the white column. At the gold columns that had chosen to stay. "You'll have them. And the ones who say yes. And you can remember the others. You don't have to hold someone to remember them." He paused. "And you can visit. If you ask first."

The field was silent for a long time.

***YES.***

The single word went out across the field, ring after ring, to every horizon. And Yuanshu understood that it was not an answer to him. It was the keeper, answering its own question for the first time. Writing its own name in its own Ledger.

***YES.***

---

***ONE MORE THING.***

The keeper's voice had changed. Not uncertain. Something else. Something heavier.

***I WAS NOT THE ONLY ONE.***

He went still.

***THE SEA IS WIDE. VERY WIDE. MANY FIELDS. MANY KEEPERS.***

***OLDER THAN ME. STRANGER.***

***I WAS BORN FROM A YES.***

***THEY WERE NOT.***

The light in the field dimmed. Just slightly. As if, somewhere very far away, a cloud had passed over a sun that wasn't there.

***THE GATE WAS OPENED. YOU OPENED IT. FROM BELOW. WITH THAT.***

The attention of the keeper rested, for a moment, on the white staff.

***THEY HEARD.***

***THEY DO NOT ASK.***

---

> **...Yuanshu.**

*I know.*

> **How many?**

***I DO NOT KNOW.***

***THE SEA IS WIDE.***

***BUT YOUR FIELD IS VERY BRIGHT NOW. VERY LOUD. A WORLD THAT ASKS. A WORLD WHERE THE DEAD STAND UP AND SAY YES AND NO.***

***NO ONE HAS EVER SEEN ONE BEFORE.***

***THEY WILL COME TO LOOK.***

He stood in the clearing at the center of the field and held the staff and did not let anything move in his face.

"Then I'll ask them too," he said.

The keeper was silent for a long moment.

Then something happened in the soft white light that, in a person, would have been a laugh. Very small. Very old. The first one it had ever made.

***YES.***

***I THINK YOU WILL.***

---

It carried him down.

Not up a thread. Not unseen. Down the stalk, in its own light, gently, the way it had set down Andrei Belov and Ama and eleven thousand four hundred others. A long slow fall through gold and white and dark and silver, with the dead around him like a flock of birds, past the black water and the lens and the long rope of the stalk, toward the curve of a small blue world turning in the dark.

He watched it come up to meet him.

Lights on the night side. Coasts. Cities. And on the southern edge of a vast black emptiness at the top of the world, a thin bright line, sixty kilometers long. Still lit. Still glittering. They had not put the candles out.

He let himself look at it this time.

Nothing up here was listening anymore.

---

They came down on the ridge at Grid Nine at dawn.

Out of the haze, which was not gray anymore but thinning, gold, burning off like morning mist in the sun. Out of a column of light that was already fading behind them, unwinding, the threads drawing apart and drifting away in every direction like dandelion seeds on the wind.

The ridge was crowded.

Eleven thousand four hundred people, sitting up on the ice in blankets and tarpaulins, bewildered, frostbitten, alive, talking. In a dozen languages. All at once. Laughing. Crying. Asking each other where they were. Nineteen thousand dead standing among them, quietly, the way you stand at the back of a room at a party where you don't know anyone, passing out the last of the expedition's tea.

And at the edge of the ridge, waiting, the living.

He came up out of the gold haze with the staff in his hand and the dead behind him, and stopped.

Su Mingli was standing at the front. She did not move. She looked at him for a long time with her face perfectly calm and her eyes very bright, and then she looked at the staff.

"*She's asleep,*" said Jiang Lan, from the small white skull, very quietly. "*Your friend. In here. With me. She's very tired. She'll want to wake up somewhere warm.*"

Su Mingli closed her eyes.

Then she nodded. Once. The way she'd nodded at the Stele on the first day.

---

Lingyue didn't say anything.

She walked across the ice to him with her sword sheathed at her hip, through the crowd of bewildered people and the quiet dead, and stopped in front of him, and looked at him for a long moment.

Then she hit him on the arm. Not very hard.

"You were gone three days," she said. "We lay on the ice for three days. Bai Lu cried twice. The twins invented a game. Qin Hao set his own sleeve on fire." She hit him again. "I thought about the box. In the Ledger. The whole time." Her voice cracked very slightly on the last word and she pretended it hadn't.

"You don't have to use it," said Yuanshu.

"I know." She looked at him. "So don't make me."

"I won't."

"Good." Shen Lingyue turned around and looked out at the gold mist burning off over the north, with her back to him and her chin up. "You still owe me a spar. That draw on the wall doesn't count. I was tired."

---

Behind his eyes, very quietly, Seed spoke.

> **The pulse.**
>
> **I counted it just now.**
>
> **There is no pulse.**
>
> **The lens is still there. In the north. But it is small now. Very small. A ring, white, like a wedding band. And it is not looking at anything.**
>
> **It is just open. A little. Like a door left ajar.**
>
> **In case someone wants to visit.**

He looked up.

There it was. On the northern horizon, in the pale morning sky, so small and faint you'd only see it if you knew where to look. A thin white ring. Like a ring someone dropped.

> **And Yuanshu.**
>
> **Further up. Past it. Where the sky goes dark.**
>
> **I am not sure. It is very far. Very faint.**
>
> **But I think there are others.**

He looked past the small white ring. Up. Into the place where the morning blue went darker and darker toward black, where in a few hours the first stars would come out.

And far, far beyond them, so faint he could not tell if it was really there, so far that it might have been in another sky entirely, he thought, for just a moment, that he saw something.

A ring.

Pale. Huge. Unimaginably distant.

And another, beside it.

And another.

Not white. Not gold.

Gray. Cracked. Clouded. Like the crystal of the cores.

Turning, very slowly, toward a small blue world that had just become the brightest thing in the dark.

> **Four.**
>
> **I count four.**
>
> **They are not looking at us yet.**
>
> **But they heard.**

---

They walked home.

It took thirty-one days. Eleven thousand four hundred living people and nineteen thousand four hundred and two of the dead, walking south together across the Wastes, in a long slow column, with the living carried on sleds when they were too weak to walk, by the dead, who did not get tired. Yan Shuang walked at the front, reading the glass, with a girl in a frosted braid beside her who would not stop asking questions about her dog.

> **Something I have not told you, because you were busy.**
>
> **Death Law: 24.6% → 26.9%. The field. The first column. Nine hundred and forty-one makers going quiet in front of us. I watched every one.**
>
> **Still Intent. Still below the line. I am keeping my promise about the stairs.**

At the wall, the north gate was open.

The whole city was there. Not quiet this time. Not with candles. Shouting. Twenty thousand people crowding the street inside the gate and the battlements above it and every window and rooftop in sight, so loud that you could hear it from the first ridge, three kilometers out.

And at the front of all of it, at the painted line on the cobbles where the city ended, stood a girl in a too-big hoodie with a green flask in her hand. Scowling so fiercely that he knew she had been practicing it for two months.

He did not walk to her.

He stopped at the line. And planted the white staff on the cobbles. And very gently, the way a keeper had learned to set something down, he asked a schoolteacher named Jiang Lan if she was ready.

"*Ready,*" said the dry voice. "*Ask her yourself. She's awake.*"

---

The staff glowed.

Soft. Gray-white. Like a candle seen through paper.

And the light came out of the small skull and went down to the cobbles beside the staff, and gathered there, and took a shape. Small. Dark hair pinned up under a field cap. A white lab coat over a gray sweater with a hole in the elbow. Quick dark eyes, exactly like her daughter's.

Zhou Wanqing stood on the cobbles of the north gate of Lingzhou, swaying very slightly, like someone stepping off a boat after a long voyage. Solid. Whole. Breathing.

She looked at her daughter.

Xiaoyu's scowl broke.

The green flask hit the cobbles and rolled away, and nobody picked it up, and nobody cared.

---

Much later, in the old banking hall of the Ossuary, Xiaoyu climbed a stepladder and uncapped a new black marker.

She did not change the big number on the board. She added a new column. At the end. After YES and ASK MY FAMILY and ONLY TO SPEAK and QUIET.

She wrote its heading in careful capitals, and then stepped down, and stood back, and looked at it with her arms folded.

*CAME HOME.*

Under it, she wrote the first number.

It was very, very large.
