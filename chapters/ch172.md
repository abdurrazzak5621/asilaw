## Chapter 172: Nine Seconds, Twenty-Two Seconds

"It's getting longer," said Xiaoyu. "Every month. Nobody's said anything about that."

She was sitting cross-legged on the marble floor of the old banking hall under the board, with a notebook on her knee and the black marker behind her ear, looking up at the column headed INSIDE. It was the third afternoon since he had come home. The Ossuary was closed. Fat Tan was at his desk under the window with a ledger. Doctor Kwan was on a stool with a laptop. Old Seven was by the bronze door. And Yuanshu was on the vault steps, where he had been most of the three days, because Seed had asked him to sit still while it thought.

"What is?" said Kwan.

"The messages. The time they take." Xiaoyu pointed up at the board with the marker. "I write it down. Every time. Under the line. Seed tells me how long it took to read out, and I write it small, at the end." She stood up and went to the wall and touched the plaster at the end of each line in turn. "Day thirty-one. Nine seconds. Day one hundred and seventy-eight. Eleven. Day two hundred and nine. Nineteen. Day two hundred and forty-one. Twenty-two." She turned around. "It's the same amount of words, mostly. Hush doesn't say much. But it takes longer to come out. Like a tape playing slow."

> **...she is right.**
>
> **I knew the numbers. I read them out. I did not put them next to each other.**
>
> **Xiaoyu. Please do not move. I would like to look at the wall.**

---

It looked at the wall for an hour.

Yuanshu could feel it working, the way you feel a room go warm when the heating comes on. Not fast. Seed had been faster than this on the mountain with water, and on the jetty, and in the gatehouse at the Western Pass. This was slow, careful, the way it worked when it did not want to be wrong.

> **What I observed.**
>
> **Eight messages. Each one has a number Hush gives it: Day thirty-one, sixty-four, ninety-eight, one hundred and twenty-seven, one hundred and fifty-eight, one hundred and seventy-eight, two hundred and nine, two hundred and forty-one. Days since the Mouth left.**
>
> **Each one arrived here on a date. Fat Tan wrote every date in his ledger. He writes everything.**
>
> **Each one took a number of seconds to read out. Xiaoyu wrote every one under the line.**
>
> **Three numbers for each message. Nobody has ever put all three side by side.**

"Put them side by side," said Yuanshu.

> **I have.**

Eight rows on the inside of his eyes. Hush's day. Blue Star's date. The seconds. And a fourth column Seed added itself: the gap between when Hush said a message was sent and when it arrived.

The gap was growing.

> **Day thirty-one arrived on our day thirty-one. No gap.**
>
> **Day one hundred and seventy-eight arrived on our day one hundred and eighty-one. Three days late.**
>
> **Day two hundred and forty-one arrived on our day two hundred and fifty-two. Eleven days late.**
>
> **The messages are taking longer to get here. And they are coming out slower when they arrive.**

---

"Because it's further away," said Kwan. "Obviously. It's moving away from us. The further it goes, the longer the message takes to travel."

> **That is the first explanation. It is the simplest. I would like it to be right.**
>
> **Here is the second. The Mouth is not further. It is slower. Its own time is running differently from ours, because it is dying, and its fires are cooling. Hush's days are getting longer. The message is not delayed. It is just written in a longer day.**
>
> **And the third. The echo is changing. The messages come the long way, through the echo, the keeper catches them, the Elder carries them down the gold thread. If the echo is getting weaker, every message has to be repeated more times before the keeper can hear it. That would make it late and slow.**
>
> **Three explanations. All of them make the gap grow.**

"Can you tell them apart?" said Yuanshu.

> **...I think so.**
>
> **They make different shapes.**

It showed him. Three curves, laid over the eight rows. If the Mouth were moving away, the gap and the seconds would grow together, in step, the way the sound of a train drops in pitch and stretches as it goes away. If the Mouth's time were slowing, the seconds would grow but the gap would stay small, because Hush would simply be counting slower. If the echo were weakening, the gap would grow in jumps, not smoothly, each time the keeper needed one more repetition to catch the words.

The eight rows did not fit any of them.

> **They fit two at once.**
>
> **The first and the second. It is moving away, and it is slowing down. Both. At the same time.**
>
> **And the slowing is getting faster. Every month, the Mouth's day is longer than the month before.**

Fat Tan looked up from his ledger. "So the fire's going out."

> **Yes. The Sixth were right. It is cooling.**
>
> **And the moving away is getting slower. Every month. Like a stone thrown up in the air.**
>
> **It is going to stop.**

---

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

Xiaoyu was still standing at the wall with her hand on the plaster at the end of the INSIDE column, where she had written the seconds small after every line for eight months without anyone telling her to.

"Stop where?" she said.

> **I do not know. I do not have a distance. I only have a shape.**
>
> **I know how the gap grows. I know how the seconds grow. I know that it is slowing.**
>
> **But a gap of eleven days could be a very slow message from close by, or a very fast message from very far away. I do not know how fast the echo travels. I have never measured it.**
>
> **I need a ruler.**

"A ruler," said Kwan.

> **Something I know the size of. Measured in echo-time. One distance where I know how long it takes, and how far it is. Then I can measure everything else against it.**

Yuanshu sat on the vault steps and looked at the board.

At the columns. All of them. The first one, from the cemetery. The Wall. THE THROAT. The voyage. CAME HOME. FROM OUT THERE.

"We went out there," he said.

> **...yes.**

"On the raft. The keeper carried us. We were gone a hundred and fifteen days. We went to the Lantern world, and the husk worlds, and the cousin world, and the place where the first Hunger died." He looked at the column headed FROM OUT THERE, in Xiaoyu's capitals. "And the Elder was with us the whole time. And whenever we could, we talked to home. Through the keeper." He looked at Kwan. "You were on the raft. With your forty pages."

> **...through the echo.**
>
> **Forty-one calls. Kwan logged every one on the raft, with the time. Fat Tan logged every one at this end, with the time. And I know how far the raft had gone each day, because I counted the drift.**
>
> **That is the ruler.**
>
> **That is forty-one rulers.**

---

It took all night.

Kwan went home at midnight and came back at two with the raft logs on a hard drive in a plastic bag and her hair in a knot, because she could not sleep, she said, and if Seed was going to find out where the Mouth was, she wanted to be awake for it. Fat Tan brought down every ledger from the vault, all of them, with the dates of every call from the voyage. Xiaoyu fell asleep on the marble floor under the board at three with her notebook on her chest. Old Seven did not move from the bronze door.

Seed laid the raft's whole voyage out in its head. Day by day. Every drift. Every call home. Every lag between a voice on the raft and an ear in Lingzhou. Forty-one measurements of how far the echo went in how long, from a raft whose position Seed knew because it had been on it.

The first ruler was wrong.

> **It does not agree with itself. The echo was faster on the way out than on the way back. That is not possible. Something is wrong with the ruler.**

It found what was wrong at four. It was the keeper. On the way out, the keeper had been carrying them, and its attention had been on the raft, close, all the time; the echo had run along that attention like water along a pipe. On the way back, the raft had been split, and half had come home alone, and the keeper's attention had been divided. The echo had been slower when the keeper was not watching.

> **The echo runs along the keeper's attention.**
>
> **That is new. I did not know it.**
>
> **When the keeper is looking, the echo is fast. When it is not, the echo is slow. Like a voice in a room where someone is listening, and a voice in an empty room.**

"So the Mouth's messages are slow," said Yuanshu, very quietly, so as not to wake his sister, "because nobody's looking at it."

> **...yes.**
>
> **The keeper does not look at the Mouth. It is afraid of it. It catches the messages because Hush shouts them into the echo, and the keeper hears its name. But it does not look.**

---

At half past five, with the high windows of the banking hall going gray, Seed had a number.

It did not say it at once. It laid it out first, the way it laid everything out now: what it knew, what it guessed, where the gap between them was. The ruler, corrected for the keeper's attention. The eight messages. The two curves, moving away and slowing down. The shape of the slowing, like a stone thrown up in the air reaching the top of its arc.

> **The Mouth is about thirty-eight raft-days away.**
>
> **That is the distance the keeper could carry the raft in thirty-eight days. If it carried it there. If it were willing.**
>
> **It is still moving. Slower every month. My best guess is that it stops about eleven months from now, at about forty-four raft-days.**
>
> **And then the fires go out. I do not know how long after that. The Sixth said a year or two. I have nothing better.**
>
> **Confidence in the distance: sixty-one percent. In the stopping: fifty-three. I am telling you because you asked me to tell you the numbers even when they are not good numbers.**

Kwan was sitting very still on her stool with her laptop on her knees.

"Forty-four days," she said. "The voyage was a hundred and fifteen."

"The voyage was there and back," said Yuanshu. "With stops."

"Forty-four days there. Forty-four back. If the keeper would carry you. Into a dying Hunger." Kwan took off her glasses and pressed the heels of her hands into her eyes. "And then what? You stand outside a cold furnace and knock?"

> **I do not know.**
>
> **I did not have a distance yesterday. I have one today.**
>
> **That is all I have. I am telling you so that it is written down.**

---

Old Seven came away from the bronze door.

He walked across the marble floor in the gray morning light, slowly, with his spear, to the end of the board. To the INSIDE column. And stood in front of it, looking up at the last line, and the one under it, and the one under that, very small, at the bottom of the wall: *SOMEONE SHOULD GO AND GET THEM.*

Then he reached up with his spear, very carefully, the way you reach to straighten a picture on a wall, and touched the butt of it to the plaster beside the words.

> **He says: forty-four days.**
>
> **He says he has marched further.**
>
> **He says: ask the keeper.**

Xiaoyu sat up on the floor, blinking, with the notebook sliding off her chest.

"What did I miss?" she said.

"Seed found the Mouth," said Yuanshu. "Because you wrote the seconds down."

His sister looked at him. Then at the wall. Then at the small numbers she had written under every line for eight months, at the end, without anyone asking.

"I just thought someone should," she said.

> **Someone should.**
>
> **I am going to ask the keeper.**
>
> **Not now. It is six in the morning, and Yuanshu has not slept, and the keeper is afraid of the Mouth, and I need to think about how to ask it something it is afraid of.**
>
> **But I am going to ask it.**
