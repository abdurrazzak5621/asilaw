## Chapter 198: Ichiro's Boat

Sister Kiyo heard it on the two hundred and ninth night of listening, and this time she did not wait until dawn.

Ren called at one in the morning. Yuanshu was on the vault steps. The Ossuary was full now, every night, with people who had come to sit under the long white wall in the dark because they could not sleep and did not want to be alone with what Blue Star had decided: forty or fifty of them, on the marble floor, on Fat Tan's chairs, on the stairs, not talking. Xiaoyu was on the stepladder. Kwan was on the desk with her laptop. Old Seven was at the bronze door.

He put the phone on speaker on the marble.

On the line, under Ren's breathing and the cedars and the bird, the monks were not talking all at once this time. There was only one voice. An old woman's voice in old Yamato, very dry, very clear, and shaking.

"*She wants you to hear it from her,*" said Ren. "*Not from Seed. From her.*"

---

> **"A boat call."**
>
> **"In Shiomi, every boat had its own call on the small bells. So that when the fog came in, and the boats were coming home blind, the women on the shore would know which boat it was by its bell before they could see it. Three notes for the headman's boat. Four for the priest's. Five, very quick, for the boat my father built."**
>
> **"My brother's boat. Ichiro's. After my father died, it was his."**
>
> **"Five notes. Very quick. A little too fast, because he was always in a hurry."**
>
> **"I heard it tonight. In the silence after the name. After our four notes. After *we hear you.*"**
>
> **"Five notes."**

Nobody in the old banking hall said anything.

> **"In the fog-code, when a boat rings its own call, it means: this is me. I am coming in. Light the lamp on the shore."**
>
> **"He is telling me it is him."**
>
> **"He is telling me he is coming in."**

---

> **I have it. The five notes. Under the long silence, after the four. Very quick.**
>
> **And after it, more fog-code. Longer than before. Faster. I have had to clean it a great deal. I will tell you what I am not sure of.**
>
> **It says:**
>
> **THE ONE WHO GROWS IS PRUNING. TOWARD YOU. WE SEE IT.**
>
> **THE COLD FIRE. WE SEE IT TOO.**
>
> **WE ARE COMING. QUIETLY. TO THE COLD FIRE.**
>
> **WAIT FOR US THERE.**

Kwan's laptop slid off the desk. She caught it. She did not look at it.

"*They know about the Mouth,*" she said. "*They know we're going.*"

> **They know the Mouth is cold. They can see it. Through whatever they see through.**
>
> **I do not know if they know we are going. I think they hope we are.**
>
> **The cold fire is thirty-nine raft-days from here. I do not know how far it is from them. Further, I think. Much further. In the deep water.**
>
> **But they are coming.**
>
> **To the same place.**

---

Sister Kiyo was still speaking. Seed had stopped translating to let her.

> **"Twelve hundred years."**
>
> **"I sat down in the hall on the evening the ring came. I watched my brother go down the hill to the boats with a sack of rice on his shoulder. He was talking to himself. He was always talking. I thought: I will tell him tomorrow that he talks too much."**
>
> **"I did not tell him."**
>
> **"I have been under a gate for twelve hundred years, wanting to tell my brother that he talks too much."**

Her voice did not shake. It was twelve hundred years old.

> **"Boy who asks."**

"*I'm here.*"

> **"You are going to the cold fire."**

"*Yes.*"

> **"He will be there. Or someone who rings his boat call. Someone he taught, out in the deep water, who rings it a little too fast."**
>
> **"I would like to ask you something. I know what your rule is. I will tell you first what I will do with your answer."**
>
> **"If you say yes, I will be very happy, under my gate, for however long it takes."**
>
> **"If you say no, I will understand. You have a great many people to bring home from that fire. Sixty-one soldiers. A child. Two hundred and eighty-one million. A corporal. I am one old woman under a gate who wants to tell her brother he talks too much."**
>
> **"Will you tell him?"**

---

He sat on the vault steps under the long white wall with the phone on the marble beside him, and forty strangers on the floor of the banking hall in the dark, and his sister on the stepladder with the marker in her hand, and could not answer for a moment.

Then he said, "*Yes.*"

> **"Good."**
>
> **"Tell him Kiyo says: you talk too much. Tell him she says: I waited."**
>
> **"Tell him she says: light the lamp on the shore."**

---

> **There is something else. I found it while she was speaking. I did not want to interrupt her.**

*Go on.*

> **The interval. Four hundred and twelve seconds, for every recording, until the night after the field. Then four hundred and eleven. Then faster.**
>
> **I thought they were ringing faster. Because they were in a hurry. Because they had seen the field.**
>
> **That is part of it. It is not all of it.**
>
> **I did what I did with Hush's messages. I put the interval next to the time each recording arrived, and next to how long the small bells took to say the name. The name is getting shorter too. A little. Every night. The way Hush's messages got longer when the Mouth went away.**
>
> **A thing going away sounds longer and slower. A thing coming closer sounds shorter and faster.**
>
> **They are not only ringing faster.**
>
> **They are coming closer. They have been coming closer since the night after the field.**

Kwan had her laptop open again. Her face was white in the light of it.

"*How far?*" she said. "*Where are they?*"

> **I do not have a ruler for the deep water. My ruler is forty-one calls from a raft in the Shallows.**
>
> **But if the echo out there runs the way it runs here, they were about six hundred raft-days away when I first heard the bell. Past the end of the arm. Across the dark between arms.**
>
> **Now they are about four hundred and seventy.**
>
> **They have come a hundred and thirty raft-days in two hundred nights.**
>
> **That is not as fast as the keeper carries. It is not slow.**
>
> **They will not be at the cold fire when we are. Not for a long time after. Perhaps a year. Perhaps more. I do not know how they travel or whether they can go faster.**
>
> **But they are coming. Quietly. They told us. And they are telling the truth.**

"*Wait for us there,*" said Kwan.

> **Yes.**
>
> **I do not know if we can wait a year at a cold fire with something pruning the arm toward it.**
>
> **I am going to tell you that now, before we go, so that nobody says yes to going without knowing it.**

---

The keeper came at three, through the Elder, and he asked it about fifty thousand.

He told it first what he would do with the answer. All of it. If it said no, they would go with fewer. If it said it did not know, they would wait until it did. And whatever it said, fifty thousand of the dead of Blue Star had said yes to going into the dark after their own, and he would like it to know that.

The gold thread through the Elder was very still for a long time.

> ***...FIFTY THOUSAND.***
>
> ***...I CARRIED A RAFT. TWELVE LIVING. SOME HUNDREDS OF DEAD. IT WAS VERY HEAVY. I HAD NEVER CARRIED ANYTHING.***
>
> ***...I HAVE CARRIED FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-EIGHT UP A ROAD INTO MY FIELD, AND DOWN AGAIN.***
>
> ***...I HAVE NEVER CARRIED FIFTY THOUSAND.***

"*I know.*"

> ***...I DO NOT KNOW IF I CAN.***
>
> ***...I THINK I WILL FIND OUT BY TRYING.***
>
> ***...THAT IS HOW YOU DO EVERYTHING. I WATCHED.***

"*That's not a yes.*"

> ***...NO.***
>
> ***...IT IS WHAT I WANT.***
>
> ***...I AM LEARNING THE DIFFERENCE.***

Something in Yuanshu's chest went very tight.

The keeper had said that before. Months ago, in this room, when he had told it he would stop the scout and it had said: *that is not yes. That is what you want.* And General Qin had said it on the frozen road at the Western Pass. And he had said it back.

> ***...ONE MORE THING.***
>
> ***...THE FEW WHO STAYED. IN MY FIELD. ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIVE.***
>
> ***...THEY HEARD. ABOUT THE COLD FIRE. ABOUT THE CHILD.***
>
> ***...THEY ASKED ME IF THEY COULD COME.***
>
> ***...NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED ME IF THEY COULD COME ANYWHERE.***
>
> ***...I SAID I WOULD ASK YOU.***

"*Tell them yes,*" said Yuanshu. "*Tell them they can say no tomorrow. And the day after.*"

> ***...I WILL TELL THEM.***
>
> ***...THEY WILL NOT SAY NO.***
>
> ***...THEY ARE LIKE YOU. THEY ARE VERY STUBBORN.***

---

When the Elder had gone, he sat on the vault steps for a while in the dark.

The forty strangers on the floor of the banking hall had heard all of it. The boat call. Kiyo. The keeper. Fifty thousand. They sat in the dark on the marble and on the stairs and did not say anything, and after a while, one of them got up. An old man in a cardigan. He walked to the foot of the long white wall, to the place where people left things, candles and playing cards and once a small brass key on a red string, and took something out of his pocket, and put it down, and went back to his place on the floor.

A box of matches.

Then a woman got up, and put down a candle. And another. A lamp, from someone's kitchen, an old oil lamp with a glass chimney. A torch. A string of fairy lights with a battery pack. A child's nightlight shaped like a rabbit.

By dawn, the floor under the long white wall was covered with lamps. None of them lit. Waiting.

Xiaoyu climbed down off the stepladder and looked at them for a long time. Then she climbed back up, to the very end of the SHIOMI column, round the corner on the third wall, and wrote, in her whispering capitals, the biggest letters she had ever written:

*LIGHT THE LAMP ON THE SHORE.*
