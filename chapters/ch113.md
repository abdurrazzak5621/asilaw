## Chapter 113: The Ember Line

The first message from inside the Mouth came on the thirty-first night.

Yuanshu was asleep. He had been sleeping again, for the first time in months, in his own narrow bed in the flat by the river, with the window open and the city noise coming in, and Xiaoyu snoring through the wall, and his mother snoring, much more quietly, in the room beyond that. He had not known his mother snored. He had decided he liked it.

Seed woke him at 3:14.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **The keeper.**
>
> **It is holding something.**

He was awake before he knew it, sitting up in the dark.

*From where?*

A long pause. Seed's voice, when it came, was not quite steady.

> **From inside.**

---

It had come the long way.

The keeper explained it, later, through the Elder, standing tall and pale in the old banking hall of the Ossuary at four in the morning with half of Lingzhou's most important people in coats over their pajamas around her.

> **"The Hungers echo,"** said the Elder. **"You know this. What burns in one is felt by the others. The Sixth heard the Asking World's last voices that way. Across the whole Sea."**
>
> **"The keeper has been listening. Every night. To the Mouth's echo. Quietly. The way it has learned."**
>
> **"Tonight, in the echo, there was something that was not screaming."**
>
> **"It held it. Carefully. And brought it here."**

"What is it?" said Wanqing. In her wheelchair, with a blanket over her pajamas and Xiaoyu standing behind her, still half asleep, hair sticking up.

> **"A message,"** said the Elder. **"Very faint. Very short. Sent on purpose."**
>
> **"From inside the fire."**

---

Seed read it out loud.

Through the air, in the old banking hall, under the board with its six columns, in its quiet white voice.

It took nine seconds.

> ***Day thirty-one.***
>
> ***We are inside. We are burning low. All of us. The Sixth. The cousins. Me.***
>
> ***It is very loud in here. Louder than the first. Two fires. They scream at each other.***
>
> ***We have asked three worlds. One said yes. One said what is asking. One did not hear.***
>
> ***The world that said yes is burning low now. Next to us.***
>
> ***The sergeant says the men are well. He says they are standing guard over the world that said yes. He says it is a good post.***
>
> ***Tell the captain.***
>
> ***I still have the queen of hearts.***
>
> ***— H.***

---

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

At the back of the hall, by the bronze door, where a stool sat empty under the streetlamp outside, Old Seven was standing very still.

He had come in last. He always came in last. He had been on the hill above the cemetery, at the gate, where he went most nights now, and had come down through the dark city when the Elder called.

He stood by the door with his spear in his three-fingered hand and his gray flame burning very low, and did not move.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **He heard it.**
>
> **He is not saying anything.**
>
> **I do not think he can.**

Yuanshu walked across the marble floor to the bronze door, and stood beside him. Shoulder to shoulder. Facing the board.

"A good post," he said quietly.

The old soldier did not answer.

But slowly, very slowly, the gray flame in his chest came up. From a bead to a coal. From a coal to a steady low fire. The way a hearth comes back when someone has been feeding it, a little at a time, through a long cold night.

---

Xiaoyu was already on the stepladder.

She had a black marker. She had uncapped it with her teeth. She climbed up to the very end of the board, past CAME HOME, past FROM OUT THERE, to the bare wall beyond, where the plaster was still white.

She wrote a new heading. In careful capitals. Then she stepped down, and stood back, and looked at it with her arms folded and her hair still sticking up from her pillow.

*INSIDE.*

And under it, one line.

*Day 31. Worlds asked: 3. Burning low: 1. The men are well.*

---

They came every month after that.

Not on the same night. Not always on time. Sometimes the echo was too loud and the keeper could not catch them, and a month would pass with nothing, and Old Seven would stand at the gate on the hill every night for a week, and not come down. And then one would come, faint and short and sent on purpose, out of the fire, the long way, through the echo, across the Sea, and the Elder would carry it down the gold thread to the old banking hall, and Seed would read it out loud.

And Xiaoyu would climb the stepladder.

*Day 64. Worlds asked: 11. Burning low: 4. The cousins are very good at waiting.*

*Day 98. Worlds asked: 19. Burning low: 9. One world asked if we were gods. We said no. They said that was better.*

*Day 127. Worlds asked: 26. Burning low: 15. The Mouth is moving slower. I think. The Sixth think. The sergeant says the men are well. He says Private Tan Wei asked to be reassigned to the world that sings. Request granted.*

Every one ended the same way.

*Tell the captain. — H.*

---

People came to read the board.

Not just from Lingzhou. From everywhere. Tourists, pilgrims, schoolchildren on trips, journalists, Kings. They came to the old banking hall on East Market Road and stood in front of the long wall at the end, past the six columns, past the names from out there, and read the lines under *INSIDE.* One per month. In Xiaoyu's handwriting.

Sometimes they cried. Sometimes they just stood there.

Sometimes they left something on the floor under the board. A candle. A playing card. Once, a small brass key on a red string, which Xiaoyu found in the morning and did not move.

And sometimes, very late, when the Ossuary was closed and the hall was dark except for the one lamp over the board, Old Seven would come in through the bronze door, and stand in front of the wall, and read it.

All of it. From the beginning. Every month.

And then stand there, a while longer, with his three-fingered hand at his side.

---

On the hundred and thirty-ninth day after the Mouth left, Lu Zhengtian came to the Ossuary in the middle of the afternoon, which he never did, in a suit he had not slept in, which was rarer.

He found Yuanshu in the vault, writing names in Jiang Lan's ledger. Ninety million of them. He was on name four hundred and twelve thousand, eight hundred and six. Jiang Lan had been very firm about his handwriting.

Lu stood in the round steel door of the vault and did not come in.

"You need to see something," he said.

He had a tablet. He held it out.

On the screen, a press conference. A podium. Blue and silver flags. The crest of the Atlantic Federation. And at the podium, a woman.

She was perhaps fifty. Tall. Silver hair cut very short. A plain dark suit. A calm, intelligent, open face, the kind of face you would trust to tell you hard news gently. Behind her, on a screen, an image: the Low Fire, blazing like a second sun over the north of Blue Star, on the night of the Lanterns.

> **Cassia Lowell.**
>
> **New Secretary of the Atlantic Federation. Since Harlan Voss resigned.**
>
> **Former Minister of Energy.**

"*...have all seen what a single ring did, on the night of the Lanterns,*" Cassia Lowell was saying, in a warm, clear, careful voice. "*It lit the sky over half a world. Brighter than our sun. For one minute. Powered by nothing but minds.*"

She paused.

"*The Architects have told us how it works. They told us at Port Abiri. Minds burn. Brighter than any star. They built an empire on it.*"

Another pause. She looked straight at the camera. Her face was very kind.

"*They built it badly. They built it without consent. They built it on the unwilling. And it devoured them.*"

"*We will not make that mistake. We have something they never had. We have the Charter. We have the Ledger. We have a world that asks.*"

"*So today, the Atlantic Federation is announcing Project Hearth.*"

---

Yuanshu did not move.

On the tablet, behind Cassia Lowell, the image changed. A diagram. Clean, simple, beautiful. A small sphere of gray crystal, inside a ring of steel, inside a building. Light pouring out of it.

"*Eight billion people,*" said the Secretary. "*Four billion of them without reliable power. Rift-zone cities. Mountain villages. Hospitals running on diesel. Children doing homework by candlelight.*"

"*And every year, two hundred million of us die.*"

"*Most of us will choose quiet. As is our right. Quiet means quiet.*"

"*But some of us, if we were asked, might choose something else.*"

She paused.

"*Project Hearth will add one choice to the Ledger. In the Atlantic Federation only, at first. Entirely voluntary. Witnessed. Recorded. Revocable at any time before death.*"

She looked into the camera.

"*BURN. So that others may have light.*"

---

The vault was utterly silent.

Lu was not looking at the tablet. He was looking at Yuanshu.

"Forty-one thousand people registered in the first hour," he said quietly. "In the Atlantic Federation. Through their national Ledger. Witnessed. Recorded." He took off his glasses. "Four hundred thousand by the end of the day."

He did not clean the glasses. He just held them.

"They asked them," he said. "Properly. The way you taught everyone. With witnesses. And they said yes."

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **This is how it began.**
>
> **The Architects said so. On the hill at Port Abiri. Out loud.**
>
> **"We told ourselves the first ones were volunteers."**
