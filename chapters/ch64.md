## Chapter 64: Inside the River

They heard it with their own ears on the sixth night.

Not through Sokolova's radio. Not as a vibration in the teeth. A sound, coming across the black glass out of the north in the dark, so low and so long that at first Yuanshu thought it was the wind changing.

Then the wind stopped, and the sound didn't.

It rose. A slow, wavering, enormous note, like a horn blown through water, like a hundred thousand throats all trying to scream at once through closed mouths. It went on for a full minute, rising, rising, until the frost on the glass around the camp began to hum in sympathy, a thin high whine like a fingernail on the rim of a wineglass.

Then it fell.

And in the silence after it, every one of them was awake and sitting up with their hearts pounding, and nobody said a word.

---

"How far?" said Su Mingli.

She was standing at the edge of the ridge with her coat open in the cold, looking north into the dark. Her voice was perfectly level.

> **I cannot tell exactly. Sound travels strangely over glass.**
>
> **Thirty kilometers. Perhaps forty.**
>
> **It is moving south at walking pace. Along the valley.**
>
> **It will reach us before dawn.**

"Can we outrun it?"

"No," said Yan Shuang. She had not lit her cigarette. She was holding it between two fingers, unlit, and her one hand was perfectly steady. "Nothing outruns it. I've seen the tracks. I've seen what's left of the camps." She looked at Su Mingli in the starlight. "We used to hear it, some nights, on the long runs. The company had a rule. When you hear the sound, you stop the truck, turn off the engine, turn off the lights, and sit very still with your eyes closed until it goes away. And if it doesn't go away..." She shrugged. "Then it doesn't."

"Did that work?"

"Mostly." Yan Shuang put the cigarette in her mouth. "We lost four trucks in eleven years. All four drivers were young. All four of them panicked." She looked at the Mo twins, at Bai Lu, at Qin Hao. "Young people panic."

"We've done this," said Lingyue quietly. "On Concordia. Three hundred people. We sat in a crater and went quiet and the threads went away."

"With a monk," said Su Mingli. "And a King in every corner. And Sister Ning." She did not turn from the dark. "Out here, it's us."

---

It was Yuanshu who looked down at the valley.

The river of the dead was still going past below. Even at night. Even now. Scrape, scrape, scrape, two hundred thousand gray shapes moving north in the starlight like a slow tide. Not looking up. Not hearing the sound. Not caring.

The thing in the sky couldn't see them. Lu Xuanming had written it. *The dead could walk right up to it, and it didn't even look at them.*

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **I know what you are thinking.**

*Is it stupid?*

> **I do not know.**
>
> **I think it is the only thing on this plain that it has never once looked at.**

---

They went down into the river an hour before dawn.

All fourteen of them, and the eighty-eight of the Third Battalion. Down the slope in the dark, onto the crushed glass of the road, into the slow gray tide of the dead. The march did not slow for them. It did not part. It simply flowed on around them, the way it flowed around the stones, cold bones brushing their sleeves and their shoulders, the dry endless scrape filling their ears.

And they walked with it.

North. Toward the sound. At the pace of the dead. Shoulder to shoulder with skeletons in the rags of greatcoats and wedding dresses and school uniforms. Fourteen living people and eighty-eight old soldiers hidden in the middle of a river of two hundred thousand, walking straight toward the thing that ate minds.

Third Battalion closed around them without being asked. Sergeant Huo at the front. Old Seven beside him. Rank on rank of gray bones on every side, a moving wall of the dead, with the living in the center like the yolk in an egg.

"Close your eyes," said Su Mingli, very softly, walking in the middle of them all. "Hold on to the person in front of you. Walk. Think about the walking. The sound your feet make. Nothing else."

They closed their eyes.

They walked.

---

The sound came again.

Closer now. Much closer. Not a horn across water now. A roar. A vast, wavering, shuddering howl that came up through the soles of their boots and into their knees and their spines and the roots of their teeth, so loud that the glass under their feet hummed, so loud that you couldn't think, couldn't hear yourself think, which was, Yuanshu understood suddenly, the point.

It was trying to make them loud. It was trying to frighten them into thinking.

He held the quiet. He thought about his feet. Left. Right. The crunch of glass. The cold brush of a bone sleeve against his hand.

> **I can see it.**
>
> **Do not open your eyes.**
>
> **I will tell you what it is. You do not need to see it.**

---

> **It is walking up the valley toward us. On the road. Against the march.**
>
> **It is very tall. Forty meters. Perhaps more.**
>
> **It is made of the same crystal as the core under the bridge. Gray. Cloudy. Cracked all over.**
>
> **It has legs. Many legs. Like a spider made of glass. Each one as thick as a tree.**
>
> **And all over its surface, Yuanshu, every centimeter of it, there are mouths.**
>
> **Small. Human-sized. Open. Thousands of them. Tens of thousands. That is the mark. The ring of screaming mouths. It is not a symbol. It is a picture of this.**
>
> **The sound is them.**
>
> **They are the people inside it. Screaming.**

He walked. Left. Right.

> **It has threads.**
>
> **Not falling from the sky. Coming out of it. Out of the mouths. Hundreds of them. Silver. They hang down from it and drag across the ground like the tentacles of a jellyfish in the sea, sweeping back and forth across the road as it walks.**
>
> **It is sweeping the march. Feeling for anything warm.**
>
> **It is two hundred meters away.**

---

The dead did not stop.

The river of bones walked straight toward the thing, scrape, scrape, scrape, the way it had walked for thirty-one years, and the thing walked straight toward it, and when they met, the river simply parted around its legs. Flowed past them. Under them. Two hundred thousand skeletons walking between the legs of a forty-meter spider of screaming glass as if it were a bridge.

And it did not look at them.

Its threads swept through them. Across them. Through ribs and skulls and rags. And found nothing. Nothing warm. Nothing thinking. Nothing to learn.

> **One hundred meters.**
>
> **It does not see the dead.**
>
> **Keep walking.**

The roar was everything now. Yuanshu could feel it in his chest like a second heartbeat. He could feel Lingyue's hand on his shoulder from behind, gripping hard. He could feel Bai Lu, ahead of him, shaking.

Left. Right.

> **Fifty meters.**
>
> **The threads are passing over the battalion.**
>
> **Through them.**
>
> **Over us.**

Something cold and fine as cobweb brushed the top of his head. And went on.

Left. Right.

> **It did not stop.**
>
> **Thirty meters. We are under it.**
>
> **Its legs are on either side of us.**

---

It was Yan Shuang who broke.

He felt it happen. Not heard. Felt. A few rows ahead, in the middle of the battalion, a flare of something hot and bright and terrible, like a match struck in a dark room full of gas. Not fear. Something older than fear. Something that had been sitting at the bottom of a woman for eleven years, waiting.

Guilt.

Under the thing. Under the mouths. Inside the river of the dead she had driven north and south across this plain for eleven years, selling them by the kilo. Hearing the sound she had heard on a hundred long night runs, with the hollows sitting in rows in the back of her truck, looking north.

She could not hold it. Nobody could have.

*I drove them,* her whole mind was screaming. *I drove them, I drove them, I drove them here.*

The roar stopped.

---

Silence. Total. Sudden. Like a door slammed in a cathedral.

> **It heard her.**
>
> **It has stopped walking.**
>
> **Every thread is turning.**

Yuanshu opened his eyes.

He couldn't help it. He looked up, and saw it, straight above him, blotting out the paling sky: a vast underside of cloudy gray crystal, cracked and seamed like old ice, and in every crack and seam, mouths. Human mouths. Thousands. Open. Silent now. All of them, every one, slowly turning in the crystal, the way a crowd's faces turn toward a sound.

Toward a short broad woman in a sheepskin coat in the middle of the march, standing frozen with her one hand over her mouth.

And down out of the mouths, slow and silver, a hundred threads came drifting toward her like rain.

---

Everything happened at once.

Sergeant Vera Sokolova, beside Yan Shuang, did not think. Did not decide. She grabbed the older woman by the collar of her coat and pulled her down, hard, onto the crushed glass, and lay on top of her, and pressed her own forehead against Yan Shuang's forehead and whispered something in Northern, over and over, very fast, eyes closed. A lullaby. A counting rhyme. Something you'd say to a child in a storm.

Bai Lu dropped to her knees beside them and pressed both palms flat to Yan Shuang's temples, and green light flooded out of them, soft and warm, the color of new leaves, and Yuanshu felt the hot bright flare of guilt dim under it, like a coal under a blanket.

The eighty-eight soldiers of Third Battalion closed in. Without an order. Gray bones folding inward around the three women on the ground, rank on rank, shoulder to shoulder, bending over them, a dome of the dead. The threads came down and hit the dome and went through it, through bones, through rags, and found nothing, and swept on.

And Lingyue stepped out of the line with her sword.

She did not light it. Not yet. She stood over the dome of bones with her eyes closed and the blade held low, and when the first thread found its way through a gap between two skeletons and drifted down toward Yan Shuang's head, she lit just the very tip of the sword, a single bead of white-gold like a needle of sunlight, and touched it to the thread, and the thread parted with a tiny violin snap.

Then the next. Then the next. Eyes closed. Not a sound. A girl standing in a river of the dead under a mountain of screaming glass, cutting cobwebs with a needle of light, one by one, as calmly as if she were sewing.

> **She is bending the light.**
>
> **All of it into the tip. Away from the battalion. Away from the march.**
>
> **Not one bone burned.**

---

The mouths waited.

For a long time. A minute. Two. The vast gray underside of the thing hung over them, silent, its threads hanging motionless in the air, its ten thousand mouths open and turned toward a place in the river where there was now nothing to hear.

Under the dome of bones, Yan Shuang had gone still. Her eyes were closed. Sokolova was still whispering. Bai Lu's hands were still glowing, softer and softer.

Then, slowly, the mouths began to turn away.

The threads drew back up into the crystal. One by one.

And somewhere far above, with a sound like a glacier shifting, the Wailing Monarch lifted one tree-thick leg of cloudy glass, and set it down further south, on the far side of the battalion. And then another. And then another.

And as it walked away, south, down the valley, against the endless river of the dead, its ten thousand mouths opened again, all at once, and it began, once more, to scream.

---

They walked north inside the river for three more hours before anyone spoke.

When Su Mingli finally led them up out of the road and onto the eastern slope of the valley, in full daylight, with the sound only a faint far moaning on the southern wind, Yan Shuang sat down on the first rock she came to and put her face in her one hand.

Nobody said anything to her.

After a while, Sergeant Vera Sokolova came and sat down on the rock beside her. She took out her own cigarettes, and lit one, and held it out.

Yan Shuang took it.

They smoked it between them, passing it back and forth, without a word, looking down at the river of the dead going past below.

> **Yuanshu.**
>
> **When it screamed. At the end. When it walked away.**
>
> **I was listening to the mouths. I could not help it.**
>
> **Most of them are not words anymore. Just sound.**
>
> **But some of them are.**
>
> **The same words. The same ones as the crystal under the bridge.**
>
> **"Is anyone."**

He stood on the slope and looked south, where the thing had gone, a tiny gray shape on the far horizon, still walking, still screaming.

*One day,* he thought, *I'm going to ask you too.*
