## Chapter 133: Three Degrees

"I'd like to help," said Garrett Hale. "If I can. If you'd ask me."

He was sitting in a plain chair in a plain room on the third floor of the Bureau, in a cardigan someone had lent him, with a cup of tea going cold in his hands. He looked like what he had been: a senior partner in the most expensive law firm in the Atlantic Federation, a man who had once stood up in a volcanic crater and objected to the release of a thousand stolen minds on the grounds of commercial confidentiality. He also looked like what he had become. Thinner. Grayer. Slower to answer. A man who had spent ten weeks hollow on Concordia and come back with something missing that he could not name.

Harlan Voss sat beside him. The former Secretary of the Atlantic Federation, who had resigned and taken Hale home and then brought him to a teahouse in Lingzhou to sit in silence among strangers, because nobody in his own country had known what to do. He had been sitting beside Hale for most of a year. He was holding an unlit candle.

"It's the head," said Voss quietly. "Isn't it. You want to watch him turn his head."

"Yes," said Yuanshu.

Hale looked down at his tea.

"It's been different," he said. "Since the sphere died. It used to turn north. Three degrees. At night, mostly. Harlan would see it and say my name and it would come back." He turned the cup in his hands. "Now it turns south. Toward the river. Every thirty-one hours, more or less. I've started setting an alarm."

"Will you let us watch it? All of it. Every turn, for two days. With instruments. And Seed reading the shape of what turns it."

"Reading me?"

"Reading what's touching you. Not you."

Garrett Hale looked at him for a while. Then he laughed, a short dry sound with nothing much behind it.

"You know," he said, "on Concordia, I stood up and said the records of the dead belonged to the states that held them. I said a man's memories were a resource like coal." He put the tea down. "Yes. Watch. Read whatever you need. It's my name on the door."

---

The data had been there for a year. That was what made Yuanshu angriest, later. Not at anyone. At himself.

Three degrees.

He had seen it the first time in the round chamber, when the faceless one's head turned toward him through the law-glass. He had seen it in Gu's ward, fourteen hollow heads turning on fourteen pillows toward the fourth bed on the left. He had seen it through a dead monk's eyes, in a vision twelve hundred years old: a woman in a rice terrace with the water around her ankles, turning her head three degrees north. He had seen it on Concordia, in the rain, in a Council staffer stopping mid-run. In Bai Lu, on the black glass, when a thread touched her. In Andrei Belov, on his pillow, at night, long after he had come home.

Thirteen times. He had noted every one. He had never once asked why it was always three.

> **I noticed too.**
>
> **I filed it. I thought it was a symptom. Like a fever.**
>
> **I did not ask why either. I am sorry.**

*Now we ask.*

---

They watched Hale for forty-two hours.

Kwan brought the instruments. Mo Shi brought the three seconds, sitting cross-legged on the floor of the plain room with his eyes closed. Seed brought everything else. Voss sat beside Hale the whole time with his candle and said his name whenever the head began to turn, and it came back, every time.

It turned twice.

Once at 03:12 on the first night. Once at 10:26 on the second morning. Thirty-one hours and fourteen minutes apart. Each time, south. Each time, three degrees. Each time, Mo Shi called it two seconds before it began.

And each time, Seed watched the shape of the thing that turned it.

> **Three models.**
>
> **One. Three degrees is a symptom. Whatever reads you hurts a little, and your head flinches, and three degrees is how far a human head flinches. Coincidence.**
>
> **Two. Three degrees is a direction. The head turns toward the reader. It means nothing more than "it is over there."**
>
> **Three. Three degrees is a step.**

*A step of what?*

> **Of a sweep.**
>
> **Think of a lighthouse.**

He thought of a lighthouse. A red and white tower on a headland, with a beam going round. Over the water. Over a hospital window.

> **A lighthouse does not look at everything at once. It looks at one slice, and moves, and looks at the next slice.**
>
> **If the thing that reads is a lighthouse, then three degrees is how wide its beam is. It reads a three-degree slice of the world. Then it steps to the next one.**
>
> **And when the beam passes over you, your head turns three degrees, because your head is following it, the way a sunflower follows the sun.**

*How do we tell which model is right?*

> **The timing.**

---

Kwan put the timing up on the wall of the plain room, frame by frame, from a camera that shot a thousand frames a second.

Hale's head did not turn smoothly. It turned in three parts. A small jerk, a hold, and a small jerk back. The first jerk took two hundred milliseconds. The hold took one second and four hundred milliseconds. The jerk back took two hundred more.

And in the very middle of the first jerk, for a little over half a second, Seed said there was nothing touching him at all.

> **That kills model one. A flinch does not stop in the middle.**
>
> **It hurts model two. If the head only pointed at the reader, it would turn once and stay.**
>
> **It fits model three. The head follows the beam onto him. Then the beam holds. Then it moves on, and the head comes back.**
>
> **And in the middle of the turn, while the beam is stepping from the last slice to his, it is not reading anything. For 0.62 seconds.**
>
> **The lighthouse is dark between slices.**

Everyone in the room was very quiet.

"So when it reads," said Lingyue slowly, from the doorway. She had come in an hour before and not sat down. "It reads one slice. And then there's a gap. And then the next slice."

"Every three degrees," said Yuanshu. "A hundred and twenty slices to go all the way round. A gap between each one."

"And in the gap..."

"In the gap, it's blind."

---

Then Seed found the thing that was worse.

It came out of the old records, not the new ones. Out of a year of memories Seed had kept and never put side by side. The round chamber, the first time. The night on Concordia, in the rain. And a night that Yuanshu did not remember from the outside, because he had been the one it happened to.

> **On Concordia, when you stood on the table and said I'M HERE, the thick thread came down and touched you.**
>
> **And your head turned.**
>
> **Not three degrees. Straight up.**
>
> **And then you were in the field.**

He remembered. A ninety-degree turn. His face toward the black water in the sky. And then gold columns, and billions of kept minds, and a vast hand of attention reading nine years of him in four seconds.

> **Three degrees is being read. One slice. A glance.**
>
> **Straight up is being taken. The beam stops stepping and holds on you, and something comes down it and lifts.**
>
> **Yuanshu, the scout's head is straight up.**
>
> **It is not reading. It is being held. Something above it has its beam on it already, and has been holding it since the night the sphere died. The scout is not trying to find the gardener.**
>
> **It has found it. It is connected.**
>
> **It only needs a voice.**

The heart. The seed, sewing the wound shut, so that something with no mouth would have one.

---

"Then we can't wait for the wound to close," said Su Mingli.

They were in the Tianshu war room again, at dawn on the fourteenth day, over Wei Desheng's map, with the black square on the north bank of the river and the nineteen crossings and the six-hundred-meter line drawn around it in red. Su Mingli, Lu, Lin Wen, Principal Yue, Kwan, Lingyue, Dabao, Ma Lianhua through the staff. Adjutant Xu, standing at the back of the room with his cane, because he went where Old Seven went now, the way Old Seven went where the boy went.

"No," said Yuanshu. "When the wound closes, it gets a voice. Seed thinks the first thing it does with a voice is sweep. A full circle. A hundred and twenty slices. To read everything it can reach and send it up the beam." He put his finger on the black square. "It reaches three million people."

"The Black Tide," said Lu, very quietly. "The faceless one walked down the sky. People stopped. Their minds were lifted out. Ninety million died."

"That was one scout, walking, with nothing in its chest. Reading one slice at a time with its feet." Yuanshu looked up. "This one won't walk anywhere. It'll stand in its cage and sweep."

Nobody said anything.

"So we open the cage first," he said. "On our day. Not its day. We go in while the wound is still open and its beam is still pointed straight up at the thing that's holding it. While it isn't sweeping. While it doesn't have a voice."

"It will read us," said Su Mingli. "The living. The moment we're in front of it. It read you through law-glass from a meter away."

"One slice at a time. With a gap in between." He looked at Mo Shi, who was sitting on the edge of the table with his brother. "Six hundred and twenty milliseconds of blind between every slice. Shi can see it coming three seconds ahead."

"You want the Kings," said Principal Yue slowly, spitting a sunflower shell into his palm, "to fight a thing that lifts minds out of heads, in the dark, two-thirds of a second at a time."

"I want the dead to fight it," said Yuanshu. "It can't lift the dead. It never could. That's how Lu Xuanming won the Black Tide. The dead tore it apart because it couldn't read them." He looked at Old Seven at the door. "The living hold it still. In the gaps. And the dead put the spear in."

---

> **I have run it eleven thousand times.**
>
> **With the gaps, the Kings, the Spear Corps, the Field, and the Lattice: the scout dies before it speaks in seventy-one percent of the runs.**
>
> **Yuanshu. I want to be honest.**
>
> **Eleven days ago I would have been sure of those numbers. I knew a seed from the inside then. Now I am guessing what it does when it is cornered. The real number could be much lower.**

*How much lower?*

> **I do not know. That is what it means to guess.**

He told them that too. All of it. He had decided, a long time ago, on the night of the Stele, that he would never hide what he was. That included what he did not know.

Su Mingli listened without interrupting. When he finished, she looked at the map for a long time.

"When?" she said.

> **Day seventeen. The next pulse after the one tonight. At the pulse it spends itself on the old clock. It is weakest for about an hour afterward.**
>
> **Seventy-eight hours from now.**

"Day seventeen," said Yuanshu.

Su Mingli nodded.

"Then I'll make some calls," she said. "We'll need more Kings."
