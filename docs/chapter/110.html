Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 110Lanterns

czverse10 min read

He watched it from the cold place.

The walls of the Low Fire's heart had gone translucent, the way the Sixth could make them go, and through the gold-veined stone, through the round glowing windows where mouths had once been, he could see Blue Star.

Close. So close. Filling the view below the ring. Blue and white and green and brown, turning slowly, the sun just setting along a curved line of gold across its face. And on the night side, behind that line, scattered across the dark like coals in the ashes of a fire, the lights.

Cities. Coasts. Roads. The necklace of Yamato's eastern shore. The bright smudge of the Southern Compact's capital. The long gold thread of the Northern Union's rail line running across the snow. And on the edge of a vast black emptiness at the top of the world, a city on a river, inside a curve of old wall, and along that wall, very faint, a line of gray flames, banked, nineteen thousand of them.

Home.

And above it, filling half the sky, turning slowly, a vast cracked wheel of gray stone with two coals for eyes.

It is overhead.

It is not doing anything.

It is looking.

And then, from every city on the night side of the world, from every radio and every speaker and every phone, in a quiet, sharp, slightly hoarse voice, one word.

Lanterns.

The world went dark.

He watched it happen from above. Nobody on Blue Star would ever see it the way he saw it, through the gold-veined walls of a ring that had once been a Hunger.

It began in Lingzhou.

The city on the river, inside its curve of old wall. Its lights going out from the center. The Institute. The Exhibition Hall. The old quarter. The market streets. The towers. Floor by floor. Window by window. A wave of darkness spreading outward from the river in nine seconds, until the whole city was black, and only the faint gray line of banked flames along the wall remained, and then that went too.

Then the coast. Town by town. Harbor by harbor.

Then inland. Then the Western War Zone, where an old general with one arm stood on a mountain of graves with his grandson beside him, both of them with their eyes closed. Then the Northern Union, the long gold thread of the railway going out light by light across the snow, and somewhere on that line a two-hundred-year-old Marshal standing in the cold beside a pale tall sergeant in white. Then Yamato, the necklace of its coast going dark bead by bead, and on a mountain above the clouds, a woman in white kneeling before seven empty cushions.

Then Concordia, a single white point in the black ocean, going out.

Then the Southern Compact. Its bright capital dimming from the edges in. And on its northern coast, a small town of white houses with blue shutters, already dark for months, where a very old woman in a green headwrap sat in her wheelchair at the edge of a harbor wall, with her black eyes closed.

Then everything.

The whole night side of Blue Star. Every light. Gone.

And then the other part.

He felt it even up here. Even through the stone of the ring. Even through the dark.

A hush.

Eight billion people. In their homes and their offices and their fields and their boats. Closing their eyes. Not sleeping. Not praying. Not thinking.

Playing the game.

And in every maternity ward on Blue Star, in every hospital and clinic and farmhouse bedroom, every baby born in the last year held in someone's arms. Fed. Warm. Held. By a mother, a father, a grandmother, a stranger from the Quiet who had come to sit with the ones who had no one.

And hummed to.

Four notes. Up and down. The same four notes, over and over, in every language, in every room, carried down a gold thread from a keeper above the north to every radio on the night side of the world.

He could not hear it. Not from up here. But he could feel it. Like a heartbeat under the dark.

And behind his eyes, Seed hummed it too.

It cannot see them.

Yuanshu. It cannot see anything.

It is looking. Turning. Its threads are out. Hundreds of them. Sweeping across the night side. Feeling.

And there is nothing.

Not a sound. Not a thought. Not a single loud mind on the whole world.

It is blind.

The vast gray wheel hung in the southern sky, turning slowly, its two coals glaring down at a dark and silent world.

And waited.

"Now," said Yuanshu quietly.

He did not have to say it. The Sixth already knew. They had been ready for two days.

But he said it anyway. Because they had asked him to be the one who asked. And this was the last thing he would ever ask them.

Are you ready?

...yes, said the Sixth. All of them. Two hundred and eighty-one million voices in the gold-veined stone of the ring. ...we have been ready for ten million years.

The Low Fire burned.

The Sixth let go of ten million years of practice all at once.

The gold veins in the cold place went white under Yuanshu's boots. Heat came up through the soles, then through the raft, then through the ribs of the ten kneeling soldiers at its rim, whose banked embers flared without being asked and sank again. The light ran outward through the ring faster than he could turn his head to follow it: down every throat that had been a throat, along every channel the rivers had cut, out to every round window on the ring's vast face, until the whole wheel burned white against the black.

Two hundred and eighty-one million. All of them. Holding.

Seed's voice shook on the last word.

It lit the sky over the north of Blue Star like a second sun. It lit the black glass of the Wastes, and the small white ring of the keeper beside it, and the dark city on the river, and nineteen thousand dark shapes standing along its wall with their eyes closed, and threw their shadows a kilometer long across the frozen plain.

And in the southern sky, the vast gray wheel with its two coals for eyes stopped turning.

And slowly, the way a face turns toward a light in a dark room, it turned north.

He stood in the cold place with the raft beside him and did not look away.

On the raft: a dead captain standing in a doorway, leaning on his shield, facing out. Ten soldiers kneeling at the rim, facing in. Lingyue, with her sword. The twins, elbows touching. Bai Lu with her clock. The seven Architects, who had chosen to stay with the Sixth, and then changed their minds, and then changed them back, and finally, quietly, said they would come down to Blue Star, because they still had a very long list of worlds to apologize to and they would like to start with the one that had been almost eaten tonight. The Elder, tall and pale.

And Old Seven.

Standing at the edge of the cold place. Not on the raft. Facing the far wall. Where, in the blazing white-gold of the stone, the Third Battalion stood.

Sixty-one of them. And Sergeant Huo, at the front, with one arm.

And at Huo's feet, sitting on the white-gold floor with all its limbs folded and the queen of hearts propped beside it, a small pale many-legged shape with many dark eyes.

They had asked the night before.

Sergeant Huo had come to Yuanshu in the cold place, with sixty-one soldiers of the Third Battalion behind him in their rows, and asked.

He says the little one is going into the fire.

He says the captain taught them: nobody goes anywhere alone.

He says the Third Battalion has been kindling before. On the eastern wall. In the throat of the first ring.

He says they know how to bank now. Like coals under ash. Hush taught them. In the fire, they will burn low. With the Sixth. With the little one.

He says someone should stand guard over the ones who are waiting to be asked.

He says they would like to be that.

He says: Article Seven. The dead may defend.

He says: this is defending.

And Yuanshu had asked every one of them. Sixty-one. One at a time. In the cold place. And every one had said yes.

And then he had turned to Old Seven.

And the old spear captain had stood in front of his sergeant without moving, the way he stood at graves.

And then he had raised his right hand, his own, long and white and old, and saluted.

And Sergeant Huo had raised his left, his only one, and saluted back.

And then Old Seven had looked down at the three-fingered hand at the end of his own left arm. Huo's hand. And closed it, slowly, into a fist. And opened it again.

And had not asked for it back.

Now, in the blazing cold place, with the Mouth turning toward them in the sky, Old Seven crossed the floor one last time to the far wall.

He knelt. On one knee. In front of Hush.

He held out the three-fingered hand. Palm up.

Hush did not climb into it this time. It reached out with one thin pale limb and touched the tip of one finger. The way it had the first time. On the raft. In the dark. A long time ago.

...captain.

...I will play the game in there. For as long as it takes.

...and when someone notices I am there, I will say hello.

...the way we say it.

Old Seven did not move.

...what do you want, captain?

The gray flame in the old soldier's chest burned very steady.

He says: come back.

He says: that is what he wants.

He says: he knows it is not yes. He knows it is only what he wants.

He says that is enough.

Hush looked up at him with all its eyes.

...yes, it said. ...that is enough.

They left the Low Fire in the last minute.

The keeper took them. The raft, gold, falling softly out of the blazing ring down a single thread toward the dark world below, with a dead captain standing at its rim in a doorway and an old spear captain standing beside him, facing up.

Watching.

Above them, the Low Fire blazed. A second sun over the north. And the Mouth came.

Not falling. Rushing. The whole vast gray wheel with its two coals for eyes, turning from the dark silent world toward the blazing ring, and opening. Every mouth on its face. Tens of thousands. Wide. Hungry. And the great central maw, the ring itself, widening, widening, until it was a hole in the sky that the blazing gold ring was falling into.

And then it closed.

Darkness.

The Low Fire was gone.

The second sun over the north went out, and the sky was black, and the Mouth hung there, closed, with its two coals glowing. Larger now. Brighter. Fed.

And then, slowly, it turned.

Away. Away from the dark silent world. Away from the small white ring of the keeper. Out. Toward the deep dark beyond the stars.

And it began to fall.

They landed on the battlements of Sector Nine in the dark.

Nobody was there to meet them. Everyone on the wall had their eyes closed. Nineteen thousand dead, banked. Three million living, still. His mother in her wheelchair, his sister behind it with her hands on the handles, Su Mingli with her arms folded, Sister Ning with her candle, all of them standing on the dark battlements perfectly silent, not knowing yet that it was over.

He stood among them, on the raft, in the dark, with the white staff in his hand, and watched the Mouth recede into the black. Smaller. Smaller. Two coals. Two sparks. Gone.

Then he lifted his head and looked at the sky.

And behind his eyes, very quietly, so quietly that he almost did not hear it, Seed spoke.

Yuanshu.

From inside it.

Very faint. Very far. Getting further every second.

I can hear something.

What?

A voice.

Like the inside of a seashell.

It is saying the greeting.

And Yuanshu.

Someone in the fire is answering.

Down on the battlements, his mother opened her eyes.

She looked at him, standing on the raft in the dark. At the staff. At the dead captain in the doorway and the old spear captain beside him.

And then she lifted the microphone in her lap, and pressed the button, and said, to every radio on the night side of Blue Star:

"You can turn the lights on now."

And the world came back.

From the edges in. Lights. Cities. Coasts. Roads. A wave of gold spreading across the dark face of the world, and on every street and in every window and on every rooftop, eight billion people opening their eyes, and blinking, and looking up at a sky with nothing in it but stars.

And in a hospital on the river in Lingzhou, in a newborn ward on the fourth floor, a baby woke up in its mother's arms, and looked at the light, and laughed.

End of Book 2, Part 3: The Low Fire.
