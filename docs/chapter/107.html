Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 107The Fall

czverse7 min read

A Hunger does not ride the currents. It falls.

Yuanshu had not understood what that meant until he felt it.

They were standing in the cold place, the living and the dead, on the raft, when the Sixth began. The gold lines in the walls brightened. Just a little. Just enough. And a low sound came up through the floor, through the raft, through the bones of the Third Battalion and the soles of Yuanshu's boots. Not a grinding. Not a roar. Something deeper. Like a great slow breath being drawn in.

And then the Low Fire fell.

Not down. There was no down. Toward. Toward a small blue world very far away, the way a stone falls toward the ground, the way water falls toward the sea. All at once, with its whole vast weight, the way only a thing the size of a world can fall.

The walls of the cold place went translucent, the way the Sixth had shown them before. And through them, through the gold-veined stone of the ring, through its round glowing windows where mouths had been, Yuanshu saw the Star Sea.

Moving.

The far small lights were streaking.

Not drifting. Streaking. Lengthening into lines, white and gold and green and blue, sliding past the windows of the ring like the lights of a city seen from a train at night. The silver currents of old keepers and the gray wakes of Hungers flashed past and were gone. A husk world, gray and cracked, swelled in one window and shrank in the next and vanished behind them in a heartbeat.

And ahead, very far, so far that it was only a faint point of blue, a world.

It was not growing yet, not visibly. But it was there.

The Sixth say: thirty-three days.

Faster than anything has ever crossed this part of the Sea.

The Twins will reach Blue Star in sixty-eight.

We will be there first.

Lingyue let out a breath she seemed to have been holding for nine days.

"Thirty-five days early," she said.

If the Low Fire holds.

The Sixth say it is very weak. Ten million years of starving. It was never meant to fall this far this fast without eating.

They say they will hold it.

They say they have held harder things.

The Low Fire was enormous inside.

On the second day, the Sixth showed them. They left the cold place and walked out through the ring, the living and the dead and the Architects and Hush in Old Seven's hand, following a gold antlered shape through passages that had once been throats, and chambers that had once been stomachs, and corridors where red rivers of burning minds had once flowed inward toward the furnace.

The rivers were gone. The channels in the walls where they had run were dry now, dark stone, and the Sixth had filled them with their own low gold, running through the stone in fine lines, like veins, like the frost-patterns of a window catching the morning sun.

And everywhere, in the walls, in the stone, in the gold, faces.

Not burning. Not screaming. Not calm and closed-eyed and waiting out a winter.

Awake.

A few hundred million minds who had chosen to stay. Every world this ring had ever eaten that had wanted to remain. Looking out of the gold veins in the walls as the living and the dead walked past. Curious. Wondering. Some of them smiling, if their faces could smile.

A cluster of great slow turtle faces in one wall, who watched the raft go past for an hour, and said nothing, and at the end of the hour said, together, enormously patient: Thank you.

A flock of bright bird-like faces, flickering like sparks, who all shouted HELLO at once so loudly that Bai Lu dropped her clock.

And in a long low chamber deep in the ring, near where the coal had been, a wall full of small pale faces with many dark eyes, like Hush. The cousin world. Who had chosen to stay, all of them, every one.

Hush climbed down out of Old Seven's hand when they reached that chamber.

It walked to the wall on its thin pale legs, and stood in front of the small pale faces looking out of the gold.

And then, very slowly, it sat down. And folded its limbs. And went still.

And all the small pale faces in the wall, one by one, went still too.

...we are playing the game, said Hush, when Yuanshu came and crouched beside it. ...together.

...it is the first time I have played it with anyone since the crack.

...you can go. I will find you later.

He left it there.

When he looked back from the end of the long chamber, the small pale shape was sitting in front of the gold-veined wall with a hundred small pale faces looking out at it, all of them perfectly still, and the queen of hearts from the Mo twins' deck propped up against the stone beside it.

On the fifth day, he told his mother about the babies.

On the thread. In the cold place, with his hand on the gold line that ran back home, faster now, shorter, humming.

Seed told it, really. Seed's idea.

The Hunger does not hear sound. It hears minds. Thought. Feeling. Want.

A baby crying is not loud because of the sound. It is loud because it wants something very badly and does not know if it will come.

Hunger. Cold. Fear. Alone.

That is what the Hunger hears. Want, with no answer.

But a baby that is warm. And fed. And held. And hummed to.

A baby that has what it wants.

That baby is the quietest thing in the world.

Quieter than monks. Quieter than Hush.

I have been watching Bai Lu's memory of the hospital ward at the Academy where she trained. The newborn wing. At night. When every baby has been fed and every one is being held.

It is the stillest room I have ever seen. Stiller than the Listening Pavilion.

There was a long silence on the other end of the thread.

Then his mother's voice, very quiet, very sharp.

"Hold them," she said. "That's the answer. Not teach them. Hold them."

Yes.

Every baby on Blue Star. During the dimming. Fed. Warm. Held. By someone who is also playing the game.

And hummed to.

"Hummed to," said Wanqing.

The sound does not matter to the Hunger.

But it matters to the baby.

Four notes. Up and down. The same four notes. Over and over.

You hummed them to him every morning until he was eleven.

He was never once loud.

On the other end of the thread, very far away, across thirty days of dark, Yuanshu heard his mother start to cry.

And then, after a while, very softly, hum.

On the eleventh night, Lingyue found him in the long chamber with Hush.

He was sitting against the gold-veined wall, beside the small pale shape that was still playing the game with its cousins. He had been there for an hour. Not playing. Just sitting.

She sat down beside him with her sword across her knees.

"You're thinking very loudly," she said.

"I know."

"About what?"

He looked at the wall. At the hundred small pale faces looking out of the gold, perfectly still.

"About the Twins," he said. "There's no lock. There's no question I can ask that makes them stop. Billions of people in their fire, screaming, and I can't ask them anything because they can't hear, and even if they could, it wouldn't matter." He looked down at the staff across his knees. "Everything I know how to do is asking. And there's nothing to ask."

Lingyue was quiet for a long time.

"You asked the second ring if it wanted to come and meet us," she said. "And it did."

"The Sixth asked that."

"The Sixth asked because they heard the first ring die. And its last thought was someone asked." She ran her thumb along her sword. "The Twins heard that too. That's why they turned. They heard it." She looked at him. "Maybe they're not coming to eat Blue Star. Maybe they're coming to find out who asked."

"That's worse."

"I know," said Lingyue. "But it means they're listening."

On the twenty-ninth day, the Sixth called them to the cold place.

The choir was standing in a circle in the air, gold and antlered, and their low warm voices had gone very quiet.

...look, they said.

The walls went translucent.

Ahead, Blue Star. Not a point anymore. A disc. Small and blue and white and very beautiful, turning slowly in the dark, with its thin bright line of atmosphere around its edge like the rim of a glass.

And beyond it, from the other side, coming toward it out of the deep dark, two gray rings.

Close. Very close. Much closer than they should have been.

...they are faster than we thought, said the Sixth. ...they have not been eating. They have been saving everything. To fall.

...they will reach your world in five days.

...we will reach it in four.

And then, as they watched, the two gray rings did something.

They turned. Toward each other. The way two hands turn toward each other before they clasp.

And began, slowly, to merge.

...they are becoming one, said the Sixth. ...the way they do. When they find a field too rich for one.

...two furnaces. Two mouths. One hunger.

...we have never seen it happen to a world that was awake.
