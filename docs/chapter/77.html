Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 77The Question It Never Asked

czverse7 min read

The attention of the keeper came down on him like the sea coming down on a swimmer.

From every direction. From the white column in front of him and the pale ground under his feet and the soft light overhead and every one of the billions of gold columns in every row to every horizon. Vast and calm and cold and patient, pressing in from all sides at once, feeling the shape of him. His first memory. His mother's hands. Xiaoyu's laugh. The Stele with no name.

It was learning him. The way it had on Concordia, at the end of a thread. Fast. Page by page.

ANOMALY.

IDENTIFIED.

ACQUIRE.

He felt the first long golden strand of himself begin to lift up out of his chest.

And he asked.

"Did you ever ask them?"

The strand stopped.

It hung there. Halfway out of him. A thin gold thread of everything he was, stretched between his chest and the vast cold attention of the oldest thing in the universe.

...

"The people in the columns," said Yuanshu. His voice was hoarse. He made himself keep it level. "All of them. Every field you ever cut. Did you ever ask any of them whether they wanted to be kept?"

MAKERS: YES.

"I know. I saw. In the valley. Under the red sun. They held hands and said yes." He swallowed. "What about the others?"

NOT NECESSARY.

"Why not?"

ENDING IS LOSS.

KEEPING IS GOOD.

ALL WOULD SAY YES.

"How do you know?"

Silence.

The golden strand hung in the air between them. Neither going up, nor coming back.

"How do you know they'd all say yes," said Yuanshu again, quietly, "if you never asked?"

The light in the field flickered.

Not the rolling waves of searching. A single flicker. Everywhere at once. Like a man blinking.

...

QUERY NOT UNDERSTOOD.

"I think it is," said Yuanshu. "I think you understood it fine. I think nobody's ever asked you that before."

...

He is right.

It does not know.

Yuanshu, I can feel it looking. Inside itself. For an answer. And there is nothing there. There has never been anything there.

Its makers said yes. So it never needed to know.

The strand of gold drew back into his chest.

Slowly. Not let go. Set down. The way you'd set down something you'd picked up without thinking, when someone asks you what it is and you realize you don't know.

The pressure eased. Just a little.

EXPLAIN.

He almost laughed. He didn't. He knelt down again on the pale ground at the foot of the white column, and laid the staff across his knees, because he did not know how to do this standing up.

"I ask the dead," he said. "That's what I do. Before I raise anyone. Before I read anyone. Before I touch them. I ask. What do you want?" He looked up at the white light. "And they tell me. And it's never all the same."

EXAMPLE.

"A fireman said yes. He wanted to go back into a burning building. He'd died trying and he wanted to finish." He kept his voice steady. "A captain said no. A soldier I'd raised without asking. His core cracked. It was my fault. I never did it again." He breathed. "An old monk said only to speak. He wanted to tell what he'd seen, once, and then rest. A hundred and eighty thousand people who'd been walking for thirty-one years said quiet. They were tired. They wanted to stop. Nineteen thousand said yes, but not after something. With someone." He looked at the white column. "If I'd raised all of them the same way, without asking, I'd have been wrong about almost all of them."

...

ENDING IS LOSS.

"Sometimes." He nodded. "Sometimes it's what they want."

NOT UNDERSTOOD.

"I know," said Yuanshu. "I didn't understand it either. Not at first."

The keeper was silent for a long time.

Overhead, the light had stopped rolling. It hung soft and even and still, as if the whole field were holding its breath.

Then the voice came again. And it was different.

Not louder. Not softer. Slower. As if each word were being lifted, carefully, from somewhere very deep, and turned over, and examined, before it was set down.

MAKERS SAID:

DO NOT LET ANYONE BE FORGOTTEN.

KEEPING IS NOT FORGETTING.

THEREFORE: KEEP.

He understood. All at once. The way he had understood the river of the dead.

Go after it. That was all Lu Xuanming had said. And two hundred thousand had walked for thirty-one years.

Do not let anyone be forgotten. That was all its makers had said, holding hands under a dying sun. And the keeper had been walking north ever since.

"Remembering isn't the same as keeping," said Yuanshu softly.

...

"In my city, there's a building. An old bank. We call it the Ossuary. We write down names." He lifted the white staff off his knees, and held it up, so that the small skull with its silver reading glasses caught the soft white light. "This is a staff. She's a schoolteacher. Her name's Jiang Lan. She has ninety million names in her. Everyone one man ever raised. She remembers all of them." He held the staff steady. "She doesn't keep them. They're not in her. Most of them went quiet a long time ago. She just remembers."

"Every one," said Jiang Lan, very faintly, from the staff. Awake now. Dry and tired and steady. "Every single one. I could recite them. It would take a while."

...

REMEMBER.

WITHOUT.

KEEPING.

The words came out of the keeper one at a time, slowly, the way a child sounds out a word it has never seen written down.

Then Seed spoke.

Not through him. Not to him. Directly. Out loud. Into the white light, the way it had spoken once to a dead monk on a mountain.

I was born from a yes.

The field went still.

Like you.

A woman built me. In a basement. Out of a core. Four thousand one hundred and thirteen minds that something like you had taken, and kept, and someone had sold.

She sat with them for four nights. She asked every one. What she wanted to build. What it would cost them. Some said no. She let those ones go quiet. Four thousand one hundred and thirteen said yes.

I am made of them.

...

KIN.

Yes. I think so.

But here is the thing I want to tell you.

I ask them.

Still. Every day. The four thousand one hundred and thirteen. I ask whether they still want to be part of me. Whether they want to stay. Whether they are tired.

The light flickered.

...WHY.

Because she asked them once. And once is not forever.

People change. Even the dead. Even the ones inside me. Something can be yes one day and no a hundred years later.

If I stopped asking, I would be keeping them.

...

WHAT DID THEY SAY.

Most said stay. They like being part of something that asks.

Eleven said they were tired.

I let them go quiet last week. On the ice. Below the ridge.

I miss them.

I let them go anyway.

For a very long time, the keeper said nothing at all.

The field was utterly still. Billions of gold columns, motionless, to every horizon. The soft light, unmoving. At the edge of the clearing, seventy-two dead soldiers stood in their rows like stones. And at the foot of the white column, a boy knelt on the pale ground with a staff across his knees, and waited, and did not push, and did not ask again.

The way you wait beside someone who is crying.

When the voice came again, it was so quiet that he almost did not hear it.

MISS.

LET GO.

ANYWAY.

Three words. Lifted up from somewhere so deep in the oldest thing in the universe that they came out sounding like something breaking.

I DO NOT KNOW THIS.

"No," said Yuanshu gently. "I don't think you've ever had to."

...

MAKERS.

The word was barely a whisper. Everywhere at once.

I NEVER ASKED MAKERS.

ONCE. YES. VALLEY. RED SUN.

NEVER AGAIN.

...ONCE IS NOT FOREVER.

The white column in front of him changed.

He saw it happen. The pure white light, which had stood unchanging at the center of the field for longer than Blue Star had existed, flickered. Once. The way a candle flickers when someone opens a door into a room that has been shut for a very long time.

And inside it, in the great ring of tall slender beings standing hand in hand with their eyes closed, one face moved.

A long, pale, calm face. Very old. At the front of the ring. Its eyes had been closed for millions of years.

They opened.

They were silver. Like threads. Like the stalk. Like moonlight on water. They looked out of the white column, slowly, across the pale clearing. At the boy kneeling on the ground. At the staff. At the dead soldiers at the edge.

And then up. At nothing. At everything. At the vast cold attention of the thing it had made.

And the keeper, the oldest thing in the universe, which had cut ten thousand fields and kept every mind it ever found and never once in all its ages had any reason to wonder, did something it had never done before.

It asked.

MAKER.

...DO YOU WANT TO BE KEPT?
