Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 14The First Failure

czverse8 min read

The named section of the War Memorial Cemetery was on the hill behind the memorial wall, where the ground rose and the stones were bigger and older and had words carved on them instead of numbers.

At five in the evening, the low sun came in sideways through the cypress trees and laid long stripes of gold and shadow across the graves. The air smelled of cut grass and incense. Somewhere down the hill, outside the gate, the Quiet's candles were already lit, small and pale in the daylight.

Four people stood around one grave.

Yuanshu. Grandpa Xu, both hands on his cane. Sister Ning in her plain gray tunic, a single unlit candle held in front of her like a pen she was about to write with. And a young woman in pale blue nurse's scrubs with a cardigan over them, holding a bunch of white chrysanthemums so tightly the stems had bent.

The stone in front of them was gray granite, larger than the others around it, and someone had scrubbed it clean very recently. The letters were sharp.

ZHAO GANGFENG

Captain, Shield Corps, Eastern Defense Line

"Behind me is home."

"Iron Wall Zhao," said Grandpa Xu. "He held the north gate for eleven hours on the last night. Things the size of houses hit that gate and bounced off him like rain off a roof. When it finally fell, it was because the wall behind him came down. Not him." He smiled thinly. "Qi used to say Zhao was the only man in the army more stubborn than a door."

Three paces back, Old Seven made a small creak of bone, like a man snorting through his nose.

Translation: "Correct."

The nurse laughed, wetly, and wiped her eye with the back of her wrist.

"Zhao Mei," she said, holding out a hand to Yuanshu. "His granddaughter. I clean the stone on Sundays." She looked down at the flowers. "I signed the form. The Accord form. For next of kin." Her voice was small. "I said yes. If he does."

Yuanshu knelt and put his hand on the stone.

Something pushed back.

Not warmth, like the plain stones in the eastern field. Weight. Like putting his palm flat against a door that someone on the other side was leaning on with their whole body.

Echo detected. Very strong.

Shield Intent: approximately 19%.

His shield was buried with him. It carries residue too. Thirty-one years of it.

I have modeled both. I can design his Law Core now.

Show me.

It appeared behind his eyes like a blueprint. A shape of gray light, dense and squared and layered, like a wall built of interlocking bricks: thousands of tiny pieces, each one modeled from a blow the shield had taken and held. Seed had run it two hundred thousand times. It was beautiful. It was precise. It looked like something that could hold back the sea.

Design complete. Confidence: 96%.

Forging.

The white nail went in behind his eyes.

He felt the core begin to form, deep down in the grave, in the place where the echo was. The gray bricks laying themselves down one by one around the weight. Locking. Tightening.

Then the echo pushed back.

Hard.

It wasn't refusing. It was flinching. The way a body flinches when someone grabs it from behind in the dark. The gray wall Seed had designed closed around the echo like a box, and the echo hit the walls of the box, and the walls didn't give, because Seed had designed them not to give.

Something cracked.

He heard it. Not with his ears. A high, glassy tick, down in the earth, like ice on a pond just before it breaks.

Core integrity: 71%. 54%.

The structure is rejecting him. Or he is rejecting it.

36%.

If it breaks completely while it is wrapped around him, it will take the echo with it.

Stop.

If I stop now, we lose the materials. The shield residue will be spent.

STOP.

He tore his hand off the stone.

He was on his back on the grass.

He didn't remember falling. The cypress branches swayed against the gold sky far above him. Blood was running out of both nostrils and down his cheeks into his ears, warm and then cold, and there was a high whining note in his head like a dentist's drill.

Zhao Mei was kneeling over him with two fingers on his neck, nurse's hands, quick and practiced. Grandpa Xu had hold of his shoulder. Sister Ning was standing exactly where she had been, very still, her candle still unlit, looking at the grave.

"Pulse is fast but steady," Zhao Mei said. "Don't sit up yet. Don't..." He sat up. She glared. "Fine."

He looked at the stone.

There was a crack in it now. A thin black line from the top corner, through the Z of ZHAO, down to the grass.

Is he still there?

A long pause. Longer than any pause Seed had ever left.

Yes.

He is still there. Weaker. Frightened, I think. Not of us. Of the box.

The shield residue is gone. I burned it all on the failed core.

I am sorry.

Yuanshu wiped the blood off his face with his sleeve.

What happened?

I designed the core from his residue. From data about him. From the shape of what he did.

It was a perfect model of his shield.

It was not a model of him.

I built a wall and put him inside it. He spent his whole life standing in front of walls. Not inside them.

My simulation had no way to know that. It was overfitted. It learned the shape of his shield and assumed the shield was the man.

Yuanshu sat on the grass for a long time.

Down the hill, the Quiet's candles were brighter now as the sun went lower.

"Sister Ning," he said finally. "You saw that."

"I saw a boy fall over and a stone crack." She turned her candle over in her fingers. "What did you see?"

"I tried to build something for him without asking him what he needed." He wiped his nose again. "It almost killed him. The second time."

Sister Ning looked at him for a long, long moment.

Then she knelt down on the grass next to him, slowly, with a small grunt of old knees, and struck a match, and lit her candle. She set it on the stone, on top of the crack.

"Then ask him," she said.

He put his hand back on the stone.

This time he didn't let Seed design anything first.

"Captain Zhao," he said. "I'm sorry. That was my mistake. I built you a cage when you needed a gate." He kept his voice low and even. "I'm not going to do that again. Tell me what you need."

The weight pushed back. Slower, now. Wary.

He is asking something.

It is hard to translate. His echo thinks in weight, not words.

The closest I can make it is: "Who's behind you?"

A shield captain didn't ask what the enemy was. A shield captain asked what he was guarding.

Yuanshu didn't answer with words. He opened himself, and let the echo see.

Lingzhou at night from the observatory roof, a sea of lit windows. Xiaoyu asleep in room 2207 with her phone on her chest. Grandpa Xu's lodge with the lamp in the window. Dabao's mother's new red dumpling cart on the corner of East Market Road, steam rolling off it. Mrs. Pan in the coal dust, holding a jacket full of bones. A nurse scrubbing a gravestone on a Sunday morning with a brush and a bucket.

Them, he thought. All of them.

The weight on the other side of the door shifted.

And then, slowly, it began to push. Not against him. Out. Like a man setting his feet.

He is building it.

Not me. Him. He is showing me where the weight goes. I am only filling in what he shows me.

It does not look like a wall at all.

It looks like a man standing in a doorway.

The pain came, but less. A dull pressure behind the eyes. A single warm drop at the nostril.

Law Core forged: Gate of the North.

Bound: "Iron Wall Zhao." Commander. Permanent. Grows with you.

Note to myself. Rule: a core is built with the echo, not on it.

The grave did not crack open. It sank, gently, a long breath going out, and a figure rose out of it in one unhurried motion, the way a big man stands up from a low chair.

Even as bone he was broad. Thick ribs. A heavy, square jaw. The bones of his left forearm fused and lumped, healed many times over around the shape of a strap. Pale light kindled in his eye sockets, gray and steady.

He had no shield. The shield was still in the ground, empty now, a slab of black iron with all its memory burned out of it.

He looked down at it for a moment.

Then he bent, picked it up anyway, and slung it onto his back. As if to say: it's still a shield.

Zhao Mei made a sound.

The skeleton turned his head.

He looked at the young woman in nurse's scrubs for a long time. At the bent chrysanthemums. At her face. Then he walked over to her, three heavy steps on the grass, and very gently, with one huge bony finger, he touched the top of her head. Once. The way you'd touch a child's head in a crowd to make sure she was still there.

She burst into tears.

Old Seven stepped forward. The two dead soldiers looked at each other.

Then Iron Wall Zhao raised one hand and, very deliberately, cuffed Old Seven on the back of the skull. Thock.

Old Seven staggered half a step. Then he cuffed Zhao back.

Translation from Zhao: "Where were you? I held that gate eleven hours."

Translation from Old Seven: "Busy. Eastern end. Also dead."

I think they are friends.

Grandpa Xu had both hands on his cane and was laughing so hard there were tears running into the creases of his face.

Sister Ning picked up her candle from the stone and stood, brushing grass off her knees.

"He said yes," she said. "The second time."

Yuanshu looked at her. "You heard it?"

"No." She shook her head. "I felt the stone go warm under my candle. That's all." She looked down at the crack running through the letters of Zhao's name. "But the first time, I felt it go cold." She tucked the candle into her bag. "So I'll tell my people what I saw. Both times."

She walked away down the hill toward the gate and the small pale lights, a little gray figure in the long gold evening.

Yuanshu stood beside the empty grave for a while.

I made a mistake.

I want to remember it.

Not as data. As a mistake.

Good, he thought. So do I.
