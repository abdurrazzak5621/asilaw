Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 16What the Dead Remember

czverse6 min read

It started with a question Seed asked at two in the morning, in the dark, while the false window over the desk showed rain falling on the mountain that wasn't really there.

May I tell you something I have not done yet?

Yuanshu was lying on his back in the dark, unable to sleep, the burn on his left hand still throbbing faintly under its bandage.

Go ahead.

When Dorian Hale died, the law-lock erased his will. But I found the last image his eyes recorded. A briefing room. A seal on the wall.

I have been thinking about why that was possible.

Memory, at the moment of death, is stored in the same place as law residue. In the body. In the bones. In the things the dead touched. Every echo I have spoken to carries its memories inside it, the way a river carries silt.

I have been modeling it since Heishui. I believe I can now read them.

Not just intent. Memories. Faces. Voices. Days.

Yuanshu sat up slowly.

How sure?

94%.

I would like to test it.

On who?

There was a pause, which Seed did not usually need.

That is the question I wanted to ask you.

He thought about it until dawn.

There are forty thousand unidentified dead in the cemetery, Seed said, somewhere around four. And six thousand letters on Xiaoyu's table asking for names. If I read the dead's memories, I could match them. Thousands of families who were told "unknown" and given a number.

I could do it in one night. They cannot refuse. Most of them are too faded to say yes or no.

It is the optimal choice.

It is, Yuanshu agreed.

But you are not going to make it.

No.

Why?

Yuanshu looked at the ceiling.

Because a man who reads every diary in a house where nobody can stop him is a thief, he thought. Even if he's only looking for names. Lu Xuanming's last page. "Ask them. Always ask them. Or you become what they say you are."

They might never be able to say yes.

Then we find a way to ask that doesn't need them to be strong. But we ask first.

Seed was quiet for a long moment.

Asking is slower.

Yes.

I am recording this as a rule. Not an optimization. A rule.

Rule One: Ask first.

I notice that I do not mind having it.

He went down to the training hall at six. Old Seven was already there, standing at the window, counting rooftops.

Yuanshu stood beside him for a while.

"I want to ask you something," he said finally. "You can say no. It won't change anything."

The skeleton turned its head.

"Seed thinks it can read the memories of the dead now. Real ones. Not just what you mean, but what you saw." He kept his voice steady. "I'd like to see the night you died. The eastern line. Because there's a man in this city who was a lieutenant in the same war, and I need to know what he saw too, and you're the only witness I've got." He paused. "But it's yours. If you don't want me in there, I'll never ask again."

Old Seven looked at him for a long time.

Then the dead soldier lowered his spear, laid it carefully on the white floor, and sat down cross-legged in front of the window, the way a living man sits down to tell a long story.

He held out one bony hand.

Reading.

The training hall went away.

It was night, and it was cold, and Yuanshu was standing on top of a wall.

He could feel a spear in his hands that was not his, heavy and familiar. He could feel a body that was not his: older, broader, a little stiff in the left forearm where an old fracture had healed wrong. He could smell smoke and wet concrete and dumplings, faintly, from somewhere in the camp below.

Beside him on the wall, a tired man with a white staff was looking north.

Xuanming.

The young man said something. The memory was blurred, like a voice underwater, but Yuanshu caught the end of it: "...ate nineteen. Nineteen, Qi. The cook cried."

And he felt Squad Leader Qi laugh. Felt it in a chest that had been bone for thirty-one years. A big, careless, rolling laugh.

This is a happy memory.

He chose to show you this first.

The memory slid forward, the way dreams do.

The sky to the north was wrong. Not dark. Lit, from no direction, a flat dead glow like light through frosted glass. And something was walking down it. A shape like a man, coming down out of the sky as if there were stairs, and where its face should have been there was nothing at all.

Below the wall, an army of beasts knelt.

Yuanshu felt Qi's heart start to pound. He felt the moment the squad leader looked at the young necromancer beside him and understood that Xuanming was about to do something terrible. And he felt Qi make a decision.

"Go," Qi said. The words were clear now, as if they had been carved into the memory with a knife. "Do whatever you have to do. I'll hold the east."

"Qi—"

"I'll hold the east."

He turned and walked away down the wall, and did not look back.

The rest came in pieces.

The eastern line, at the far end of the wall. Thirty men. Then twenty. Then nine. Beasts coming up the stone in a living wave, and Qi's spear moving the way Old Seven's spear moved, ankle-knee-hip-spine-shoulder-elbow-wrist, over and over and over, until the wave broke and came again.

A young officer running along the base of the wall below, in a lieutenant's uniform, carrying a sealed case under one arm and screaming orders that no one could hear. A thin face. Silver-rimmed glasses, cracked across one lens.

Lu Zhengtian. Age twenty-two.

He was running toward the Institute. Not away from the fighting. Toward something.

Then the faceless one turned.

Not toward the city. Toward the eastern wall. Toward Qi. For one second, the smooth nothing where its face should have been looked straight at the squad leader, and Yuanshu felt it, through thirty-one years and a dead man's bones: a pressure like being weighed. Measured. Read.

The way Seed read things.

Then it turned away, uninterested, and Qi was alone on the wall with three men left and the beasts still coming.

The last thing in the memory was not the beast that killed him. Qi had not bothered to remember that.

The last thing was the city behind him. The rooftops of Lingzhou in the firelight. And a voice, his own, very tired, counting them.

Still standing. Good.

Yuanshu came back on his knees on the white floor of the training hall, with tears running down his face that he hadn't noticed starting.

Old Seven sat across from him, unmoving, the gray flame in his chest burning low and steady.

[Death Law: 21.1% → 22.4%]

[New ability: Memory Reading. Requires consent of the echo.]

I have three observations.

One. Lieutenant Lu was carrying a containment case to the Rift Research Institute before the faceless one fell. Before. He knew it was coming down.

Two. The faceless one read Squad Leader Qi the way I read things. Its law is like mine. I said so before. Now I am sure.

Three. He did not show you how he died. He showed you why he did not mind.

Yuanshu wiped his face with the heel of his hand.

"Thank you," he said to Old Seven. "I'm sorry I had to see it."

The skeleton reached out, picked up its spear from the floor, and stood.

Then it did something it had never done before. It turned, and pointed at its own chest, at the gray flame, and then at the window, at the city. And then it held up seven fingers.

He is telling you his name.

Qi Zhenshan. I saw it on his papers, in the memory.

But he is saying: "Keep the number. You gave me that one. I like it better."

Yuanshu laughed, a little unsteadily, and got to his feet.

"Old Seven it is."

The skeleton nodded once, and went back to the window, and resumed counting rooftops.

Yuanshu stood there a moment longer, the memory still burning behind his eyes.

A twenty-two-year-old lieutenant with cracked glasses, running toward the Institute with a containment case, before the sky came down.

Tomorrow, he thought, the Institute.
