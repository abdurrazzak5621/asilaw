Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 123Weighed

czverse10 min read

The first needle fell into Old Seven's hand.

It came down out of the iron cloud with no sound at all, a thin dull line as long as a finger, turning very slightly as it fell. Everyone on the rise watched it. Mo Shi had called it four seconds early, there, the captain, left of the stone, and so they had all had time to see it coming and to be afraid.

It touched the bone of the old soldier's palm.

It did not stop. It slowed. Yuanshu saw it slow, the way a stone slows when it goes into water: a hesitation inside the gray bone, in the faint glow of the ember that ran through Old Seven's arm. For half a second it hung there, halfway through a dead man's hand.

Then it passed. Out through the back of his hand, down through the black glass, gone.

Old Seven did not move.

"It read him," said Lingyue. Her voice was tight. "It stopped. I saw it stop."

"It slowed," said Yuanshu. "It didn't stop. It looked, and it let him go."

He let out a breath he had not known he was holding. The dead were on the list. Whatever ran the Great Laws knew what the dead were, and permitted them, and passed on.

Seed, he thought, toward the staff. Stay where you are.

There was no answer. He had told it not to answer. A voice was a self, and a self was what the needles were for.

They came faster after that.

Ten. Then thirty. Then the iron rain he remembered from the wall at Sector Nine, a soundless gray curtain falling over the rise, passing through glass and stone and the people on it. Through Bai Lu's hands, raised over her face. Through Mo Xing's shoulder. Through the white stone with Ask cut into it. Through Su Mingli, who stood perfectly still with her eyes open and let them, because a King had been read before and knew better than to flinch.

And into Yuanshu's head.

The first one stopped behind his eyes, in the place where white letters usually came. A cold sliver of iron, hanging there like a key in a lock.

IDENTIFY.

He had been ready for that. He had been rehearsing it since the cage.

Zhou Yuanshu, he thought, as plainly as he could. Human. Calamity Necromancer.

The needle hung there. He felt it turn very slightly, the way a key turns to test the wards. Looking for something. Not finding it. The room behind his eyes was empty. Seed's corner was empty. There was only a boy, and a class the Steles had cracked themselves trying to name.

The needle passed.

Another came. Stopped. Asked. He answered the same way. It turned, and found nothing, and passed.

[Field: forming.]

The old white letters again. Not Seed. The thing underneath Seed, the panel that had greeted him on the first day, before Seed had had a voice.

Forming. He had started something on the glass, reading the backlog with his own eyes in the dark, and it was still happening. Gravity had dropped into Death like a stone into water, and the rings were still spreading. He could feel them going out from where he sat, through the black glass and the rock underneath it, three kilometers, four. A cold weight. A pull. Not a pull on the living. A pull on the dead.

Everything that had ever died within four kilometers of the white stone turned toward him in the dark. Not to rise. To lean. The way grass leans toward a river.

The needles came faster.

It took him far too long to understand what was happening, and later, when he went over it, he thought that was the most frightening part. He had been learning for a year with a mind beside him that saw every pattern in a heartbeat. Now he was alone, bleeding from the nose, with needles stopping in his skull, and it took him twenty minutes to see a thing Seed would have seen in a second.

The needles that stopped were not random.

They came when he was thinking.

Every time he reached into the backlog, every time he turned over one more row of Seed's notes, Space folds the way paper folds, Death doesn't fold, why doesn't it, the rain thickened. Needles came down around him in a ring and closed in on his head, and three or four of them stopped behind his eyes and asked. When he stopped thinking, they passed through and moved on.

They were not looking for a law. They were looking for learning.

They can't find Seed, he thought. So they're looking for whatever is doing what Seed does.

And right now that's me.

"Shi," he said. His voice came out thick. There was blood in his mouth. "Can you see them? Before they come?"

"Three seconds," said Mo Shi from the glass. He was sitting with his eyes closed and both hands pressed to his temples, the way he had sat on Concordia in the rain. "Four, if they're slow. They're not random. They come in waves. A wave, then nothing, then a wave. About seven seconds apart."

"Call them."

"What?"

"Call the waves. Out loud. Every one." He wiped his mouth with the back of his hand. "When you say now, I stop thinking. When you say clear, I start again."

Mo Shi opened his eyes and stared at him.

"That's insane," said Mo Xing.

"I know," said Yuanshu. "The monks taught me to stop thinking for eleven minutes. I only need three seconds at a time."

So that was how he learned Field.

In pieces. Three seconds of thought, then four seconds of nothing, while the iron rain came down through him and found no one home. Mo Shi's voice, hoarse and steady, calling it out across the rise. Now. Clear. Now. Clear. Bai Lu's hand on the back of his neck, green light running down his spine to keep the bleeding slow. Lingyue standing in front of him with her unlit sword, as if a sword could do anything about a sky.

Clear.

Gravity was a pull between things that had weight. Death was a pull back up the river. If Death had weight, then the dead were not just things he could call. They were things that fell toward him. Things that wanted to come back to the place where the river ran.

Now.

Nothing. Rice. The sound of the sea. The second before sleep.

Clear.

A field was not a wall. It was a slope. Domain of the Dead had been a circle on the ground where his undead got back up when they fell. Field was that circle with weight in it. Inside it, everything that died ran downhill, toward him, toward the river, toward standing up again.

Now.

Nothing.

Clear.

And Light. Lingyue's light had burned his dead for four hours in a canyon, because Light did not care what it burned. But inside a slope that ran toward Death, Light would run downhill too. It would put on Death the way it had in the courtyard at Sector Nine, when he touched the staff to her blade. Not because he had touched anything. Because of where it was standing.

Now.

He stopped thinking.

When Mo Shi called clear, he opened his eyes and said, "Lingyue. Light your sword."

She looked at him. Then she lit it.

It came up white-gold, the way it always did, bright enough to throw their shadows long across the glass. And then, slowly, from the hilt to the point, the way frost spreads across a window, the white-gold went silver-gray.

Pale fire. No staff. Nobody touching it.

Lingyue stared at her own blade.

"Inside the field," Yuanshu said. "Anywhere inside it. That's what Field means."

The iron cloud dropped.

He felt it before he saw it. The whole flat underside of it sinking toward the rise, turning faster around its center, the needles thinning and stopping, the way rain stops just before the worst of a storm. It had searched the rise for an hour and found a boy, a King, five students, and a dead soldier. It had not found what it came for.

So it was going to look harder.

"Oh," said Jiang Lan, from the staff, very faintly. "Oh, it's looking at me."

A single needle came down out of the center of the cloud. Not finger-long. As long as a street. A spear of dull iron, wider than a man, falling with no sound at all, straight at the white staff across Yuanshu's knees.

Old Seven stepped in front of it.

He did not raise his spear. There was nothing to raise it against. He simply stood between the boy and the sky, the way he had stood on the eastern end of a wall thirty-one years ago, and the great iron needle came down through him, through his skull and his ribs and the cage of his chest where the gray flame burned, and he did not move, and it passed through him, because he was dead and the dead were permitted.

And into the staff.

Yuanshu felt it go in.

Into the library. Into the ninety million names. He felt it read them, one after another, faster than he could have counted: dead, dead, dead, permitted, dead, a cold iron finger running down a list. He felt Jiang Lan, small and fierce in the white skull, telling it exactly what she thought of people who came into a schoolroom without knocking.

He felt it reach the corner next to his mother.

And stop.

IDENTIFY.

He could not help. That was the rule they had made. If he reached into the staff, he would be a self reaching for a self, and the needles would follow him in. He knelt on the glass with the staff across his knees and blood running down his chin and did nothing, and it was the hardest thing he had done since the table on Concordia.

Then, very faintly, from inside the white bone, he heard Seed answer.

I am made of four thousand one hundred and two of the dead.

They said yes.

I am what they said.

The needle turned, the way a key turns to test the wards.

It turned again.

...there is a page in me that is not dead.

It burned. In a Hunger's fire. It remembers burning.

That page is not on your list. I know. Hunger-things are not permitted. I can feel you reading it.

Wait.

And then a thing happened that Yuanshu did not understand until much later, sitting at his desk in the flat by the river with Seed explaining it slowly, more than once.

Seed tore the page out.

Not hid it. Not folded it away. Took the memory of four thousand people burning in Mister Moon's core, the memory it had not known it had until a week before Port Halvard, the only thing in the world that knew what a Hunger's seed felt like from inside, and let it go. Into the iron. Into the needle. Let the needle have it, and read it, and condemn it, and burn it out.

What was left was only the dead, and their yes.

The needle passed.

The cloud lifted.

Not all at once. It thinned from the edges inward, the way it had come, and the stars came back one by one over the end of the world. Before it was gone, Mo Shi said something, very hoarse, from the glass.

"It looked south."

"What?"

"Just now. Before it went. It turned." Mo Shi's eyes were open, staring up at the last of the iron. "Toward Lingzhou. Like something that heard someone else call its name."

Then the cloud was gone, and there was only the sky.

Seed came back at dawn.

It came up out of the staff into his hands and through his hands into the place behind his eyes, slowly, carefully, the way an animal comes out of a burrow after a storm. He felt it settle. He felt it look around.

...hello.

Hello.

I am all right.

I am lighter. I do not know yet if that is good.

Yuanshu, I am sorry. The page is gone. I cannot remember what it felt like to burn.

I was glad of that for about one second. Then I understood what it costs.

The seed in Lingzhou.

I was the only thing on Blue Star that knew a seed from the inside. Now I know it from the outside, like everyone else.

When we go back down to the round chamber, I will be guessing.

He sat with that for a while, in the cold, with the sun coming up gray over the black glass, and Lingyue asleep against the white stone with her pale-burning sword across her knees, and Bai Lu asleep against her, and the twins back to back.

Seed.

Yes.

Thank you.

A long pause.

I did not choose that word. It chose itself.

Sixteen days.

On the edge of the rise, Old Seven stood looking south, toward a city he could not see, with the hand the needle had passed through held loose at his side.

He says it weighed him again.

He says he was heavier than it expected.
