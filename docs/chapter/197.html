Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 197All Shall Be Asked

czverse8 min read

"Twelve days," said Chair Adaeze Okafor. "Not forty. You can have twelve."

She said it in the crater on Concordia, in person, at the long black volcanic-glass table, with the five delegations and the empty Yamato chair and Ren on the sixth screen, and General Han beside the Huaxia delegate with a sheet of paper in front of him covered in his small square handwriting. It was raining. The ring of white stone benches was wet. Nobody had gone under cover.

"Article Eleven," said Okafor. "Where the answer of one would be acted upon in the name of all, all shall be asked." She folded her hands. "Mister Zhou wishes to take a voyage to a dying Hunger thirty-nine raft-days into the Shallows, carried by the keeper, to bring home sixty-one dead soldiers of Blue Star, a child of a world that asks, two hundred and eighty-one million minds who are getting cold, and a corporal who has forgotten what he wanted. Something is pruning its way along the arm toward the keeper. The voyage may arrive at the Mouth at the same time." She looked down the table. "General Han. You said, three months ago, that you'd like the next time something above the sky asks anyone on Blue Star for anything, it has to ask us too."

"I did," said General Han.

"Nothing above the sky is asking. Mister Zhou is." Okafor looked at him. "Is this ours to ask about?"

General Han looked at the sheet of paper in front of him for a long moment.

"The keeper carries them," he said. "The keeper is Blue Star's ally now. It asked us for help, and we gave it, and it will be the one carrying the voyage past whatever is pruning the arm." He put his finger on the paper. "If the voyage draws that thing's attention to the keeper, it draws it to Blue Star. The keeper's field is above our sky." He looked up. "Yes. It's ours. Everyone's."

"Then we ask," said Okafor. "Twelve days. Everyone. Living and dead." She took off her glasses. "And this time, Mister Zhou, I want you to stand in front of them and say what you said to General Han. All of it. Including the part you're not sure of."

He did.

Not in person to six billion people. On every screen and every radio on Blue Star, at once, from the vault steps of the Ossuary in Lingzhou, with the long white wall of names behind him and the staff across his knees. Twenty-two minutes. Fat Tan set up the camera. Xiaoyu sat on the floor out of shot with her arms around her knees, mouthing every word along with him.

He told them everything.

The Mouth. Hush. Sixty-one soldiers. The Sixth. The cousins. Two hundred and eighty-one million. Liang Fu. The cold. Thirty-nine raft-days. The keeper's yes, if it was cold. The fields going dark along the arm, two of them, one every nine nights. What Seed thought it was. What Seed did not know.

"If you say yes," he said, "I'll go, with whoever chooses to come, and we'll try to bring them home. We might not come back. The keeper might not come back. If something is pruning the arm and it sees the keeper carrying us past it, it might come here sooner than it would have." He looked at the camera. "If you say no, I'll stay. I'll sit on these steps. Xiaoyu will write their names on the wall when Hush stops sending messages. And we'll never know what Liang Fu would have wanted, if he'd had the chance to want something again."

He did not tell them which he wanted. He had thought about it for three days. He decided they had a right to answer without knowing.

"You can say no," said Zhou Yuanshu, to Blue Star. "Nobody will ask you twice."

It was not like the bell.

The bell had been forty days, and the asking had been slow and strange and new, a world learning to ask itself something for the first time. This was twelve days, and Blue Star knew how now. The booths were already built. The radio scripts were already written. Brandt went on television every night again in his glasses. Orlov's soldiers put stones in buckets at the foot of every wall in the Northern Union. Ngozi Eze stood in the fish market at Kesse on the first morning, and the same broad old woman behind the same slab of ice said yes before Ngozi Eze had finished explaining, and then said, "Tell him to bring the soldiers home. Somebody should come home from out there."

On the hill above Port Abiri, Akosua Mensah said yes to a clerk with a clipboard, sitting on the grass beside two stones and an empty place where a wheelchair had stood. "Tell him she'd have gone herself," she said. "In the white boat."

In Yamato, under the gates, the queue went all the way down the mountain. At the six hundred and twelfth gate, a thin old man in a gray robe stopped sweeping and stood in it, and when he reached the table at the top he said he did not know what he wanted yet, but he knew what a cold child sounded like, and he said yes.

The dead were asked too.

Through Adjutant Xu, down the Lattice, to every unit on the Commission's register. He told them what he had told the living. Every word. And this time, before he asked, he said one more thing.

Last time, some of you said no, and you held what you'd promised to hold, and I didn't ask you twice. Some of you told me afterward you'd have liked to be asked again. So I'm asking. Not to cross a border. To come and get our own. You can still say no. You can say no a hundred times. I'll still ask.

It took six days for the answers to come back, because the Western Legion asked each other very carefully, nine by nine, the way Sergeant Ma said a miner checks every prop.

The Western Legion.

Sergeant Ma Dashan says: the pass said no last time. The pass was right. Shouting brings things down.

He says: this is not shouting. This is going into a mine after the men who are still down there, when the air is going bad.

He says: a miner always goes back for the men.

Twenty-five thousand six hundred and ninety-five. Yes. All of them.

He says: but somebody has to hold the pass. He says the four thousand who lay down last year said: ask us again in a year. He asked them. He says a thousand of them stood back up.

He says: they will hold the pass. The rest of us will come.

Yuanshu did not say anything for a long time.

The First Legion. Yes. Seventeen thousand five hundred and fifty-three. Ma Lianhua says: we came home from a throat of fire once. We know the way.

The cemetery companies. Yes.

The Watch of the Shelf. Nana Ampofo says a pilot does not leave the harbor. She says: but she would like to send forty who know how to watch water in the dark. She asked them. They said yes.

The monks under the gates. Sister Kiyo says she would like to listen from here. She says someone should keep listening to the bell while you are gone, and she is very good at it.

The Spear Corps.

Two hundred and seventy-two.

Old Seven did not ask them. They told him.

On the twelfth night, the count came in.

Okafor read it from the crater, in the rain, on every screen on Blue Star.

"Yes: seventy-one point four percent. No: twenty-two point two. Something else: six point four." She put the paper down. "Blue Star says go."

Then she said something she had not said the first time.

"Mister Zhou. The Council would like to tell you what it will do with this answer. Article Eleven." She folded her hands on the black glass. "If you go, and you don't come back, we'll hold your city. Su Mingli and Marshal Orlov and General Han and whatever the rest of us have. We'll keep your sister on the stepladder and your mother at the microphone. We'll listen to the bell. We'll keep the Ledger." She looked at him through the screen. "And if something comes along the arm while you're gone, we won't wait for you. We'll ask ourselves. In six minutes if we have to."

"Thank you."

"Don't thank us," said Adaeze Okafor. "We'd have liked to be asked. You asked." She took off her glasses. "Go and get them."

That night, on the Sector Nine battlements, in his old place, with his back against the parapet and the staff across his knees, he counted.

Not the vote. The voyage. Seed had already counted it. He counted it again anyway, the way you count your money twice before a long journey.

Twenty-five thousand of the Western Legion. Seventeen thousand of the First. Six thousand from the cemetery hill. Forty from the shelf. Two hundred and seventy-two of the Spear Corps. Old Seven. Adjutant Xu. Jiang Lan, in the staff. His mother's fragment, asleep.

And the living, who had not been asked yet, because he had wanted the world's answer first.

Fifty thousand dead.

The keeper carried a raft last time. Twelve living and a few hundred dead.

It has never carried fifty thousand.

I am going to have to ask it if it can.

I am going to tell it first what we will do with the answer.

I am going to tell it that if it says no, we will go with fewer.

And I am going to tell it that whatever it says, fifty thousand of the dead of Blue Star said yes to going into the dark after their own, and I would like it to know that, whatever it decides.

I think it would like to know.

Lingyue came up the stairs at midnight with her grandfather's sword and sat down beside him without asking.

"I'm coming," she said.

"I haven't asked you."

"Ask me, then. Properly. With what you'll do with it." She did not look at him. "I'll still say yes. I just want to hear you ask."

He asked her.

She said yes.

Then she leaned her head on his shoulder, the way she had on the night of the forty days, slowly, and left it there, and they sat on the wall in the dark and looked north at the small white ring over the end of the world, and past it, at the dark, where somewhere thirty-nine raft-days away a child of a world that asks was holding a playing card in the cold, and somewhere further away than that, something was moving along the arm, one garden at a time.

Neither of them said anything for a long time.

It was the best part of the night.
