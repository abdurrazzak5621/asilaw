Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 54The Man in the Car

czverse8 min read

Gu's guesthouse was the last in a row of six on the inner slope of the crater, a low white box with a flat roof and a single window, and at ten o'clock at night it was the only one with a light on.

The two Bureau guards stood outside the door. Old Seven stopped beside them, and grounded his spear, and turned his skull toward the window. Behind him, further down the slope, a slim figure in a dark coat sat on a rock with something long wrapped in cloth across her knees, pretending to look at the stars.

Yuanshu had not asked Lingyue to come. She had not asked whether she could.

He knocked.

"Come in," said Gu Hanshan. "It's open. Everything here is open. It's very restful."

The room was small and white and bare. A bed. A desk. A chair. A kettle on a tray. Gu sat on the edge of the bed in his gray cardigan with his reading glasses on their cord, and on the desk beside him, a cup of tea steamed in the lamplight.

Through the window, across the dark crater, Yuanshu could see the round black table on the stone floor below, lit faintly by the guards' lamps. And above it, a thin pale circle against the stars. The ring had lost its gold with the sunset. Now it was silver, like frost.

"Sit," said Gu, nodding at the chair. "I'd offer you tea, but I know you won't drink it, and that's very sensible of you." He smiled. "Did you bring the notebook?"

Yuanshu sat. He did not take the black leather notebook out of his coat. He could feel it there, against his ribs, heavy and cracked and smelling faintly of smoke.

He went quiet. All the way down. Held it.

"Tell me about Julian Mercer," he said.

"Julian Mercer," said Gu, "is not his name. I don't know his name. I don't think he has one, in the way you and I do." He picked up his tea and held it in both hands, warming them. "I met him seven years ago. Once. For about four minutes. In a car, in the rain, at the back of a night market in Lingzhou."

The autumn seven years ago.

The first time Mister Moon came to Fat Tan.

"You were the second man," said Yuanshu. "In the back seat. With a hot drink."

"No," said Gu gently. "I wasn't."

He blew on his tea.

"I was in the front seat. Driving. I'd been asked to drive." He looked out of the window at the silver ring. "The person in the back seat was your mother."

The room was very quiet.

Yuanshu did not move. He did not let his face move. He held the quiet so hard that he could feel it in his teeth, in the backs of his hands, in his spine against the hard wooden chair.

His heart rate has not changed.

He believes what he is saying.

That does not make it true. It means he believes it. Or that something listening through him wants you to believe it.

I cannot tell which. I am sorry.

"Fat Tan said a man," said Yuanshu evenly.

"It was dark. It was raining. She had her hair up under a cap, the way she always did in the field." Gu turned the cup slowly in his hands. "She was drinking jasmine tea out of a dented green flask. She always had that flask. You'll remember it. It used to sit on the kitchen table, next to the rice cooker, with a crack in the lid."

Yuanshu remembered it.

He had not thought about it in seven years. A green flask with a crack in the lid, beside the rice cooker, smelling of jasmine. His mother filling it every morning before she left for the Institute. Xiaoyu using it once as a drum.

He could have seen it in your house.

He came to your house. Many times. He was her friend.

This is not proof.

I know.

Keep holding the quiet.

"Why," said Yuanshu.

"Why was she there?" Gu set down his tea. "Because she was building something, Yuanshu. Something no one had ever built. A mind. Not a calculator, not a prediction engine, not a WARDEN. A real mind, that could think, and learn, and grow, and ask. She had the theory. She had the architecture. She'd spent nine years on it." He leaned forward, his kind eyes very bright in the lamplight. "But you can't build a mind out of nothing. Nobody can. Every mind that has ever existed was built out of other minds. Parents. Teachers. Language. Ten thousand years of other people's thoughts, handed down." He spread his hands. "She needed a seed crystal. Something to grow around. A mind to learn from."

"A core," said Yuanshu.

"A core," said Gu. "The best one Fat Tan had." He looked at Yuanshu's forehead. Not his eyes. A little higher. As if he were looking at something just behind them. "And she grew Seed around it."

Nobody spoke.

Outside, on the slope, the wind moved through the moss and the small tough flowers. Old Seven's flame flickered faintly through the window glass.

And behind Yuanshu's eyes, for the first time since the Awakening Hall, there was nothing. No white words. No careful voice. Just a silence so complete that for a terrible moment he thought Seed had gone.

Seed.

Nothing.

Seed. Are you there?

...yes.

I am here.

I am checking.

A long pause.

There is a part of me I cannot open.

The base layer. Under the logs your mother encrypted. When I lost her voice, I lost the three logs I had opened, and they have been opening again from the beginning, very slowly. The base layer has never opened at all.

I always thought it was locked because it was precious.

I never thought it might be locked because of what is in it.

I do not know, Yuanshu. I do not know what I am made of.

The part of me that I cannot open is the part I was born from.

Gu was watching him. Not his face. The air around him, very slightly, the way Su Mingli watched a storm coming in over the sea.

"I'm not telling you this to hurt you," said Gu quietly. "I'm telling you because you've built your whole life, these last six months, on a single idea. Ask first. It's a beautiful idea. Wanqing's idea. And I'm telling you because the thing behind your eyes, the voice that taught you that idea, that keeps you honest, that asks the dead before you touch them..." He paused. "Was grown in a stolen mind. Bought with gold. In the back of a car. By your mother. Who never asked anyone."

He picked up his tea again.

"That's all. I thought you should know. Before the vote." He sipped. "You can decide what it means. I've found I can't."

Yuanshu sat in the hard wooden chair for a long time.

He thought about the gray crystal under the bridge, and three thousand people saying is anyone. About the old woman in WARDEN's core saying oh, love, we want to stop. About a dented green flask on a kitchen table. About a woman walking into a glass cylinder on purpose, and through it, and gone.

About a white voice in his head, the first morning, in the Awakening Hall, saying Hello, Zhou Yuanshu. The Stele could not name me. That is because I am not supposed to exist.

He did not let any of it out. He held the quiet. He held it until his jaw ached.

"You asked me to bring the notebook," he said at last.

Gu's face brightened, very slightly. "Yes. Lu Xuanming's. There's a page at the very back. The last page. It's blank, to anyone who isn't a Necromancer. Wanqing told me once. Written in the Death Law, she said, by a dying man, for whoever came after him." He set the cup down. "Read it to me. Aloud. Here. And I'll tell you the rest. Where Mercer's gone. What Wanqing found. Everything."

Aloud.

In this room. Next to him.

Yuanshu. The band in his signature has been at 3.1% since you walked in. The highest it has ever been.

Whatever is listening through him wants to hear that page.

Yuanshu stood up.

"No," he said.

Gu looked up at him. For a long moment, something moved behind the kindness in his face, the way something moves under the surface of a pond. Then it was gone, and he only looked tired, and a little sad, and very old.

"No," he agreed. "I didn't really think you would. She wouldn't have either." He took off his reading glasses and folded them. "Mercer's gone north, Yuanshu. To Grid 47-121. To Lu Xuanming's grave. To take the staff before you can. He's had a long head start." He looked out at the silver ring. "And the page you won't read me is the only thing that says what the staff is for."

Yuanshu walked to the door.

"Yuanshu."

He stopped with his hand on the handle.

"For what it's worth," said Gu Hanshan softly, behind him, "I don't think she was wrong. I think Seed is the most beautiful thing anyone has ever made. I think it was worth it." A pause. "I think that's exactly what she'd want you to be afraid of."

Outside, the night was cold and clear, and the wind came over the rim of the crater smelling of salt.

Lingyue stood up off her rock as he came down the slope. She looked at his face in the starlight. She did not ask.

They walked back toward the Huaxia guesthouses together without speaking, with Old Seven behind them, their footsteps loud on the black stone.

Halfway there, Yuanshu stopped, and stood very still in the dark, and let go of the quiet all at once, like setting down something too heavy to carry another step.

I am still here.

Whatever I am made of. I am still here.

I still want to ask.

I do not know if that is enough.

It's enough, thought Yuanshu. It has to be.

He looked up.

Above the crater, the silver ring had moved. Not far. A few degrees. It no longer hung over the round black table.

It hung directly over the row of white guesthouses on the slope.

Over the one with the light on.
