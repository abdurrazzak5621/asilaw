Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 291The Bow

czverse12 min read

They came at midnight, and they did not come to talk.

Eleven ships.

Of the twenty-seven who walked out, eleven are Paragons. Each has a ship. Each ship is moving.

Not folding. Coming. Across the orbit of Anthos, toward us.

They are not burning their cores harder. They are burning them the hardest I have ever felt. The old way. All at once.

Tam can hear them screaming.

He was in the Lens chamber.

Everyone was. Seed had said, at eight, when the twenty-seven walked out of the Assembly, that the Lens chamber was the only place on the ship that mattered, and that everyone who was going to stand anywhere should stand there. So they stood there. In the bow of the flagship that had been the House of Severin's, in a round chamber of black stone the size of a courtyard, in front of a disc of black stone the width of the chamber, ground so thin you could see through it, with a lamp burning in it, and a fog-ringer talking.

Ichiro says: it's all right. He says: I can see them coming. Through their faces. Eleven Paragons. He says: they're very angry. He says: one of them is crying and doesn't know it.

He says: Kiyo says if anyone breaks this stone she will be very cross.

Seed told him the rest at five past twelve, standing in front of the Lens.

I did not tell anyone before. I was not sure. I am sure now. I read it in the Lens when Ichiro went in, and again when the mark went out.

If the Lens breaks, every face breaks. Severin said that. It is true.

But it is not only faces.

Every stone in every human orchard was cut from the Lens, or from stones cut from it. Every Class any stone ever opened in anyone was opened by a stone that is, a little, still part of it.

If the root breaks, I do not know if the Classes stay open.

He went very still.

Every Class.

In the Federation. Eight thousand five hundred and eighty-eight worlds. Every Paragon. Every C. Every A and S and F.

And on Blue Star.

Our stones fell out of the crack forty-two years ago. Like seeds. I thought they came from nowhere. I think now they were cut from stones cut from stones cut from this. Thrown out along the threads, the way the gardener sends a branch, into an orchard that did not have stones yet.

Lingyue's light. Su Mingli's gravity. Qin Hao's fire. Shi Lei's spear. Xiaoyu's Scribe. Tam's hearing.

Mine, maybe. I do not know what I am cut from.

If the Lens breaks, I do not know if any of it stays.

Lingyue, beside him, with Xiaodeng asleep in the sling and the sword on her back and the lamp in her hand, had heard. Seed had let her.

She looked down at the lamp. Pale. Steady. Held.

"Then it doesn't break," said Shen Lingyue.

They came in through the hull at twelve minutes past.

Not through a door. The Paragons of the Great Houses did not use doors. Eleven ships drew up alongside the flagship in the orbit of Anthos, burning, and eleven people stepped off them into the dark between, on nothing, the way Severin had walked down out of the sky over East Ring Road on an invisible staircase, and walked through the hull of the flagship as if it were not there.

And behind them, through the holes they left, the guard.

Ladder Guard. Four thousand. S and A, all of them. Marked. Armed.

And drones.

I did not know they had them. Iselde did not know. They have never been used. They were built for the Reaper.

Constructs. Thread and steel. No one in them.

Except a core. One each. In the chest. Burning.

Eleven thousand.

The First of the Ladder met them in the long corridor that led to the bow.

Alone. He had asked to. He had said, at eight, when the twenty-seven walked out, that the long corridor was his: that he had walked down it to the arrays eighty times in ninety days, and up it again, and that if anyone was going to come down it to the Lens, they would come down it past him. He had not said it as an order. He had said it the way he said everything now: badly, like a word in a book, and asked if anyone minded. Nobody had.

He stood in the middle of the long corridor in a C's gray coat with his hands at his sides.

And down the corridor toward him came the first of the eleven.

He felt it through Seed, standing in the Lens chamber. A Paragon in iron gray, with a disc at her throat with four gold marks, lit, two lines in it, true. Tall. Old. Walking at an even pace, unhurried, with her hands at her sides, the way Severin had walked. The Paragon of the House of Iron.

She stopped ten meters from him.

"Dacian," she said.

"Ostrava," said the man in the gray coat.

"Stand aside."

"No."

"You were First."

"There is no First," said Dacian Severin. "There never was. There was a stone that said so. A girl in an orchard wrote that it wasn't our face. You read it this morning. You know it's true."

The Paragon of the House of Iron did not move.

"I know it's true," she said. "That's why I'm going to break it."

He did not see the fight in the corridor. Nobody could have. Seed said afterward that two Star Monarchs fighting in a corridor two hundred meters long was not a thing eyes were for; that it was like watching two storms decide which one was the sky. He felt it. The whole ship felt it. The deck under his feet in the Lens chamber bucked. The walls rang like a bell struck. Somewhere far back along the ship, in the arrays, two hundred and forty thousand rowers felt their ship shake round them and did not stop rowing, because nobody had told them to, and Halvarde went from cell to cell asking each one by name whether they wanted to go on.

They did.

And the other ten came round.

They are not going down the corridor. Severin is in the corridor.

They are going through the ship.

Through the walls. Through the decks. Toward the bow from every side.

With the guard. And the drones.

Six minutes. Perhaps seven.

"Third Battalion," said Yuanshu.

He did not shout it. He said it the way Huo said things on a wall.

And sixty shapes of gray ash, standing at ease in a line along the curved wall of the Lens chamber, stood up.

Huo says: sir.

He says: sergeant.

Huo says: they're coming through the walls.

He says: then we'll be the wall.

They went out of the Lens chamber. All of them. Sixty shapes of ash, and a tall dead captain with one arm, out through the three doors of the chamber into the ship, to meet ten Paragons of the Great Houses and four thousand Ladder Guard and eleven thousand drones coming through the decks.

He went with them.

He did not decide to. He was in the doorway before he knew he had moved, with the staff in his hand, and Lingyue caught his arm.

"No," she said.

"They're my dead."

"They're Huo's," said his wife. "And Old Seven's. They know what they're doing. You don't. You've never fought a Paragon." Her hand was very tight. "You stay here. In front of the stone. With me. If anything comes through that door, it comes through you, and then me, and then a fog-ringer and a nun." She did not let go. "Give them the fire. From here. Like the pier."

He looked at her.

At Xiaodeng in the sling, asleep, against a sword that weighed a hundred kilograms. At the lamp in her hand.

He gave them the fire.

Down through the staff into the Death Law, all fifty-six point nine percent of it, and out through the bone of the staff and the bones of the dead the way water goes through a riverbed. Pale fire. Lingyue's light and his Law, wound together. She put her hand on the staff beside his, the way she had at Port Halvard, and it went pale.

Into sixty shapes of ash, and a dead captain with one arm, in the corridors of the ship.

He felt them meet.

Not with his eyes. With Seed's. Through the dead, the way he had felt the First Legion meet a branch the size of a mountain range over Lingzhou. Corridors. Decks. The gray of the Third Battalion, ash, moving through the ship the way they had moved along the eastern wall in the Black Tide, the way they had stood at the edge of the cold fire: not fast. Not stopping. And coming toward them through the walls, burning, eleven thousand drones of thread and steel with a core in each chest, built ten thousand years ago to fight the Reaper and never used.

The Third Battalion went into them.

They cannot see us.

The drones. They were built to fight the Reaper's hands. They see bright. They see burning. They see marks.

The dead are not bright. The dead are not marked.

The drones cannot see the Third Battalion.

And the Third Battalion, sixty shapes of ash, with pale fire on the shapes of their spears and swords and one pick, went through eleven thousand drones in the corridors of the flagship the way the gleaners had gone for the dead at the rim of the island: low, fast, between, and where they went, constructs of thread and steel came apart.

And the cores came out.

He felt every one.

That was the thing he had not expected. Each drone the Third Battalion took apart had a core in its chest. A person. Pressed. Burning. Put in a construct ten thousand years ago or a hundred or ten, and never asked. And when the construct came apart round them, the core did not fall. It hung in the corridor, in the dark, burning, with nobody holding it for the first time.

Not dead. Not alive. Pressed. Like the cells in the walls.

Ask them, he thought.

I am.

Ichiro is. Through the Lens. Every one, as it comes free.

Most of them say: the door.

They have been in the drones for a very long time.

He opened it. From the Lens chamber, standing in front of the stone with his wife's hand on the staff beside his. Down at the bottom of the Death Law, the cool dark place, the door, a little open. He opened it the rest of the way, and held it, and they came. Out of the corridors of the flagship. Out of drones of thread and steel. Small lights, burning, going past him on the threshold, one after another, the way the old ones had gone past him from the palm of the hand at Port Halvard.

They count. The drones. They are constructs with a weave. Like walkers.

Two minutes.

And then the guard.

Four thousand. S and A. Marked. Armed. Living. Coming through the decks behind the drones toward the bow, and they could see the Third Battalion, because they were not drones. They were people. And they knew what the dead were.

The Third Battalion did not go into them.

Huo says: sir. They're alive.

He says: I know.

Huo says: they're coming for the stone.

He says: I know.

Huo says: Article Seven. The boy's rule. The dead may defend.

He says: yes.

Huo says: then we defend. We don't conquer. We don't kill anyone who doesn't have to be killed.

He says: that is not a question, sergeant.

And sixty shapes of ash, and a dead captain with one arm, stood in the corridors of the flagship of the House of Severin in front of four thousand living soldiers of the Ladder Guard, and did not strike.

They held.

The way they had held the eastern wall. The way they had stood at the edge of the cold fire so the cold came to them first. In the corridors. Facing out. With pale fire on their weapons and their weapons not moving, so that four thousand Ladder Guard, S and A, marked, armed, coming through the walls to break the root of every face in the Federation, came round a corner and found themselves facing a line of the dead that would not go aside and would not strike.

Huo says: you can go back.

Seed is carrying it. In the Federation's language. Without the grade.

Huo says: or you can try to go through us. We're ash. We've stood in front of worse. But if you go through us, you go through the Third Battalion of the Eastern Line, and we've never once been gone through.

Huo says: I'm telling you first.

The Ladder Guard stopped.

One minute.

Yuanshu.

The ten Paragons are past the guard.

They did not come through the corridors. They came through the hull. From outside. Round the bow.

They are in the walls of this chamber.

The curved black stone of the wall of the Lens chamber, behind the disc, behind the lamp, behind the fog-ringer, cracked.

He turned round.

Lingyue had already turned. She had her hand off the staff and on her grandfather's sword, and she drew it, all hundred kilograms, one-handed, with Xiaodeng asleep in the sling against her chest, and stood in front of the Lens.

The wall came apart.

And through it, out of the dark of orbit, burning, came ten Paragons of the Great Houses of the Lamplit Worlds, with faces at their throats that were not their faces, and two lines of true writing in each of them that they had read at dawn and could not unread.

They stopped.

On the edge of the broken wall. Ten of them. Looking at the disc of black stone the width of the chamber, ground so thin you could see through it, with a lamp burning in it. At a woman in front of it with a sword in one hand and a sleeping baby in a sling. At a boy beside her with a white staff, who had stood on a platform in the Assembly the day before and said every one of their wants back to them, exactly, so that they were heard twice.

"Move," said the first of them. A man in the blue of the House of Teller. Young, for a Paragon. A hundred, perhaps. His voice was shaking.

"No," said Shen Lingyue.

"You can't stop ten of us."

"I know," said Lingyue. "I'm not moving anyway."

And from behind them, down the long corridor, at an even pace, unhurried, out of a fight between two storms deciding which one was the sky, came the First of the Ladder.

In a C's gray coat, torn. With blood on his face. With one arm hanging.

Walking.
