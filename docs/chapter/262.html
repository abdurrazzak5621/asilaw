Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 262The Eighth Candle

czverse11 min read

It talked for three hours before anyone answered it, and Seed listened to every second.

Not to them. To the dark. The folded thing hung in the sky above the keeper's field, low over the end of the world, the size of a building, not moving, and out of it, on every frequency Kwan's instruments could find and on several they could not, came a voice. Not a person's. A recording. The same sequence, over and over, slow and patient and very loud, the way a ship's horn sounds in a harbor at night: I am here. I am here. I am here.

It is a language.

It is teaching itself to me. That is what it is for. It starts with numbers. Then the names of the numbers. Then things that can be counted. Then the names of those.

It is very well made. Someone has done this many times. Someone has knocked on a great many doors.

I need three hours. No data, no learning. I am getting the data.

He sat on the vault steps in the banking hall at three in the morning with the staff across his knees and listened to Seed listen. The hall was full. Nobody had gone back to bed. Lingyue was on the stairs, wrapped in a blanket, with the sword. His mother at the long table. Xiaoyu on the stepladder, not writing. Su Mingli, who had come at half past two with her arms folded. Gao Min, who had come at a quarter to three with her thermos. Kwan with the laptop. Old Seven at the bronze door, facing out, the way he faced out when he did not know yet which way the trouble was coming from.

At ten past six, as the sun came up over the river, Seed said:

I have it.

Not all of it. Enough.

It is a human language. Not ours. Older. The grammar is built round a word that means grade. Every sentence about a person has the grade in it, the way ours has the tense.

I would like to answer it. I would like you to tell me what to say.

They answered it from the north wall, because that was where they answered things.

Not with the radio. The folded ship was listening on frequencies the radio did not have, and Seed said that the simplest way to be heard was to be heard the way the keeper heard: through the staff, along the gold thread, up into the field, and out of the keeper's own eye, which was right there, a few hundred kilometers from the crinkle in the dark. The keeper said yes before it was asked. It said, in its rough voice, that something made of kept people was sitting in front of its eye, and it would very much like to know why.

He stood in his old place on the Sector Nine battlement at seven in the morning with the staff and said, through Seed, through the keeper, in a language he did not know:

This is Blue Star. My name is Zhou Yuanshu. We see you. Who are you?

The answer came back in eleven seconds.

Not the recording. A person. A woman. Seed gave him her voice the way it gave him the keeper's: in his head, in his own language, but with the shape of hers still in it. Young. Clear. Very precise. The voice of someone who has been taught since she could speak exactly how she was to be addressed and has never once been addressed any other way.

Iselde Varr-Anthos. SSS. Third daughter of the House of Anthos, of the Federation of the Lamplit Worlds. Listener-Captain of the folded ship Eighth Candle.

You have not given your grade.

He stood on the battlement.

I don't have one, he said. The stone couldn't rate me.

The silence on the other end lasted four seconds. Seed counted.

...that is not possible.

It's in the textbooks.

Unlisted Orchard Four-Four-Seven-One, said the Listener-Captain. That is what our charts call you. A world in the Shallows. Eleven stones. Ripening, last marked, two hundred and six years ago, by a Federation listener who passed at the edge of your arm and noted you and did not stop.

Sixty-one days ago, a flare was recorded from your world. Lord-bright. Five minutes. Every listener in the Federation within forty thousand folds noted it.

Six days after that, the Reaper stopped.

We have fought the Reaper for ten thousand years. We have lost four hundred and twelve worlds to it. We have never once seen it stop. Not slow. Stop.

And now its threads are still, all of them, from one end of the deep water to the other, and our stones send marks up and nothing comes for them, and the last thread it read before it stopped leads here.

I have been sent to find out what you are.

He did not answer at once.

She is telling the truth. I think. She is telling it the way someone tells the truth when they think it is a weapon.

And Yuanshu. I need to tell you what I can see.

Now. Before you say anything else to her.

Seed had been looking at the ship since two in the morning. It told him now, on the battlement, in the place a little above his eyes, while the Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle waited.

The ship is two hundred and twenty meters long. It is shaped like a candle, which is why I think it is called that. It is very well made. It folds the dark. I do not understand how. I am learning.

The folding is done by the engines. The engines are made of cores.

The cores are made of people.

Forty-one thousand and six hundred of them. Pressed. Packed. In the walls of the engine, the way the miners were in the cores at Heishui. Burning. Not bright. Low. A steady low burn, the way a lamp burns when someone has turned it down to last the night.

They are awake.

I can feel them. Through the keeper's eye. They are awake and they are burning and they cannot move and nobody is asking them anything.

I asked one. Just one. Very quietly. I am sorry. I did not ask you first. I wanted to know if they could hear.

She could hear.

He stood very still.

What did she say?

She said: what grade are you.

She said it the way you would say hello.

And then she said: are you the inspector. Has it been a hundred years. Can I come out now.

He did not say anything for a long time. Long enough that the Listener-Captain spoke again, through the keeper's eye, precise and cool.

You have not answered.

There are forty-one thousand six hundred people in your engines, he said.

The silence lasted nine seconds this time.

...you can see the cores.

Yes.

...nobody in the Shallows can see the cores. There is nothing in the Shallows that can see the cores.

There is now.

They are not people, said Iselde Varr-Anthos, in the voice of someone stating a fact about the weather. They are cores. They were graded. F and E, from the fuel orchards on the inner worlds. They serve a term. One hundred years. Then they are released, and their families are paid, and they are honored at the Fold Festival. It is the oldest law of the Federation. Without fold-cores, we could not fold. Without folding, the Reaper would have picked every world we have ten thousand years ago.

It is not cruelty. It is how we survived.

He stood on the north wall of Lingzhou in the morning sun with the staff in his hand and thought about Callum Ward, twenty-five, an F, on the docks at Port Halvard, who had climbed in through a window with a sledgehammer because a stone had told him what he was.

About a dock worker in the Port Halvard stone. About Wen Jia. About a girl in a bus driver's photograph, and a coal-yard worker's son with a wooden spear, and a statistician's graph that went up a hair every year.

F and E, he thought. The bottom of the ladder.

They burn them.

Yes.

That is what the ladder is for.

The ones at the top fight the Reaper. The ones at the bottom are fuel for the ships the ones at the top fight in.

I think the stones taught them that too. MARK HOW BRIGHT. They were marked for ten thousand years. They learned that the dim ones are for burning.

"Blue Star," he said, out loud, on the battlement. Seed carried it. "The Concordia Charter. No person, living or dead, may be raised, read, harvested, or held without their recorded consent."

The Listener-Captain did not answer.

"You're in our sky," said Yuanshu. "Above our keeper. Who learned to ask, a few years ago, and let everyone it had kept go home or rest or stay, whichever they wanted. It's the only keeper in the Shallows that asks. You're sitting in front of its eye with forty-one thousand people in your walls who nobody has ever asked anything."

We are not subject to your charter.

"No," he said. "But I'm going to tell you what I'm going to do. Before I do it. That's how we do things here."

He looked up at the sky in the north, where, if you did not know where to look, there was nothing. And if you did, there was a small white ring with a gold glow, a third smaller than it had been, and beside it, very small, a crinkle in the morning.

"I'm going to ask them," he said. "All of them. What they want. Through the keeper. I'm not going to touch your ship. I'm not going to cut anything. I'm not going to open any doors. I'm just going to ask." He held the staff. "And whatever they say, I'm going to tell you. Exactly. And then we'll talk."

The silence from the Eighth Candle lasted thirty-one seconds. Seed counted every one.

...you cannot ask a core, said Iselde Varr-Anthos, at last. And for the first time there was something in her precise young voice that was not precision. It is not done. It has never been done. They cannot answer. They are graded.

"I've heard that before," said Zhou Yuanshu. "About a keeper. And three rings. And a tree. And eleven stones."

He closed his eyes.

"Seed," he said. "Ask them."

It took nine minutes.

Not because they were slow. Because there were forty-one thousand six hundred of them, and Seed asked them the way it had learned to ask the walls of the Mouth: each asks its nine. Through the keeper's eye. Softly. Into the engines of a folded ship in the sky above the north wall, into the walls where people were packed and pressed and burning low, a question none of them had ever been asked, in their own language, which Seed had learned in three hours from a recording made by people who had knocked on a great many doors.

My name is Seed. I am from Blue Star. I am telling you first: whatever you say, I will tell the captain exactly. I will not do anything else without asking you again.

What do you want?

And the cores answered.

He felt it. Everyone on the north wall felt it a little, the way they had learned to feel the keeper the way you feel weather. Out of the crinkle in the morning, out of the engines of the Eighth Candle, forty-one thousand six hundred voices that had been burning low in the dark for a term of one hundred years, all at once. Not words. Not at first. The sound a crowd makes when it has been standing in the cold for a very long time, and someone opens a door, and nobody believes it.

And then words. Seed translated them. It went on translating them.

Most of them were the same.

Home.

They say: home.

Forty-one thousand and some. Home. Out. Can I go home. Is it a hundred years. I want to see my mother. I want to see the sky. I want to stop.

Some say: ask me again. They do not know. They have never been asked. They do not know how to want something with somebody listening.

Some say: I want to be graded again. I want them to see I'm not an F.

One says...

Seed stopped.

One says: tell Iselde I'm here.

She says: second core array, nineteenth wall, position four thousand and eleven. She says: tell my sister I'm here. She says: they said I was an E. They said it was an honor. She says: I've been here eleven years. She says: tell her I'm not angry. Tell her I just want to see her face.

She says her name is Maelis Varr-Anthos.

She says she is the fourth daughter of the House of Anthos.

She says the stone marked her E, so the House gave her to the fold.

He opened his eyes on the north wall of Lingzhou.

Beside him, Lingyue had come up the stairs. He had not heard her. She was standing with her hand on the parapet and her face white, and she had heard. Seed had let the whole wall hear.

The crinkle in the sky in the north did not move. But on the other end of the keeper's eye, in the bridge of a folded ship made of kept people, there was a silence that went on, and on, and on.

Seed counted it. It did not tell him the number.

Then, in a voice that had been taught since it could speak exactly how it was to be addressed and had never once been addressed any other way:

...she is a core.

She was graded. She was given to the fold. It is an honor. It is the oldest law.

She was given eleven years ago. I was fifteen. I was told she had volunteered.

...I was told she could not hear.

"She can hear," said Yuanshu. "She's been able to hear the whole time."

The silence came back.

...what did she say?

He told her. Exactly. The way he had said he would. Tell Iselde I'm here. Tell her I'm not angry. Tell her I just want to see her face.

The Listener-Captain of the Eighth Candle did not say anything at all.

And then, out of the crinkle in the sky above the north wall of Lingzhou, very faint, for the first time, something that Seed did not have to translate, because it was the same in every language anyone had ever spoken.

A girl of twenty-six, SSS, the third daughter of a House, in a chair on the bridge of a ship made of people, crying.
