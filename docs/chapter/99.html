Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 99The Long Carry

czverse10 min read

"They can't hear me," said Yuanshu. "In the fire. Nobody can hear anything in there. You said it yourself."

He was kneeling in the cold place with the staff across his knees, in front of seven ancient figures sitting in a circle on a floor of dark stone. Behind him, the living. Around them, the dead. Beyond the curved walls, muffled, distant, the roar of a sun made of screaming.

...yes, said the oldest of the seven, in its voice like grinding stone. ...that is why it is impossible.

"Then we take them out first," said Yuanshu. "Somewhere quiet. Where they can hear. And then we ask."

The seven did not move.

...where.

...there is nowhere. The Hunger is the only thing in this part of the Sea. Everything it touches, it eats.

"Not everything," said Yuanshu.

He looked up. Past the curved dark ceiling of the cold place. Past the fire. Past the gray ring and the black Sea and the far small lights. Back the way they had come. Across forty days of currents. To a small white ring hanging over the end of a world, with a door left ajar.

And a thread.

Dark now. Dim. Banked. So faint you could only see it if you knew where to look. Running from the rim of the raft, out through the fire, through the throat of the Hunger, across the Star Sea. All the way home.

The keeper.

It held three thousand at Port Abiri. It held them gently. Until they could hear.

It has held billions. For six hundred and forty million years.

It is the only thing in the Sea that knows how.

"Elder," he said. "Can it reach this far?"

The Elder was kneeling at the edge of the raft, tall and dim and pale, her silver eyes half closed. She did not answer for a long time.

"It has never reached this far."

"It has never reached into a Hunger. No keeper has. Keepers and Hungers do not touch. They cross the Sea in different currents. They pass each other in the dark and do not speak."

"If it brightens the thread, the Hunger will see it. All of it. All the way home."

"It will see the keeper."

"It will see your world at the other end."

Yuanshu looked at the dim thread.

"Then I have to ask it," he said. "Not tell it. Ask."

He put his hand on the rim of the raft, where the thread began.

And he did not go quiet this time. He was in the cold place. Nothing could hear him here except the seven, and Hush, and the people he had come with.

Keeper, he thought, up the thread, across the dark. It's me. I'm in the middle of the Hunger. In a cold room at the center of its fire.

There are billions of people burning out there. Every world it ever ate. They can't hear anything. They can't answer anything. They're on fire.

I'm asking you to come and carry them out. The way you did at Port Abiri. All of them. Somewhere cool. Somewhere quiet. Until they can hear.

If you do, the Hunger will see you. It'll see the thread, all the way home. It'll know where you are. It'll know where we live.

You can say no.

No answer came. Seed counted the seconds and did not say the number.

Then, very faintly, down the thread, across forty days of dark, a voice. Not everywhere. Not vast. Small. Close. The voice of something that had spent the last six months learning how to make itself small enough to talk to people.

...I HEARD YOU.

...I HAVE BEEN LISTENING. THE WHOLE TIME. VERY QUIETLY.

...I AM GETTING BETTER AT IT.

A pause.

...BILLIONS.

...BURNING.

...I NEVER LOOKED AT THE HUNGERS. I PASSED THEM IN THE DARK. I DID NOT WANT TO SEE WHAT THEY DID.

...I KEPT. THEY BURNED. I THOUGHT WE WERE DIFFERENT.

...WE WERE NOT SO DIFFERENT. NEITHER OF US ASKED.

Another pause. Longer.

...YES.

...I WILL CARRY THEM.

...I WANT TO.

...THAT IS THE SECOND THING I HAVE EVER WANTED.

The thread brightened.

Not slowly. All at once. From a faint dark line to amber to gold to a blazing shining white-gold so bright it lit up the whole cold place like noon, so bright that the seven ancient figures in their circle raised their long heads and the seams in their faces opened wide.

And beyond the curved walls, Yuanshu felt the Hunger see it.

The roar changed. Rose. Sharpened. The whole vast burning sun of the furnace turning, the way the spire's eye had turned, the way the ring had turned over the Lantern sea, toward a single bright line running out of its own heart and away across the dark.

It sees.

Yuanshu, it sees the thread. All of it. All the way home.

"I know," he said.

"Then you'd better be quick," said Jiang Lan.

The cold place had no door. It had never needed one. It had been sealed at the center of the fire for longer than Blue Star had existed.

The thread needed a door.

The oldest of the seven rose. Slowly. Unfolding its long thin limbs at too many joints, standing up in the center of the circle for the first time in an age. It walked, very slowly, to the curved wall of the cold place, where the faint worn markings ran in lines and patterns. And it laid one long thin hand flat against the dark stone.

And the wall opened.

Not much. A crack. A narrow gap, two meters wide, in the curved dark wall of the only room in the Hunger the fire could not reach.

And through the gap, the fire came in.

The heat hit them like a fist. The roar. The pull. Billions of voices. Is anyone is anyone is anyone. Pouring in through the crack like water through a broken dam.

Toward the living. Toward the staff. Toward the dead.

Iron Wall Zhao stepped into the gap.

He did not wait to be asked. He had never waited to be asked. He had stood at the bronze door of the Ossuary every night for a year. He had set himself in front of the press section on Concordia and raised his shield over thirty frightened journalists. He had stood in the street at Port Abiri while the spire's roots came at a running sergeant's back.

He was a wall. That was what he was. That was what he had always been.

A broad old skeleton in the rags of a gate captain's coat, with a great empty shield of rusted iron, setting his feet in the crack in the wall of the cold place and raising the shield across it, edge to edge, filling it. A man standing in a doorway. The way Seed had first seen him, a long time ago, on a hillside, when a boy finally asked him what he needed.

The fire hit him.

And he held.

He burned.

Yuanshu saw it happen. The rust on the shield going red. Then orange. Then white. The iron glowing, softening at the edges, beginning to run. And the gray flame in the cage of his ribs, behind the shield, the flame that was Captain Zhao Gangfeng, flickering, being pulled, drawing out of him in a thin bright stream toward the furnace.

He did not move.

He held the crack. And the fire poured against him, and the thread ran out past him, blazing white-gold, out through the gap and into the furnace.

And the keeper came.

It came down the thread the way gold rain had come down the stalk. All at once. Pouring. Not into the cold place. Past it. Out through the gap beside the burning shield, into the fire, into the vast gray-red sun of screaming minds.

Reaching.

Yuanshu felt it. Through the staff. Through Seed. The keeper reaching into the fire the way it had reached into the spire at Port Abiri. Gently. Into a burning house. For someone it could hear screaming.

And lifting.

One. Ten. A hundred. A thousand. Out of the fire. Up the thread. Gold. Out through the gap in the wall of the cold place, past a burning shield, along the shining line across the dark, away, away, toward a field above the sky with empty columns standing like doorways.

A Lantern face. A face like Hush's. A face with four eyes and gray skin. A woman in a straw hat from a different world.

Out.

Out.

Out.

It is working.

Yuanshu, it is working.

They are going up the thread. Millions. Tens of millions.

And in the field, where they arrive, where it is cool and quiet, they are stopping screaming.

They are looking around.

They are asking where they are.

"Then ask them," said Yuanshu. "All of them. As fast as you can."

So they asked.

Seed. Jiang Lan, from the staff, in her dry brisk voice, faster than anyone, ninety million names' worth of practice. The Elder, kneeling at the edge of the raft with her silver eyes closed, sending her glass-note voice up the thread. The keeper itself, in the field, among the arriving, learning as it went. And Yuanshu, kneeling in the cold place with the staff in his hands and his face wet, asking, and asking, and asking.

Do you want the fire to stop?

The answers came back down the thread like rain.

Yes.

Yes.

Yes. Please. Yes.

Make it stop.

Yes.

One billion.

Two.

The furnace is shrinking, Yuanshu. I can feel it.

At the crack, Iron Wall Zhao was going.

His shield was white. All of it. Glowing. Running at the edges like wax. His arm bones behind it had gone gray and cracked. And the gray flame in his chest was a single thin thread now, drawn out and out toward the fire, barely holding.

Yuanshu went to him.

He knelt on the cool dark floor beside the burning shield, as close as he could get to the heat, and put his hand flat on the stone.

"Captain Zhao," he said. "What do you want?"

Captain Zhao did not answer for a moment.

Then, very faintly, the way it had answered on the first night, when a boy in a cemetery asked a dead captain who's behind you, the flame flickered.

He says: hold.

He says: until it's done. Then quiet.

He says: he is a wall. Walls stand until the people behind them are safe. Then they can fall down.

He says: thank you for asking.

"Thank you," said Yuanshu. His voice broke. "Thank you for holding."

Four billion.

Five.

The furnace is half its size.

Six.

The roar was dropping. The pull was weakening. Through the gap, past the burning shield, the vast gray-red sun was dimming. Shrinking. Its rivers running thin.

Seven.

Seven and a half.

Yuanshu.

There are only a few left.

A small stream. Near the top. Thin. Very old. Burning the longest of anything in the fire.

The keeper cannot reach them. They are too deep. Too old. Too quiet in their burning.

They are...

He knew.

He turned.

Hush was already walking to the gap.

It did not ask anyone's permission. It walked across the cool dark floor of the cold place on its many thin pale legs, past the living, past the seven, past Old Seven, who reached for it, and missed, and did not reach again.

It stopped at the gap. Beside the burning shield.

And looked out at the fire. At a thin stream near the top of the shrinking sun. Very old. Burning.

...that is my world, said Hush. ...the last of them.

...they cannot hear anyone else.

...they might hear me.

...I know the greeting.

It looked back. At Old Seven. All of its dark eyes.

...captain.

...I will come back.

And then the last child of the Asking World stepped through the gap, past the white-hot shield of a dead captain, into what was left of the fire.

And Old Seven's gray flame, in the cage of his ribs, went down to a single bead, and stayed there, while he stood at the edge of the cold place with his spear in his three-fingered hand and watched, and did not move, and did not look away.
