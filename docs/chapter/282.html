Book 4: Other Orchards

Chapter 282Ichiro

czverse11 min read

He talked all night, and everyone on Blue Star heard him, and nobody minded.

It began in the bow of the Severin flagship, two hundred kilometers over Lingzhou, in a chamber only the House of Severin entered, inside a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard, ground so thin you could see through it. It began the moment the crossing ended, at twenty-three minutes past eleven on the eighth night. Seed felt it start. Wen Jia felt it. The old man at Port Abiri felt it and said oh no, he's started, in a voice Seed said was fond.

And it went out.

Not to Blue Star. Not yet. Through the Lens. Because the Lens was the root, and every stone cut from it was, a little, still part of it, and a fog-ringer of twenty-two who had been quiet for nine hundred years and talked to fish had just sat down in the root and found that he could be heard by everything it had ever been cut into.

He is talking to the cells.

All of them. Four million one hundred and eighty thousand. Through the layer that stops their ears. Not as sound. As a mark. Thousands of marks. One after another. Very fast.

He is not tying them properly. He is tying them the way he rings a boat call. Too fast in the middle.

They are getting through anyway.

He heard some of it. Seed let him. Sitting on the platform of the Awakening Hall at midnight with his back against the warm black stone, beside the body of a nun of twelve hundred years who sat cross-legged with her hand flat on it and a lamp in her lap, still burning. On the shore.

Hello.

Hello. Hello. Can you hear me? Not hear. Read. You're reading this. I know you can't hear. They stopped your ears. That's all right. I'm not going to say anything you need ears for.

My name's Ichiro. I'm from Shiomi. It's a fishing village. It doesn't exist any more, except it does, it's in a fog in a bay on a world called Blue Star, and my sister's there, and she's also here, she's holding a lamp, I'll show you in a minute.

I'm in the Lens. The big black stone in the front of the ship. The one that reads everybody. I'm sitting in it. I'm sorry, I know it's yours, it's not yours, it's the House's, I know. I'm sitting in it anyway.

I'm going to talk to you for a while. I talk too much. Everyone says so. My sister says so. She's right. She's always right. It's very annoying.

I'm not going to open anything. I'm not going to burn anything. I'm not going to take anyone out. Not tonight. I'm just going to talk to you, and then tomorrow night I'm going to ask you something, and I'm telling you first so you have a day to think about it.

I'm going to ask you what you want.

That's all. Nobody's going to make you do anything with the answer. Someone's just going to hear it.

Tam was on the step below him. He had been there since the First of the Ladder walked up out of the ring of children into the sky. He had not moved. His face was turned up and his eyes were shut.

"They're reading it," he said. "All of them."

He did not open his eyes.

"They've stopped singing. All the ones who were singing. They've stopped to read." His voice was very steady. "Seri's reading it. In the fourth array. She keeps reading the bit about the fishing village."

He was quiet for a while.

"Some of them are crying," said Tam. "You can't cry in a wall. They're crying anyway."

The First of the Ladder reached the Lens at a quarter past midnight.

Seed felt it. Ichiro felt it; he stopped talking, for the first time in an hour, which Seed said was a record, and then started again. Iselde, on the bridge of the Eighth Candle, felt it through the cores of the Rowed Fleet, who were not deaf and could hear everything. She called him on the keeper's eye, at once.

He is in the chamber.

The C's on the flagship are talking. The engineers. The Lens chamber's door was opened for the first time in nine hundred years by someone who was not a Paragon of the House at a reading.

He is standing in front of it.

He has not done anything.

Ichiro says: he's here.

He says: the man in white. He's standing in front of me. In the chamber. Looking at the Lens. He's got a little bit of it in his hand. Cracked. Black.

He says: he doesn't look angry. He looks like a man who's come home and found someone sitting in his chair.

He says: hello.

He did not hear what passed between them. Nobody did. It was in the Lens, in the chamber in the bow of the Severin flagship, between a fog-ringer and the First of the Ladder, and Ichiro said afterward that it was not his to tell, and Kiyo said that was the first time in his life he had ever not told something, and that she was proud of him.

But he heard the end of it. At three in the morning. Seed carried it, because Ichiro asked it to.

Ichiro says: he asked me to get out.

He says: not ordered. Asked. He said the word. In the Federation's language. Without the grade. He said it very badly. He said it like a man saying a word in a language he learned from a book.

He says: I told him no.

He says: I told him I was asked to come here by a lot of people, and I said yes, and I'm going to stay until everyone in the walls has been asked what they want. And then he can ask me again. Tomorrow. That's the rule.

He says: he said: if I break the Lens, you go with it.

He says: I said: yes. And so does the Ladder. Every mark on every face of every person in the Federation was cut from this stone. If you break it, you break all of them.

He says: he didn't know that. Nobody in his House knew that. They thought it was a treasure. They didn't know it was the root.

He says: he stood there for a long time.

He says: then he sat down.

He says: he's sitting on the floor of the chamber. In front of the Lens. With his back against the wall. With the cracked bit in his hand.

He says: he hasn't said anything for an hour.

He says: Kiyo's holding the lamp up. In the Lens. So he can see it.

He says: he's looking at it.

The ninth night came, and the First of the Ladder did not turn the Lens on Blue Star.

He did not have to. It was already turned. It had been turned on everything since twenty-three minutes past eleven on the eighth night, when a fisherman sat down in it. At nine o'clock on the ninth night, the hour the Fleet Marshal had said, two hundred kilometers over the city, in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, a disc of black stone the width of a courtyard, ground so thin you could see through it, opened.

And Blue Star was asked.

He was on the north wall. Everyone was. Half of Lingzhou, and the Candle's children, and the night watch, and the dead along the battlements in their rows of nine, and a woman with a sword on her back and a month-old baby in a sling and a lamp in her hand, held.

It was not a voice. It was not a sound. It came the way the Singers' song had come on the first night: up out of the stones of the city and the river and the sky, the way you hear a thing you have always known and forgotten. Except that it was not beautiful the way the song had been. It was a young man's voice, quick, a little too fast in the middle, in every language on Blue Star at once, and it was talking.

Hello. Hello, Blue Star. Can you hear me? This time you can. You don't have ears that are stopped.

I'm Ichiro. From Shiomi. It was a fishing village in Yamato. Some of you know it. Most of you don't. It's in a fog in a bay now. My sister's there. She's also here. She's holding a lamp. Look up. North. Over the end of the world. There.

And in the north, low over the end of the world, beside the keeper's small white ring and the Elder's vast gray one and the small gold point at the end of a branch nobody could see, among ninety burning cathedrals of the Lamplit: a light.

Not white. Not gold. Not the colors of a House.

Pale. Steady. The size, from the ground, of a single star. The color of whale oil in a fog.

A lamp.

On the north wall, Lingyue made a sound.

That's her. That's Kiyo. She's twelve hundred years old and she's holding a lamp up inside a stone the size of a courtyard in the front of a ship so you can all see where the shore is. She's very annoying. I love her very much.

I'm in a stone that used to read people. Every person. Everything that breathes. And mark them. And put them on a ladder.

I'm not going to do that.

I'm not going to read you. I'm not going to mark you. I'm not going to open anything in you. You've got your own stones for that, and there's a girl in one of them who's very good at it, and she'd kill me if I tried.

I'm just going to ask.

And I'm telling you first: nobody's going to do anything with the answer. Not tonight. Someone's just going to hear it. That's all. Someone's going to hear it, and know you said it.

That's all I'm for. I talk too much. But I listen too. Everybody forgets that.

So.

Eight billion people on Blue Star.

Four million one hundred and eighty thousand in the walls of ninety ships over your heads.

Seventeen thousand four hundred in the walls of twelve ships that rowed here.

And everyone on every ship who wears a face cut from this stone. I can see you too. All of you. Hello.

And one man sitting on the floor in front of me with his back against the wall and a cracked bit of me in his hand.

What do you want?

Eight billion people stood still.

He felt it. On the north wall, and in the city below, and across the curve of the world, the way he had felt Lanterns spread across the night side of Blue Star when his mother said the word. Not quiet. The opposite of quiet. Eight billion people being asked the same question at the same moment by a fog-ringer in a stone, and every one of them, at once, finding out what they would say.

He did not know what they said. Nobody did. Ichiro had told them first: someone would hear it, that was all. He heard it. All night. Seed said he did not stop listening once.

But on the north wall, beside him, he heard some.

A soldier of the night watch, with his helmet in his hand, saying, quite loudly, to nobody: I want to go home and see my kids. A woman behind him saying I want to stop being afraid. One of the Candle's children, a girl, saying something in the Federation's language that Seed translated as I want to learn to swim properly. Dabao, enormous, A-rank, Unbroken Wall, with tears running down his face into his collar, saying I want my mum's cart to have a roof. Fat Tan, at the top of the Sector Nine stairs, saying nothing, and then, very quietly, I want to stop keeping things in boxes.

Xiaoyu, at the parapet, with the marker in her hand, uncapped, saying: I want to write it all down.

And Lingyue.

She stood beside him with a sword on her back and a baby in a sling and a lamp in her hand, and looked up at a lamp in the north, and said, in a voice he had never heard her use:

"I want her to be asked."

He did not answer.

He found he could not. He stood in his old place on the Sector Nine battlement with the staff in his hand and a dead captain with one arm at the top of the stairs and a lamp in the north, and was asked, by a fog-ringer in a stone, what he wanted.

And for the first time since a cemetery at three in the morning when he was eighteen, he did not know what to say.

That is allowed.

Ichiro says that is allowed.

He says: lots of people don't know. He says: he hears them not knowing. He says it sounds like the sea.

He says: ask me again tomorrow, boy. That's what you'd say.

And from two hundred kilometers over their heads, in the bow of a ship forty times the size of the Candle, through the Lens, through the keeper's eye, through Seed, through the staff, very quietly, so that only the people who could hear the net could hear it, a man's voice.

Calm. Courteous. Not absolute any more.

The voice of someone who has never once in his life needed to raise it, and has never once been asked a question, and is sitting on a floor with his back against a wall, answering one.

Seed carried it. It did not tell him whether it had asked first. It did not need to.

...I wanted to have been asked.

...when I was twelve. On the morning. Before the stone read me and said SSS and my father stood up in the box.

...I wanted someone to ask me first.

...well, said Ichiro, from the Lens, very gently, for once not too fast in the middle at all. ...now someone has.
