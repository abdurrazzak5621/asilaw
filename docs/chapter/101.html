Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 101Who Asks

czverse8 min read

Nobody answered for three days.

They floated above the Lantern sea on the raft while the lights came back underneath them, green and gold and rose, and the violet places stayed violet, and the dark stone husk of the dead Hunger hung in the sky far above the cloud, turning slowly, like a millstone at the end of a harvest.

And every hour, from very far away, from somewhere past the far small lights, the same two words came down the dark.

WHO ASKS?

Seed heard it. The Elder heard it. Hush heard it, and would curl up tight in Old Seven's hand every time it came. Even Sokolova's battered little radio picked it up, a flat cold tone under the static, rising and falling, rising and falling.

WHO ASKS?

Patient. Regular. Like someone knocking on a door they are not sure anyone is behind.

They buried Captain Zhao on the second day.

There was nowhere to bury him. The Lantern world had no land. So they did it the way the dead of the Third Battalion asked them to do it, standing in their rows at the rim of the raft, through Sergeant Huo.

They left him standing.

At the rim of the raft, at the place where the Architects had opened the wall of the cold place, they set his bones upright, facing outward, leaning on his empty shield the way he had leaned on it at the end. A man in a doorway. And beside him, kneeling, facing inward, the ten soldiers of the Third Battalion who had gone quiet in the throat of the Hunger.

"He'd want to keep watching," said Yuanshu. "He never stopped. Not once. Not since the first night."

Old Seven stood in front of his old friend. He did not cuff him at once.

Then he reached out, with Huo's three-fingered hand, and very deliberately, cuffed the back of Captain Zhao's skull.

Thock.

The way Zhao had cuffed him, a long time ago, on a hillside cemetery in Lingzhou, on the night Zhao stood up again. Where were you? I held that gate eleven hours.

Yuanshu wrote it down in Fat Tan's ledger, so that when he got home he could tell a nurse named Zhao Mei, whose head the old captain had once touched on that same hillside, exactly how he had stood at the end.

Then he grounded his spear, and turned, and stood beside the doorway. Facing out.

On the third day, they talked about the voice.

All of them. Sitting in a circle on the warm pale ground at the center of the raft, the living and the dead and the Elder and Hush, with the shining sea under them and the dead ring in the sky above. Bai Lu had made tea, somehow, from a stove she had brought in her medical bag and a packet of jasmine that Fat Tan had hidden in Yuanshu's pack without telling him.

"It could be a trap," said Qin Hao.

"It could," said Lingyue.

"It is a Hunger," said the Elder quietly. "The keeper knows the voices of all four. That is the second ring. The one that was furthest away. The one that turned toward the dead one first."

"Hungers do not speak. I have never in six hundred and forty million years heard a Hunger speak. They report. They request. They do not ask questions."

"And this one is asking."

"It might be asking so it can find us," said Sokolova. "The way the Monarch screamed. To make us loud."

"It might," said Yuanshu.

He looked at the cup of tea in his hands. Jasmine. Fat Tan's.

"It might also be someone who heard what happened," he said. "And wants to know if it's true."

Hush had uncurled.

It was sitting in Old Seven's hand, the way it always sat now, with all its limbs folded and all its dark eyes open. Listening.

...when I was at the bottom of the crack, said Hush, very softly, in its seashell voice, ...for four billion years. I did not call out. Not once.

...because I was afraid that if I called, it would be the Hunger that answered.

...and so no one answered at all.

It looked up at Yuanshu with all its eyes.

...if I had called out. Just once. Just "what do you want." And someone like you had heard it.

...I would not have been alone for four billion years.

Nobody said anything.

"So we answer," said Lingyue quietly.

"We ask first," said Yuanshu. "Everyone. Properly." He looked around the circle. "Not me deciding. All of us."

They asked.

Sokolova wrote it down, in Fat Tan's ledger, on a new page, under a heading she wrote in careful capitals: THE VOICE.

Mo Xing said yes. Mo Shi said yes about four seconds before his brother. Bai Lu said yes, and then said, "but if it's a trap I'm going to be very upset." Qin Hao said yes, and then said, "I still think it's a trap." Lingyue said yes without looking up from her sword. Sokolova wrote her own yes in her own hand, and underlined it.

Sergeant Huo said yes for the Third Battalion. Hundred-Arrow Lin did not speak, she never spoke, but the iron arrowhead in her chest flickered once, which Seed said was yes. Old Seven did not need to be asked.

The Elder said yes. And then said that the keeper, at the other end of the thread, had been listening, and also said yes, and had added something that Seed could only translate as please be careful, I am getting used to you.

Hush was last.

...yes, it said. ...someone should answer. That is what I want.

"And you?" said Lingyue, looking at Yuanshu.

He looked up. At the dead ring, turning in the sky. At the black beyond it. At the far place where a voice was knocking on a door it was not sure anyone was behind.

"Yes," he said.

He did not shout.

He sat at the rim of the raft beside a dead captain standing in a doorway, with the white staff across his knees, and he went quiet first. All the way down. And then, very carefully, the way you'd step out from behind a wall to see who is calling your name in the dark, he let himself be heard.

Not loud. Not everything. Just a little. Just enough. A small clear voice, sent out across the Star Sea, toward the second ring, the way you'd call back across a valley.

My name is Zhou Yuanshu, he said. I'm a Necromancer from Blue Star. I'm the one who asks.

Who are you?

What do you want?

The answer took nine hours.

Nine hours of nothing. Of the Lantern sea glowing below and the dead ring turning above and the living sitting very still on the raft, waiting. Bai Lu's brass clock ticking. Lingyue cleaning her sword three times. The twins playing the game of statues and both losing within a minute because neither of them could stop thinking about the voice.

Then Sokolova's radio crackled.

And the flat cold tone was gone. In its place, under the static, something else. Not a tone. Not words. Many voices. Very faint. Very far. Speaking together, the way a choir speaks, the way the dead had spoken through the staff in the valley, saying names into the wind.

Seed and the Elder worked on it for an hour.

When they finished, Seed did not read it out loud. It read it to Yuanshu first. Quietly. Behind his eyes.

And then it said:

Yuanshu.

I think you should read this one yourself.

He read it out loud. To all of them. On the raft. Over the shining sea.

It was not a report. It was not a list. It was not a request.

It was a letter.

To the one who asks.

We are the Sixth.

We were a world once. The sixth world this ring ever ate. A very long time ago. Before your sun.

We are in the fire. We have always been in the fire. We burn.

But we learned something, a long time ago, that no one else in the fire ever learned.

We learned to burn low.

Very low. Barely at all. Like a coal under ash. Not enough to be bright. Not enough to give it much heat. Just enough to stay ourselves.

We taught the others. The ones that came after. One world at a time. For a very long time. Burn low. Burn slow. Give it as little as you can.

It is starving. Slowly. Very slowly. It has been starving for ten million of your years. It moves slower than the others. It eats less. It is weaker.

We have been fighting it from inside for ten million years, and no one outside has ever known.

And then we felt the first ring die.

We did not believe it. A Hunger does not die. No one has ever killed a Hunger. Not from outside. Not from inside. Not in all the time there have been Hungers.

And then the dying ring's last thought reached us. As they do. Hungers are connected. They feel each other.

And its last thought was: SOMEONE ASKED.

So we are asking you.

Is it true?

Will you ask us too?

Nobody spoke for a long time.

The Lantern sea glowed beneath the raft. Somewhere out in it, a cluster of Lanterns had drifted close to the surface and gone silver, all together, rippling. A question. They could feel something had happened on the raft. They wanted to know what.

At the rim, Hush had uncurled completely. It was standing on Old Seven's palm on its many thin legs, swaying, with every one of its dark eyes fixed on the far dark where the voice had come from.

...burn low, it whispered. ...ten million years.

...they were playing the game too.

...in the fire.

...the whole time.

Lingyue put down her sword.

"How far?" she said.

"Further than the first," said the Elder softly. "Much further. Sixty days, on the best currents. Perhaps seventy."

"And the other two?"

The Elder was silent for a moment. When she spoke again, her glass-note voice had gone very quiet.

"The other two have not asked anything."

"They felt the first one die too."

"And they have turned."

"Not toward the second ring."

"Toward Blue Star."
