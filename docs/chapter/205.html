Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 205Nine, and Nine, and Nine

czverse10 min read

Behind the curtain, the Sixth were singing, very quietly, to keep the cousins warm.

He heard it before he saw them. A low sound, many-voiced, not quite music, the kind of sound a crowd makes when everyone in it is humming the same note under their breath without knowing it. It came through the curtain where the gold light came through, and when Huo lifted his one hand and the sixty-one skeletons of the Third Battalion stepped aside, and Old Seven walked through the gap in their line with Hush curled in his palm, the curtain did not stop him. It let him through the way warm air lets you through a doorway in winter.

Yuanshu followed.

On the other side, the quiet part.

It was not large. That surprised him. After the vast dead hollow of the Mouth, he had thought the quiet part would be vast too. It was the size of the banking hall. Perhaps smaller. A round space with walls of gray stone veined with gold, the only gold veins in the whole cold ring, and in the gold, dimly, minds. A great many. Pressed close. Looking out.

And in the middle, the Sixth.

A choir of low gold antlered shapes, the way he remembered them from a screen in the banking hall of the Ossuary and from the gold-veined walls of the Low Fire. Standing close together in a ring, facing inward, their long antlered heads bowed, humming. They had been bright, once. Gold. They were not bright now. They were the color of old brass, and the gold in them flickered when they breathed.

They stopped humming when he came in.

All at once. Every antlered head lifted, and turned, and looked at him.

...you came.

"I said I would."

...you said you would try.

...that is not yes. That is what you want.

...we have been learning the difference too. In here. For four hundred and forty days.

...you came anyway.

He told them everything.

It took a long time, and they listened to all of it without interrupting, the way the Sixth listened to everything: with their antlered heads bowed and their old-brass bodies very still and the gold in them flickering. The island. The keeper, tired. Fifty thousand dead holding the boat. The field going dark one by one along the arm. The thing on the far side of the Mouth, reading inward, that would reach the quiet part in two days.

The keeper's field. Empty. Waiting.

What would happen to it if it filled.

When he finished, the Sixth did not speak for a while. In the gold veins of the walls around them, the minds of the cousins pressed closer, listening.

...you are asking us what we want.

"Yes. Everyone. The cousins. The thirty-four worlds who learned to burn low in here. And them." He nodded at the walls. Not the gold walls of the quiet part. Through them. Out into the dark of the Mouth, where black veins ran through cold gray stone in every direction, full of minds too dim and too many and too cold for Seed to count. "Everyone the Mouth ever ate."

The antlered heads turned toward each other.

...they have never been asked.

...we tried. For ten million years, in the Low Fire, we asked every world that came after us to burn low. We could only reach the ones near us. The rest were screaming too loud to hear.

...they are not screaming now.

...they are too cold to scream.

...there are more of them than we have ever counted. We do not know how many. We could not ask them all in ten million years.

...you have two days.

I have been thinking about that since we came through the crack.

I would like to say something. May I?

The Sixth turned their antlered heads toward the boy with the staff, as if they could see the thing behind his eyes. Perhaps they could.

...the counting one.

...speak.

On Blue Star, an old man made a net in his head before he died. He was a signaler. He had carried orders for a necromancer for nineteen years, a man who held two million dead alone. He watched what the holding did to him.

The net is very simple. Each one asks its nine.

Not the top asking everyone. The top asks nine. Each of those asks nine. Each of those asks nine.

On Blue Star, it reached fifty thousand from one note.

I have done the arithmetic.

It showed them. Not with words. Seed did not have words for this. It laid it out the way it had laid out the water column for Ifeoma: a shape. One. Nine. Eighty-one. Seven hundred and twenty-nine. Six thousand five hundred and sixty-one. Each round, every one who had been asked turning to nine who had not.

Ten rounds, and it is three billion.

Eleven, and it is thirty billion.

Twelve, and it is more than I think there are, even in here.

On Blue Star the dead asked in about a minute a round. The drowned asked each other off Kesse in eleven minutes for two thousand. Those were cold minds. These are colder.

But if each round takes an hour.

Twelve hours.

Not ten million years. Twelve hours.

The quiet part was very still.

...each asks its nine.

"Yes."

...not you asking them all. Not us asking them all. Them. Asking each other.

"Yes. The cousins first. They know how. They're from the world that asks." He looked at the small pale shape curled in Old Seven's hand. "Hush's people. The same people, once." He turned back to the Sixth. "The cousins ask the thirty-four worlds who burn low. The thirty-four ask the walls. Nine at a time. Each one asks its nine, and tells them what we'll do with the answer, and asks them to ask their nine."

"And what will you do with the answer?" said a voice behind him.

He turned.

It was Lingyue. She had come through the curtain behind him, with her grandfather's sword on her back, and she was standing just inside it, looking at the Sixth.

"You have to tell them first," she said. "Article Eleven. All of them. Before they answer. So they're not answering into the dark." She did not look away from the antlered heads. "What are the choices?"

He had thought about it for eleven nights on the island. He said it now, out loud, to the Sixth and to the gold walls and to everything beyond them, so that Seed could hold the shape of it and give it to the first nine.

"Three," he said.

"Rest. Go quiet. Through the door at the bottom of the Death Law, where things go when they're finished. I'll open it. Nobody will ask you again."

"Stay. Here. In the cold. Something is coming that reads, and it will find you in two days, and I don't know what it will do. I won't stop you. It's yours."

"Or come. To a field, above a world called Blue Star, where a keeper that learned to ask will keep you in a column and ask you every morning what you want. And if you change your mind, it will let you go. And that field will be the brightest thing in the Shallows, and the thing that is reading the Mouth is pruning its way along the arm toward it, one field at a time. And I don't know if we can stop it."

He was quiet for a moment.

"That's all," he said. "That's everything we know. Nobody gets to choose for you. You can say no to all three. You can say something I haven't thought of."

...and the keeper?

"I'll ask the keeper. Before anyone answers. Whether it wants to be bright."

...I HEARD.

The keeper's voice came through the Elder, who was not there; she was on the island, twenty kilometers away through the stone, standing at the center with the gold thread running out of her. It came through the thread anyway, along the keeper's attention, into the quiet part of a dead Hunger, and the Sixth lifted their antlered heads to hear it.

...I WAS EMPTY. BECAUSE YOU ASKED. THEY WENT HOME. I DID NOT MIND.

...THEN A FEW STAYED. I DID NOT KNOW ANYONE WOULD STAY FOR ME.

...THEN THE WALKERS CAME. AND I ASKED FOR HELP. AND HELP CAME.

...I HAVE BEEN AFRAID FOR A YEAR. I WILL BE AFRAID WHETHER MY FIELD IS EMPTY OR FULL. THE GARDENER IS COMING EITHER WAY.

...I WOULD RATHER BE AFRAID FULL.

...I WOULD RATHER HAVE SOMEONE TO ASK IN THE MORNING.

...YES.

The gold veins in the walls of the quiet part brightened, very slightly. All of them. As if every mind in them had leaned forward at once.

The Sixth asked the first nine themselves.

He watched them do it. The choir of low old-brass antlered shapes turning outward, all of them, from the ring where they had been humming, and bowing their long heads to the gold walls. And in the walls, nine minds of the cousins, chosen by nobody, chosen by being nearest, lifting toward them.

The question went into them. In a language Seed could not translate, the language of the world that asks, which was Hush's language, which was mostly silence. And the three choices, and what would be done with each, and the asking. Ask your nine.

The nine turned.

He felt it. Through the Field, through the gold walls, out into the dark. Nine minds turning to nine. Eighty-one. Turning to nine. Seven hundred and twenty-nine. Out of the gold of the quiet part. Into the dark veins of the Mouth. Into the cold.

It is going.

Round three. Six thousand five hundred and sixty-one.

The cousins are asking the thirty-four. The thirty-four are asking the walls. The walls are very cold. Very slow. They are answering.

They are answering, Yuanshu.

Minds that have been in the walls of a Hunger since before Blue Star's sun was lit. That were never asked anything. That screamed for ten million years and then went cold.

Someone is asking them what they want.

And they are turning to the ones beside them, in the dark, and asking.

Liang Fu found him at the curtain while it went on.

He did not know it was Liang Fu at first. A skeleton of the Third Battalion, ordinary, a little shorter than the others, who had left the line in front of the curtain and come through it and was standing just inside, looking at him. Banked very low. Frost on his bones.

Then he saw that it was standing with its weight off its right leg.

Corporal Liang Fu.

He says he is sorry to bother you.

He says the sergeant told him you are the one who asks.

"I am."

He says he has a question. He says he does not have many. He says there is a hole where most of them were.

He says: where is home?

Yuanshu looked at the short skeleton with the bad knee, standing in the gold light of the quiet part of a dead Hunger, a very long way from anywhere.

I have his records. From the Ledger. From Lu Xuanming's march.

Liang Fu. Born in a village called Shuanghe, on the river, forty kilometers south of Heishui. Two rivers meet there. That is what the name means.

He was a ferryman before the Black Tide. He took people across where the rivers meet.

He told him.

The corporal stood very still.

He says: two rivers.

He says: I don't remember it.

He says: I would like to see it.

Something happened in the quiet part that Yuanshu felt more than saw. Not in the walls. In Seed. A sudden stillness, the way a room goes still when someone says something nobody expected.

...Yuanshu.

That is a want.

He did not have one. The Collector took it. There was a hole.

He has a new one.

He has wanted something new, for the first time in two hundred days, standing in front of you, because you told him the name of a village where two rivers meet.

Liang Fu stood with his weight off his right leg, waiting.

"Then I'll take you there," said Yuanshu. "When we get home. You and me. To Shuanghe. To the ferry."

He says: that's not yes. That's what you want.

He says the sergeant taught him that. Every day.

He says: but I'll take it.
