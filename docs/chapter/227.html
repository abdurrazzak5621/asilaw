Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 227The One Who Grows

czverse8 min read

Kwan saw its shape on the thirtieth day, and the first thing she said was that it was impossible, and the second thing she said was that it was a tree.

She said both of them in the old banking hall of the Ossuary at four in the morning, standing in front of the long white wall with a laptop held open in her arms, and her glasses pushed up into her hair, and her face the color of the plaster. Yuanshu was on the vault steps. He had not been sleeping. Nobody had. The gardener had been coming along the arm for a month, and every night it was a little nearer, and every night the keeper came down the gold thread at three and said, in its rough new voice without the Elder: IT IS STILL COMING. I AM STILL AFRAID.

"Look," said Kwan. "Just look."

She had been watching it through the keeper's eye for thirty nights. Not seeing it; nobody could see it. It gave no light. It cast no shadow. It was a place in the dark of the Sea where the far lights bent. Where the stars behind it, the fields and worlds and husks, slid sideways a little, the way the stones on the bottom of a stream slide sideways when you look at them through moving water.

She had mapped the bend.

Every night. Every star. How far it slid, and which way, and when. Thirty nights of it. And then she had put all thirty nights together, and asked Seed to draw what was bending them.

On the laptop screen, in pale gray lines on black, the shape.

It was a tree.

Not like a tree. A tree. There was no other word. A trunk, very thick, going back into the dark along the arm toward the place where the Mouth had been, and further, past the end of the arm, out into the deep water between arms, where Seed's map had nothing. Branches. Coming off the trunk, forking, forking again, spreading through the dark of the Shallows in long slow curves, the way the branches of an old oak spread over a field.

And on the branches, here and there, at the ends, gray dots.

Rings.

Three of them, at the tips of three branches, near the far end of the arm, where three gold dots had gone dark one at a time while an island hurried through the night. And more. Further back, along the older branches, deeper in. Gray dots. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Hanging from the branches of a tree the size of the arm, in the dark, like fruit.

"They're keepers," said Kwan. Her voice was not steady. "Pruned ones. Every gray dot is a keeper that was above a world. And they're all on the branches." She touched the screen. "It doesn't send them. It grows them. They're on it. They're part of it."

...the one who grows.

That is what the fog-code said. In the boat bell. Sister Kiyo said there was no word in the fog-code for gardener. They used the nearest word they had.

The word for the rice. The terraces. Growing.

It was not the nearest word.

It was the right one.

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

Lingyue had come down the stairs. Xiaoyu was on the bottom step. Old Seven was at the bronze door. Hush was on the windowsill in the back room, in the dark, and it had turned all its eyes toward the open door.

"A tree," said Yuanshu.

Yes.

And the reader. The thing that pruned three fields, and read the Mouth, and took the Sixth.

It was the tip of a branch.

The newest growth. Reaching out along the arm. Finding fields. Reading them. Taking the brightest. Pruning the ones that did not give.

The way the tip of a vine reaches along a wall.

And now the branch is coming here.

"Why?" said Lingyue. "If it's a tree. Trees don't hunt."

Seed did not answer at once.

No.

They grow toward light.

It took all night. Seed worked it out the way it worked everything out now: what it knew, then what it guessed, then the gap between them, out loud, in the old banking hall, while Kwan drew lines on the laptop and Xiaoyu wrote the important parts on the back of a ledger sheet in capitals.

What I know.

It is shaped like a tree. Trunk, branches, tips. The pruned keepers hang on it like fruit.

The tips move. Slowly. Along the arm. Toward fields.

It took the Sixth when they burned high. It took the brightest columns from fields for a hundred thousand years. The walkers carried them. The keeper thought they were weather.

It went still for two months after the Sixth. Then it came straight here.

What I guess.

A plant grows toward light. It does not decide to. It does not want to. It turns because the light is there, and the light is food.

The Collector taught every keeper that kept minds are bright. Brighter than any star. It is in the Elder's people's dreams. It is in the Architects'. Minds burn brighter than anything. Keep them.

I think the Collector planted the tree.

I think it taught the tree that kept minds are light.

And the tree has been growing toward them ever since.

"And the keeper's field is full," said Kwan slowly.

The brightest field in the Shallows. Two hundred million cousins. The walls of the Mouth. The few who stayed.

And the Sixth, burning high in the eyes of the Mouth for thirty-one hours, singing. Brighter than anything any branch had ever touched.

It ate them. It sat still for two months. And then it turned toward the next brightest thing.

Toward us.

"So it's not coming for Seed," said Xiaoyu.

She said it from the bottom step of the stairs, in her pajamas, with the ledger sheet on her knee and the marker in her hand. She said it the way she said things when she had just understood them, fast, before anyone could stop her.

"The rings were. The scout asked for gatherers. Collect the vessel. Prune what it touched. The rings did that. Or tried." She looked at the laptop. "But the tree isn't the rings. The tree's just growing. Toward the light. It doesn't know about Seed. It doesn't know about anything." She looked at the place above her brother's eyes. "It's not hunting you. It's hungry."

...yes.

I think that is right.

I have been afraid for a month that it was coming for me.

It is worse than that.

It is not coming for anything. It is just growing. And everything I counted is in the way.

Lingyue had not sat down. She was standing in the middle of the marble floor with her grandfather's sword on her back, looking at the pale gray tree on the laptop screen.

"You can't ask a plant what it wants," she said.

Nobody answered her.

"You can ask the keeper. You can ask a ring, because it was a keeper once. You can ask the dead and the drowned and a sack made of thread." She did not look away from the screen. "Things that were someone. Or are. Or could be." Her voice was very flat. "A plant isn't anyone. It doesn't want. It grows. That's all it does."

"Then what?" said Kwan.

"Then we cut it," said Shen Lingyue.

The banking hall was very quiet.

"The branch," said Lingyue. "The tip. The bit that's reaching here. It's not the whole tree. It's the newest growth. If you cut the tip off a vine, it stops growing that way. It grows another way." She turned around. "Qin Hao's fire burns what binds. My light falls. Su Mingli's gravity. Orlov's ice. Fifty thousand dead who can walk up to things that can't see them." She looked at Yuanshu. "We've cut threads. We've cut walkers. We've cut rings out of the sky. We can cut a branch."

"And the rest of the tree?"

"Grows another way," said Lingyue. "Somewhere else."

"Toward someone else's field," said Yuanshu. "Someone else's world."

She did not answer.

She is not wrong.

I have run it. Cutting the tip. With everything we have. It is the best number I have run for anything about the tree.

And the tree grows another way. Toward the next brightest field. And that field does not have a keeper that learned to ask, or a boy with a staff, or fifty thousand dead who will walk up to things.

It has nobody.

"I know," said Lingyue. Her voice was not flat any more. "I know. I'm not stupid. I'm saying it because somebody has to say the thing that works." She turned her grandfather's sword over on her back, the way she did when she was thinking, without looking at it. "You're going to say ask it anyway."

"I don't know what I'm going to say."

"Yes, you do," said his wife. "You always do. You just sit on a step for six hours first."

From the back room, through the open door, came a sound.

Small. Soft. A sound like something many-legged crossing a stone windowsill and dropping onto a wooden floor. And then Hush came out into the banking hall, slowly, swaying, all its limbs out, all its dark eyes open, between the people standing on the marble, to the middle of the floor, in front of Kwan and the laptop and the pale gray shape of a tree the size of the arm.

It looked at the tree for a long time.

...in my world, there were trees.

...before the ring. On the surface. Above the crack. Very old. Very slow. We played under them, when we were small, before we played statue.

...my people asked them things.

Lingyue turned and looked at it.

...not with words. Trees do not have words. You put the question down at the root. In the soil. Where it drinks.

...and you wait. A long time. A season. A year.

...and sometimes, the tree grows a different way.

...not because it decided. Trees do not decide. Because something was put down where it drinks, and it drank it, and it grew around it.

...that is what asking is, to a tree.

Nobody in the banking hall said anything.

...you cannot ask the tip. The tip is only reaching.

...you have to find where it drinks.
