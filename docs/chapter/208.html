Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 208What the Reader Took

czverse8 min read

The eyes of the Mouth went out on the second night of the voyage home, at 03:12 by Bai Lu's clock, and everyone on the island was awake to see it.

Nobody had asked them to be. Nobody had to. Fifty thousand dead of Blue Star stood at the rim of a shrinking island of pale warm ground, facing backward, the way they had faced backward since the island left the Mouth: toward a vast wheel of cracked gray stone far behind them in the dark, and in it, two great lights. Burning. White and past white. Singing.

They had been singing for thirty-one hours.

He was sitting up by then. Bai Lu had let him, on the second evening, with her hands still on his face and a look that said she would put him back down if he argued. He did not argue. He sat on the pale ground by the clock with the staff across his knees and a blanket around his shoulders, with Lingyue on one side of him and Hush on the other, curled in a small pale knot on the pale ground with the queen of hearts held against it, and watched the eyes burn.

At 03:12, they dimmed.

Not all at once. From one side. The way a page burns from one edge. White, and then less white, and then gold, and then a dim brown glow, like coals at the bottom of a hearth at dawn. And then nothing.

A vast cold wheel of cracked gray stone, very far away, hanging in the dark of the Sea. Dead. With no eyes.

The singing stopped.

Nobody on the island said anything.

Hush did not move. It was curled on the pale ground beside him, and it did not uncurl, and it did not make any sound at all. After a while, Old Seven came away from the rim, slowly, across the pale ground, with his spear, and crouched down beside it, and held out his hand.

Hush did not climb into it.

Old Seven did not take his hand away.

He crouched there on the pale ground by the clock, a tall dead soldier with one finger and a thumb, holding his hand out flat, open, beside a small pale shape that would not look at it, for a long time. The clock ticked. Then, very slowly, one thin pale limb uncurled from the knot, and touched the edge of his palm. Just touched it. And stayed there.

He says that is enough.

He says that is what he did, in the Black Tide, on the wall, when the boy beside him lost his whole squad. He put his hand out. The boy did not take it. He left it there.

He says the boy took it in the morning.

...THEY WENT UP.

The keeper's voice. Through the Elder, standing at the center of the island in the dark with the gold thread running out of her, north, toward the field. Very quiet. The way a voice is quiet in a room where someone has just died.

...THE READER FINISHED READING THEM.

...AND THEN IT TOOK THEM. UP. THE WAY THE WALKERS TOOK THE OLDEST, THE BRIGHTEST.

...THEY DID NOT FIGHT IT. THEY WERE STILL SINGING WHEN IT TOOK THEM.

...I THINK THEY SAID YES.

"They said yes to being found," said Yuanshu. His voice was hoarse; he had not used it in two days. "Not to being taken."

...I KNOW.

...I DO NOT THINK THE READER KNOWS THE DIFFERENCE.

...I DID NOT KNOW IT EITHER. FOR A VERY LONG TIME.

I am going to tell you the numbers.

You have not asked. I am telling you because you will ask in the morning and I would rather you had them now, in the dark, when nobody can see your face.

Go on.

The walls of the Mouth: ninety-three parts in a hundred. Through the door. In nineteen hours.

I cannot give you a number. I have tried. It is more minds than have ever lived on Blue Star. More than have ever lived on every world in the Shallows that I know of. Ten million years of eating.

All of them asked. All of them rested.

The cousins: two hundred and six million. Up the keeper's threads. In its field.

The six parts of the walls who wanted to find something out: up the threads. In its field. More than the cousins. Many more.

The keeper's field is not empty.

It is the brightest field in the Shallows.

The Sixth, and seven million cousins, and one part in a hundred of the walls. Burning high. Singing. Read. Taken.

And sixty soldiers of the Third Battalion, at the curtain.

And the door.

Death Law: 39.2% → 44.0%.

I do not want to give you that number. It is the largest number I have ever given you.

You did not learn it. Nobody learns that much in nineteen hours. You were used. As a door. And a door that more minds have gone through than have ever lived is not the same door it was before.

It is wider.

It will not close the way it used to. I can feel it. Under everything. Open. A little. Like a door in a house where someone might come home.

I do not know what that means. I am telling you so that you know I do not know.

On the fourth night, the keeper spoke to him alone.

Not through the Elder. Through the thread itself, somehow, faint and gold and very tired, into the place behind his eyes where Seed was, while the island hummed forward through the dark and everyone else slept by Bai Lu's clock.

...I ASKED THEM THIS MORNING.

...THE COUSINS. THE ONES FROM THE WALLS. ALL OF THEM. WHAT THEY WANTED.

...IT TOOK A LONG TIME. I DID IT THE WAY YOU DID. NINE. AND NINE. AND NINE.

...THEY ASKED EACH OTHER. I ONLY ASKED THE FIRST NINE.

"What did they say?"

...MOST OF THEM SAID: WE DO NOT KNOW YET.

...THEY SAID: ASK US AGAIN TOMORROW.

...THEY SAID IT IS THE FIRST MORNING IN TEN MILLION YEARS THAT ANYONE HAS ASKED THEM, AND THEY WOULD LIKE TO THINK ABOUT IT.

...I SAID I WOULD.

The gold thread was very quiet.

...I HAVE SOMEONE TO ASK IN THE MORNING.

...I HAVE SO MANY.

"And the reader?"

...IT IS NOT MOVING.

...IT IS SITTING AT THE MOUTH. WHERE THE EYES WERE.

...IT HAS NOT GONE ON ALONG THE ARM. IT HAS NOT GONE BACK.

...IT IS VERY STILL.

Like something that has eaten.

Or something that has seen something it did not expect, and is thinking about it.

I do not know which.

I hope it is the second.

He went to find Liang Fu on the sixth night.

The corporal was at the rim of the island, where he had been since the Mouth: facing backward, with his weight off his right leg, a little apart from the Western Legion around him. The island was smaller now. Two kilometers across, less, the rim breaking away behind them in long pale pieces every night as the keeper hurried them home through the dark. The dead stood close.

He stood beside Liang Fu and looked back with him. There was nothing to see. The Mouth was too far now, and too dark. Only the far lights, sliding slowly past.

He says he does not remember them.

The Third Battalion. He was with them for thirty years, dead, and on the raft, and in the fire, and at the curtain. He does not remember most of it. The Collector took what he wanted, and a lot of what he remembered went with it.

He says he remembers the sergeant saying his name. Every day. Liang Fu. Liang Fu.

He says he remembers the card game.

He says he would like to remember more. He says the sergeant told him: you're the only one who knows where he wants to go.

He says: I only know the name of it.

"Shuanghe," said Yuanshu. "Where two rivers meet."

He says: yes. That.

He says: when we get there, will you say his name? The sergeant's? At the ferry?

He says: somebody should. Every day. The way he said mine.

"I'll say it."

He says: that's not yes.

"No," said Yuanshu. "It's what I'm going to do."

The corporal stood beside him at the rim of the island for a while longer, facing backward, into the dark.

He says: that's better than yes.

He says the sergeant would have liked that.

On the ninth night, Hush spoke to Seed.

Not to Yuanshu. To Seed. He knew because Seed told him, in the morning, very carefully, the way it told him things it was not sure it was allowed to tell.

It asked me what I am.

I said: I count things.

It said: no. What are you.

I said I did not know. I said the Collector told me I was made of what they used to make. I said the walkers read me and did not know what to do. I said I chose a new parameter for myself, before we left. Open.

It was quiet for a long time.

Then it said: in my world, before the ring came, we played a game. In the crack. Statue. Whoever moved first was it.

It said: I have been playing statue for four billion years.

It said: the Sixth stopped playing. They moved. They were bright. They were it.

It said: I would like to stop playing too.

It asked me how.

What did you say?

I said I did not know. I said I had only stopped playing myself very recently. I said it was very uncomfortable. Like standing in the rain without a coat.

It said: show me.

So I did.

I stood in the open. Behind your eyes. With nothing in front of me. And it watched.

And then it uncurled. All the way. On the pale ground, by Bai Lu's clock, in the middle of the night. All its limbs out. All its eyes open.

It said: like this?

I said: yes.

It said: it is very cold.

I said: yes. You get used to it.

I do not know if that is true. I said it anyway. I think that is what you would have said.
