Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 31The Investigator

czverse7 min read

The roof of the Rift Research Institute was a flat gray field of concrete, slick with rain, crowded with the things buildings keep on their roofs and nobody ever looks at. Air vents like giant mushrooms. Satellite dishes tilted at the sky. A water tank on rusted legs. Cables running everywhere in black bundles, tied down with plastic straps.

At 4:30 in the morning, it was lit by the blue lamps of three Bureau security cars parked in the street below, flashing slowly, painting the rain blue and dark and blue again.

Yuanshu stood in front of the weather mast.

It looked like every other weather mast in the city. A pole of white steel, ten meters tall, with a little spinning cup on top for the wind and a box halfway up for the instruments. It had a maintenance tag on it from eleven years ago. Nobody had touched it since.

Except someone had.

The instrument box was open. Its little door hung on one hinge. Inside, where the wind sensor's circuit board should have been, there was a black lump of something melted and fused, still faintly warm in the cold rain, smelling of burned plastic and something else. Something sweet and metallic, like a struck match dipped in honey.

It destroyed itself after it transmitted. Very thoroughly.

But I can still see the shape of what it was. The way you can see the shape of a burned letter in the ashes.

It was not a radio. It did not use any frequency your instruments can detect.

It used law. The same structure I saw in the notebook. In Specimen Zero.

It was built by them. Or by someone who learned from them.

Director Lu stood beside him with no umbrella, rain running off his silver-rimmed glasses. He hadn't said anything for a long time.

"Eleven years," he said finally. "That mast has been on my roof for eleven years."

"Who has roof access?"

"Everyone," said Lu. "Maintenance. Security. Every senior researcher. There are two hundred keys." He took his glasses off and didn't wipe them. "I'll have a list by noon. It won't help."

A car door slammed in the street below.

Not a Bureau car. The engine sound was wrong: deeper, heavier, military. Yuanshu walked to the edge of the roof and looked down.

A dark green armored jeep had pulled up behind the Bureau cars, with the insignia of the Western War Zone on its door. A young man was getting out of the passenger side. Tall. A red military-cut jacket, rain-dark at the shoulders. He looked up at the roof, and even from forty meters, in the dark, Yuanshu felt the air around him get very slightly warmer.

Fire Law. SS rank. Level 31.

Qin Hao. Eldest grandson of General Qin Zhenyue of the Western War Zone.

He is not here by accident.

The young man held up something to the Bureau guards at the door. A folded paper. They read it, looked at each other, and stood aside.

Two minutes later he came out onto the roof through the stairwell door, shaking water off his hair.

He looked at the melted weather mast. He looked at Director Lu, dripping. He looked at Old Seven, standing by the water tank with the rain hissing on the gray flame in his chest, and did not flinch at all. Then he looked at Yuanshu.

"So you're the ASI," he said. "Shorter than on television."

"So are you," said Yuanshu. "I've never seen you on television."

Qin Hao's mouth twitched. It might have been the start of a smile. It was gone before it decided.

"Qin Hao. Western War Zone, Investigations." He took the folded paper out of his jacket and held it up between two fingers. "Warrant from the Western War Zone's Military Court. Eight months ago, a convoy of ours was ambushed on the Western Pass. Forty-two soldiers. They weren't killed. They were emptied." He said the word flatly, the way people say words they've had to say many times. "Standing up. Eyes open. Breathing. Nobody home."

Yuanshu went still.

"We found tire tracks," Qin Hao went on. "Trucks. Not military. They came after the ambush and took four of the bodies away. The ones that were still breathing." He put the warrant away. "We followed the tracks as far as the Wastes. Lost them. Then two months ago, a crate turned up at a scrapyard in the west with a crystal in it, and a mark on the crystal." He held up a phone. On the screen: a ring of tiny screaming mouths. "Then a week ago, Heishui was on the news. Your mine. Your crystal. Same mark. And then the Northern Union's whole outpost, and your Grid Nine footage, and those trucks." His jaw tightened. "So I asked my grandfather for a warrant and drove eleven hours. I got here at four. Your Bureau's front desk said everyone important was on the roof."

"You came for the smugglers."

"I came for my forty-two." He looked at the melted box in the mast. "And because somebody's sending things into the sky from a building full of crystals, and I'd like to know who before they do it from one of mine."

They went down to the second sublevel together, the three of them, and Old Seven behind.

Qin Hao stood in front of the twenty-three tanks for a long time without saying anything, watching the crystals pulse violet in their slow, out-of-time rhythm. The smallest one, the fist-sized one, was missing from the end of the first row. It sat in its steel case on a desk at the back of the room, gone a pale gray, pulsing slowly.

"That one's different," said Qin Hao.

"Seven thousand people walked out of it," said Yuanshu. "Three thousand stayed."

Qin Hao turned and looked at him.

"Stayed," he said.

"Some of them are waiting for someone they were taken with. Some want their names known. Some..." Yuanshu nodded at the gray crystal. "Some want to help."

Qin Hao looked at the crystal for a long moment. Then, slowly, he walked over to the desk, and crouched down in front of the steel case until his eyes were level with the slow gray pulse.

"Forty-two soldiers," he said quietly, to the crystal. "Western Pass. Eight months ago. Sergeant Wu was in charge. Big man. Laughed like a donkey." His voice didn't change. "If any of you were with them. If you saw them." He stopped. "Never mind. That's stupid. You can't hear me."

They can hear him.

Not through the air. Through you. You are standing close enough.

Wait.

The gray crystal pulsed.

Then, very faintly, it pulsed again. Faster. Twice. Three times. Like someone knocking on a door.

One of the three thousand is asking a question.

"Wu Dagang?"

Qin Hao's head came up.

"What?"

"It's asking," said Yuanshu slowly. "Wu Dagang. Is that Sergeant Wu?"

The young man in the red jacket stayed crouched in front of the case. He didn't move for a long time. When he finally spoke, his voice had gone strange and careful.

"Yes," he said. "That's him."

"He is in here."

"Not in this one. In a bigger one. We were in the same sack for a while. Before they split us. He kept telling jokes. He would not stop."

"He wanted someone to know the punch line of the last one. He never got to finish."

Qin Hao made a sound. It was not quite a laugh.

"That's him," he said again, very quietly. "That's definitely him."

When they came back up into the gray morning, the rain had stopped.

They stood on the Institute steps. The Bureau cars were gone. A street sweeper was working its way down the gutter in the early light, pushing a long gray wave of rainwater in front of it.

Qin Hao lit a small flame in the palm of his hand. Not for anything. Just to look at it. It burned steady and orange in the cold air.

"I came here to find out whether you were part of this," he said, without looking up. "The crystals. The Wastes. The trucks. My grandfather thinks a Necromancer who can raise armies is the most dangerous person in Huaxia, and if anybody's buying emptied soldiers, it's you." He closed his hand and the flame went out. "I saw that crystal knock."

"And?"

"And I think you're the most dangerous person in Huaxia." He looked up. "I also think you're the only one who's going to find Sergeant Wu." He put his hands in his pockets. "The Western War Zone's warrant gives me authority over anyone trafficking military remains. The Night Market of Bones is full of military remains. Your Bureau can't touch it, it's on the wrong side of the checkpoint." He shrugged. "I can."

He is offering you a key.

He does not like you. He is offering it anyway.

I think that is a good sign about him.

"The market opens after midnight," said Yuanshu.

"So I'm told."

"You'd have to go in as someone else. They'd recognize a War Zone uniform."

"So would they recognize a seven-foot skeleton," said Qin Hao. He glanced at Old Seven, standing at the bottom of the steps. Old Seven glanced back. "What's your plan for him?"

Yuanshu thought about it.

"He's a skeleton," he said. "We're going to a market that sells skeletons."

Qin Hao stared at him.

Then, for the first time, the twitch at the corner of his mouth went all the way into a grin.

"That's disgusting," he said. "I'm in."
