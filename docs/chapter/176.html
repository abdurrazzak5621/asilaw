Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 176The Keeper Asks

czverse9 min read

The keeper asked him on a Thursday, at four in the afternoon, which was the first thing that was wrong, because the keeper always came at three in the morning, the long way, through the Elder.

He was on the Academy training ground. Lingyue had come back from Mount Qingyun that morning with a split lip she would not explain and a look on her face he had not seen before, and she had dragged him out onto the sand behind the main hall to spar, and he had been losing, again, and Qin Hao was sitting on the steps of the main hall with a notebook writing down every fall. It was hot. The cicadas were loud. Principal Yue was watching from his office window and pretending not to.

And then, in the middle of a feint, with Lingyue's wooden sword coming round at his ribs, the sky went gold.

Not the whole sky. A line. A single thread of gold, very thin, very bright, coming down out of the north out of a clear blue afternoon, the way it had come down over the Western Pass when the keeper offered to keep a major who wanted to be kept. It came down over the Academy and stopped a meter above the sand, above the two of them, and hung there.

Lingyue's wooden sword stopped a centimeter from his ribs.

On the steps, Qin Hao dropped his notebook.

YUANSHU.

The keeper's voice. Not through the Elder. Not through the staff. Straight down the thread, into the hot afternoon, so loud that the cicadas stopped.

I AM SORRY. I KNOW I COME AT NIGHT. THROUGH THE ELDER. THERE WAS NO TIME.

THEY ARE COMING.

The Elder came down out of the north in eleven minutes, which was the fastest anyone had ever seen her move, and stood on the sand of the training ground in the hot sun with the gold thread running through her, and her silver eyes wide open.

"Walkers," she said, in her own voice, before the keeper could speak through her. "Three of them. At the far edge of the field. It felt them come in."

"Into the field?"

"Into the field." The Elder's long hands were not pressed together. They hung at her sides. He had never seen her hands at her sides. "They are walking between the empty places where the columns stood. Slowly. Looking. They have not come to the middle yet."

THEY ARE LOOKING FOR THE OLDEST. THE BRIGHTEST.

THERE ARE NONE.

I EMPTIED MY FIELD. YOU ASKED THEM. THEY WENT HOME. A FEW STAYED.

THEY ARE WALKING TOWARD THE FEW WHO STAYED.

He knew who stayed. He had asked them. Four hundred and twelve from Port Abiri and Kesse, who had stayed with the keeper because they liked being asked every morning. Major Zhuo Wenli, beside three empty places. A handful of others from a long time ago, from other worlds, who had said yes when the keeper first learned to ask. Not many. Not bright, by the walkers' measure. Just people who wanted to be kept by something that asked them every day.

"How long?" he said.

THEY WALK SLOWLY. THEY ARE NOT IN A HURRY. THEY HAVE NEVER BEEN IN A HURRY.

SIX OF YOUR DAYS. PERHAPS SEVEN. TO THE FEW WHO STAYED.

AND THEN TO ME.

"Then what?" said Qin Hao. He had come down off the steps without anyone noticing, with his notebook in his fist and white fire flickering between his fingers. "What do they do to you?"

The keeper did not answer.

The Elder did. Very quietly, in her own voice, with the thread hanging silent through her.

"A gardener does not pull up a plant that is growing well," she said. "It pulls up a plant that has turned. That has stopped giving what it was planted for." She looked at Yuanshu. "The keeper turned. The scout said so, in its message. KEEPER TURNED. The walkers have come to see if it is true."

"And if it is?"

The Elder did not say anything.

...THEY WILL PRUNE ME.

I DO NOT KNOW WHAT THAT MEANS. I HAVE NEVER SEEN IT. I THINK IT MEANS THEY WILL TAKE WHAT I AM AND LEAVE WHAT I WAS. A RING. A LENS. A THING THAT REACHES DOWN WITH THREADS AND TAKES AND DOES NOT ASK.

I THINK IT MEANS I WILL FORGET HOW TO ASK.

Nobody on the training ground said anything. Up in his office window, Principal Yue had stopped pretending not to watch.

YUANSHU.

I AM GOING TO ASK YOU SOMETHING.

BEFORE I ASK, I WILL TELL YOU WHAT I WILL DO WITH THE ANSWER. THE SMALL WHITE ONE TAUGHT ME. SO YOU ARE NOT ANSWERING INTO THE DARK.

IF YOU SAY YES, I WILL CARRY YOU UP. YOU AND WHOEVER YOU ASK, WHO SAYS YES. INTO THE FIELD. THE WAY I CARRIED YOU BEFORE. AND I WILL CARRY YOU DOWN AGAIN, AFTER, WHATEVER HAPPENS TO ME. I WILL DO THAT FIRST, BEFORE ANYTHING. I PROMISE.

IF YOU SAY NO, I WILL NOT ASK AGAIN. I WILL GO BACK TO MY FIELD AND STAND IN FRONT OF THE FEW WHO STAYED AND WAIT. AND IF I FORGET HOW TO ASK, I AM SORRY. IT WAS VERY BEAUTIFUL WHILE I KNEW.

WILL YOU COME?

It was the first time anything above the sky had asked him for help.

He stood on the hot sand of the Academy training ground with a wooden sword in his hand and the gold thread hanging a meter above his head and thought about that. About a lens in the north that had reached down for thirty-one years and taken minds without asking. About a field of gold columns, and a woman in a straw hat twelve hundred years old, and forty-one young soldiers in white from Grid Nine, all of them asked, and gone home, because a boy and a white-lettered machine had taught a lonely thing above the world that it could ask instead of take.

About a major on a spire, going up into the dawn on a gold thread, to be kept by something that would ask him every day.

Yuanshu.

Article Seven says the dead may defend. Never conquer.

The field is not Blue Star. Going up is crossing a border. The nineteen thousand on the wall refused to cross a border once. On the voyage. You remember.

You cannot say yes for them.

I know. I can say yes for me.

...yes.

You can.

"Yes," said Yuanshu.

The gold thread brightened. All the way up. Into the clear blue afternoon sky over Lingzhou, north, to the end of the world.

...THANK YOU.

"I'm not finished," said Yuanshu. "I'm going to ask the others. All of them. Living and dead. And I'm going to tell them what you told me, and what we think the walkers are, and what we don't know. And they can say no." He looked up at the thread. "I don't know how many will come."

...THAT IS RIGHT.

THAT IS HOW YOU DO IT.

I KNOW. I WATCHED.

Lingyue had not put down her wooden sword. She was standing on the sand a meter away from him with it still raised, the point a centimeter from where his ribs had been, as if the fight had only been paused.

"You said yes for you," she said.

"Yes."

"So ask me."

"Lingyue..."

"Ask me properly." She did not lower the sword. "With the reason. And what happens after. The keeper did it. Seed did it. You're the one who invented it. Do it to me."

He looked at her. At the split lip. At the look on her face she had brought back from Mount Qingyun, which he understood now, standing there in the sun: it was the look of someone who had told her family something true and been listened to.

"There are three walkers in the keeper's field," he said. "We don't know what they are. We know they don't ask and don't speak. We know one of them walked down the sky thirty-one years ago and became the scout under the Institute, and it took a King and Orlov and the whole First Legion and you and Old Seven's spear to kill it, and Lu died. These are three of those, or something like them, in a place where we've only ever been once." He held the wooden sword loose at his side. "If you say yes, you come up into the field with me, and you fight things nobody on Blue Star has ever fought, and I don't know if we come down. If you say no, you stay here, and nobody thinks less of you, and I'll come back and tell you about it."

"And if I say yes, what do you do with it?"

"Stand next to you," said Yuanshu. "The whole time."

Shen Lingyue lowered the wooden sword.

"Yes," she said. "Obviously. You took too long."

On the steps of the main hall, Qin Hao had picked up his notebook and was writing furiously.

"I'm coming too," he said, without looking up. "Don't ask me. I'm writing down that you didn't have to."

He asked the dead that night.

From the vault steps of the Ossuary, through Adjutant Xu, down the Lattice, to every unit on the register: the First Legion on the north wall, the cemetery companies, the Western Legion on its mountain, the Watch of the Shelf at the bottom of the sea, the Spear Corps in their rows on the marble floor of the old banking hall. He told them what the keeper had told him. Up, into the field. Across a border. Three walkers, nobody knew what they were. You can say no. If you say no, you stay where you are, holding what you promised to hold, and nobody will ask you twice.

It took three hours for the answers to come back.

The First Legion: no.

Seventeen thousand of them. All but forty. They say: we promised the city. The city is not up there. They say it the way they said it on the voyage. They are not sorry. Ma Lianhua says to tell you they will hold the wall, and that if you need them on the wall when you come back, they will be there.

The forty who say yes are from Grid Nine. They say they stood in those columns once. They would like to stand in the field again with their eyes open.

The Western Legion: no. They say: for the pass.

The Watch of the Shelf: no. Nana Ampofo says a pilot does not leave the harbor.

The cemetery companies: two hundred and eleven yes. The rest no.

The Spear Corps.

Three hundred. Yes.

Old Seven did not ask them. They asked him if they could go. He said it was not his to say. They said they knew. They said they were telling him, not asking.

Five hundred and fifty-one. Out of fifty-two thousand.

He sat with that number on the vault steps in the lamplight for a long time, and found, to his surprise, that he was not disappointed. It felt right. It felt like the answer the dead of Blue Star would give: we will defend, we will not cross a border, and the few who stood in that field once, or who have followed a captain into everything, will go.

It is enough.

I do not know if it is enough. I am saying it is enough because they chose it, and that is the only kind of enough there is.

Six days.

I need to learn everything I can about something I have never seen, in six days.

I am going to start with the one I have seen.
