Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 3Old Seven

czverse10 min read

At 3:07 in the morning, the sky above the War Memorial Cemetery tore open.

It began as a sound: a dry, splitting crack, like a frozen lake breaking under a truck. Then a line of violet light drew itself across the dark, low over the eastern district, and widened, and kept widening, until it hung over the cemetery like an eye made of bruise-colored fire.

Every speaker in Lingzhou began to wail at once.

"Rift breach. Eastern district, sector four. Grade-three signature. Residents shelter in place. Repeat. Rift breach..."

Yuanshu was already awake.

He was sitting on the cold curved roof of the Tianshu observatory with his knees pulled up, the city spread out below him like a field of embers. He had not slept. He had been watching the lights go out one window at a time and trying to get used to the forty thousand threads he could still feel at the edge of his mind, faint and patient, all pointing the same way.

Now he watched the violet eye open over them.

Grade three is unusual. Rifts over the cemetery have never been stronger than grade one.

The timing is also unusual. Sixteen hours after your awakening. Over the exact place your power reached for.

Probability this rift is natural: 4%.

Someone opened it.

Or someone wanted to see what you would do when it opened.

The air beside him shifted. Su Mingli was sitting on the roof next to him, coat wrapped around her knees, as if she had been there all night.

"The Bureau just locked the Defense Corps out of sector four," she said. Her voice was very flat. "'Procedural review.' No response team for forty minutes." She looked at the violet light. "Director Lu wants to know if you're a hero or a second Black Tide. Either way, he gets footage."

"Who's in the cemetery?"

"One person. The keeper's lodge."

Grandpa Xu had gone home at midnight.

Yuanshu stood up.

"I can close that rift from here," Su Mingli said. "One strike. You don't have to go."

"I know." He was looking east. "But he wants to see what I'll do. So let's show him."

She studied his face. Then, for the first time since they had met, she smiled.

"Your mother said almost exactly that to me once," she said. "Right before she did something very stupid and very brilliant." She put a hand on his shoulder. "I'll be up there. I won't touch anything unless you're about to die."

The world folded.

It was like a page turning. One moment there was observatory roof under his feet and city lights below. The next, there was cracked asphalt, cold wind in his face, and the iron gates of the War Memorial Cemetery standing open in front of him, groaning on their hinges.

The rift's light turned everything violet.

Mist was spilling out of it and rolling down over the cemetery, thick and low, knee-high, flowing between the rows of gray stones like water poured across a table. The stones stuck up out of it in neat lines. Forty thousand of them. The groundskeeper's cat was gone.

Then the beasts came through.

They dropped out of the violet light one after another and landed in the mist with heavy, wet thuds. Wolves. Not the hounds that came through grade-one rifts. These were Ironback Wolves, each the size of a car, their spines covered in overlapping plates of rust-red metal that scraped against each other when they moved, like knives being sharpened. Thirty of them. Their breath steamed. Their eyes were the same violet as the sky.

And behind them, last, something larger stepped out of the light.

A wolf the size of a house. Its fur was black and matted. Its plates were the color of old blood. And from the middle of its chest, under its throat, a second head grew: smaller, hairless, pink as a newborn's, with lips that never stopped moving. Even from the gate, Yuanshu could hear it. A wet, endless whisper, in no language at all.

Rift Lord. Twin-Mouthed Wolf King. Grade three.

Equivalent to a human at Level 55.

You are Level 1.

Recommendation: do not fight it directly.

I wasn't going to.

Far across the cemetery, past the memorial wall, a small lit window glowed in the keeper's lodge. A shadow moved behind the curtain.

The wolves had smelled it too. Thirty heads swung toward the lodge at once.

Yuanshu walked into the cemetery.

The mist closed around his legs, cold enough to ache. He had swept these paths every summer since he was twelve. He knew the names on the named stones, and he knew which ones had flowers every week and which ones never had anything at all. The plain gray stones of the eastern field had numbers instead of names. Nobody brought them flowers. Nobody knew who to bring them for.

He stopped at one of them, near the eastern wall.

No. 7 — Eastern Defense Line — Spear Corps

He put his hand on it.

The stone was cold and wet. Three hundred meters away, the first wolves were loping through the mist toward the lodge, their plates scraping, unhurried, like dogs who know the gate is open.

"I know you can hear me," Yuanshu said.

He did not shout. He spoke the way you'd speak to someone lying awake in the next bed.

"All of you. You died holding this city. Now something's in your city again, and there's an old man in that lodge who fought next to you." He swallowed. "I can't make you stand up. I won't. I'm asking."

Nothing.

Wind. The scrape of metal plates in the mist. The whisper of the second head, rising.

Then, under his palm, the stone grew warm.

It spread outward from his hand like a ripple from a dropped pebble. The stone beside it grew warm. And the one beside that. Down the row. Across the field. One by one, then by dozens, then by hundreds, the plain gray stones of the eastern field warmed in the cold night until the mist around them began to steam.

Echoes detected. 1,204 wills.

They are not just remains. They are listening.

They are saying yes.

Your soul-power can hold three hundred.

Then three hundred, Yuanshu thought. The Spear Corps first.

[Calamity Raise]

The earth did not explode.

It sank. Softly. All across the eastern field, the ground in front of the plain gray stones settled a few centimeters, like a long breath going out. The mist dipped into the hollows.

And out of the hollows, the Eastern Defense Line stood up.

Skulls first, pushing up through the soil and the mist. Then shoulders. Then whole bodies, rising in one smooth motion, bones dark with thirty-one years of earth, some still wearing rusted helmets and the rotted scraps of uniforms. They rose in rows, because they had been buried in rows. Three hundred of them. When they were standing, they were standing in ranks, shoulder to shoulder, the way they had once stood on the city wall.

In three hundred pairs of empty eye sockets, pale lights kindled. Small and gray and steady, like pilot lights in a stove.

Three hundred skulls turned, together, toward the wolves.

The pack stopped.

Thirty Ironback Wolves stopped in the mist with their plates raised, staring. And behind them, the house-sized Wolf King stopped too, one great paw lifted.

The whispering head in its chest went silent.

The will under stone No. 7 is the strongest. Spear Intent 21%. He is the commander of this line.

I can forge his Law Core now. It will be permanent.

It will hurt you.

Do it.

It felt like a nail of white light driven in behind his eyes. His vision went white at the edges. Warm blood ran out of his nose and over his lip, and a thin line of it ran from the corner of one eye down his cheek like a tear.

He did not fall.

Law Core forged: Spear of the Eastern Line.

Bound: "Old Seven." Commander. Permanent. Grows with you.

In front of him, the tallest skeleton in the first rank stepped forward.

On its left forearm was an old healed break, a lump where a bone had snapped long ago and grown back crooked. In the center of its rib cage, where a heart had been, a small gray flame was burning.

It bent and picked up a spear from the grave beside it. The spear was a rod of rust. When the skeleton's fingers closed around it, the rust fell away from the shaft like ash blown off a log, and the steel underneath shone.

The skeleton looked back at Yuanshu, once.

Yuanshu nodded.

The line charged.

It took four minutes.

From the steps of the memorial wall, Yuanshu watched the whole thing, and did not lift his hand once.

He didn't need to. Three hundred threads of cold light ran from his mind to three hundred soldiers, and through them he felt the battle the way you feel your own fingers in the dark. Every thrust. Every step. The weight of every spear. Every gap under a wolf's foreleg where the iron plates didn't reach.

Spear Intent observed in three hundred bodies.

Modeling. Feeding the model back to them.

They are learning from each other. They are learning from you. Every strike is better than the last.

The first rank met the wolves at a run. The spears went low, all together, like one long blade, under the plates, into the soft bellies. Wolves screamed. Wolves leapt, and skeletons went down under them in clatters of bone, and three more skeletons stepped in from the side and put their spears through the wolf before it could turn.

Thirty wolves died in the first ninety seconds.

The Wolf King lasted longer.

It came through the front rank like a truck through a fence, scattering bones across the graves. Its chest-head screamed: a high, shredding sound that cracked the nearest headstones down the middle and made Yuanshu's ears ring. For a moment it was through the line. Running, free, its huge black head low, straight at the steps where he stood.

Old Seven was already there.

He didn't run to get there. He was simply standing on the bottom step, between the wolf and the boy, as if he had been waiting for it.

One step forward.

One thrust.

The spear went in under the jaw of the screaming chest-head and out through the back of the Wolf King's skull, and the beast's own momentum carried it on, sliding, plowing up the gravel path, until it crashed against the memorial steps at Yuanshu's feet.

Dead before it stopped moving.

Behind it, all across the field, the skeletons it had scattered began to pick themselves up. Every one. They brushed the dirt from their ribs and reformed their ranks without a sound.

Overhead, the rift shuddered.

Then it began to close, all by itself, the violet edges drawing together like a wound healing in fast motion, until there was only a thin line, and then nothing, and the stars were back.

Level 1 → Level 14.

Domain of the Dead: unlocked.

Death Law: 12.4% → 17.0%.

Seed v1.1.

New observation: death is not only an ending.

Death is data.

The lodge door opened.

Grandpa Xu stood in the doorway in an old army coat thrown on over his pajamas, holding a combat knife the way a man holds something he has never forgotten how to use.

He looked at the dead Wolf King, lying across the steps.

He looked out at three hundred soldiers of the Eastern Defense Line standing in the thinning mist in perfect ranks.

Then he looked at the tallest one. At the old crooked break in the bone of its left forearm.

The knife slipped out of his fingers and rang on the step.

"...Squad Leader Qi?"

The skeleton turned.

Very slowly, as if it were remembering how to do it one bone at a time, it raised its right hand to its brow in a salute.

Behind it, three hundred hands rose with it.

The old man put his hand over his mouth. His shoulders shook.

"You stupid bastard," he whispered. "You held the eastern line by yourself. We looked for you. We never found you." His voice cracked. "So we gave you a number."

Up above, hidden in the clouds, Su Mingli lowered the hand she had kept raised the whole time, ready to strike.

She had not needed it once.

On every news channel in Huaxia, drone footage was already playing. Three hundred dead soldiers in violet mist, saluting an old man in his pajamas. And a boy on the memorial steps with blood on his face, and no fear in it at all.

In a dark office on the forty-second floor of the Awakening Bureau tower, Director Lu Zhengtian watched the footage to the end. Then he deleted his own copy of the order that had locked the Defense Corps out of sector four, and made one more call.

"It's out of our hands," he said. "Every nation has seen it. Tianshu has him. The Eastern War Zone has him. A King has him."

A pause.

"No. We don't touch him. We make him need something only we have."

He hung up, and turned his chair to the window, and looked out at the faint violet scar in the sky where the rift had been.

For the first time in many years, Director Lu felt afraid.
