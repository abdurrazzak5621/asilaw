Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 100Ash Ring

czverse10 min read

Nobody in the cold place breathed.

The gap in the curved dark wall stood open. In it, Captain Zhao Gangfeng held his shield, white-hot, running at the edges like wax, his arm bones cracked and gray behind it. Beyond him, through the narrow opening, the furnace. Shrunken now. Dimmed. A sun burned down to a coal. And near its top, a single thin stream of very old fire, the last thing still burning.

And somewhere in it, too small to see, a child of the Asking World.

I cannot see Hush.

I cannot see it at all.

It is quiet. It is in the fire and it is quiet and I cannot find it.

That means it is all right.

I think.

Old Seven stood at the edge of the gap with his spear in his three-fingered hand. He had not moved since the small pale shape walked past him. His flame was a single bead. His skull was tilted.

Listening.

Yuanshu did not hear it. Seed did not hear it. Neither did the Elder, or the seven ancient figures in their circle, or Jiang Lan in the staff.

Only the dead heard it.

Old Seven's skull came up. Sharp. And Sergeant Huo, at the rim of the raft, turned his head. And all sixty-two soldiers of the Third Battalion, at once, in their ring, lifted their skulls toward the gap.

They hear something.

Yuanshu. In the fire.

A voice. Very small.

They say it sounds like the inside of a seashell.

And then Seed heard it too. Faint. So faint. Coming through the gap past the burning shield, from the thin old stream at the top of the dying sun.

Not a question. A greeting. The oldest greeting on a dead world.

...what do you want?

And then, after a long, long silence, from inside the fire, other voices. Many. Old. Hoarse. Like wind across a field of dry grass. Answering. For the first time in four billion years.

...what do you want?

...what do you want?

...child. Is that you?

Yuanshu put his hand flat on the floor of the cold place and closed his eyes.

He did not hear the rest. He did not ask Seed to translate it. Some things are not yours to hear. A child who had played statue for four billion years at the bottom of a crack, standing in the last of a dying fire, talking to its people. Its parents, perhaps. Its friends. The others who had been playing the game in the crack, that morning, when the ring came.

It went on and on.

And then, through the gap, very faint, the seashell voice again. Steady.

...they say yes.

...they say: make it stop.

...they say they are very tired. They say they would like to be quiet now. Properly. Not burning.

...they say they are very proud of me for winning.

That was the last.

Every mind in the fire has been asked.

Every one has answered.

Yuanshu.

Every one said yes.

The oldest of the seven ancient figures rose from its place in the circle.

It walked, slowly, unfolding its long thin limbs, to the center of the cold place. To the very middle of the circle, where Hush had sat. And it knelt there. And laid both its long thin hands flat on the dark stone of the floor, where the worn markings ran together into a single small round shape, like a seal, like a keyhole.

...we built this, it said, in its voice like grinding stone. ...so that it could never be used.

...so that no one could stop it unless every mind it was burning agreed.

...we thought no one could ever ask them all.

It lowered its long smooth head over its hands.

...we were wrong.

...we are glad we were wrong.

And it pressed down.

There was no sound.

That was what everyone remembered afterward. No explosion. No roar. No crack of breaking crystal. Just, all at once, through the gap in the wall of the cold place, the light changed.

The furnace went out.

Not dimmed. Not faded. Out. The last coal of the gray-red sun, which had burned at the heart of the Hunger since before Blue Star had a moon, went dark in an instant. Like a candle pinched between two fingers.

And the pull stopped.

And the roar stopped.

And in the sudden total silence, at the gap, the white-hot shield of Captain Zhao Gangfeng began, slowly, to cool. From white, to orange, to red, to the dull dark color of old iron.

Hush came back through the gap.

It walked in on its many thin pale legs, slowly, swaying, past the cooling shield, into the cold place. It was smaller than before, Yuanshu thought. Or it was holding itself smaller. All its limbs drawn in close. All its dark eyes half closed.

It walked straight to Old Seven.

And the old soldier crouched down, the way a tall man crouches to talk to a child. And held out his three-fingered hand.

Hush climbed into it. All of it. Curled up in the palm of a dead man's hand, its limbs wrapped around itself, like a cat that has come in from the rain.

...they went quiet, it said, very faintly. ...all of them. Together. Holding each other. The way we used to sleep in the crack, when we were small.

...they told me to go and see a world that asks.

...they told me that was what they wanted for me.

Old Seven's other hand came up, very slowly, and cupped itself over the small pale shape, the way you'd shelter a candle from the wind.

And his gray flame, which had been a single bead for an hour, came up. Slowly. Gray. Warm. Steady.

Captain Zhao did not turn around.

He stood in the gap in the wall with his shield raised, cooling, dark. His arm bones behind it were cracked and gray. His flame was a thread.

Yuanshu went to him. He knelt beside him on the cool dark floor.

"It's done," he said quietly. "You held it. Everyone's safe."

The old captain's skull turned. Slowly. Toward him.

He asks: everyone?

"Everyone," said Yuanshu. "Every one of them. They're with the keeper now. In the field. Somewhere cool. They're being asked what they want. Properly. One at a time."

The captain looked at him.

Then he lowered the shield.

Very slowly. Inch by inch. Until its bottom edge rested on the floor of the cold place. And he set his cracked hands on its rim, and leaned on it, the way an old man leans on a gate at the end of a long day.

He says: then the people behind the wall are safe.

He says: walls can fall down now.

He says: quiet.

He says: here. Facing the right way.

"Yes," said Yuanshu. "Thank you, Captain. For everything. From the first night."

The flame in Captain Zhao's chest flickered once.

Then, gently, the way a candle goes out at the end of a very long evening, it went out.

And he did not fall. He stayed where he was, standing in the doorway, leaning on his shield. A man in a doorway. Facing the right way.

The seven ancient figures did not go quiet.

Yuanshu asked them. On his knees, in the circle, with the staff across his knees, the way he asked everyone.

The oldest one looked at him with the soft gray light in the seam of its face.

...we have been quiet for a very long time.

...we were quiet because we were ashamed. Because we built a thing that ate the Sea, and we could not stop it.

...it is stopped.

...we would like to see what that is like. For a little while.

...and then we would like to go and find every world it ate. Every husk. And go to each one. And stand there.

...and say we are sorry.

...out loud.

Yuanshu looked at them.

"That'll take a very long time," he said.

...yes, said the old voice. ...we have a very long time.

They came out of the mouth of the Hunger in the dark.

The raft. The living, holding each other. The Elder, tall and pale. Sixty-two soldiers of the Third Battalion. Hundred-Arrow Lin, a drift of ash with an iron arrowhead for a heart. Old Seven with a small pale shape curled in his hand. Seven ancient figures in gray, sitting at the very center of the raft, looking out at the Sea with the seams of their faces open. And at the rim, ten soldiers kneeling, quiet, facing inward. And one standing in a doorway, leaning on a shield, facing out.

Behind them, the ring.

It was not gray anymore. It was not cracked with red. The fire in its veins had gone out with the furnace, and now it was only a ring of dull dark stone, enormous, turning very slowly in the black, like a millstone at the end of a harvest when the miller has gone home. A husk. A shell. Empty.

Dead.

The Elder steered the raft away from it on a long silver current, and for a while nobody looked back.

The Lantern world was dark when they came back to it.

The whole sea. Black. Not a light anywhere. Not a flicker.

They came down through the cloud and hung above the dark water, and nobody said anything, and Yuanshu felt something cold close around his heart.

The light that goes dark might not come back, Hush had said.

Then, beneath the raft, one Lantern lit.

Old. Large. Every color at once, shifting slowly. The oldest one. The last to go dark.

The first to come back.

It glowed there under the surface for a long moment, alone, in the black sea.

Then the one beside it lit. Green. And the one beside that. Gold. And then, spreading out from beneath the raft in a great slow ring, Lantern by Lantern, the way the light had gone out, the light came back. Green and gold and rose and blue, flooding out across the sea, faster and faster, until it reached the horizon and the whole world was shining again.

Not all of it. Here and there, scattered across the sea, small dark spaces. Where the violet ones had been. Where they had chosen to glow, and been seen.

And at every one of those dark spaces, as the light came back, the Lanterns around it turned, all together, slowly, violet.

Remember.

That night, on the raft, above the shining sea, the gold thread to the keeper hummed.

Yuanshu put his hand on it.

And down the thread, across forty days of dark, from a field above the sky and a small white ring and a city on the edge of the Wastes, came a voice he had not heard in sixty days.

Quiet and sharp and slightly hoarse.

"...Yuanshu?"

"Mama."

A long breath, very far away.

"The keeper told me. All of it. The Lanterns. The dimming. The cold place." His mother's voice was not steady. She did not try to make it steady. "I built it. While you were gone. The thing I told you about. For making a world a little less bright. Kwan helped. And Sister Ning. And four thousand people on the wall." A pause. "We tested it last week. The whole city. Sixty seconds. Every light in Lingzhou went dark. Every mind on the wall went quiet. The Lanterns showed us how." Another pause. "It works, Yuanshu. It works."

He closed his eyes.

"Xiaoyu wants to talk to you," said his mother. "She's grabbing the... Xiaoyu, give him a second... she says you still owe her for the key..."

He laughed. Out loud. On a raft in the Star Sea. For the first time in sixty days.

Yuanshu.

He was lying on his back on the raft, afterward, looking up. Lingyue asleep beside him. The twins asleep back to back. Bai Lu asleep with her brass clock wound again and ticking softly by her ear. Sokolova sitting at the rim, writing names in Fat Tan's blank ledger. The dead in their ring.

Death Law: 27.5% → 28.4%. A whole furnace of minds, asked one at a time. Nobody has ever been that close to Death and come back.

And the other three.

The other rings.

I have been watching them since we came out of the mouth.

He went still.

They felt it die.

They are turning.

All three. Very far away. Toward the dead one. Toward where it was.

And Yuanshu.

One of them is doing something I have never seen a Hunger do.

What?

A long pause.

It is sending something.

Not a report. Not a list. Not a request.

Toward us. Toward the raft. Very faint. Very far.

I have been listening to it for an hour. The Elder helped.

It is two words.

He sat up.

On the rim of the raft, Old Seven had turned his skull toward the dark, with a small pale shape curled in his hand. Hush had uncurled. All its limbs. All its eyes open. Staring out into the black.

It says:

WHO ASKS?
