Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 163Off Watch

czverse11 min read

The stars went out in the north first, and then in the east, and then all of them, until the sky over the Adeyemi was a single black lid from horizon to horizon, and the sea under it was black, and the only light in the world was the gray flame in three hundred chests on the afterdeck and the dull red glow of the binnacle on the bridge.

There was no cloud. That was the first thing he noticed. At the Elite breakthrough there had been a cloud, a slow wheel of iron over the battlements of Lingzhou, and a spear the length of a street. This was not a cloud. This was the sky itself, coming down. Not falling. Leaning. The way the ceiling of a cave seems to lean when you have been in it too long.

The air went hard.

It has started.

I am going behind her now. I will be able to see out. I will not be able to run anything big. If I speak, it can hear me.

I will speak three times. No more. I have chosen when.

This is the first.

You are not alone. You were alone last time. You are not alone now. Forty thousand of them, and me, and her.

Go.

And then Seed was gone. Not gone. Behind something. He felt it go, the way you feel someone step behind a curtain in a room with you: still there, still breathing, but out of the light.

He sat down cross-legged on the steel deck of the Adeyemi, laid the staff across his knees, and pushed.

The glass was bigger than he remembered.

That was the theory, and he had learned it in school like everyone else, and lived it once on a battlement: your body at the peak of a realm was a glass filled exactly to the brim, and to break through you cracked it from the inside and let it become a bigger glass. At the Elite breakthrough it had been close around him, a small hard curve. Now it was enormous. He could feel the walls of it far out in every direction, the way you feel the walls of a cathedral in the dark by the echo, and everything he had learned in a year and a half was inside it, pressed right up to the brim. The Field. The Banked Field. The drain. The three-degree model. The water.

And outside the glass, the sky leaning on it.

He pushed. The sky pushed back.

It was nothing like the iron spear. The spear had been one thing, coming down. This was everything, everywhere, at once: a weight on every part of the glass at the same time, even, patient, enormous, like the weight of the sea on the hull of a submarine. It did not hurry. It did not need to. It knew exactly how heavy he was, because it had weighed him.

It had weighed everything he carried.

He felt it find the Lattice.

It went into the net the way water goes into a sponge.

He felt it through the Relay, through Adjutant Xu sitting on his coil of rope at the stern with his cane across his knees and his skull bowed. The weight of the sky, pressing on the glass, found the threads that ran out of the glass to everything bound to him, and followed them, and went down them, all at once, to forty thousand one hundred and eight of the dead of Blue Star who had said yes.

To the Western Pass. To twenty-one thousand soldiers on a mountain of graves in the snow, standing in their rows, holding each other, nine and nine and nine. To Sergeant Ma Dashan at the gate with a pick on his shoulder. To the north wall of Lingzhou and the river gate and the cemetery hill. To the bottom of the sea off Kesse, where a thousand drowned fishermen stood on the dark seabed around a harbor pilot. To the three hundred on the afterdeck, who stood in their rows on the steel in the black night with their spears grounded and their gray flames steady, and did not move.

Each one took a piece.

He felt it happen. The weight that had been pressing on every part of the glass at once, even and patient and enormous, thinned. Not lifted. Spread. Forty thousand ways. Each of them holding one forty-thousandth of the sky, which was almost nothing, which was the weight of a hand on a shoulder.

The glass held.

He pushed.

The first crack ran across the glass at 23:14, and it was the right kind.

Not a break. A widening. The walls of the glass gave, outward, the way the walls of a lung give when you breathe in, and everything inside rushed out into the new space and filled it. The Field went out from him across the black sea. Five kilometers. Six. He felt it go past the bow of the Adeyemi and keep going, out across the dark water, a slope running downhill toward him from further away than it had ever run. Seven kilometers.

The sky leaned harder.

Not on the glass. On the net. It had felt the glass give, and it did what any weight does when the thing under it shifts: it pressed on the part that was weakest.

The weakest were the oldest.

He felt the first of them go out on the cemetery hill above Lingzhou. A soldier of the cemetery companies who had died in the Black Tide at sixty-eight, the oldest man on the wall that night, more than thirty years ago, whose ember had been small and low and tired for a long time. He had said yes. He had known what one in a thousand meant. He took his piece of the sky, and held it for a moment, and then went out, the way a candle goes out in a draught, quietly, all at once.

Then one on the Western Pass. Then three on the river gate. Then one at the bottom of the sea.

...forty-one.

That was Seed's second time. One word. A number. Spoken from behind the curtain, very faintly, in a voice that cost it something to use.

He had asked it to tell him the numbers, even when he did not want them.

At 23:40, the sky looked inside.

He felt it come. Not a weight now. A gaze. The thing that had happened on the battlement eighteen months ago, the audit, the cold iron attention that had gone through him looking for whatever did not belong and found it and taken it apart. It came down out of the black lid of the sky and went into the glass, into the new wide space where everything he had learned was spreading out to fill the walls, and began to look.

Through the Field. Through the Banked Field. Through the drain and the Lattice and the three-degree model and the long slow model of the sea. It looked at everything. It was not hurried. It had all night.

It came to the staff.

He felt it stop there. At the white staff across his knees. At Jiang Lan, a schoolteacher's ember, dry and steady, who did not hide things and did not try to. The gaze went past her.

And then it found the other one.

A small, low, quiet thing in the dark of the staff, the size of a held breath. A mother keeping watch. It had found her once before, he understood suddenly. When the field let Zhou Wanqing go. It had looked at what was left of her in the staff and decided it was nothing that mattered, a scrap, a held breath, and moved on.

It looked again.

Behind her, in a space exactly its size, Seed did not move.

The gaze stayed.

It was looking more closely. He could feel it. The way someone looks at a wall that is a little too thick. The way a guard looks at a cart that sits a little too low on its axles.

No, he thought. Please.

His mother went loud.

For the third time. He had seen it twice: in the field above the sky, when the gold columns turned toward him and something in the staff blazed so bright it hurt, and in the fire, when the Low Fire came down and something in the staff blazed again and he got out. Both times, it had been a fragment of Zhou Wanqing doing the only thing it could do, which was be seen.

She was seen now.

Not bright. Not like before. She did not have that much left. But she stood up in the dark of the staff, in front of the curtain with Seed behind it, the way a woman stands up from a chair by a window when someone knocks at the door, and turned to face the gaze. And the gaze, which had been looking at a wall that was a little too thick, found instead a small fierce bright thing looking straight back at it.

Here, said something that did not have words. Here. It's me. Only me. There's nothing else. Look at me.

It looked at her.

For three seconds, the whole weight of the attention of the sky over the open sea rested on a fragment of a woman the size of a held breath, standing in the dark of a white staff on the deck of a ship. And she let it. She held still and let it look. At her. Only her.

And it decided, again, that she was nothing that mattered.

It moved on.

The glass broke open at 23:51.

Not broke. Opened. The walls went out from him all at once in every direction, the way a held breath goes out, and the Field went with them. Eight kilometers. Nine. Nine point eight. A slope running downhill across the black sea to him from further away than the horizon, and everything that had been pressed up against the brim rushed out into the new space and settled there, the way water settles in a bigger glass, with room to move.

The sky let go.

He felt it lift off the net. Off forty thousand dead who had each held a piece of it and could put it down now. Off the Western Pass and the river gate and the cemetery hill and the bottom of the sea. It went up. Away. The way the iron cloud had gone up, eighteen months ago, slowly, turning, into nothing.

In the north, one star came out.

Then another.

Then all of them.

I am back.

That was the third time. I said I would speak three times. This is the third. I have chosen it.

[Level 61.]

[Lord.]

Field: nine point eight kilometers.

Death Law: 35.1% → 36.4%. You learned something in there. You learned that carrying is not the same as holding. I have written it down under Grandpa Xu's name.

Of the forty thousand who said yes: forty-seven went out. I have their names. The oldest was sixty-eight when he died in the Black Tide. The youngest was a drowned boy of eleven from Sandu who said yes because the others did. I am going to read all of them to you. Not now. In the morning.

And Yuanshu.

She is not on watch any more.

He was sitting on the steel deck of the Adeyemi in the starlight with the staff across his knees, and he could not see properly, and his face was wet, and he did not know when that had happened.

Is she...

She is there. She is not gone. She is resting. The way the dead rest when they have done what they stood up to do.

She moved back. Into the place where she was before she moved over for me. She sat down.

I think she has finished her watch.

"She has," said Jiang Lan, from the staff, very quietly. "Let her sleep, young man. She's earned it more than any of us."

The satellite phone rang at four in the morning.

Xiaoyu. In Lingzhou, at noon, in the Ossuary, under the board. He could hear the marker squeaking on the plaster in the background. She was crying and laughing at the same time and trying to do neither.

"Mom woke up," she said. "From her nap. In her chair. An hour ago. She sat up and said: she's off watch. Just like that." A sniff. "And then she said: tell him I'm proud of him. And then she asked for tea." A pause. "Ge. What happened?"

"I'll tell you when I get home."

"You're a Lord." It was not a question. "Everyone felt it. Su Mingli felt it on the wall. She came down to the Ossuary and said it to the board. Out loud. She said: he's a Lord now, and then she went back up." Another sniff. "And Qin Hao called. He said to tell you he was still first."

"He was."

"He said he knew you'd say that," said Xiaoyu. "He said: tell him I'm writing it down."

At dawn, Yuanshu stood up on the afterdeck of the Adeyemi to stretch his legs, and his legs kept going.

He rose about a meter and a half off the steel deck, slowly, very straight, the way a cork rises in a bucket, and hung there in the cold morning air above three hundred dead soldiers of the Spear Corps, with his arms out and the staff in one hand and an expression on his face that made Hu Dabao, at the rail, start laughing so hard he forgot to be seasick.

Lords fly.

I did tell you. You were not listening. You were crying.

To come down, stop wanting to be up. It is like the water. Do less.

He did less.

He came down onto the deck on his heels, hard, and sat down.

Old Seven, at the rail, did not move. But his gray flame flickered, once, in a way that Seed said, afterward, after being asked three times, was the closest thing to a laugh the old soldier had done in a year and a half.

The Adeyemi went on north through the morning, toward Yamato.
