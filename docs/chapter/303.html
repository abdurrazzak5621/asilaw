Book 5: The Empty House

Chapter 303Later

czverse9 min read

The mark from home reached them on the eleventh day, on the carry road, nineteen hours after Xiaoyu sent it.

He knew it was hers before Seed read it. Her marks had a shape. They came down the net the way her capitals went onto a wall: square, black, no wasted strokes, and true, so that even at the end of a thread so long he could barely feel the far end of it, they arrived whole. He felt it come in under him, out of the dark behind, the way you feel a letter pushed under a door.

It is from Qin Hao. Through Xiaoyu.

It is a graph.

Seed read it to him. All of it. The weights, day by day, in Bai Lu's numbers. Gao Min's tiredness column, one to five, in the margin of the rota. The three flat days. The line going up. The arithmetic: two thousand five hundred in ten days, six thousand in a month. And at the bottom, in his sister's capitals, a line that was not Qin Hao's.

LINGYUE SAYS: DON'T TURN ROUND BECAUSE OF THIS. IF YOU TURN ROUND BECAUSE OF US SHE'LL HIT YOU. SHE ASKED THE WORLD. NOT EVERYONE SAID YES. ENOUGH DID. FOR NOW.

He did not say anything. He went on along the carry road inside the warmth of the Collector, with Old Seven cold and steady beside him, and the line of the graph sat in him like a stone in a shoe.

I was wrong.

Seed said it about an hour later. He had been waiting for it.

I ran the holding all night, before we left. Every way I could think of. Time and people. People and time. I never ran distance. I did not think a thread could pull harder for being longer. On Blue Star, it never did. A Stele's thread is the same length every year.

Qin Hao saw it because we stopped. If we had not stopped at the first orchard, he would have seen a curve and thought it was something else.

That is the twelfth error. It is the worst one. It is the only one that costs anyone anything.

I am annoyed.

At Qin Hao?

At me. Qin Hao was right. I would like to tell him he was right. It will take nineteen hours to reach him. By then he will have found a thirteenth.

He almost laughed. On the carry road, in the dark between the arms, a very long way from anything, he almost laughed.

Run it now, he thought. With the distance. All of it.

I am.

It took Seed most of the day.

He felt it work. Not the way it used to, in the early days, in the cemetery, fast and hot and certain, a thousand runs a second and a number at the end. Slower. More careful. The way you count money you cannot afford to get wrong. It ran the holding again with Qin Hao's line in it, and the rota, and the stones, and the world's not everyone, enough for now. It ran the carry road: how fast the Collector went, which it could measure, and how far the house was, which it could not.

I cannot give you an answer. I can give you a shape.

The cost to the holders goes up with how far we are. It goes down again when we come back. It does not go down when we stop. Stopping only holds it where it is.

So every day we spend out here costs what that day's distance costs.

The furthest days cost the most.

So we go fast.

Yes. As fast as it will go. Straight to the house. No stops.

And then home. And on the way home, every day is cheaper than the one before.

He saw it before Seed said it. He had been seeing it all day, at the edge of everything, the way you see a cliff in the dark.

The wants, he thought.

Yes.

The first orchard took three days. The cost stayed flat for three days. That was cheap, because we were near. Every orchard further along the road will be more expensive to stop at than the one before.

If we stop to give wants back on the way out, we will pay the most for it.

If we give them back on the way home, we will pay less for every one. And less for the next.

The arithmetic is very clear.

The Collector is not going to like it.

The second old stone joined the carry road on the twelfth day.

He felt it the way he had felt the first: a gray thread coming in out of the dark from one side, older than the web, hand-made, running into the worn pale cable and joining it. Another of the first orchards. Another stone on another hill somewhere, still marking, every year, a field where nothing ripened. And under the stone, if he went down, he knew what he would find.

The Collector slowed.

He felt it gather, the way it had gathered at the first. The warmth all round them leaning, very slightly, toward the gray thread. And in the middle of all the wants it carried, more than Seed had a number for, packed like sand, he felt a few of them stir. Warmer than the rest. Older. From there.

I have nine from there, said the Collector. Nine children. On nine reading mornings. One every few hundred years, at the end, when there was almost nothing left to pick. I came after the hands and found them and asked.

I would like to give them back.

The last child said: give them all back.

He did not answer at once.

He had known this was coming since the morning. He had run it with Seed and seen the line, and known that the next gray thread would come, and the Collector would slow, and he would have to say it. He had not known it would feel like this. Like standing at the top of a path with someone old, who has waited a very long time, and telling them they cannot go down to the beach.

We can't stop, he thought. Not now.

The warmth did not move.

He told it. All of it. The way Seed had told him: the line, the cost, the holders in the hall on East Ring Road and on the beach at Kesse and in the valley below the shrine, getting more tired every day the road got longer. Lingyue, asking the world. Not everyone saying yes. Enough. For now. He told it that every day out here cost more than the one before, and that the days on the way home would cost less, and less, and less, and that if they gave back the wants on the way home, they could stop at every gray thread on the whole carry road and it would be cheaper than stopping at this one now.

He told it that he wanted to give them back. He wanted to go down that thread more than he wanted almost anything. He was a necromancer, and there were people in a hill down there who had never been asked anything.

But not now, he said. Later. On the way home. I promise.

The Collector did not answer for a long time.

It did not move. It had stopped. Seed said it had not stopped completely; it was still going along the carry road, very slowly, the way an old person still moves a little when they have stopped at a corner, shifting their weight. But it had very nearly stopped.

I have never been asked to wait, it said at last.

I have waited. A very long time. For things to ripen, and for the hands to come, and for wants to be given to me. That is not the same. Nobody ever asked me to.

I do not know how.

It's not hard, he thought. You just don't do the thing yet. And then you do it later.

What if there is no later?

He did not have an answer to that. He had promised. That was all he had.

Old Seven turned. The cold steady ember on the carry road beside him, one-armed, that had not said anything for two days, turned its attention to the warmth all round them, the way he turned to face a new recruit on the eastern wall.

He says: there's always a later. That's what a soldier's for.

He says: on the eastern wall, in the Black Tide, they couldn't bury their dead. Not while the wall was being held. They laid them down behind the line, in rows, and they said: later. When the wall holds. We'll come back for you.

He says: some of the men who said it didn't live to come back. Most of them did.

He says: the ones who didn't, someone else came back for. That's how later works. You promise it, and if you can't keep it, the man next to you does.

He says: the boy has a man next to him.

The warmth of the Collector was very still.

You came back for them?

He says: he didn't. He was one of the ones laid down behind the line.

He says: the boy came back for him. Thirty years later. At three in the morning. And then for the rest of the line.

He says: that's later.

The Collector went on.

It did not say yes. It did not say anything. But it went on, past the gray thread, past the second of the first orchards, along the worn pale cable into the dark. Slowly at first, and then faster, faster than he had ever felt it go, the way an old person walks faster past a door they have decided not to knock on, so that they will not change their mind.

And as it went past the place where the gray thread joined, he felt it do something.

It reached out. Not down the thread. Only to the place where the thread met the carry road. And it left something there. Small. Warm. The way you leave a stone on a gate post to show someone you have been by.

It has left a mark.

Not a want. One of the nine is still in it. It has left something like a mark. On the place where the thread joins.

It says: later.

It says it has never written anything before. It says it does not know if it is doing it right.

It says it wants to be able to find the place again.

He felt it do it again on the fourteenth day, at the third gray thread. And on the fifteenth, at the fourth. And on the seventeenth, at the fifth and sixth, which came in close together, one from either side. Each time it slowed, and gathered, and he felt the old wants stir in it, warmer than the rest; and each time it did not stop. It reached out to the place where the thread joined, and left something there, small and warm, and went on.

Later.

Later.

Later.

Death Law: 59.4%.

It has not moved. We have not opened the door since the hill.

I am telling you because you have stopped asking, and I think that is because you are afraid to.

Do not be. The wall is not going anywhere. Neither are we.

On the eighteenth day, nineteen hours late, a mark came in under him out of the dark behind. Square. Black. Whole.

THREE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AT THE PEAK SHIFT. ACROSS ELEVEN STONES. A MAN DIED ON THE TWO O'CLOCK. HE WAS EIGHTY-ONE. HE SAID YES. BAI LU SAYS HIS HEART. HIS FAMILY SAYS HE WANTED TO.

WEN JIA ASKED HIM. HE SAID HE'D WAIT FOR YOU. HE'S BY THE STONE.

THE TWO O'CLOCK DROPPED TO SIX HUNDRED THE NIGHT AFTER. THEN IT CAME BACK.

LINGYUE SAYS HOLD ON. SHE SAYS DON'T YOU DARE.

He went on along the carry road in the dark, inside the warmth of something very old that was learning, gray thread by gray thread, to say later, and he did not turn round.

It was the hardest thing he had ever not done.
