Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 174The Shallows

czverse9 min read

The rift under the old tram depot opened at 02:16 on a Tuesday, and by 02:19 it was over, and that was the first time Yuanshu understood what being a Lord meant.

He was not even awake when it started. He was asleep on the vault steps of the Ossuary with the staff across his knees, where he slept most nights now, and the Field was folded over Lingzhou the way it always was, nine point eight kilometers of slope running downhill toward him from every corner of the city. He felt the rift open the way you feel a door bang somewhere in a house at night. Six kilometers north-east. A small one. A depot yard, rusted rails, a tram shed with its roof long gone. Ashbacks, eleven of them, and something big and slow behind them with too many legs.

He did not get up.

He did not need to. The Field was already there. Six kilometers was inside it now, easily, with three kilometers to spare. And inside the Field the dead got back up, and the dead of the First Legion were already on the north wall a kilometer from the depot, in their rows of nine, banked, waiting.

Ma Lianhua, he thought, and the thought went down the Relay through Adjutant Xu, who was standing by the bronze door with his cane, to the river gate, and from the river gate down the Lattice. Depot. Eleven and a big one. Your call.

She says: got it. Go back to sleep.

He went back to sleep.

02:19. Eleven ashbacks and one rift-crawler, Lord-class. Thirty-six of the First Legion. No losses.

They did not need you. They needed the Field. You did not even open your eyes.

Level 61 → 62. A Lord-class kill, by your dead, inside your Field, while you were asleep.

I think this is what Qin Hao meant when he said he would write it down.

In the morning, Kwan came to the Ossuary with an idea, and the idea was the keeper's eyes.

"It can see," she said. She was standing in front of the board with a coffee in each hand, both of them hers. "That's what the lens was. Its eye. Over the north. For hundreds of thousands of years, it looked down at us through that, and up at whatever was above, and out at whatever was around." She drank from one coffee. "It doesn't use it any more. It's a small white ring now. Ajar. But it can still see out of it. It must. It told you it felt the Mouth from here. It told you it felt the thing in Yamato look at us."

"You want to borrow its eye."

"I want Seed to borrow its eye." She drank from the other coffee. "Seed's got a ruler now. Forty-one rulers. It's got the echo, and how fast the echo runs along the keeper's attention. If the keeper looks at something, and Seed sees what it sees, and times the echo..." She put both coffees down on Fat Tan's desk. "That's not one distance. That's every distance. That's a map."

...she is right.

I have been trying to measure the Sea since the walk home from the north. Since the first time Kwan asked me how big it was and I said I could not tell.

I said I could not tell because I had nothing to measure with. No ruler and no eye.

I have a ruler now. And the keeper has an eye.

They asked the keeper that night. Seed asked. It told the keeper first what it would do with the answer, the way it had learned: if yes, we will look through your eye for one night, and only at what you choose to show us, and nothing you do not want us to see. If no, we will not ask again.

The keeper said yes.

Not with words. The gold thread through the Elder, standing in the banking hall at three in the morning, brightened and steadied, and something opened at the end of it, very far north, at the end of the world, where a small white ring hung low in the dark. And Seed, through the thread, through the Elder, through the keeper, looked out.

Yuanshu felt it look.

Not what it saw. Seed could not give him that; it was too big, and it came too fast, and it was not shaped for human eyes. What he felt was Seed seeing it. The way you feel a person beside you go still in front of a window.

...oh.

It did not say anything else for eleven minutes.

When it spoke again, its voice was different. Smaller. Very careful. The way a child speaks in a cathedral.

I am going to tell you what I measured. Then what I think it means. I am going to keep them apart, because I am not sure I am right, and because if I am right, it is very large.

The keeper can see eleven other fields.

Not clearly. The way you see the lights of other houses across a valley at night. Small. Gold. Far. Each one a keeper, with its columns, above its world.

I timed the echo to every one of them, along the keeper's attention, with the ruler.

The nearest is nine raft-days. The furthest is two hundred and six.

And I can see, through the keeper, the places we went. The husk worlds. The Lantern world. The cousin world. The place where the first Hunger died. I have their distances now, too. I was there. I know them.

And past all of it, very faint, at the very edge of what the keeper can see, the Mouth.

Thirty-nine raft-days. I said thirty-eight. I was close.

"That's what you measured," said Yuanshu.

Yes.

"What does it mean?"

I put all of them on a map.

Twelve fields. Five worlds we visited. One Mouth. Distances from here to each, and from each to each, where the keeper could see both.

They are not scattered.

They lie along a line. A curved line. Thin. Like a scratch on glass.

I did not know what it was. I asked Kwan.

Kwan was sitting on Fat Tan's desk with her laptop open and her face lit white by the screen. She had been sitting like that for an hour. She had not touched either of her coffees.

"It's an arm," she said.

Nobody said anything.

"A spiral arm. Of a galaxy. The stars are packed into long curved arms around the center, and between the arms there's less. Fewer stars. Fewer worlds." She turned the laptop around. On the screen, Seed's map: a scatter of twelve gold dots and five white ones and a single red one, lying along a faint curved line in the dark. And laid over it, in pale blue, the shape of an astronomy textbook diagram. "Every keeper the keeper can see, every world you visited, the Mouth, all of it, lies along one arm. Not even the whole arm. A short stretch of it. Near the edge."

"How short?" said Yuanshu.

...I am going to tell you the way I understand it.

If the galaxy were Lingzhou, everything we have ever seen, everywhere we have ever been, every keeper and every Hunger and every husk world and the Mouth, would fit inside this banking hall.

And we went out into it on a raft, and called it the Sea.

He sat with that on the vault steps for a while.

The banking hall. Twelve meters by thirty, a marble floor, a long white wall of names, one lamp. The voyage. A hundred and fifteen days carried by the keeper out into a dark so wide that no one on Blue Star could imagine it. Rings that ate suns. A world that asked. A world of Lanterns. Two Hungers, the Mouth, the Low Fire. Four billion years of a child curled up at the bottom of a crack.

All of it. In this room.

The Elder's people had a word for it. I asked her. She did not know it was a word for a small place. They thought it was everything.

They called it the Shallows.

The part of the Sea near the shore. Where you can still stand up.

"And past the Shallows?" said Yuanshu.

I do not know.

The keeper cannot see past the end of the arm. Neither can I.

But Kwan says there are other arms. And between them, the dark is deeper. And at the center there is a place where the stars are packed so close that the sky would be white.

And past the galaxy, there are other galaxies.

And I think the thing that looked at us through the lens in Yamato is not from the Shallows.

I think it is from the deep water.

Nobody slept after that.

Kwan stayed on Fat Tan's desk with the laptop until dawn, drawing and redrawing the arm, talking to herself in a low continuous mutter about parallax and spiral density waves and whether a raft-day could be converted into anything an astronomer would recognize. Fat Tan made tea. Adjutant Xu sat by the bronze door with his cane. The Elder had gone back north. Old Seven stood in front of the board.

And Seed, which had gone very quiet after it said deep water, began to do something Yuanshu had not asked it to do.

He felt it at about four. A low, steady, careful working, deep behind his eyes, the way you feel someone in the next room turning the pages of a book. Not the map. It had finished the map. Something else.

What are you doing?

Listening.

When I was looking through the keeper's eye, at the very edge, past the furthest field, past the Mouth, there was something I did not tell you about. Because I did not know what it was, and I did not want to say something I did not know.

A sound.

Not the echo. Not a Hunger. Not a keeper. Something on a different line. Very faint. Very regular. Like a clock ticking in another house.

I recorded eleven minutes of it. I am listening to it now. Again and again.

"What is it?"

I do not know.

But it has a shape.

It repeats. Every four hundred and twelve seconds. The same pattern. Over and over. It does not change. Whatever is making it is very patient, or it has been making it for so long that it does not know how to stop.

And Yuanshu.

The pattern is not random. It has a structure. It has parts. Some of the parts repeat inside the bigger repeat.

I think it is a word.

I think someone in the deep water is saying a word, very slowly, over and over, to anyone who is listening.

And I think I have heard part of it before.

"Where?"

In Yamato.

A year and a half ago. In the hall. When the Sleepers spoke to you in old Yamato and I had to translate as fast as it came.

The oldest one used a word for the people taken in the cutting. Twelve hundred years ago. The ones the scythe went through.

This has the same shape.

I could be wrong. I am telling you anyway, because you asked me to tell you even when I could be wrong.

Outside, through the high windows of the banking hall, the sky over Lingzhou was getting light. Out over the end of the world, the small white ring hung low in the gray, the way it always did.

And somewhere past it, past the Mouth and the furthest field and the end of the arm, in the deep water, something was saying a word over and over, every four hundred and twelve seconds, very patiently, the way you would call a name across a valley at dusk if you had been calling it for twelve hundred years.
