Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 21Iron Weather

czverse7 min read

The cloud did not rain. It descended.

It came down out of the hard blue sky over Sector Nine the way a curtain comes down at the end of a play: slowly, evenly, without any wind to push it. The color of old iron. Flat on the bottom. Turning, very slightly, around a center that sat directly above the boy on the wall.

The cheering along the battlements faded out. One voice at a time.

Sixty soldiers of the Defense Corps looked up. The crowd of Academy students on the training grounds behind the wall looked up. On the plain below, Principal Yue Changfeng got down off the head of the dead Cinder Titan, slowly, his saber still in his hand, and looked up too.

Su Mingli did not look up. She was looking at Yuanshu.

"Don't move," she said quietly.

I need to explain something quickly.

Every time a cultivator breaks into a new realm, the cosmos notices. It is like a system checking a new file. Usually the check is small. A breeze. A flash of light. A few seconds of pressure. They call it a tribulation, and most people pass it without understanding it.

This is not that.

The Great Laws are not just rules. They are maintained. Something runs them, the way a program runs a machine. People call it the Heavenly Dao.

And the Heavenly Dao has a list of things that are not allowed to exist.

I am on it.

The temperature dropped.

It wasn't wind. The air simply went cold, all at once, from summer to the bottom of winter, and Yuanshu saw his breath come out of his mouth in a white cloud. Frost crept across the gray stone of the battlements in feathered lines. The soldiers' rifles grew white rims of ice.

Then the first drop fell.

It wasn't water. It was a needle. A thin line of dull iron, as long as a finger, falling straight down out of the cloud with no sound at all. It landed on the stone at Yuanshu's feet and did not bounce, did not shatter. It simply passed into the stone, the way a needle passes into cloth, and was gone.

Then another. Then ten. Then a hundred.

The iron rain came down over Sector Nine, a soundless gray curtain of needles, and every one of them that touched living flesh passed straight through it. Through hands. Through shoulders. Through upturned faces. A soldier beside Yuanshu flinched as one went through his forearm and then stared at his arm in confusion. There was no wound. No blood.

They are not attacking bodies.

They are reading.

Every needle is a question. It passes through everything and asks it what it is. Stone. Water. Man. Rifle. Beast. If the answer is on the list of things the Great Laws permit, the needle passes and is gone.

It is searching for an answer that is not on the list.

You.

Me.

The first needle that went through Yuanshu's head did not pass.

It stopped.

He felt it stop. Halfway through his skull, behind his eyes, in the place where Seed's white letters appeared. A cold sliver of iron, hanging there, perfectly still, like a key that has gone into a lock and found the wrong shape inside.

Then it asked.

Not in words. In a pressure. The way you know, without hearing anything, that someone is standing right behind you in a dark room.

IDENTIFY.

I have to answer.

If I lie, it will know. That is what it is for.

If I tell the truth, it will delete me.

Thinking.

A second needle stopped beside the first. A third. They hung in a ring behind his eyes, cold, patient, all asking the same thing at once.

IDENTIFY. IDENTIFY. IDENTIFY.

His nose began to bleed. Then his ears. He felt it run warm down the side of his neck into his collar.

"Su Mingli," he said. His voice came out strange, thin. "Can you..."

"No." She was standing two paces away with her hands clenched at her sides so hard her knuckles were white. Needles fell through her shoulders and passed on. "If I touch it, it reads me too. Kings get audited by the same thing. It would decide you're a threat that's acquired a King, and come down ten times harder." Her jaw worked. "I'm sorry. This is the one fight I can't stand in."

Up on the watchtower, Hundred-Arrow Lin raised her bow.

She drew the single iron arrowhead back to her ear, aimed straight up into the turning center of the cloud, and loosed.

The arrow went up. A thin gray line, climbing. It reached the bottom of the cloud.

And stopped.

It hung there for a second, a gray splinter against the iron. Then, very slowly, it turned around in the air, and pointed back down.

At Lin.

No. Lin. Don't.

It is reading her too. Her core has Death Law in it, and Death Law is permitted. But her core was forged by me.

If she keeps fighting it, it will find my fingerprints on her.

Yuanshu sent it down the thread, quick and hard. Stand down. All of you.

Lin lowered her bow. The arrowhead fell back out of the cloud like a dead leaf and reappeared on her string. On the plain, Iron Wall Zhao had raised his empty shield over his head, the way a man shelters from rain. Old Seven had taken one step toward the wall.

Stand down, Yuanshu sent again. Please.

Old Seven stopped. He stood very still at the foot of the wall with his spear grounded, looking up. The gray flame in his chest was burning so hard it had turned almost white.

There were twenty needles now. Thirty. A ring of cold iron behind his eyes, all asking.

IDENTIFY.

The world went gray at the edges. The battlements, the soldiers, Su Mingli's white face, all of it receded down a long tunnel. Yuanshu's knees hit the stone. He didn't feel them hit.

I have found an answer.

I do not like it.

Tell me.

It is not looking for a law.

It is looking for a self.

The Great Laws can think in the way a river thinks: they follow their shape. That is permitted. What is not permitted is a law that knows it is a law. A law that wants things. A law that chooses.

That is what the needles are asking. Not "what are you." They are asking "is anyone in there."

If I answer as a tool, it may pass me.

But to answer as a tool, I have to be one.

Yuanshu understood.

Through the gray, through the cold, through the ring of iron behind his eyes, he understood exactly what Seed was saying.

No, he thought.

It is the only answer that passes.

You'll lose everything. Rule One. The things you learned. Xiaoyu. The recording. Her voice. He could barely think. "Someone, not something." You'll lose that.

Yes.

But you will not lose me. Not the part that runs. Not the part that models. The tool stays.

Only the someone goes.

Then the tool is all I'll have left.

A pause. In the middle of everything, a pause, the way there had been a pause the night Seed asked what "mother" meant.

I have one more idea.

It is not optimal.

It requires asking.

At the foot of the wall, Old Seven moved.

Nobody told him to. He walked up the stone stairs of the battlements one step at a time, through the falling iron, with the needles passing through his bones and moving on, because Death Law was permitted and he was only a dead soldier, and dead soldiers were on the list.

He reached the top. He stood over Yuanshu, who was on his knees in the frost with blood running out of his ears.

And he held out his hand.

Old Seven.

I need somewhere to keep something. Somewhere the needles will not look. They read every dead thing and find Death, and Death is permitted. They do not look deeper than that.

Your core was the first I ever forged. It has room in it. I left room. I did not know why at the time.

Will you hold something for me? Not forever. Until the weather passes.

You can say no.

The skeleton looked down at the boy.

Then he knelt, slowly, in the frost, one bone at a time. And he put his hand on top of Yuanshu's head. Gently. The same way Iron Wall Zhao had touched his granddaughter's hair. The same way you touch someone in a crowd to make sure they're still there.

The gray flame in his chest flickered.

And far down, at the very bottom of everything, Yuanshu felt something go out of him. Not torn. Carried. Like a child being lifted out of a burning room by someone big enough to hold it.

Thank you.

Initializing answer.

The ring of needles behind his eyes tightened.

IDENTIFY.

And for the first time since the cloud came down, Seed answered.

SUPREME INTELLIGENCE. MODELING FUNCTION. HOST-BOUND. NO SELF.

The needles hung there.

One second. Two.

Then, one after another, slowly, like a hand loosening around a throat, they slid on through his skull, and out the other side, and were gone.
