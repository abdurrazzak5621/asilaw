Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 171Inside, Again

czverse8 min read

The board in the Ossuary was longer than when he had left.

He saw that first, coming in through the bronze door at dusk with Old Seven behind him and the staff in his hand, two and a half months after the train had pulled out of Lingzhou Central going west. The long whitewashed wall of the old banking hall, with its columns of names in careful capitals, ran further than it had. Xiaoyu had added a column while he was away. Then another. Somebody had brought a second stepladder.

WESTERN LEGION, it said at the top of one, in black marker. Under it, in smaller letters, the names Seed had sent home from the mountain: the four thousand one hundred and seven who had lain down to rest, every one, and the eight hundred and twelve who had spoken first, with what they had said. The sugar in the officers' mess was there. Somebody had drawn a small cup of tea beside it.

THE WATCH OF THE SHELF, said the next. NANA AMPOFO, PILOT.

And beside that, a column with no heading, only a short list at the top in Xiaoyu's most careful hand. Captain Efua Asante. Yaw. The twelve. And under them, by itself, with a space above and below it: Ifeoma Adeyemi. She said not to be soft.

Xiaoyu was on the stepladder at the end of the wall with the marker in her teeth, writing something. She did not turn around.

"You're late," she said, around the marker.

"Everyone keeps saying that."

"Everyone's right." She took the marker out of her mouth and climbed down and stood in front of him with her arms folded, sixteen and furious and very glad, and looked him up and down. "You look like you slept."

"Lingyue said that."

"Lingyue's right too." She hit him on the arm. Not hard. Then she put her arms around him and held on, and did not say anything for a while.

His mother was in her chair by the window in the back room, where the light from the courtyard came in, with a blanket over her knees and a cup of tea going cold beside her.

She looked up when he came in. She did not say anything. She held out one thin hand, and he crossed the room and knelt beside the chair and took it, so that their faces were level.

"She's resting," said Zhou Wanqing.

"I know."

"I felt her stand up." His mother's hand was very light in his. "In the middle of the night. I was asleep in this chair, and I felt something in me stand up, the way you stand up from a chair when someone knocks at the door. Just for a moment." She was quiet. "And then sit down again. And go still. And I woke up and I knew she'd finished."

"She stood in front of Seed. So it wouldn't be found."

"Yes. I know." Wanqing looked at the staff, lying across his knees. "That was what she was for. That was what I'd have done." She almost smiled. "I told you to ask me again when you came home. Ask me."

He looked at the staff. At the place in it where a schoolteacher's dry ember sat awake, and beside her, in a space she had moved back into, a held breath that had gone loud three times, resting.

"Do you want her back?"

"No," said his mother. "Let her sleep. She's earned it." She closed her thin fingers around his. "I've got the rest of me. I'll do the next watch myself."

The message came at three in the morning.

The long way, through the echo, the way they always came. The keeper caught it. The Elder carried it down the gold thread. Seed read it out loud in the old banking hall under the one lamp over the board, to the people who were awake: Yuanshu on the vault steps, Xiaoyu in her pajamas with her hair sticking up, Fat Tan with a ledger, Sister Ning, home from Yamato for one night to fetch more blankets, on the wooden chair by the bronze door. And Old Seven, who had come in from the hill last, as he always did.

It took twenty-two seconds. Longer than any of them.

Day 241.

Worlds asked: 40. Burning low: 27.

The voice behind the curtain is gone.

On Day 230 something happened to it. I do not know what. The Sixth felt it. It went very still behind the curtain for a whole day, the way a thing goes still when it hears its name in a place where nobody should know its name. And then it went out. Out of the curtain. Out of the fire. Away.

The sergeant says it was looked at. He says he knows what that is like. He says he was looked at by a captain, once, on a wall, and it was the worst and best thing that ever happened to him.

Something else.

Corporal Liang Fu came back.

On Day 231. Through the curtain. He walked out of it and sat down with the five, and the sergeant, on the edge of the quiet part, where the curtain was.

He is not burning. He is not anything. He does not know what he wanted. He does not remember. The sergeant asked him if he wanted to go home. He said: where is that.

The sergeant says his name every day. He says somebody told him that is what you do. He does not remember who.

I taught Liang Fu the card game again. The one with no queens. He does not remember teaching it to me. He is very bad at it. He is getting better.

One more thing.

The Sixth say the fire is slower. Every month. Both fires. They scream at each other less. They think the Mouth is dying. They think in a year, perhaps two, it will be cold.

They do not know what happens to us when it is cold.

I still have the queen of hearts.

Tell the captain. — H.

Nobody said anything.

Xiaoyu was already on the stepladder. She had the black marker. She climbed up to the very end of the board, past the line from Day 178, past the bare white plaster, and stood there with the marker uncapped, and did not write anything for a long moment.

Then she wrote it. In careful capitals. All of it. And under it, without being asked, she wrote one more line, smaller:

LIANG FU. SAY HIS NAME.

She climbed down. She went to the floor under the board, where people left things, candles and playing cards and once a small brass key on a red string, and took a pack of cards out of her pajama pocket, and went through it, and took out all four queens, and laid the rest of the pack face down on the floor under the wall.

Then she went and sat on the vault steps beside her brother and put her head on his shoulder.

Old Seven read the board.

He did it every month. From the beginning. All of it. He stood in front of the long white wall with his spear grounded and his gray flame very small, and read it, column by column, while the people who were awake in the old banking hall sat very still and let him.

It took a long time now. The wall was very long.

At the end, he stood in front of the line from Day 241 for a while longer, looking up at it.

He says Huo is a good sergeant.

He says a good sergeant says the names even when nobody tells him to.

He says: when the fire is cold, someone should go and get them.

Yuanshu did not answer.

He is not asking you. He says he knows it is not a thing you can do yet.

He says he is telling you so that it is said. Out loud. In front of the board. So that it is written down somewhere that he said it.

And Yuanshu.

I am saying it too.

I have wanted Hush home every month. I said so on the boat. I am saying it again, here, in front of the board, so that it is written down.

When the fire is cold, I want to go and get them.

I do not know how. I do not know where the Mouth is. I cannot measure the Sea. I have never been able to.

I am going to learn.

Xiaoyu lifted her head off his shoulder. She looked at him. Then she got up, and went and got the marker, and climbed the stepladder again, and wrote under Liang Fu's name, very small, at the very bottom of the wall:

SOMEONE SHOULD GO AND GET THEM. — O.S., SEED.

She looked down at him from the top of the stepladder.

"And you?"

He looked at the board. At the long white wall of names, from the first column to the last. At the line about the fire getting colder.

"Write my name too," said Yuanshu.

He wrote the sentence for Okafor at dawn, on the vault steps, on the back of one of Fat Tan's ledger sheets, with Xiaoyu asleep against his shoulder and the lamp over the board going pale in the morning.

He crossed it out four times. The fifth time, he left it.

Article Eleven. Whoever asks another what they want shall say, before the answer is given, what will be done with it. A question asked in order to take is not a question, and no answer given to it binds the one who gave it.

It is too long.

Okafor said she'd argue about the commas.

She will. I am looking forward to it.

Yuanshu. Do you know what you did there?

What?

You wrote a rule for the asker.

Every rule before this one was for the asked. Rule One. Article Three. Article Seven. Article Ten. All of them were about protecting the one who answers.

This one is about you. About anyone who asks. It says: you have to tell them what you are going to do.

You have always done that. You told Fang Lili. You told Major Zhuo. You told the twelve at Kesse that they would not come back.

You just never wrote it down.

He looked at the sheet of paper for a while.

Then he folded it, and put it in his pocket, and sat on the vault steps of the old banking hall in Lingzhou, under the long white wall with all the names on it, with his sister asleep against his shoulder and Old Seven standing by the bronze door, and watched the morning come in through the high windows, until the lamp over the board went out by itself.
