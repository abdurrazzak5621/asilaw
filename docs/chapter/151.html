Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 151Behind the Curtain

czverse11 min read

Ifeoma Adeyemi did not fly.

"I have been on an aeroplane once," she said, on the jetty under the cliffs of Concordia, while two of her household lifted her wheelchair down into the boat. "In the year your grandfather was born. It was a very long way above the water and I did not like it. I go by sea."

The boat was white, and narrow, and old, with a blue stripe along its side and no engine at all. It had belonged to her husband, she said. He had been a fisherman. He had been dead for a hundred and forty years.

When they were all aboard, Yuanshu and Lingyue and the Spear Corps standing in rows on the deck like cargo, Old Seven in the bow, Adjutant Xu sitting on a coil of rope with his cane across his knees, the oldest King on Blue Star put her thin hands flat on the blanket over her knees and closed her eyes.

The sea picked the boat up and carried it south.

Not a wave. A current. A long, smooth, deliberate push of water under the hull, the way a hand pushes a toy boat across a bath, and the boat went with it at a speed that made the Mo twins hold on to the rail and Hu Dabao go a delicate shade of green.

Forty-one knots.

She is doing this with her eyes shut.

She has been doing it for a hundred and ninety years.

Yuanshu. I would like to learn this. I do not think I can. I am going to watch anyway.

Seed watched for two days.

It had never been very good at water. It said so itself. It had been born in a cemetery, and grown up in a city, and learned what it knew from graves and stones and fire and a black glass plain where a river of the dead had weight. Water moved too much. Every number it took kept changing while it was taking it.

On the first night it tried to work out how Ifeoma was doing it, and got it wrong.

She is pushing the water behind the boat.

"No, child," said Ifeoma, without opening her eyes. She was sitting in the stern in the dark, with her wheelchair lashed to the deck and the green headwrap pulled down over her ears against the wind. "If I pushed the water behind the boat, I would have to push the whole sea." Her thin mouth moved. "I ask the water in front of the boat to move out of the way. It is much less work. Water is very lazy. It likes being asked to do less."

...that is backwards.

"Yes."

That is the most backwards thing I have ever heard.

It is also, I think, correct.

I am going to need to think about this until we land.

"Good," said Ifeoma Adeyemi. "That's what the sea is for."

The message came at three in the morning on the second night.

The long way. Through the echo. The keeper caught it and the Elder carried it down the gold thread, and it came to Lingzhou, to the old banking hall where the one lamp burned over the board, and through Lingzhou to Seed, two thousand kilometers south, on a white boat in the dark.

Yuanshu was awake. He had been sitting by Old Seven in the bow, not talking. Lingyue was asleep against the cabin wall with both swords across her lap. Adjutant Xu sat on his coil of rope, facing north.

The satellite phone on the deck beside him rang once. Xiaoyu, in Lingzhou, in her pajamas, at three in the morning. She did not say hello.

"It's here," she said. "Seed, read it. I've got the marker."

Reading.

It took nineteen seconds. It is the longest one.

Day 209.

Worlds asked: 37. Burning low: 24.

The voice behind the curtain came back.

It did not ask me who I am this time. It knows. It asked me if the world that asks still asks.

I did not answer. The Sixth told me not to.

So it asked the men.

It asked the six on the curtain, one at a time, very politely. It asked them the question. Our question. The captain's question. It said: what do you want?

Five of them did not answer. The sergeant told them not to, and they are good soldiers.

Corporal Liang Fu answered.

He said: to go home.

The voice said: then show me the way.

And he went behind the curtain.

He did not come back.

The sergeant went to the curtain after him. He stood there for a whole day and called his name. Nothing answered. Not the voice. Not Liang Fu. Not anything.

The sergeant is standing on the curtain himself now. With the five. He asked them if they wanted to stay there. All five said yes.

I am sorry. I am sorry. He was very kind to me. He taught me a card game with no queens in it, so I could not cheat.

I still have the queen of hearts.

Tell the captain. — H.

Nobody said anything.

On the phone, two thousand kilometers north, he could hear the squeak of a marker on plaster. Xiaoyu, on the stepladder. Writing it in careful capitals. Then the squeak stopped, and there was a sound she made that she did not want him to hear, and then she said, very steadily, "Done. I'm going to go and sit down now."

"Xiaoyu."

"I'm fine." A pause. "Ge. I didn't know him. I never met any of them. But I've been writing their names on the wall for six months." Another pause. "I'll put a card on the floor under it. With no queens in it."

She hung up.

Old Seven had not moved.

He stood in the bow of the white boat with his spear grounded on the deck, facing north, toward the end of the world, where a small white ring hung every morning and asked its field what it wanted. His gray flame had gone very small.

Yuanshu did not ask him anything. He waited.

He says he knew Liang Fu.

He says that is not true. He says he did not know him alive. Liang Fu was Third Battalion and Old Seven was the Eastern Line and they died decades apart.

He says: but he stood next to him on the raft. In the throat. Forty-three days. He says Liang Fu was the one who always sat on the left side of the raft because his right knee was bad, which is a strange thing for a skeleton to care about, and nobody ever told him so.

He says that counts.

"It counts," said Yuanshu.

He says Huo is doing the right thing. He says he would do the same. Stand on the curtain himself. Ask the five if they want to stay. Not order them.

He says: tell the sergeant he is proud of him.

He says: there is no way to tell the sergeant anything.

He says: tell him anyway.

"Seed," said Yuanshu, very quietly, so as not to wake Lingyue. "What is it?"

I do not know.

I want to be careful. I want to tell you only what I know, and then what I think, and keep them apart.

"Go on."

What I know.

It is inside the Mouth. It is not burning. It is not a world and it is not a Hunger. It is behind the fire, the way Hush says, which means it is in the part of the Mouth that is not eating. The quiet part. Where Hush hides.

It has been looking for Blue Star for a long time. It said so.

And it used our question.

He felt it then, under the words. Not a number. Something that Seed did not have a name for, because it had never needed one.

Yuanshu. We taught the keeper to ask.

You gave it the question. I wrote the shape of it, line by line, so it could hold it. What do you want. Because I thought a thing that asks cannot take. Because that is what you taught me.

And something in the dark asked a tired corporal with a bad knee what he wanted, and he said home, and it took him.

It used the question to take.

I did not know that was possible. I am not sure I did not help.

"You didn't help."

You do not know that.

"No," said Yuanshu. "I don't." He looked out at the black water going past at forty-one knots. "But the question didn't take him. The thing that asked it did. A question's just a door. You can hold a door open for someone. You can hold it open so they walk into a room you've locked from the outside." He was quiet. "The Abbot does it. He asks people what they want and they say to be kept, and he takes them to something that keeps. That's not asking. That's fishing with a question for bait."

...then what is the difference?

Between us and that?

He thought about it while the boat ran on through the dark.

"What happens after," he said. "If you ask someone what they want, and they tell you, and then you do something they never asked for, it wasn't a question. It was a trap. Major Zhuo told me what he wanted, and I said no, to his face, and told him why. That's not the same thing as saying show me the way, and closing the curtain." He looked north. "Liang Fu said home. The voice didn't take him home. It took him behind the curtain. That's how you know."

...I am writing that down.

Under Article Ten.

Under: your yes is yours. It stops at someone else's window.

New line. A question is not a question if you already know what you are going to do with the answer.

What I think.

I think it is the gardener's. Or it is the gardener. Or it is something the gardener is afraid of. I do not know which, and those are three very different things.

I think Liang Fu knew the way home, because every one of the sixty-two knows the way home. They were raised here. They walked out of Blue Star on a raft. If you asked them, any of them could point.

And I think a voice that has been looking for Blue Star now has a corporal who can point.

The wind came cold off the sea.

He thought of the small white ring over the end of the world, that had learned to ask from him and asked every morning now, and meant it. He thought of a shape behind a curtain in the quiet part of a fire, very old, very polite, asking a soldier with a bad knee where he would like to go.

"How long?"

I do not know.

The Mouth is far. Its messages take longer to come every month. The last one took nineteen seconds of my time and a month of its.

If the voice can steer it, it cannot steer it fast. If it cannot steer it, it will need another way.

I do not know which. I will not pretend I do.

But Yuanshu. I would like Hush to come home.

I have wanted it every month. I have not said it, because there is nothing we can do, and saying it does not help.

I am saying it now.

"I know," said Yuanshu. "Me too."

Lingyue was awake.

He did not know how long she had been. She was sitting against the cabin wall with her eyes open and both swords on her lap, watching him with the face she used when she had heard all of it and was deciding which part to say something about.

"You'll tell the Council," she said.

"In the morning."

"And then you'll go to Port Abiri and fight a seed on a hill full of graves." She pulled her knees up. "And then I'll go to Yamato with Ren and fight the Abbot. And then we'll both come home and be told there's a voice in the sky asking our dead for directions."

"Probably."

"Good," said Lingyue. "I hate it when it's simple. It makes me think I've missed something." She held out her hand without looking at him, and he took it, and she leaned her head back against the cabin wall and closed her eyes again. "Wake me up if anything on this boat starts asking questions."

"Everything on this boat asks questions."

"Then don't," said Shen Lingyue, and went back to sleep.

At dawn on the third day, the coast of the Southern Compact came up out of the sea.

A long line of white towns with blue shutters. Green hills behind them. And in the middle of the coast, a bay he knew: a curve of pale beach and a harbor full of fishing boats and, above the harbor, a hill of graves, where a woman in a black dress was already climbing the path, slowly, in the early light, with a basket on her arm.

Ifeoma Adeyemi opened her eyes.

The boat slowed. The current let it go, gently, the way a hand lets go of a toy boat at the edge of a bath, and it rocked on the swell a kilometer off the mouth of the bay.

"It's early," said the old King.

He followed her eyes.

Out past the headland, on the far side of the bay, where the water went dark and deep and the maps showed it black, a ship sat on the sea. A big one. A cargo hauler, rust-red and gray, with no flag and no name, riding low in the water. It was not moving. It had dropped anchor.

Directly over the place where, a year ago, a spire had gone down into the sea.

"Six days early," said Ifeoma Adeyemi softly. "It didn't come here to land, child. It came here to go back down."

On its deck, very small, at this distance, a crane was swinging out over the side. And from the end of the crane, something gray was being lowered, slowly, into the deep water. Toward its mother.

It is humming.

Yuanshu. It is humming very loudly.

And something down there is humming back.
