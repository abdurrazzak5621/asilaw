Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 92The Last One

czverse10 min read

The Elder brought the raft down onto the husk as gently as a leaf settling on still water.

It took six hours. Out of the current. Across the dark. Toward the gray world, which grew and grew until it filled half the sky, then all of it, a vast cracked bowl of ash turning slowly beneath them. Down. Toward the dark side. Toward one deep crack, kilometers wide, that ran down from the gray surface into a blackness so total that even Seed could not see the bottom of it.

The raft settled on the lip of the crack, a few meters from the edge.

Nobody stepped off.

"The air of the raft will hold a little way past its edge," said the Elder. "Not far. A few meters. The ground here has no air. No warmth. Nothing."

"The living must stay on the raft."

"I'm going down," said Yuanshu.

"You will die."

"No, he won't," said a dry, brisk voice from the small white skull at the top of the staff. "Not if he holds on to me. I can keep a little warmth around one person. I did it for sixty-eight years in the ground. I can do it for a few hours in a hole." A pause. "Don't make it more than a few hours."

Lingyue did not argue.

She stood at the edge of the raft with her sword across her back and watched him walk off the pale ground onto the gray ash of the husk, with the staff glowing softly in his hand and a faint warm shimmer around him like heat over a road. She watched Old Seven go after him. And Hundred-Arrow Lin. And Sergeant Huo, with one arm.

"Three hours," she said. "Then I come down after you. Pale fire. Whether you like it or not."

"I know."

"I'm not asking."

"I know," said Yuanshu.

The crack went down for a long time.

They climbed. The dead did not need light, and he had the staff, and in its soft gray-white glow the walls of the crack went past them, gray and cracked and dead, layer on layer, like the walls of a canyon cut through ash. Sometimes there were spires sticking out of them. Small ones. Broken. Gray crystal, crumbling at the touch, like the spire in the bay at Port Abiri if it had been dead for four billion years.

And sometimes there were other things.

Shapes. Pressed into the ash of the walls. Like fossils in rock. Long. Thin. With many limbs. Curled. Some of them very small. Some of them curled around the small ones.

He did not look at them for very long.

There is nothing in them.

Not even ash. Not even nothing.

They were burned so long ago that even the shape of what was burned has gone.

Old Seven climbed ahead. Steadily. With his skull tilted. Listening. And every so often he would stop, and turn, a few degrees, and climb on, the way he had walked across the field above the sky, toward a sound too faint for anyone but the dead to hear.

At the bottom of the crack, there was a stone.

That was all it looked like. The crack narrowed, and narrowed, until it was a slot barely wide enough to stand in, and at the very bottom of the slot, in the dark, in the ash, there was a stone. Round. Smooth. Gray. About the size of a crouching person. Perfectly still.

Old Seven stopped in front of it.

And knelt. On one knee. The way he knelt at graves.

Yuanshu came down the last of the slot and stood beside him, with the staff glowing, and looked at the stone.

...that is it.

That is the quiet one.

Yuanshu. It is alive.

He knelt beside Old Seven.

He held the staff close. He went quiet. All the way down. And he looked at the stone the way Seed looked.

It was alive.

Barely. So barely that it was almost not. A mind, inside the stone. Or a mind that had become the stone, he could not tell. Curled up tight. Smaller than small. Holding perfectly still. Not thinking anything. Not dreaming anything. Not wanting anything. Not even waiting. Just... being, very quietly, at the bottom of a crack on a dead world, the way a seed is, in the ground, through a winter.

A very long winter.

Four billion years.

Yuanshu. It has been holding still for four billion years.

Since the Hunger came.

It must have gone quiet the moment it arrived. All the way down. Further than the monks. Further than your mother. Further than Nkechi.

So far that it stopped being anything a Hunger could find.

And it never stopped.

He did not know how to reach it.

You could not hum to it. It had never heard a song he knew. You could not ask it anything. It had not heard a question in four billion years. If you called it, if you were loud, it would only curl tighter, the way Nkechi had curled tighter every time the fire roared.

He knelt in the ash at the bottom of the crack in the dark and did not know what to do.

Beside him, Old Seven laid down his spear.

He set it on the gray ash, carefully, the way you'd set down something you would need again. And then he reached out with both his hands, the long white one and the shorter grayer one with three fingers, and laid them flat on the stone.

Not pushing. Not asking. Not anything.

Just there.

The way you'd put your hands on the shoulders of someone sitting very still at a funeral. To let them know someone else was in the room.

They stayed like that. Old Seven did not tire. Neither, it turned out, did the stone.

An hour. Two. Yuanshu did not move. Hundred-Arrow Lin and Sergeant Huo stood further up the slot, two shapes in the dark, keeping watch. The staff glowed. Jiang Lan's warmth held around him, steady, faintly flickering at the edges.

And Old Seven knelt with his hands flat on the stone and his gray flame burning low and warm and steady, and did not do anything at all.

Yuanshu.

Something.

Very small.

Under Old Seven's hands, so faint that Yuanshu would never be completely sure he had not imagined it, the stone grew warm.

Not much. A degree. Less. The way a stone grows warm after a hand has rested on it long enough.

And then something inside it moved.

Not the stone. The thing inside. The mind. Curled up tight for four billion years. Uncurling. A little. Just a little. The way a fist uncurls in sleep.

And a voice came out of it.

It was not a voice. Not like the dead. Not like the Elder's glass note. It was older than either. A sound like the inside of a seashell, like wind across a field of dry grass, so faint that Seed had to reach for it with everything it had.

And it said one thing.

Seed translated it. And when Yuanshu heard what it was, he put his hand over his mouth.

...what do you want?

Hello, the Elder had said. Out on the raft. On the seventh day. They had a word for asking. It was the first thing they said when they met. "What do you want?" Like hello.

It was not a question. Not really. It was a greeting. The oldest greeting on a dead world. The first thing anyone on this world had said to anyone, every time they met, for as long as there had been anyone here.

And this was the last of them. Curled up in the dark at the bottom of a crack for four billion years. And the first thing it said, when someone finally came and put their hands on it and waited long enough, was hello.

What do you want?

He did not know what to say. He knelt there in the ash with his hand over his mouth.

Then he took it away.

And answered. The way you answer a greeting. The way you'd answer anyone.

I want you not to be alone anymore.

The stone was silent.

Then the seashell voice came again. Fainter. Wondering.

...alone.

...yes.

...I have been. A long time. I think.

...it is very hard to remember how long. I stopped counting. Counting is loud.

Four billion years, Yuanshu thought. Very gently.

...ah.

...that is a long time to be quiet.

It told them, slowly. Over hours. In pieces. Seed put the pieces together.

It had been young, when the Hunger came. Not a child. Not an adult. Something between. It had been in a crack, much like this one, with others. Playing a game. Its people had a game about hiding. About being still. About who could be quietest longest. It was very good at the game.

When the ring opened in the sky, and the gray rain came down, and its world began to burn, it had been in the crack, playing. And it had kept playing. Because it did not know what else to do. Because the others in the crack with it were burning, and screaming, and it was so frightened that it held still, and held still, and held still, and did not stop.

...I was winning.

...I thought, if I win, someone will come and tell me the game is over.

...no one came.

Yuanshu could not speak.

Beside him, Old Seven did not take his hands off the stone.

...you came.

...is the game over?

Yes, thought Yuanshu. You won. You won the whole game. You were the best there's ever been.

You can stop now.

The stone was silent for a long moment.

Then, slowly, so slowly that it took nearly an hour, the stone began to change.

It did not crack. It did not break. It softened. The smooth gray surface going faintly translucent, like frosted glass, and then a little clearer, and then clearer still. And inside it, very small, curled up tight, there was a shape.

Long. Thin. Many-limbed. Pale. Like the fossils in the walls of the crack. But not ash. Not gone. Alive. Its limbs folded around itself the way a sleeper hugs their knees.

It was perhaps the size of a cat.

And it opened its eyes.

Many of them. Small. Dark. Shining like wet stones. All looking out through the clouded glass at the old soldier kneeling in front of it with his hands on its shell.

...oh.

...you are dead.

Old Seven inclined his skull. Very slightly. Yes.

...and you came anyway.

...the Hunger eats the dead. It ate ours. It ate everything. Even the embers.

...you came anyway.

Old Seven did not answer. He did not need to.

"We're going to meet it," said Yuanshu, aloud, to the small pale shape in the softening stone. "The Hunger. Not this one. Another. It's coming toward my world. We're going out to meet it before it gets there." He paused. "I don't know if we'll come back."

The many dark eyes looked at him, one after another, as if each wanted its own turn.

...what do you want?

It was not a greeting this time. It was a question.

I want to stop it, he thought. Without burning anyone. I don't know how. Nobody does.

The small pale shape was quiet for a very long time.

...I know some things.

...I watched it. For a little while. Before I stopped watching. Before I stopped everything.

...I know how it eats.

...I know where its fire is.

The many dark eyes blinked. All at once. Slowly.

...and I know the one thing it cannot do.

What?

...it cannot burn what is quiet.

...it can see the dead. It can see the loud. It can see anything that glows.

...but it cannot see me.

...it has never been able to see me. Not once. In four billion years.

...I would like to come with you.

...if you want me to.

He did not have to think about it.

But he asked anyway. Properly. The way he asked everyone. Kneeling in the ash at the bottom of a crack on a dead world, with an old soldier's hands on a softening stone.

Are you sure? You've been safe here for four billion years. Out there, it might find you.

The small pale shape looked at him with all its eyes.

...I have been safe for four billion years.

...I would like to be something else now.

...yes.

Three hours, Lingyue had said. Then she'd come down.

It had been five.

He heard her before he saw her. Footsteps on the gray ash of the slot above, fast, furious, and the soft cold silver glow of pale fire coming down the crack toward them like a lantern carried by someone who had been very, very worried.

The small pale shape in the stone turned all of its eyes toward the light.

...oh, it said, in its seashell voice. ...she is very loud.

"Yes," said Jiang Lan dryly, from the staff. "You'll get used to her."
