Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 8Bonefield Hollow (Part 1)

czverse8 min read

Bonefield Hollow was a canyon forty kilometers east of Lingzhou, two kilometers long and three hundred meters deep, and its walls were not made of stone.

They were made of bones.

From the lip of the canyon at dawn, it looked like a crack in the earth packed with old snow. Then the light came up, and you saw what the snow was. Rib cages as long as bridges, fused into the cliffs. Skulls the size of collapsed houses, staring out of the walls with sockets you could have parked a truck in. Spines running down the slopes in white zigzags, like frozen rivers. Everything packed together so tightly, over so many years, that it had turned into a kind of rock.

A rift had opened here in the first year of the Catastrophe and never fully closed. Whatever had fought inside it had been fighting for a very long time.

Yuanshu stood at the mouth of the canyon with Old Seven beside him and a tablet in his hand. The wind coming out of the hollow smelled of chalk and cold dust.

Grade-two dungeon. Average beast level: 15 to 25.

Boss: unknown.

Previous clears by Lingzhou teams: eleven. Previous clears by a single awakener: none.

Law residue density: very high. Mostly Death. This canyon is a library.

"You're not going in alone," said a voice behind him.

Hu Dabao came jogging up the gravel road, red-faced and sweating, with a brand-new tower shield strapped to his back. It was very nearly as tall as he was. The price sticker was still on the rim.

"My mom sold the cart," he panted, before Yuanshu could say anything. "Well. The old cart. She bought a new cart and this shield, with the A-rank grant from the city. She says if I let you walk into a bone canyon by yourself, she'll put me in the dumplings."

"Dabao. This is a test. Master Su said fifty undead and no help."

"She said no help," said Dabao. "I'm not help. I'm a wall. Walls don't count."

That is not logically valid.

It is also very difficult to argue with.

Yuanshu sighed. "Fine. Stay behind Old Seven. If I say run, run."

"I never run." Dabao said it with total seriousness, one hand on his heart. "It's in my talent description. Unbroken Wall. I looked it up."

"Then I won't say run."

They had taken three steps into the canyon when the sky came down in front of them.

A beam of white-gold light slammed into the gravel twenty meters ahead. There was no sound for a split second, and then a huge, flat crack, and a wave of hot air that blew Dabao's hair back. When the light cleared, the gravel where it had landed had melted into a shallow bowl of glass, still glowing at the edges, ticking as it cooled.

In the middle of the bowl stood Shen Lingyue.

Slim sword at her hip. Black hair tied high. The dark training uniform of the Shen family, not a crease out of place. She looked down at the steaming glass crater she was standing in, and then up at the two of them, as if they were the ones who had arrived in an unusual way.

"The Shen family holds a clearing permit for this dungeon," she said. "You're on my list."

Yuanshu looked at her. "Master Su sent you."

"Master Su told my grandfather you were walking into a grade-two dungeon alone with a three-day deadline. My grandfather told me." She stepped out of the crater. Her boots crunched on the cooling glass. "Nobody sent me."

Her breathing is fast. There is dust on her boots from the road, not from the sky.

She ran most of the way here. Then she flew the last part so she could land like that.

"This is a test," Yuanshu said. "Fifty undead. No help."

"I'm not help," said Shen Lingyue. "I'm competition. I'm going to hit Level 20 before you."

Dabao looked from one of them to the other with the expression of a man watching a very good television drama.

Yuanshu considered arguing.

Then he considered the odds that arguing with Shen Lingyue had ever worked for anyone.

Approximately zero.

"Fine," he said. "Try to keep up."

The corner of her mouth moved. Very slightly. It might have been the first time anyone in Lingzhou had seen it happen.

The first kilometer belonged to the crawlers.

They heard them before they saw them. A dry, rustling patter from inside the walls, like a thousand fingernails tapping on a thousand tables. Then the walls began to move.

They came out of the gaps between the bones. Things like centipedes, each as long as a bus, built out of vertebrae: hundreds of linked white segments, rippling, with sharpened ribs for legs and a pair of mandibles at the front that opened and closed with a sound like garden shears. They poured down the canyon walls on both sides. Hundreds of them. They had no eyes. They hunted by the shaking of the ground, and three people walking down a canyon of loose gravel made the ground shake a great deal.

"Formation," said Yuanshu, and raised his hand.

[Calamity Raise]

The canyon floor was nothing but bones. He chose in less than a second.

Forty-nine skeletons clawed their way up out of the gravel around them: the remains of smaller rift beasts that had died in this canyon over forty years. Lizard things. Dog things. Things with too many joints that nobody had ever named. Pale lights flickered on in their eye sockets, and without being told, they spread out into a ring around the three living people, shoulder to shoulder, facing out.

Old Seven walked to the front of the ring and lowered his spear.

The crawlers hit from both walls at once.

Dabao planted his tower shield at the left side of the ring, and the first crawler smashed into it headfirst with a sound like two cars colliding.

He slid back half a step. His boots dug two furrows in the gravel.

Then, astonishingly, he set his feet again, and laughed.

"It's so heavy!" he yelled over his shoulder. "Bro! It's so heavy! I'm fine!"

On the right, Shen Lingyue drew her sword.

She didn't seem to swing it. She just moved, a blur of gray uniform and black hair, and a line of white-gold light passed through four crawlers in a row. For a heartbeat they kept coming. Then each of them came apart, segment by segment, in a neat diagonal line, like a sentence someone had drawn a line through. She landed on the far side of them, light, balanced, and did not look back.

Light Law. SSS Dawnbreaker.

The first time I have seen a Light Law used up close. It is extremely clean. Extremely fast.

And extremely hostile to your undead.

She had been right next to three of his skeletons when she struck.

They didn't burn.

Yuanshu felt it through the threads: a wash of heat across three of his soldiers, enough to scorch their bones gray, nowhere near enough to end them. She had bent the edge of her light around them. Deliberately. Mid-swing.

She glanced back at him over her shoulder.

"I don't kill allies," she said, and went back to cutting.

It took them four hours to clear the first kilometer.

Four hours of noise. The patter of crawlers in the walls. The clang of mandibles on Dabao's shield. The thin, singing hum of Shen Lingyue's sword. Old Seven at the front of the ring, never stepping back, his spear moving ankle-knee-hip-spine-shoulder-elbow-wrist, over and over, the same thrust a thousand times, each one perfect. And the skeletons around them, shifting, closing gaps, moving as one creature.

When the last crawler came apart, the canyon floor was a field of white segments. The new shield on Dabao's arm had three deep gouges across its face, and the price sticker was gone. Shen Lingyue had a single thin cut across one cheekbone, which she seemed personally offended by.

Yuanshu sat down on a fallen rib the size of a bus.

Level 14 → Level 18.

Undead lost: zero.

You did not lose a single raised soldier.

Every time one of them was about to break, you moved it. And every time you could not move it fast enough, Old Seven or Hu Dabao or Shen Lingyue was already there.

I want to understand this. You were not commanding them as a group. You were commanding them as one body.

That's what a line is, Yuanshu thought. Old Seven taught me that.

On the next rib over, Shen Lingyue was cleaning her blade with a white cloth.

"Level?" he asked.

"Nineteen," she said, without looking up.

He didn't say anything.

She looked up. Her eyes narrowed.

"How did you know?"

"I didn't say anything."

"You were thinking it," said Shen Lingyue, "very loudly."

Flat on his back on the gravel with his shield on his chest, Dabao started to laugh so hard he began to cough.

They made camp where the canyon narrowed, at the start of the second kilometer.

Here the walls rose higher and closer together. The bones in them were older, gray and black instead of white, and the light from the sky came down in a single thin strip far overhead. Dabao got a small fire going in a ring of vertebrae. Shen Lingyue sat on the far side of it with her sword across her knees, eyes closed, not quite asleep.

Yuanshu sat with his back against an old skull the size of a tent and looked down the canyon, toward the dark.

Something was down there. He could feel it the way you feel a storm coming before the clouds arrive: a pressure behind the ears.

There is a law signature ahead. Very large.

Death Law. But wrong. Mixed with a rift core.

Something is using the bones of this canyon the way you do.

Without asking.

At the edge of the firelight, Old Seven turned his skull toward the dark.

Slowly, he lowered his spear to guard. The way a soldier does when he has seen the enemy and knows exactly how far away it is.

Then, in the walls of the canyon, the bones began to creak.

Quietly at first. A tick. A groan, like an old house settling at night. Then more of them, and more, up and down both walls, from the floor to the thin strip of sky, until the whole canyon was creaking at once.

All of the bones. All together.

Like something enormous, breathing in.
