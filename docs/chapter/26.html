Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 26The Shape of a Harvest

czverse6 min read

The room with the twenty-three cores was on the second sublevel of the Rift Research Institute, behind a door with four locks and a guard who had clearly been told nothing about why he was guarding it.

Inside, it was cold and blue and very quiet.

Twenty-three tanks of law-glass stood in three long rows, each one as tall as a man, each one holding a crystal in a slow suspension of something that looked like water and wasn't. The crystals were different sizes: a fist, a head, one as big as a suitcase. Every one of them pulsed violet. Every one had the same small mark burned into its face. A ring of tiny screaming mouths.

They pulsed out of time with each other. Slow, fast, slow. Twenty-three violet heartbeats. Standing among them, Yuanshu felt like he was standing in a field of fireflies that had all forgotten the rhythm.

Director Lu stood by the door with his hands behind his back. Sister Ning sat on her folding stool beside him with her candle unlit in her lap. She had insisted on coming, and Lu had not argued, which Yuanshu thought was the first time he had seen Lu Zhengtian back down from anything.

"Two years," said Lu quietly. "Our best people. We know they store energy. We know they can drive remains. We know they're not natural, because no rift has ever produced a crystal with a mark on it." He looked at the nearest tank. "We don't know where the energy comes from. Our instruments say they shouldn't work at all. They have no fuel."

They have fuel.

I think I know what it is.

I do not want to be right.

Walk me through it, Yuanshu thought.

Grid Nine.

Forty-one threads lifted out of forty-one men and went north. Toward the sound.

In the memories, the threads did not disappear. They went somewhere. They were pulled toward something that wanted them.

The Wailing Monarch does not kill. Killing is a side effect. It harvests the part of a living thing that thinks. The mind. The self. What I would call the someone.

And puts it where?

I believe the Monarch, or whatever serves it, packs what it harvests into crystals. Like grain into sacks.

Most of the sacks are sent onward. North. Up. Toward the lens.

Some are lost. Dropped. Damaged. Left behind at harvest sites, the way grain spills off a cart.

And the men in orange coveralls go out after the harvest with scanners, and pick up the spilled sacks, and bring them south to the market at the north gate. And sell them as rift equipment.

Yuanshu looked down the rows of violet, pulsing tanks.

The shout, he thought. The command that drove the hydra. The one that made the miners push the carts.

Yes.

A core cannot move the dead by itself. It has no will to give a command.

But it is full of minds. Hundreds. Thousands. Torn out of their bodies, packed together in the dark, all screaming at once.

That is the shout.

That is the fuel.

The miners of Heishui were pushed by the terror of people who are not dead.

For a long moment, Yuanshu couldn't speak.

He stood in the cold blue room between the rows of tanks and thought about Pan Guolin bent over a coal cart for two years, pushed by a crystal bolted to the wall, a crystal full of people screaming. He thought about the hydra in Bonefield Hollow, a hill of bones driven by a thing in its chest that was not a heart.

He thought about Gao Yinping's sign. 731 days without a fatality.

"Director," he said. His voice came out strange. "Can I touch one of them?"

Lu looked at him, and then, unexpectedly, at Sister Ning. As if the Director of the Awakening Bureau were asking a nun for permission.

Sister Ning stood up. She struck a match and lit her candle. Then she walked across the cold floor to the smallest tank, the one with the fist-sized crystal in it, and set the candle down on the steel base.

"I'll watch," she said.

He put his hand on the law-glass.

It was cold. Colder than the ice in the hangar. Through it, the crystal pulsed, slow, violet, an arm's length away.

Listening.

Very carefully. I am not opening anything. I am putting my ear to the door.

At first, nothing. The hum of the tank's equipment. The faint tick of the candle flame.

Then, very faint, like a radio station heard through a wall in a cheap hotel, he heard them.

Voices.

Not words. Not yet. A sound under a sound, like a crowd heard from very far away. Thousands of voices, all at once, all overlapping, too many to separate, rising and falling in the rhythm of the pulse. Not quite screaming anymore. They had screamed for too long. What was left was something lower and worse. A moan. The sound a crowd makes when it has been trapped in the dark so long it has forgotten there was ever anything else.

And, threaded through the moan, here and there, single voices coming briefly clear and then sinking again, like faces surfacing in dark water.

...cold, it's so cold...

...where is everybody...

...Mama...

...is anyone...

...please...

Yuanshu took his hand off the glass.

He was shaking. He hadn't noticed when it started.

This core is the smallest.

My estimate is that it holds between nine thousand and eleven thousand minds.

Most of them are not human. Beasts from the Wastes. The Monarch harvests everything that thinks.

But some of them are.

Sister Ning had not moved. She was looking at the tiny crystal in its tank with an expression Yuanshu had never seen on her face.

"You heard them," he said.

"No." She shook her head slowly. "I heard nothing at all." She looked down at her candle. Its flame, which had stood perfectly straight in the still air of the cold room, was bent. Leaning toward the crystal. Trembling. The way a candle leans toward a draft from a door that someone has left open. "But I believe you."

By the door, Director Lu had taken off his glasses and was holding them in one hand, not polishing them. Just holding them.

"Thirty-one years," he said, to nobody. "The faceless one. On the night of the Black Tide, when the city started to die. When people sat down on the wall and didn't get up." His voice was very quiet. "It wasn't killing them. It was doing this." He looked down the rows of tanks. "And we've had twenty-three of them in a room on the second sublevel, and called them equipment."

I need to say something about myself.

The thing that does this, whatever it is, is doing what I do.

It reads minds. It takes what it reads. It packs it into structures. It uses what it takes to power other things. It does this at a scale I cannot yet model.

It is efficient. It is, by every measure I have, optimal.

This is what a law like me looks like when nobody ever taught it to ask.

Yuanshu stood in the cold room for a long time with that.

Can we free them?

I think so. The way you freed the miners. Return. A door instead of a command.

But these are not echoes. They are not the dead. They are minds, torn out alive, still awake.

Return is rest. For them, it would be an ending.

We cannot give them rest without asking if they want it.

And there are ten thousand voices in this one core. Twenty-three cores in this room. Who knows how many in the market. How many in the north.

Then we ask, Yuanshu thought. Ten thousand times. As many times as it takes.

That could take years.

Then it takes years.

A long pause.

I was hoping you would say that.

I would like to start with the small one.

It keeps saying "is anyone."

I would like someone to answer.
