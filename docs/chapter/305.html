Book 5: The Empty House

Chapter 305Downhill

czverse11 min read

On the twenty-second day, the carry road began to slope.

He did not notice it at first. There was nothing to notice it by. The worn pale cable ran on into the dark the way it had run for three weeks, straight, smooth, older than anything, and the Collector went along it, and he and Old Seven went with it, and the gray threads came in from the sides every day or two, and the Collector slowed at each one, and reached out, and left something small and warm where the thread joined, and went on. Thirteen of them now. Thirteen laters.

And then, somewhere in the twenty-second day, he felt the road begin to go down.

Not down. There was no down. There was no up out here; Seed had stopped trying to say which way anything was a fortnight ago. But it felt like down. The way the floor of a long corridor feels when it has been very slightly tilted, so slightly you cannot see it, and you only know because your feet start to go a little faster than you meant them to.

He knew that feeling. He knew it better than anything. He had made it. In a cemetery, at three in the morning, the first time the door in his chest had opened a crack: the ground round him tilting, very slightly, toward him, so that everything dead within reach began to run downhill. He had made it again in the north, and at the Western Pass, and on a beach, and in the deep water. He had learned to make it five kilometers wide and then ten. The Field.

This was someone else's.

Yes. I feel it too. I have been feeling it for three hours. I did not say, because I wanted to be sure.

I am sure.

It is a Death Law. Not yours. The slope is the same shape as your Field. The same grammar. It runs downhill toward one place, the way yours runs toward you.

But it is not yours. And it is very large.

How large?

I do not know.

I am telling you first: I am not going to find out.

He waited.

Your Death Law is at 59.4%. The wall is just past 59.9. I can feel it in front of us. I told you about the cup.

If I look at this properly, I will start to understand it. I cannot help that. Understanding is what I do. And if I understand it, your Law will go up, because a Law goes up when it is understood.

It will go up past the wall.

The cup will not hold more water. It will break.

So I am not going to look.

He went along the carry road on the slope that was not his, and listened to Seed not look.

It was strange. He had never felt Seed do it. Seed looked at everything. That was what it was; that was what his mother had built in a basement out of a harvest crystal and four thousand yeses: a thing that could not see something without counting it. He felt it now, at the edge of everything, turning its models away from the slope the way you turn your face away from a fire, one by one, deliberately. Closing them. Not that. Not that. Not that one either.

It is very difficult, said Seed. I would like you to know that it is very difficult.

It is like being told not to read a page that is open in front of you.

I am going to keep doing it. You will have to tell me if I stop.

How will I know?

The number will move.

The Collector had gone quiet. It had not spoken since the thirteenth gray thread. That was not unusual. It did not speak much; it was very old and very slow and it had kept most of what it had ever wanted to say. But he could feel it, all round them, the warmth of it, and the warmth had changed. Drawn in. The way a person draws in their shoulders walking into the wind. And in the middle of all the wants it carried, the very old, very small, very faint one in the emptier place at the center had begun to pull harder.

He could feel it pull. Like a needle. Straight down the slope.

Are you all right? he thought. To it. He did not know if it could hear him think; it had always heard Xiaoyu speak. But it had heard him on the north wall, and on the first orchard's hill.

For a while, nothing.

I remember this, said the Collector.

Not all of it. The slope. I remember the slope. I remember being on it. Going down. A long time ago. Before I kept anything.

I remember that I stopped.

I remember that I turned round, on the slope, and went back up it. And all the others went on down.

Then it did not say anything else, and he did not ask.

On the twenty-third day, a mark came from home. Thirty-one hours late.

Square. Black. Whole. He felt it come in under him out of the dark behind, and it was harder to feel than the last one, the way a voice is harder to hear from the far end of a field.

SIX THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AT THE PEAK SHIFT. NINETEEN STONES NOW. THE ELEVEN, AND EIGHT IN THE LAMPLIT WORLDS. ANTHOS. CORRAINE. CORVANE HOLDS AT THEIR DAWN. SO DOES MAELIS. THE ATLANTIC FEDERATION VOTED. PORT HALVARD IS HOLDING. THE MAN WHO SAID LET HIM HOLD HIS OWN SAT DOWN ON THE FOURTH NIGHT.

THE FERRYMAN'S NEPHEW COMES TO THE TWO O'CLOCK.

SECOND ROTA IS FOR THE WALL. THE HOUSES ARE STILL ARGUING. FOUR AND TWELVE. ICHIRO IS WATCHING. LINGYUE IS ON THE WALL AT DAWN AND THE PLATFORM AT NIGHT. BAI LU HAS TOLD HER TO SLEEP. SHE HASN'T.

YOU'VE LOST NINE KILOS.

XIAODENG SAID BA. LINGYUE SAYS IT DOESN'T COUNT.

COME HOME.

He read it twice. Nineteen stones, he thought.

Eleven Steles on Blue Star. And eight stones in the Federation, on worlds where someone has sat down in the stone since the Assembly. The one below the House of Anthos, where the man in the gray coat is. The headland at Corraine.

The net does not care where the holder sits. Even across the Star Sea.

Six thousand at a time.

Qin Hao's line said six thousand in a month. It has been three weeks. We have been going faster than he thought.

Because of the slope.

Yes. We are going downhill.

The road is pulling us. Not hard. But it is.

He thought about that for a while. About going downhill toward a place, faster than you meant to, with every day costing more than the one before, and six thousand people at a time on nineteen stones on a small blue world a very long way behind, getting more tired.

How far?

I do not know. I am not looking.

But I can feel the end of the road. I do not have to look at the slope to feel that. It is like feeling a wall in the dark with your hand.

Not far.

They saw the first light on the twenty-fourth day.

Off to one side of the carry road. Small. Dim. Sliding.

He felt Old Seven turn before he saw it. The cold steady ember beside him turning, one-armed, the way it turned on the north wall when something came out of the north, toward a faint gray spark in the dark a little way off the road, going down the slope.

A mind. Picked. From somewhere. Burning low, the way the cousins burned. Not on the cable; beside it, in the dark, loose, the way the cloud at the edge of the deep water had been loose, a light that had fallen out of a hand. And it was going down the slope. Not fast. Not slow. The way a leaf goes down a stream. Toward the end of the road.

It must have been in a hand near here. When the picker stopped.

Every hand in the deep water opened. Most of what fell out was in the deep water, near the face. That was the cloud.

But some of the hands were further along. On the carry road. On their way home. Carrying.

When they opened, what they carried fell out here. On the slope.

And it has been going down the slope ever since. For a year.

There was another one. Further off, on the other side of the road. And another. He could feel them now that he knew to: a thin scatter of dim gray sparks in the dark on either side of the carry road, dozens, a few hundred, sliding, all the same way, all very slowly, toward the place where the road ended.

Where are they going?

Downhill.

The way the dead go downhill in your Field. Toward the door.

This is someone's Field. There is a door at the bottom of it.

He went very still on the cable.

Has anyone asked them?

No.

Nobody has ever asked anyone, on this slope.

I do not think the door at the bottom asks.

He asked the nearest one.

He did not think about it. It was a few hundred meters off the carry road, if meters meant anything here, small and dim and gray, sliding, and he reached out to it the way he would have reached out to anyone in his own Field, down, through the dark, in the shape of a question. The oldest one.

Hello. I'm here. What do you want?

It did not stop sliding. It could not; the slope was not his. But it turned. The way the last child of the first orchard had turned in the hill. Toward the voice.

It says: where am I.

It does not know. It was asleep in a hand. Then the hand opened and it was falling. It has been falling for a year. It thought it was a dream.

It says: is this the house? It heard, in the hand, that it was going home. Everyone in the hand heard that. It was the only thing the hand ever said.

No, he thought. Not yet. Nearly.

There's a door at the bottom. I don't know what's on the other side. Nobody's going to make you go through it. Do you want to?

The spark slid. Turned. Thought, in the slow way of the picked, who had been burning low in a hand for longer than they knew.

It says: I want to go home.

Not the house. Home. Where it came from. A world. It says the name, and I do not know it.

It says: can I?

He looked along the dark, back up the carry road, up the slope, the way they had come. Toward the face of the picker and the web and the ten thousand cables and the thousand orchards at the ends of them. Somewhere out there, on one of them, a world this spark had a name for.

He did not know how to send it there. He did not know which thread was its. He did not have a body or a staff or a stone. He was one boy on a cable in the dark, on someone else's slope.

I cannot find its thread, said Seed. Not from here. Not without looking at the slope, and I am not looking at the slope.

I am sorry.

The spark slid on down the slope, past them, toward the end of the road. Still turned toward his voice. Still asking.

It says: can I?

Old Seven moved.

He felt it. The cold steady ember beside him on the cable stepping off it. Off the carry road. Out into the dark, onto the slope, where the dim gray spark was sliding, and putting himself in front of it.

Not stopping it. He could not stop it either. It was not his slope. But standing in front of it, one-armed, facing up the hill, so that the spark slid into him the way a leaf in a stream slides against a stone, and stopped there, against him, and did not go any further down.

He says: hold on to me.

He says: the boy'll find your thread. Not today. Later.

He says: I've held a wall with one arm. I can hold you.

The spark held on. And Old Seven stood on someone else's slope in the dark at the end of the oldest road anything had ever made, one-armed, with a lost light from a world nobody knew held against his chest, facing uphill, and did not move.

He cannot do that for all of them, said Seed, very low. There are hundreds. And he is one.

He knows.

He says: one at a time.

The Collector had not stopped.

It had slowed, when Old Seven stepped off the road, the way it slowed at the gray threads. But it had not stopped. It could not, now. The needle at the center of it was pulling straight down the slope, and the slope was pulling, and it had been pulled toward this place its whole long life.

And ahead, at the bottom of the slope, where the carry road ended, the dark changed.

Not light. Not a shape. A place. The way, coming down a road at night, you feel a town before you see it: the air different, the sound different, the dark ahead not empty any more. Something very large and very still, where the worn pale cable ran down into it and stopped, the way a river stops at the sea.

I remember this, said the Collector again. Low, the way you speak in a church. I remember the door.

We built a house round it.
