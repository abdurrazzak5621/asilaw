Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 20Sector Nine

czverse8 min read

"Twenty-two," said Su Mingli, "is not enough."

Breakfast at Tianshu was in a long stone kitchen with a real window at one end, cut through forty meters of mountain to let in a single shaft of morning sun. It fell across the table like a spotlight.

Su Mingli had a map of the Northern Wastes spread across the table in the middle of that light, held down at the corners by a teapot, two cups and a small horned skull that Hundred-Arrow Lin had brought back from somewhere and declined to explain.

"Bonefield Hollow was the first step," she said. "Twenty. You did it. But since then a Northern Union King has gone the color of paper at the sight of your shard, someone has been selling branded cores out of the north gate for two years, and Specimen Zero has turned its head." She tapped the black emptiness at the top of the map. "And this week, the Institute's northern sensors started reporting the same pattern they reported thirty-one years ago. Three days before the Black Tide." She looked up. "Something up there is looking down again. So here's the next step. You don't go anywhere near the Wastes until you're at Level 30. Awakener peak. Not one level less."

"How long do I have?"

"However long it takes."

At current rates, in standard dungeons, with all three Law Cores: nineteen days.

Unless something larger comes to us.

The emergency sirens went off before he could answer.

It was not a city siren this time. It was the wall.

Lingzhou's northern wall ran for sixty kilometers along the edge of the Wastes, and it had its own alarm: a long, low horn from the watchtowers, three blasts, then silence, then three more. Every child in the city knew what it meant. Few of them had ever heard it.

Grade 3 incursion. Northern wall. All Defense Corps units respond.

Su Mingli was already standing. "Sector Nine. That's the Academy section." She looked at him. "Principal Yue's wall."

Yuanshu was already moving.

The world folded, and they were on the battlements.

Sector Nine of the northern wall was forty meters of gray stone, old and patched, with the Lingzhou Martial Academy's training grounds spread out behind it and the Northern Wastes spread out in front: a dead plain of cracked earth and black glass, running to a horizon that shimmered like heat on a road.

A kilometer out from the wall, the air was torn open.

The rift was tall and narrow, like a wound made by a knife, and violet light poured out of it in pulses. Out of the light came the beasts. Not wolves. Things like rhinoceroses made of slag and cinders, each the size of a truck, their backs crusted with glowing coals, their footsteps setting the black glass of the plain on fire. Hundreds of them. And behind them, still half inside the rift, something far bigger was pushing its way through: a shape like a hill made of embers, with two horns as long as bridges.

Ashback Behemoths. Level 25 to 32. Approximately 400.

Rift Lord: Cinder Titan. Grade 3. Equivalent to Level 58.

The Corps garrison on this sector is sixty men. Reinforcements are eleven minutes out.

The herd will reach the wall in four.

A broad, bald, sun-browned man in his sixties was standing on the battlements in a faded blue tracksuit, a long curved saber hanging loose in one hand and a paper cone of sunflower seeds in the other. There was an old scar right across the top of his scalp, like a part in hair he didn't have.

Yue Changfeng. Commander of Sector Nine, and principal of the Lingzhou Martial Academy, whose training grounds lay right behind this wall. A Lord at the very peak of his realm. Seed had his file open before Yuanshu's feet finished landing.

He saw them arrive and grinned around a mouthful of seeds.

"Master Su. Mister Zhou." He tossed the sunflower seeds over the wall. "Good timing. I was about to have to do something about this myself, and I've got a bad back."

"Can you hold the Titan?" asked Su Mingli.

"I can hold anything for eleven minutes," said the principal. "Can't hold four hundred of the little ones at the same time." He looked at Yuanshu. "That's your job, I think."

Yuanshu stepped up to the edge of the wall.

"Yes," he said.

He did not call the cemetery. The cemetery was twenty kilometers away and the dead there had done enough.

He called his commanders.

Old Seven was at his left shoulder before the thought finished, spear lowered. Iron Wall Zhao came up out of the shadow of the stairwell with his black shield on his back and set its edge down on the battlements with a sound like a bell. And on the highest point of the nearest watchtower, a figure made of ash and gray smoke drew a bow made of the same, a single iron arrowhead glowing dull red at its tip.

Then he looked down at the plain in front of the wall.

The Northern Wastes were full of the dead. Forty years of rift beasts had died on that ground, and their bones lay under the black glass in drifts.

No wills. Only echoes. Remains.

They cannot say yes or no.

They are not soldiers. They will not mind.

[Calamity Raise]

The black glass of the plain cracked in a line a kilometer long.

And out of it rose a wall of bone.

Two hundred skeletons of beasts that had died on this ground: lizard-things, dog-things, things with too many legs. They clawed out of the earth between the wall and the herd and locked together shoulder to shoulder, and at the center of the line, where the herd would hit hardest, Iron Wall Zhao dropped off the battlements, landed in a crouch that cratered the glass, and set his shield.

The first Ashback Behemoth hit him at full charge.

It stopped.

Not him. The Behemoth. It stopped dead against the black iron shield as if it had run headfirst into the side of a mountain, and the coals on its back flared and went dark, and before it could stagger back, Old Seven stepped around the edge of the shield and put his spear through its eye.

Directing. 200 bones, three commanders.

Feeding Zhao's Shield Intent to the front line.

Feeding Old Seven's Spear Intent to the second.

Lin does not need anything from me.

Hundred-Arrow Lin did not loose arrows. She loosed one arrow, over and over again. It left her string, crossed a kilometer of burning air in a straight gray line, killed a Behemoth through the eye, and appeared back on her string before the body hit the ground. Then again. Then again. She stood on the watchtower with her feet in the same position and did not move anything but her arms, the way she had once stood in a tower above the north gate.

Seed counted for her. Yuanshu heard it in the back of his mind, steady as a metronome.

Thirty-one. Thirty-two. Thirty-three.

The skeleton wall held. Where it cracked, Zhao was there. Where a Behemoth broke through, Old Seven was there. Where a dozen massed together to charge, a single gray arrow found the leader's eye and the charge fell apart.

And Yuanshu stood on the battlements with his training spear in one hand and directed it all, three commanders and two hundred bones and the burning plain, the way you'd play a piece of music you had only just learned and somehow already knew.

Out on the plain, the Cinder Titan finally tore itself free of the rift, raised its horns, and roared.

Principal Yue Changfeng stepped off the wall.

He didn't jump. He just stepped off, in his tracksuit, and walked down the air as if it were a staircase, and his saber came out of its sheath with a sound like a train going through a tunnel. The Titan swung its horns at him. He cut one of them off at the root.

"Keep the little ones off me!" he shouted back at the wall, cheerfully.

The reinforcements arrived at minute eleven. There was very little left for them to do.

The plain in front of Sector Nine was a field of smoking cinders and broken bone. Four hundred Ashback Behemoths lay dead across it. The skeleton wall stood exactly where it had risen, cracked and scorched but unbroken. Iron Wall Zhao had not moved one step backward from the spot where he'd landed.

In the middle of the plain, Principal Yue was sitting on the Cinder Titan's head, wiping his saber on his tracksuit and complaining loudly about his back.

Behemoths killed under your command: 397.

Lin: 104. She would like you to know.

Level 22 → Level 30.

A soft, deep chime sounded in the back of Yuanshu's mind. Not Seed's version chime. Something else. Heavier.

Awakener realm: peak.

Breakthrough available: Elite.

Yuanshu let out a long breath and lowered his spear.

Along the battlements, sixty soldiers of the Lingzhou Defense Corps and a crowd of Academy students who had been watching the entire time from the training grounds were staring at him. Then someone started cheering, and the cheer ran down the wall like fire had run across the plain.

Old Seven walked back up the stairwell, spear on his shoulder, and stood beside him. Iron Wall Zhao came after, his shield scorched black from rim to rim. Hundred-Arrow Lin stepped down off the watchtower and held up one smoky hand with four fingers spread, then one finger alone.

One hundred and four.

She is reminding you. Again.

Yuanshu laughed. "I heard."

It was Seed that noticed first.

Wait.

What?

The breakthrough. It is available. But something else heard the chime.

Yuanshu looked up.

The sky over the Northern Wastes was clear. Hard blue. Not a cloud from horizon to horizon.

And then, very slowly, a single cloud began to form directly above him.

It was not gray. It was not white. It was the dull color of old iron, and it did not drift with the wind. It turned, slowly, in place, like an eye that had just opened and was trying to find its focus.

This is not weather.

It is a tribulation. A Forbidden Law tribulation.

The heavens test every breakthrough. But ordinary breakthroughs do not draw this.

This one is not for your realm.

It is for me.

On the wall beside him, Principal Yue had stopped complaining about his back. Su Mingli had gone very still. Far to the north, beyond the horizon, the air shimmered once, faintly, like light bending through glass.

The iron cloud turned above Yuanshu's head, and found him, and held.

The cosmos has noticed Intelligence again.

And it remembers why it erased it.
