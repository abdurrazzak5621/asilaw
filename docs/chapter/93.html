Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 93Hush

czverse8 min read

The twins named it.

Nobody asked them to. It happened on the first day after the husk, when the small pale many-limbed thing came out of its softened stone for the first time and stood on the warm ground of the raft, swaying slightly, all its dark eyes blinking at the light, as if it had forgotten what light was.

Mo Xing crouched down in front of it. Very slowly. With his hands on his knees.

"What's your name?" he said.

The small pale shape looked at him with all its eyes.

...we did not have names. Not the way you do.

...we had what we wanted. That was the name. It changed every day.

"What do you want today?" said Mo Shi, crouching beside his brother.

The many eyes blinked.

...today I want everyone to be a little quieter.

...you are all very loud. I am not used to it.

The twins looked at each other.

"Hush," said Mo Xing.

"Hush," agreed Mo Shi.

The small pale shape considered this for a long time, swaying on its many thin legs. Then it folded them, all at once, and sat down on the pale ground, and wrapped its limbs around itself.

...yes. That is what I want. I will be that.

Hush did not like to be touched.

Hush did not like loud voices, or sudden movements, or Qin Hao's fire, or Lingyue's pale sword when it was lit, or the sound Bai Lu's brass clock made when it ticked. Hush spent the first three days sitting very still at the edge of the raft between two soldiers of the Third Battalion, inside the ring of the dead, with its limbs wrapped around itself, looking out at the Star Sea.

But Hush liked Old Seven.

It would not go near the living. It would not go near the Elder, whose white light it said was very bright, like a song. But wherever Old Seven stood, at the rim of the raft with his spear, Hush would be a meter away, sitting on the pale ground. Not touching. Just there.

"It's like a cat," said Bai Lu, on the fourth day, watching it. "A very old, very frightened cat. That likes one person."

"It's like Lingyue," said Qin Hao.

Lingyue hit him on the arm without looking up from cleaning her sword.

On the fifth day, Hush taught them about the Hunger.

It did not do it on purpose. It was sitting beside Old Seven at the rim, as always, when Yuanshu came and sat down a few meters away on the warm ground, with the staff across his knees, and looked out at the dark.

And after a long time, without being asked, the seashell voice came out of the small pale shape.

...it is a ring.

He did not move. He did not look at it. He kept his eyes on the dark.

...you know that. You have seen it from far away. A gray ring.

...it is very big. Bigger than my world was. Bigger than yours.

...the ring is not the Hunger. The ring is the mouth.

Seed was listening. So was the Elder, at the center of the raft, very still. So, he realized after a while, was everyone. Lingyue had stopped cleaning her sword. The twins had stopped arguing. Sokolova had put down her notebook. They sat where they were, on the pale ground, not looking at Hush, not moving, the way you sit very still near a bird that has landed beside you.

...the ring opens. Like the lens of your keeper. And the gray rain comes out. Seeds. Like the spire you told me about, in your sea. A seed lands on a world. It grows. It bites. It eats.

...and it sends what it eats back up. To the ring.

...into the middle.

What's in the middle?

Hush was silent.

...fire.

It drew it for them.

With one thin pale limb, on the warm pale ground of the raft, the way a child draws in sand. A circle. Large. And inside it, a smaller circle. And inside that, a dot.

...the ring. The mouth.

...inside, the furnace. Where everything it eats goes to burn. Every world. Every mind. Every ember. All burning together. It is like a sun.

...it is the brightest thing I have ever seen. I saw it once. Through the clouds, from the bottom of my crack, before I stopped looking. It lit up the whole sky of my world. Gray-red. Like a coal.

The thin limb touched the dot at the very center.

...and in the middle of the fire, there is a cold place.

Yuanshu turned his head and looked at Hush for the first time.

Cold?

...a small place. At the very center. Where the fire does not burn.

...I do not know what it is. I could not see it. Only that it was there. A little dark spot in the middle of the sun.

...like the eye of a storm.

The quiet one at the bottom of the spire.

Nkechi. Playing statue.

The one thing the fire could not find.

Yuanshu, there is someone in the middle of the Hunger who is being very, very quiet.

He looked at the dot in the center of the two circles, drawn on the warm pale ground of the raft.

"How do we get there?" said Lingyue quietly.

Everyone turned.

She had put down her sword. She was looking at Hush. Not at its many eyes. At the drawing on the ground.

"How do we get to the cold place?" she said. "Through all of that?"

Hush curled a little tighter.

...you do not.

...the Hunger sees anything that glows. Anything warm. Anything loud.

...you glow. All of you. The living most. You are so loud. You think so much.

...and the dead glow too. Their embers. Your soldiers. Your skeleton.

...and the staff.

...the staff is the brightest thing on this raft. It is like a bonfire.

...if you go near the ring, it will see you from a very long way away.

"And you?" said Yuanshu.

The many eyes blinked.

...it cannot see me.

...I am cold. I am quiet. I am nothing it knows how to find.

...I could go.

Nobody said anything.

Then Old Seven, at the rim of the raft, turned his skull away from the dark. Down. Toward the small pale shape sitting a meter from his feet.

And very slowly, very carefully, he crouched. Down on his heels. The way a tall man crouches to talk to a child.

Hush looked at him with all its eyes.

Old Seven held out his hand. The shorter, grayer one. With three fingers.

Palm up. Open. Not reaching. Just there.

Hush looked at it. The three fingers did not move. Neither did Hush.

Then, slowly, one thin pale limb unwound from around its body. Reached out. And touched, very lightly, the tip of one of the three bony fingers.

And stayed there.

...he says I should not go alone.

...he says nobody should go anywhere alone.

...he says that is what a captain is for.

On the sixth day, they practiced.

Hush taught them. Not on purpose, again. It simply sat in the center of the raft one morning, by Bai Lu's clock, and folded all its limbs, and went still. Not quiet. Still. Further than quiet. Until Seed, watching it, said the small pale shape had stopped showing up at all. Not to the Hunger. To anything. Even to Seed.

...this is the game.

...whoever is still longest wins.

...you are all very bad at it.

They were.

Qin Hao lasted nine seconds. His fire kept wanting out. Bai Lu lasted thirty, and then started worrying about whether everyone else was all right, and lost. Sokolova lasted four minutes, and said afterward, flatly, that she had been thinking about Grid Nine, and that it was very hard not to. Lingyue lasted six minutes, and her face was perfectly calm, and when she opened her eyes she said, very quietly, that she had been thinking about a box in a Ledger the whole time, and that it was not quiet at all.

Yuanshu lasted eleven. The same as on the mountain with the monks.

The Mo twins lasted forty-one minutes.

Together. Sitting back to back on the warm pale ground with their eyes closed and their elbows touching. Perfectly still. Breathing at the same rhythm. Not thinking anything. And when Hush finally said, in its seashell voice, ...you can stop now. You won, they opened their eyes at the same moment, and looked at each other, and grinned.

"We used to do this as kids," said Mo Xing. "In the dark. When our parents were fighting."

"If you're still enough," said Mo Shi, "nobody notices you're there."

Hush looked at them with all its eyes.

...yes.

...that is exactly how it works.

On the eighth day, the Elder stopped the raft.

She did it without warning. One moment they were riding a long silver current through the dark. The next, the raft slowed, and turned, and stopped. Hanging in the black. Not moving.

The Elder stood at the center with her eyes open. Wide open. Silver. Looking ahead.

"There is a field."

"Ahead. In the current. Between us and the Hunger."

"I did not know it was there. It is very small. Very faint."

Yuanshu came and stood beside her and looked.

Far ahead, in the dark, a light. Tiny. A faint soft green, like the inside of a wave. Flickering. Very slightly. On and off. On and off. Like a firefly. Like a heartbeat.

And far, far beyond it, very small, but not as small as it had been, a gray ring.

Turning.

Coming.

"The Hunger will pass it," said the Elder softly. "On its way to your world. It will pass very close."

"It will reach that field in thirty-one days."

"Thirty-one days before it reaches you."

At the edge of the raft, Hush had uncurled. All its limbs. It was standing on its many thin legs, swaying slightly, with every one of its dark eyes fixed on the small green flickering light.

...there are people there, it said, in its seashell voice. ...I can hear them.

...they are very loud.

...they do not know it is coming.
