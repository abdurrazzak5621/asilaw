Book 2: The Star Sea

Chapter 109Burn Bright

czverse8 min read

"No," said Yuanshu.

The Sixth did not argue. They stood in their gold-veined wall on the last screen in the banking hall of the Ossuary, a choir of low gold antlered shapes, and waited, the way they had waited for ten million years.

Everyone else in the hall was very quiet.

...you have not heard it yet, said the Sixth gently.

"I know what it is. I can see it in your faces." His voice was not steady. He let it not be. "You want to let it eat you."

...yes.

They explained anyway.

Because he had asked them to, a long time ago, in a cold place at the center of a dying coal. Ask first. Then decide. They would not decide without telling him everything.

...it is starving, said the Sixth. ...it has fallen a very long way without eating. It will not leave until it eats. If your world hides, it will wait. If your world never makes a sound, it will still wait. For as long as it takes.

...but if it eats something large enough, it will be satisfied. For a while. A Hunger that has eaten well moves on. It always has. It does not stay at a field it has already fed from. It goes looking for the next.

...the Low Fire is very large. A whole ring. A few hundred million minds who have chosen to stay in it.

...if we burn bright. Once. All of us. As bright as we can. Brighter than your world. Brighter than the keeper.

...it will see us. It will turn. It will come.

...and it will eat us instead.

"And then it leaves," said Su Mingli quietly.

...and then it leaves.

"With you inside it."

...with us inside it.

The silence in the banking hall went on.

Then the Sixth said the rest.

...and that is the part you have not heard.

...we have been in the fire before.

...we know what to do there.

Their gold light, on the screen, went very low. Lower than Yuanshu had ever seen it. A deep warm ember-orange, like the coal of the second ring at the end, like a hearth at three in the morning.

...we went into the first fire screaming. Like everyone. And we learned, very slowly, to burn low. And then we went to every world that came after us and asked them to burn low too. For ten million years.

...and the Hunger starved.

...this time, we will not go into the fire screaming.

...we will go in already burning low. Already knowing how. A few hundred million of us. All at once.

...and the moment we arrive, we will start asking.

Wanqing, in her wheelchair, had gone very still.

...the minds in that fire are screaming so loudly that we could never reach them from outside. Through the echoes. We tried. For ten million years.

...but from inside, it is different. From inside, you are close. From inside, you can sit down beside someone in the fire and wait, very quietly, until they notice you are there.

...your Necromancer taught us that. He did it to a girl in a spire. To a child of the Asking World at the bottom of a crack. To billions in our own fire.

...he just sits down beside them, and waits.

...we will do that.

...to every world in the Mouth's fire. One at a time. However long it takes.

"You'll starve it," said Wanqing quietly. "From inside."

...yes. Slowly. Very slowly. Ten million years, perhaps. Perhaps less. There are more of us this time. And we know the way.

...and one day it will be too weak to fall.

...and one day after that, someone will come and find it. Hanging dark in the Sea. Starving. And everyone inside it burning low.

...and they will know what to do.

Nobody said anything.

Then, from the screen with the mountain in Yamato, Shirasagi Ren spoke for the first time.

"The Sleepers," she said. "On my mountain. They sat in silence for twelve hundred years so that someone would remember. So that when the ring came again, there would be someone to tell." She looked at the gold antlered choir. "This is that. Isn't it."

...yes, said the Sixth. ...this is that.

"Not all of you," said Yuanshu.

His voice cracked. He did not stop.

"You can't decide for all of you. A few hundred million. You asked every one of them, in the second ring, if they wanted to stay. They said yes to staying. Not to this. Not to being eaten again." He gripped the staff. "You have to ask them. Every one. The way you asked them to burn low. The way I asked them if they wanted it to stop."

The Sixth looked at him from their gold-veined wall.

Then, very slowly, the choir inclined their long antlered heads.

...yes.

...that is why we told you first.

...we wanted you to be the one who asks.

It took two days.

He went back up the gold thread to the Low Fire on the first morning. The keeper carried him. He stood in the cold place at the heart of the ring with the white staff in his hand, beside a raft where a dead captain stood in a doorway, and he asked.

Every face in every gold vein in every wall of the ring.

The Sixth want to burn bright. To lure the Mouth here. To be eaten instead of Blue Star. And then, from inside the fire, to start asking everyone in it to burn low.

You chose to stay. You didn't choose this.

Do you want to?

You can say no. If you say no, the keeper will carry you to its field. Somewhere cool. Somewhere quiet. Where you can rest, or stay, or go quiet, whatever you want. Nobody will think anything. Nobody will ask you twice.

The great slow turtles took an hour to answer. Every one said the same word, with enormous patience: Yes.

The bright bird-like faces shouted YES all at once so loudly that the walls rang.

The world of minds so strange Seed could not translate them answered with a long breath held, and held, and held.

And in the long low chamber deep in the ring, where a hundred small pale many-eyed faces looked out of the gold, the cousin world of the Asking World answered in voices like the inside of a seashell.

...what do we want?

...we want no one else to sit in the dark for four billion years.

...yes.

Two hundred and eighty-one million: yes.

Nineteen million: no. To the keeper.

Nobody asked them twice.

And Hush.

He found it in the long low chamber, sitting in front of its cousins in the wall, with the queen of hearts propped beside it. Not playing the game. Just sitting.

He crouched down beside it.

"Hush," he said. "Your cousins said yes. They're going into the fire."

...I know. I heard.

"You don't have to go with them."

...I know.

"You can come down. To Blue Star. With me. With Old Seven. There's a city with a wall full of candles, and a teahouse, and an old banking hall with a board that has every name on it. You'd be the first one from out there to see it."

Hush was quiet for a long time.

...I would like that.

...I would like that very much.

...I have been alone in the dark for four billion years. And then I came out. And there were people. And a captain. And cards with red on them. And a world that asks.

...I would like to see it.

It looked at the wall. At a hundred small pale faces looking out of the gold.

...but they are my cousins.

...they are the only ones left who know the greeting.

...and the minds in the Mouth's fire are screaming.

...someone should go and sit beside them. And wait. Until they notice.

...I am the best in the Sea at waiting.

He could not speak.

Behind him, in the doorway of the long chamber, Old Seven had come. He stood with his spear in his three-fingered hand and his gray flame burning very low, looking at the small pale shape sitting in front of the wall.

Hush turned all of its dark eyes toward him.

...captain.

...I said I would come back. When I went into the first fire. And I did.

...this time I do not know if I will.

Old Seven did not move.

Then he walked across the long chamber, and knelt down in front of Hush, on one knee, the way he knelt at graves. And held out his hand. The shorter, grayer one, with three fingers.

Palm up. Open.

Hush climbed into it. And curled up. And stayed there.

For a long time.

The Mouth reached Blue Star at dusk on the fourth day.

It came over the edge of the world from the south, out of the deep dark beyond the stars, and it did not stop at the edge of the sky. It came in. Close. Closer than the first had come to the Lantern world. So close that it filled the whole southern half of the heavens from horizon to zenith, a vast cracked gray wheel of stone, turning slowly, with two coals burning side by side at its center like a pair of eyes.

And on Blue Star, at that moment, in every city and every town and every village and every farm and every boat on every sea, eight billion people heard the same word.

On every radio. On every speaker. On every phone. In the same quiet, sharp, slightly hoarse voice, in every language, translated by a keeper above the north.

"Lanterns."

And the world went dark.
