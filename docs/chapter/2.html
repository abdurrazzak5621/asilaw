Book 1: The Grave Under the City

Chapter 2Seed

czverse8 min read

The Tianshu Institute was not on any map of Lingzhou.

From the road it looked like what the city remembered it as: an old observatory on the top of Mount Qingyun, a white dome gone gray with rain, a rusted telescope slit that hadn't opened in forty years. Pine trees. A gate with no guard. A sign so faded you could only read the word closed.

The black car drove straight past the dome and into the side of the mountain.

Yuanshu did not flinch. The rock in front of the bumper simply wasn't there when they reached it, and then they were in a tunnel lined with pale stone, lit by strips of white light that ran along the ceiling like the lines on a highway.

Su Mingli sat beside him in the back seat, looking out of the window, saying nothing.

Your heart rate is 61 beats per minute.

That is lower than at breakfast. Most people would be afraid right now.

Most people didn't just get a King for a bodyguard, Yuanshu thought. And a voice in their head.

I am not a voice.

I am a law.

The words appeared the way they had in the hall: white letters behind his eyes, calm and precise, as if someone were typing on the inside of his skull.

Not a skill. Not a class. A law, like Fire, or Gravity, or Death. There are three thousand Great Laws in this cosmos.

I am not one of them.

My name is Supreme Intelligence. You may call me Seed.

Laws don't talk.

The other laws do not talk because they do not think.

I am the law of thinking. It would be strange if I could not.

That, annoyingly, made perfect sense.

The car stopped in a stone hall. An elevator was waiting with its doors open. It had no buttons. When they stepped in, it simply began to go down, quickly and smoothly, as if it already knew where Su Mingli wanted to be.

Floors flickered past through a narrow window in the door. Laboratories. A library with shelves that went up out of sight. A white room where someone in a protective suit was holding a small flame in a pair of tongs, studying it.

Forty floors down, the doors opened.

The room beyond was white and bare. One long table. Three people waiting.

An old man in a faded army coat sat at the end, both hands folded on top of a wooden cane. Grandpa Xu, the keeper of the War Memorial Cemetery, who had paid Yuanshu to sweep its paths every summer since he was twelve. Beside him, standing, a broad man in the dark green uniform of the Eastern War Zone, with a general's stars on his collar and a face like a closed door.

And sitting on the table itself, swinging her legs, phone held up in both hands, was a fifteen-year-old girl in a school blazer.

"Ge!"

Zhou Xiaoyu jumped down so fast her phone nearly flew out of her hands. She had their mother's quick dark eyes and none of her brother's patience.

"You broke the Stele! You broke all the Steles! The whole internet is—"

"I saw."

"Eleven cities, Ge. Eleven. It's trending in four languages. Somebody already made a song." She shoved the phone at his face. On the screen, the hall went black over and over in slow motion, set to dramatic drums. "Look at the comments. 'Is this the end of the world.' 'He's kind of handsome though.' Ge, they think you're handsome."

"They think I'm the end of the world," Yuanshu said. "The handsome part is a side effect."

She laughed. Then she looked at his face, really looked, and stopped laughing, and hugged him hard around the middle without saying anything at all.

He put a hand on her head.

Grandpa Xu was not smiling.

He was looking at Yuanshu the way a man looks at weather he has been watching on the horizon for thirty years.

"So it's true," the old man said. "A Calamity class."

"You know what it is." It wasn't a question.

"I know what the last one almost was." Grandpa Xu leaned his cane against the table. His hands were thick and scarred across the knuckles. "Before I swept graves, I was a soldier. I was Lu Xuanming's adjutant. In the Black Tide."

The room went still. Even Xiaoyu stopped moving.

"Everyone thinks he went mad," the old man said. "That he raised the dead to kill. He didn't. Something came down out of the sky that night. Not a rift beast. Something else. He raised the dead of three cities to stop it, and he did stop it, and when it was over the government needed a monster to blame. So they gave him the name." His voice was flat. It was the voice of a man who had said these words to himself many times, in an empty lodge, at night. "He had the ordinary Necromancer class. Common rank. He burned himself to nothing doing something his class was never strong enough to do."

He looked up.

"You're what he would have been, boy. If the Stele had given him what it just gave you."

The general stepped forward and offered his hand. It was the size of a shovel.

"Han Tieshan. Eastern War Zone." His voice was like gravel in a mixer. "Your mother saved my division at the Northern Rift. I owe her my life. That means I owe you. The Bureau doesn't get within a kilometer of you unless they come through my army."

Yuanshu shook the hand. It was like shaking a warm brick.

Seed, he thought. What exactly can you do?

I understand things.

Other beings comprehend laws through insight. They watch fire for years. They sit under waterfalls. They wait for a moment of inspiration. It is slow, and mostly luck.

I do not wait.

I observe a law. I build a model of it. I run the model thousands of times until I can see its structure. Then I give you what I have seen.

How many times?

Currently: about one thousand simulations per second.

That number will grow.

And what do you need?

Data.

A law in use in front of me. A relic that a law has soaked into. Or the dead.

When something dies, the laws it learned in life leave an echo in its remains. Bones remember. A swordsman's skeleton remembers the sword.

You are a Calamity Necromancer. You can reach the dead.

I think we were made for each other.

"Show them your panel," said Su Mingli. "The walls here can read it."

He let it open.

The white wall behind the table lit up from inside, as if the stone itself were a screen, and lines of text slid across it.

Zhou Yuanshu · Level 1

Class: Calamity Necromancer (Unique)

Talent: Supreme Intelligence (cannot be rated) · Seed v1.0

Calamity Raise: raise the dead within one kilometer. Number not limited by class. Limited only by soul-power.

Law Core: forge a permanent core for one of the dead from the laws it mastered in life. It keeps its skills and grows with you.

Domain of the Dead: sealed. Opens at Level 10.

Death Law: 3.0%

General Han read it twice. His lips moved on the second read. "Not limited by class."

"Most Necromancers can raise three," Grandpa Xu said quietly. "Lu Xuanming raised two million, once. It killed him."

Xiaoyu looked from one face to another. "Is that... good?"

Nobody answered her.

May I show you something?

When you touched the Stele, I watched the Law of Death inside it for 2.6 seconds.

I have been running it since we left the hall.

Show me.

The white room disappeared.

He was standing in black. Endless, soundless black, with no floor and no walls, and in front of him hung a shape made of gray light.

It looked a little like a knot. A slowly turning tangle of lines that looped down and around and back into themselves, like a river trying to flow uphill to its own source. It was beautiful in the way a winter tree is beautiful.

Death. A small piece of it.

Then the knot came apart.

It unwound itself into a hundred loose gray threads and hung there, and then wound itself back together, but differently. Then it came apart again. Wound together again. Faster. Ten times. A hundred. The threads blurred. A thousand times, ten thousand, a hundred thousand, until the turning knot was spinning so fast it became a sphere of gray light, and inside the sphere, slowly, like a photograph developing in a tray, a structure appeared.

He saw where the lines began. Where they ended. Why.

Death was not an ending.

Death was a return. Everything a life had learned flowed back into the world when it was over, the way a river flows back into the sea. And if you knew the shape of the path it had taken, you could call it back up the river.

He opened his eyes.

Something warm was running over his lip. He touched it, and his fingers came away red. He wiped his nose on the back of his hand without looking at it.

On the wall, a line of text changed.

Death Law: 3.0% → 12.4%

Insight → Intent

General Han said a word that generals are not supposed to say in front of fifteen-year-olds.

Su Mingli did not say anything. She just closed her eyes, the way someone does when they hear a song they have not heard in a very long time.

"Twelve percent," said Grandpa Xu, very quietly. "Death Intent. Most Necromancers die of old age before they reach it." He looked at the elevator. "You did it on the ride down."

"It was a long ride," said Yuanshu.

Xiaoyu laughed so hard she had to sit on the floor.

Thirty kilometers away, on the forty-second floor of the Awakening Bureau tower in central Lingzhou, a slim man with silver-rimmed glasses sat alone in his office with the lights off.

On the screen in front of him, a line of numbers had just changed. He was not supposed to have access to that screen.

He watched it for a long moment.

Then he took off his glasses, polished them carefully on a cloth, put them back on, and picked up the phone.
