Book 3: The Deep Water

Chapter 207The Eyes of the Mouth

czverse9 min read

He held the door open for nineteen hours, and for most of them he did not know where he was.

It started at the end of the twelfth hour, when the Sixth turned from him and walked out of the quiet part through the curtain, all of them together, a choir of old-brass antlered shapes going out past the line of the Third Battalion into the dark of the Mouth, toward the place where the two rings had joined and the veins ran together in great dark knots like the roots of an old tree. Toward the eyes.

He did not watch them go. He could not. He was already sitting cross-legged on the gray-gold stone of the quiet part with the staff across his knees and his eyes shut, going down.

Into the Death Law. All thirty-nine point two percent of it. To the bottom. To the cool dark door at the bottom of everything, where things went when they were finished.

He opened it.

All the way. Wider than he had ever opened it. Wider than Tannery Square, wider than the gleaners, wider than the four thousand on the Western Pass or Major Zhuo's column in the field. He opened it the way you open the doors of a house when there is a fire and everyone inside has to get out.

And ninety-three parts in a hundred of the walls of the Mouth began to come home.

He would not remember most of it.

He would remember fragments, afterward, the way you remember a fever. The Field around him like a great slope, the steepest it had ever been, running downhill from every black vein in every cold gray wall of a ring a thousand kilometers across, toward the small warm space where he sat. And down the slope, toward him, through him, minds.

Not a stream. A river. A flood. Ten million years of the Mouth's eating, pressed into its walls, cold, and asked, nine by nine by nine, and answering rest. Coming loose from the veins the way frost comes loose from a window when the sun reaches it. Running down the slope of the Field in long faint glittering currents from every direction at once. Into him. Through him. Out through the door.

Gone.

Each one, as it went, said something.

Not to him. He did not think it was to him. To whatever was nearest, at the last, the way a person says something as they go through a door, to nobody, to the room. Most of it he could not understand. It was in languages that had been dead since before Blue Star's sun was lit. But Seed caught some of it. Seed tried to catch all of it, and could not, and caught what it could, and some of it, later, it gave him.

Thank you.

Thank you for asking.

Is it over?

I had a daughter.

Thank you.

Where are we going?

It's quiet.

Thank you.

Hour four.

You are bleeding from the nose and the ears. Bai Lu is with you. Lingyue is holding your hand. You do not know that.

The keeper's thread is in the quiet part. A great many threads. Gold. Coming down from the dark into the gold walls, and the cousins are going up them. Two hundred and six million. And the six parts of the walls who want to find something out. Up the threads. Out of the Mouth. Toward the field.

The keeper is crying. I did not know it could. It says it has never had anyone come to it because they wanted to.

And Yuanshu.

The eyes.

He felt them open.

Even through the door, even through the flood of minds running down the slope through him, even with his own eyes shut and blood running out of them. Somewhere far behind him in the dark of the Mouth, at the place where the two rings had joined, something lit.

Two things.

Two great furnaces at the heart of a dead Hunger, that had been cold for eleven days, that had burned for ten million years with everything the Mouth had ever eaten. Gold. Then white. Then something past white that his mind did not have a color for. Two eyes in a vast wheel of cracked gray stone hanging in the dark of the Sea, opening.

The Mouth's eyes.

He had seen them once, over Lingzhou, filling the sky. Two coals. The most frightening thing he had ever seen.

They were not frightening now.

They were the Sixth.

They are burning high.

All of them. The Sixth, and the seven million cousins who said stay, and the one part in a hundred of the walls.

They are not screaming. I thought they would. Burning high is what the first fire did to them. They went in screaming.

They are singing.

Yuanshu. They are singing the same note. The low one. The one they hummed in the quiet part to keep the cousins warm.

It is so loud.

Every keeper in the Shallows can hear it. Every field. Every world.

And the reader on the far side has stopped reading inward.

It has turned.

It was the eighth hour. Or the ninth. He did not know. He knew that the flood through the door had not stopped, and that his body was somewhere he could not find, and that someone was holding his hand very hard and saying his name every few minutes, once, the way the Quiet say a name, and that somewhere behind him two great furnaces were burning brighter than anything had ever burned in the Shallows, and singing.

And he knew when the reader reached them.

He felt it the way he had felt it from the island, watching a gold light on the far side of the dark go out from one side, like a page burning. Something very large and very patient coming up beside the eyes of the Mouth. Looking at them.

Reading them.

Slowly. From the edge. The way it read everything.

It is reading the Sixth.

They are letting it. They are not hiding. They are not burning low. They are burning as high as they can, singing, and letting it read them one slice at a time.

It is very slow.

It has never seen anything this bright in a Hunger. It wants to read all of it.

It is not reading anything else.

At the fourteenth hour, Huo came through the curtain.

Yuanshu did not see him. Seed told him afterward. The sergeant with one arm came through the curtain from the line where the Third Battalion stood facing out into the dark, and stopped in front of Old Seven, who had not moved from the curtain in fourteen hours, with Hush in his hand.

Huo says: sir. The cousins are nearly gone up the threads. The walls are going through the door. The boy can't hold it much longer. Bai Lu says.

Huo says: the island has to go. While the thing's looking at the eyes.

Huo says: someone has to stand at the curtain and tell the ones in the walls who are slowest that the door is still open, until the last one's through. Or they'll be left. In the cold. With the reader.

Huo says: the Third Battalion has been standing at the edge of the quiet part for four hundred and forty days so the cold came to them first.

Huo says: we can stand here a little longer.

Old Seven did not answer.

Huo says: sir. That's not a question.

He says he learned that from you.

The door stayed open for nineteen hours.

At the end of it, the flood through him was not a flood any more. It was a trickle. Then single minds, very slow, very cold, the last of the walls, from the far edges of the Mouth where the cold was deepest, coming down the long slope of the Field one at a time, the way the last few people come out of a theater after the lights go up. Each of them saying something, at the door, to no one.

And at the curtain, sixty-one skeletons of the Third Battalion, telling each one, as it went past them in the dark, that the door was still open.

The last one through said: Is there anyone else?

And Huo, at the curtain, said: No. You're the last. Go on.

And it went.

He closed the door.

He came back to himself on the pale warm ground of the island, lying on his back, with Bai Lu's hands on his face and Lingyue's hand in his and the brass travel clock ticking somewhere near his head, and the island humming under him, moving, fast.

He did not know how he had got there. He found out later: Hu Dabao had carried him, twenty kilometers through the crack in the Mouth's rim, with his shield on his back and the boy in his arms, and had not stopped once, and had not said anything when he set him down.

He turned his head.

Behind the island, very far now and getting further, a vast wheel of cracked gray stone hung in the dark of the Sea. And in it, two eyes. Burning. White and past white. So bright that the dark around the Mouth was not dark for a thousand kilometers; it was lit, the way the sky is lit over a city at night.

And beside the eyes, something he could not see. Very large. Very patient. Reading.

They are still singing.

I can hear them. From here. Through the keeper.

It is reading them. It has read about half.

They are still singing.

He lay on his back on the pale ground and looked at the eyes of the Mouth burning in the dark behind them, getting smaller.

Beside him, at the rim of the island, Old Seven stood facing backward, toward the Mouth. Not forward. He had never once faced backward on any island or raft or road. In his hand, the small pale shape of Hush had uncurled, and was holding itself up on its many thin limbs on the old soldier's palm, all its dark eyes open, looking at the two great lights in the dark.

It did not say anything.

It held up the queen of hearts. Toward the light. And held it there.

Huo is not on the island.

The Third Battalion is not on the island.

They were at the curtain, telling the last of the walls that the door was still open, when the island had to go.

They knew.

Huo said: that's not a question.

Sixty of them.

Not sixty-one.

Huo sent one back. Through the crack, with Dabao, while Dabao was carrying you. He did not ask him. He told him. He said: you're the only one of us who knows where he wants to go now. Go there.

At the rim of the island, a little way along from Old Seven, a short skeleton of the Third Battalion stood with his weight off his right leg, facing backward too, toward the burning eyes in the dark.

He says the sergeant told him to tell you something.

He says: Shiomi said wait for us at the cold fire.

He says: somebody should.
